# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 3: Documents, Filing & Records
### Lesson 2: Creating and Formatting Professional Documents

**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Plan a business document before formatting it.
2. Apply headings, spacing, lists, tables and page elements consistently.
3. Use templates and styles efficiently.
4. Perform content and presentation quality checks.
5. Prepare a document for appropriate final distribution.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Professional document production is not simply typing. Administrators must organise information so readers can find what they need, apply consistent organisational standards and ensure that the final file remains usable when printed, shared or converted to PDF.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Style** — A reusable set of formatting rules applied to document elements.
- **Hierarchy** — Visual and logical organisation showing the relationship between headings and content.
- **Header/Footer** — Repeating information at the top or bottom of pages.
- **Page break** — A deliberate instruction to start content on a new page.
- **Table** — Rows and columns used to organise structured information.
- **PDF** — Portable Document Format, commonly used for controlled final distribution.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Plan before formatting
Identify purpose, audience, required sections and source information first. Formatting too early can waste time when the structure later changes.

### 2. Use visual hierarchy
A reader should recognise the title, main headings, subheadings and body text. Use consistent heading levels rather than manually making random lines bold and large.

### 3. Keep typography readable
Use approved fonts and sensible sizes. Avoid mixing many fonts, excessive capital letters or decorative effects that reduce readability. Consistency normally looks more professional than complexity.

### 4. Paragraphs, spacing and alignment
Use paragraph spacing deliberately rather than pressing Enter repeatedly. Keep alignment consistent. White space helps separate sections and reduces visual clutter.

### 5. Lists and tables
Bullets are useful for unordered points; numbered lists are useful for sequences or referenced steps. Tables are useful for structured comparisons, schedules and responsibilities. Avoid forcing long narrative paragraphs into narrow table cells when ordinary text would be clearer.

### 6. Page control
Check page breaks, headings stranded at the bottom of pages, blank pages and awkward table splits. Use headers, footers and page numbers according to organisational standards.

### 7. Templates and styles
Use current templates to reduce repetitive setup. Styles make global formatting changes easier and improve consistency. Do not manually reformat every heading when a document tool can apply a defined style.

### 8. Images and visual elements
Use visuals only when they add information. Keep proportions correct and provide captions or labels where useful. Avoid low-quality decorative images in formal administrative documents.

### 9. Final quality review
Check content accuracy separately from formatting. Then inspect layout in the intended output format. A document that looks correct while editing may change when converted or printed.

### 10. Finalisation and distribution
Confirm approval and document status. Use an appropriate file format and meaningful filename. If distributing a final non-editable reference copy, PDF may be suitable, while collaborators may require an editable source file. Follow organisational practice.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A three-page meeting pack has inconsistent heading sizes, a table split awkwardly across pages and a footer showing last month's date. The administrator applies the approved heading styles, adjusts the table/page break, corrects the footer, verifies the meeting details and checks the exported PDF before distribution.

## Practical Activity
Create a two-page internal briefing containing a title, two heading levels, one bullet list, one numbered process and a small table. Apply an approved or simulated professional style. Export or preview it as a final document and record five checks you performed after formatting.

## Key Takeaways
- Plan content before polishing appearance.
- Consistent hierarchy improves readability.
- Styles and templates reduce manual formatting errors.
- Lists, tables and page controls should serve the information.
- Always inspect the final version that recipients will actually receive.
