# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 3: Documents, Filing & Records
### Lesson 6: Records Retention, Retrieval & Disposal

**Estimated study time:** 45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the records lifecycle from creation to disposal.
2. Distinguish active, inactive and archived records.
3. Follow an organisational retention schedule.
4. Retrieve records while preserving access controls and traceability.
5. Dispose of records securely when authorised.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Keeping every record forever is not good records management, but deleting information simply because storage is crowded can also create serious risk. Organisations need rules defining how long different records are retained and how they are eventually disposed of. Administrators help apply those rules consistently.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Retention period** — Required length of time a record must be kept under applicable organisational or legal requirements.
- **Retention schedule** — Organisational schedule defining retention and disposal rules for record categories.
- **Active record** — Record regularly needed for current work.
- **Archive** — Controlled storage for records retained but not needed frequently.
- **Disposal** — Authorised destruction or deletion of records after applicable requirements are met.
- **Legal hold** — Instruction preventing disposal of relevant records because of litigation, investigation or another formal requirement.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. The records lifecycle
Records are created or received, used, stored, retained and eventually archived or disposed of. Controls should apply throughout this lifecycle.

### 2. Active and inactive records
Active records support current operations and need convenient authorised access. Inactive records may be moved to archive storage while their retention period continues.

Archiving is not the same as destroying.

### 3. Retention schedules
Organisations may have different retention periods for financial, employee, customer, contract and operational records. Administrators should use the approved retention schedule rather than guessing a period.

Specific legal requirements can vary by record type and context, so refer uncertain cases to the responsible records, legal, compliance or management function.

### 4. Retrieval
Retrieval should be timely and controlled. Confirm the requester's authority where records are restricted. Track physical records removed from controlled storage when required.

### 5. Preservation of integrity
Do not alter historical records simply to make them look cleaner. Corrections should follow approved processes that preserve appropriate evidence of what changed.

### 6. Disposal authorisation
A retention period expiring does not always mean immediate deletion. Check whether a legal hold, audit, investigation, unresolved dispute or organisational instruction prevents disposal.

Use required approval before destruction.

### 7. Secure disposal
Sensitive paper records may require secure shredding or approved destruction services. Electronic disposal should follow IT and records procedures, recognising that moving a file to a recycle bin may not equal secure deletion.

### 8. Disposal records
Some organisations maintain evidence of what record categories were destroyed, when, under which authority and by what method. This demonstrates controlled rather than accidental disposal.

### 9. Avoid unofficial archives
Employees sometimes keep old copies “just in case” after official disposal. This can defeat retention and confidentiality controls. Follow the approved system rather than creating private shadow archives.

### 10. PA responsibilities
PAs may handle executive correspondence, board material, contracts and sensitive records. They should know where authoritative records belong and involve the correct owner when retention or disposal is uncertain.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
An administrator is asked to clear a storeroom of old files. Instead of shredding everything older than five years, the administrator identifies record categories, checks the approved retention schedule, confirms whether any holds apply, obtains required authorisation and uses the approved secure disposal method. Age alone does not determine disposal.

## Practical Activity
Create a sample retention register with five record categories. Do not invent legal periods; instead include columns for “approved retention rule/source,” active location, archive location, disposal authority and disposal method. Explain the steps you would follow before destroying one category.

## Key Takeaways
- Records have a controlled lifecycle.
- Retention rules differ by record category.
- Retrieval must preserve confidentiality and traceability.
- Disposal requires authority and consideration of holds or other restrictions.
- Sensitive records should be destroyed through approved secure methods.
