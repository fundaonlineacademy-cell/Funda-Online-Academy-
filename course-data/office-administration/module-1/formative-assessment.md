# Module 1 Formative Assessment Bank
## Office Administration Foundations

**Bank size:** 32 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 15 random questions per attempt  
**Pass standard:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Prerequisite:** Complete all eight Module 1 lessons.

## Lesson 1 — The Role of Office Administration

1. Which statement BEST describes office administration?  
A. Performing only reception duties  
B. Coordinating information, records, communication and routine processes  
C. Making all management decisions  
D. Working only with physical files  
**Answer: B**

2. A manager asks for three quotations by Friday. One arrives on Tuesday. When is the task properly controlled?  
A. As soon as the first quotation is forwarded  
B. When the request is deleted from email  
C. When the remaining quotations and deadline are tracked and followed up  
D. When the supplier is told to contact the manager directly  
**Answer: C**

3. Which action BEST demonstrates confidentiality?  
A. Sharing customer information with any colleague who asks  
B. Discussing employee records in reception  
C. Leaving sensitive documents on a printer  
D. Sharing information only through authorised channels with appropriate people  
**Answer: D**

4. Why is follow-up important in administration?  
A. It confirms expected actions reach completion  
B. It removes the need for deadlines  
C. It allows administrators to approve every decision  
D. It replaces accurate recordkeeping  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 2 — Professional Conduct, Reliability & Workplace Standards

5. Which behaviour BEST demonstrates reliability?  
A. Waiting until a missed deadline to explain a problem  
B. Communicating early when a commitment is at risk  
C. Accepting every request regardless of capacity  
D. Avoiding difficult tasks  
**Answer: B**

6. An administrator discovers they sent the wrong internal attachment. What is the BEST first professional approach?  
A. Hide the mistake  
B. Blame the recipient  
C. Assess the impact and follow the appropriate correction/reporting process  
D. Delete all related records  
**Answer: C**

7. Which is an example of a professional boundary?  
A. Promising a manager will approve a request without authority  
B. Using confidential information for personal benefit  
C. Refusing to communicate with colleagues  
D. Not making decisions on behalf of a manager unless authorised  
**Answer: D**

8. Professional respect in the workplace should apply to:  
A. People across roles and levels  
B. Senior managers only  
C. Customers only  
D. Employees in the same department only  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 3 — Office Structures, Roles & Reporting Lines

9. What does a reporting line show?  
A. The location of office supplies  
B. The formal supervision relationship between roles  
C. Every person an employee may communicate with  
D. Customer reporting requirements  
**Answer: B**

10. An administrator collects supplier quotations, but a manager must approve the purchase. This illustrates that:  
A. administration and approval are always the same role  
B. suppliers decide internal authority  
C. coordination and approval authority can be separate  
D. administrators should avoid procurement processes  
**Answer: C**

11. When should a routine issue normally be escalated?  
A. Immediately to the Managing Director in every case  
B. Only after all records are deleted  
C. Whenever the requester sounds impatient  
D. When it exceeds authority, carries material risk or requires higher-level intervention  
**Answer: D**

12. Which question is MOST useful when routing a request?  
A. Which process or department owns this matter?  
B. Who is the most senior person available?  
C. Can I avoid recording it?  
D. Who can I forward it to without checking?  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 4 — Core Administrative Workflows & Daily Routines

13. What is a dependency?  
A. A completed task  
B. Something required before work can continue  
C. A type of office furniture  
D. A permanent priority level  
**Answer: B**

14. A task is waiting for a manager's approval. What should the administrator do?  
A. Mark it closed immediately  
B. Remove it from the task register  
C. Keep it visible with a waiting status and follow-up point  
D. Approve it personally  
**Answer: C**

15. Which handover is MOST useful?  
A. “There are things to do tomorrow.”  
B. “Check my emails.”  
C. “Supplier issue outstanding.”  
D. “Supplier X awaits PO confirmation; Finance needs signed approval; update due by noon.”  
**Answer: D**

16. Why are checklists useful for recurring processes?  
A. They reduce reliance on memory for important repeated controls  
B. They eliminate all judgement  
C. They remove the need to update procedures  
D. They make every task urgent  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 5 — Office Policies, Procedures & SOPs

17. Which statement BEST distinguishes policy from procedure?  
A. A policy is always verbal; a procedure is always written  
B. A policy states requirements or principles; a procedure explains how work is normally performed  
C. A policy applies only to managers  
D. There is no difference  
**Answer: B**

18. Why should an administrator check the current approved procedure?  
A. Older versions are always easier  
B. Procedures never change  
C. An old version may contain outdated controls or instructions  
D. It allows the employee to ignore authority limits  
**Answer: C**

19. A senior employee asks reception to bypass a visitor-control procedure. What is the BEST response?  
A. Bypass it because the employee is senior  
B. Allow entry without recording anything  
C. Invent a new rule  
D. Follow the approved procedure and escalation route for the exception  
**Answer: D**

20. An administrator notices an outdated telephone number in an approved SOP. What should they do?  
A. Raise the issue with the appropriate owner for controlled correction  
B. Quietly create an unofficial replacement policy  
C. Ignore it permanently  
D. Delete the SOP  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 6 — Work Prioritisation & Time Management

21. Urgency primarily refers to:  
A. how expensive a task is  
B. how soon action is required  
C. how senior the requester is  
D. how difficult the task feels  
**Answer: B**

22. Two authorised managers give conflicting deadlines that cannot both be met. What should the administrator do?  
A. Secretly choose one  
B. Ignore both  
C. Clarify the conflict and seek authorised prioritisation early  
D. Promise both without checking capacity  
**Answer: C**

23. What is a planning buffer?  
A. Time reserved only for lunch  
B. A list of completed tasks  
C. A method for making every request urgent  
D. Unallocated time that allows for interruptions and unexpected work  
**Answer: D**

24. Why should a large task be broken into next actions?  
A. It makes progress, scheduling and dependencies clearer  
B. It removes all deadlines  
C. It prevents collaboration  
D. It guarantees no interruptions  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 7 — Professional Office Etiquette

25. A visitor arrives early and the host is still in a meeting. What is the BEST response?  
A. Ignore the visitor until the host appears  
B. Acknowledge the visitor, follow access procedure and explain what will happen next  
C. Send the visitor directly to the meeting room  
D. Discuss the host's private schedule in detail  
**Answer: B**

26. Before sending a business email, which check is especially important?  
A. Whether the email is as long as possible  
B. Whether every employee is copied  
C. Recipients and attachments are correct  
D. Whether informal slang has been added  
**Answer: C**

27. Which behaviour BEST demonstrates discretion?  
A. Repeating private information because it was overheard  
B. Discussing staff disputes at reception  
C. Posting workplace details online  
D. Avoiding unnecessary disclosure of information encountered through the role  
**Answer: D**

28. Professional etiquette in a diverse workplace includes:  
A. avoiding assumptions and communicating respectfully  
B. insisting everyone uses the same informal style  
C. guessing preferred names repeatedly  
D. treating senior employees more respectfully than others  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 8 — Handling Routine Administrative Problems

29. What should generally come first in routine problem-solving?  
A. Assigning blame  
B. Establishing the relevant facts  
C. Promising a solution before investigating  
D. Deleting evidence of the problem  
**Answer: B**

30. A workaround is:  
A. a permanent policy change  
B. the root cause of a problem  
C. a temporary method that keeps work moving without permanently fixing the cause  
D. an unauthorised approval  
**Answer: C**

31. A confidential document is found in a public area. What should happen first?  
A. Leave it there while investigating who printed it  
B. Photograph and share it with colleagues  
C. Ignore it  
D. Secure the information and then follow the appropriate reporting/investigation process  
**Answer: D**

32. Repeatedly finding outdated documents in meeting packs MOST strongly suggests:  
A. a process or version-control weakness should be investigated  
B. every meeting should be cancelled  
C. administrators should stop using documents  
D. the problem should be ignored if corrected each time  
**Answer: A**
