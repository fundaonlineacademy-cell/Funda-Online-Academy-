# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 4: Customer & Office Front Desk
### Lesson 1: Front-desk Responsibilities & Professional Presence

**Estimated study time:** 45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the operational role of a front desk.
2. Present a professional and welcoming first point of contact.
3. Balance service with security and confidentiality requirements.
4. Manage common front-desk priorities and interruptions.
5. Maintain accurate reception records and handovers.

## Why This Lesson Matters
The front desk is often where customer service, security, administration and organisational reputation meet. Reception staff may answer calls, receive visitors, monitor access, accept deliveries and support meetings at the same time. Professional presence therefore means much more than being polite; it requires organised control.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Front desk** — The office function that receives visitors, calls and routine enquiries.
- **Professional presence** — The combination of conduct, communication and presentation that builds confidence.
- **Visitor flow** — Movement of visitors from arrival through waiting, host contact and departure.
- **Access control** — Procedures governing who may enter particular workplace areas.
- **Reception register** — Record used to capture approved visitor, delivery or contact information.
- **Handover** — Transfer of relevant outstanding information and responsibilities between staff.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. The front desk as an operational control point
Reception is not merely a waiting area. It may coordinate appointments, route enquiries, protect access, receive documents and connect external people with internal teams.

### 2. Professional first contact
Acknowledge people promptly. Use a respectful greeting and establish how you can assist. Maintain a calm tone even when several demands arrive at once.

Professional presence should be consistent in person and on the telephone.

### 3. Appearance and workstation control
Follow organisational dress and presentation standards. Keep the reception area orderly, especially where confidential papers or screens are visible. Avoid displaying visitor lists, employee details or internal correspondence unnecessarily.

### 4. Service and security together
A friendly approach does not mean bypassing access rules. Confirm appointments and visitor requirements according to procedure. If a visitor is unexpected, use the authorised escalation route rather than allowing access based only on confidence or status claims.

### 5. Managing multiple demands
When a call rings while a visitor arrives and a courier needs a signature, prioritise safety, immediate acknowledgement and the most time-sensitive controlled action. Brief acknowledgement can prevent a waiting person from feeling ignored while you complete another short task.

### 6. Front-desk information
Know approved information such as office hours, general contacts, meeting locations and standard visitor procedures. Do not reveal internal schedules, private numbers or sensitive staff information simply because somebody asks.

### 7. Deliveries and collections
Follow procedures for parcels, documents and courier collections. Check recipient details, signatures, timestamps or reference numbers where required. High-value or confidential deliveries may require additional controls.

### 8. Records
Visitor records, messages and delivery logs should be accurate and stored appropriately. Record only information required by procedure and protect it from unnecessary exposure.

### 9. Waiting areas
Keep the area safe and presentable. Inform visitors if there is a meaningful delay. Where refreshments or facilities are provided, follow office standards rather than making assumptions.

### 10. Handover
At shift or lunch changes, communicate expected visitors, unresolved enquiries, waiting deliveries and unusual incidents. Good handovers prevent the next receptionist from starting without context.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A visitor arrives for a 10:00 appointment while the receptionist is completing a courier handover. The receptionist acknowledges the visitor immediately, finishes the controlled signature, confirms the appointment, follows sign-in and access procedure, notifies the host and provides an update if the host is delayed. The visitor receives service without weakening courier or security controls.

## Practical Activity
Create a front-desk opening checklist and closing checklist. Include reception appearance, systems, expected visitors, visitor records, telephone readiness, deliveries, confidential material and handover items. Then write how you would respond when three demands arrive at once.

## Key Takeaways
- Reception combines service with operational control.
- Professional presence depends on behaviour and organisation, not appearance alone.
- Access rules should not be weakened for convenience.
- Front-desk records and handovers protect continuity.
- Confidential information must remain protected even in busy public-facing areas.
