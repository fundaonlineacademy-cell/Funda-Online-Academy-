# Module 4 Formative Assessment Bank
## Customer & Office Front Desk

**Bank size:** 32 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 15 random questions per attempt  
**Pass standard:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Prerequisite:** Complete all eight Module 4 lessons.

## Lesson 1 — Front-desk Responsibilities & Professional Presence

1. Which statement BEST describes the front desk?  
A. A waiting area only  
B. An operational control point combining service, communication, access and records  
C. A department that approves every decision  
D. A location for storing all confidential documents openly  
**Answer: B**

2. What does professional presence include?  
A. Appearance only  
B. Seniority only  
C. Conduct, communication, organisation and appropriate presentation  
D. Speaking formally at all times  
**Answer: C**

3. A visitor arrives while the receptionist is signing for a courier parcel. What is the BEST response?  
A. Ignore the visitor until finished  
B. Abandon the courier process  
C. Send the visitor behind reception  
D. Acknowledge the visitor promptly while completing the controlled courier step  
**Answer: D**

4. Why should visible reception documents be controlled?  
A. They may contain sensitive or personal information  
B. Paper is always untidy  
C. Visitors should never see any documents  
D. Documents prevent telephone calls  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 2 — Receiving Visitors & Access Procedures

5. Why should expected visitor information be reviewed in advance?  
A. To avoid greeting visitors  
B. To reduce delays and prepare access arrangements  
C. To publish visitor names  
D. To eliminate verification  
**Answer: B**

6. An unexpected visitor claims to know the CEO personally. What should reception do?  
A. Give immediate unrestricted access  
B. Refuse all assistance  
C. Establish purpose and use the authorised verification/referral process  
D. Ask another visitor to decide  
**Answer: C**

7. Why should visitor registers be protected from unnecessary viewing?  
A. They may contain personal information about other visitors  
B. They must always be secret from security staff  
C. They cannot contain times  
D. They replace access procedures  
**Answer: A**

8. What may be required when a visitor leaves?  
A. Permanent access card  
B. Personal employee phone number  
C. Copy of another visitor's record  
D. Sign-out and return of temporary pass where applicable  
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 3 — Handling Calls, Messages & Enquiries

9. What does triage mean at reception?  
A. Filing old records  
B. Quickly assessing an enquiry for priority and routing  
C. Transferring every call to management  
D. Closing enquiries without records  
**Answer: B**

10. Which message is MOST usable?  
A. “Someone called Accounts.”  
B. “Please call back.”  
C. “Naledi, 082 xxx xxxx, invoice 4521, requests status before 15:00; second enquiry today.”  
D. “Customer unhappy.”  
**Answer: C**

11. Why should reception avoid promising another employee's decision?  
A. It may be outside reception's authority and control  
B. Customers dislike decisions  
C. Decisions never need communication  
D. It makes calls shorter  
**Answer: A**

12. What should happen to unresolved enquiries at handover?  
A. Delete them  
B. Restart from zero tomorrow  
C. Ignore them  
D. Transfer status, owner, next action and timing  
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 4 — Customer Service Standards for Office Staff

13. Why is friendliness alone insufficient customer service?  
A. Customers prefer silence  
B. Service also requires accuracy, reliability and follow-through  
C. Friendly employees cannot use systems  
D. Politeness creates risk  
**Answer: B**

14. What is expectation management?  
A. Promising the fastest possible result  
B. Avoiding deadlines  
C. Giving realistic information about what will happen and by when  
D. Telling customers internal staff problems  
**Answer: C**

15. A customer asks when an unconfirmed delivery will arrive. What is the BEST response?  
A. Invent a date to reassure them  
B. Say it will definitely arrive tomorrow  
C. Blame the courier  
D. Explain that the date is being confirmed and provide a realistic update time  
**Answer: D**

16. Repeated similar complaints may indicate:  
A. an underlying service or process problem  
B. customers are always unreasonable  
C. the front desk should stop recording complaints  
D. no action is needed  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 5 — Managing Complaints & Service Recovery

17. What should happen FIRST when receiving a complaint?  
A. Promise compensation  
B. Listen and understand the concern  
C. Blame another department  
D. Close the complaint  
**Answer: B**

18. What is service recovery?  
A. Ignoring a service failure  
B. Writing a new policy for every complaint  
C. Controlled action taken to correct or respond to a service failure  
D. Automatically refunding every customer  
**Answer: C**

19. Which complaint requires prompt escalation?  
A. Request for office hours  
B. Question about a meeting room  
C. Request for a brochure  
D. Report of a serious confidentiality breach  
**Answer: D**

20. Why should complaint outcomes be recorded?  
A. To prevent duplicate handling and support trend analysis  
B. To make customers repeat the complaint  
C. To avoid root-cause review  
D. To replace all service standards  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 6 — Appointments, Waiting Areas & Visitor Flow

21. What is a bottleneck?  
A. A visitor badge  
B. A point where demand exceeds available process or space capacity  
C. A completed appointment  
D. A type of meeting invitation  
**Answer: B**

22. A host is delayed. What should reception do?  
A. Repeatedly promise “five minutes” without checking  
B. Reveal the host's private schedule  
C. Give a realistic update and check the next appropriate arrangement  
D. Tell the visitor to find the host  
**Answer: C**

23. Does arriving early give a visitor automatic access to the host?  
A. Yes, always  
B. Yes, for important visitors only  
C. Yes, if reception is quiet  
D. No, appointment and access procedures still apply  
**Answer: D**

24. Why do group visits require advance planning?  
A. They may create sign-in, badge, seating and escort bottlenecks  
B. Groups never need access control  
C. Group visitors do not need hosts  
D. It allows reception to skip records  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 7 — Front-desk Security, Privacy & Confidentiality

25. What is social engineering?  
A. Designing office furniture  
B. Manipulating people into revealing information or bypassing controls  
C. Updating a visitor register  
D. Training security guards only  
**Answer: B**

26. Why should claimed urgency not override verification?  
A. Urgent requests are never real  
B. Reception should never help anyone  
C. Urgency can be used to pressure staff into bypassing controls  
D. Verification applies only to slow requests  
**Answer: C**

27. What is tailgating?  
A. A visitor following an authorised person through a controlled door without proper access  
B. A long customer queue  
C. A delayed courier  
D. A repeated callback  
**Answer: A**

28. How should suspicious behaviour be handled?  
A. Confront the person beyond your training  
B. Ignore it  
C. Publish it online  
D. Follow authorised security/escalation procedures  
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 8 — Front-desk Records, Handover & Daily Control

29. What should opening controls include?  
A. Only cleaning the desk  
B. Systems readiness, expected activity, handover and relevant security checks  
C. Deleting yesterday's messages  
D. Giving visitors access cards in advance  
**Answer: B**

30. Which handover item is MOST useful?  
A. “Things still pending.”  
B. “Busy day.”  
C. “Supplier callback due 09:00; Sipho/Procurement owns response; ref PO-771.”  
D. “Check emails.”  
**Answer: C**

31. Why should unresolved front-desk work remain visible?  
A. So tasks do not disappear between interruptions or shifts  
B. To keep the desk busy  
C. To avoid closing any record  
D. To increase visitor waiting time  
**Answer: A**

32. Why review daily exceptions?  
A. To count only completed calls  
B. To avoid process improvement  
C. To replace security procedures  
D. Recurring exceptions can reveal wider operational weaknesses  
**Answer: D**
