# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 4: Customer & Office Front Desk
### Lesson 8: Front-desk Records, Handover & Daily Control

**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Maintain accurate front-desk records for visitors, messages and deliveries.
2. Use opening and closing controls to prepare reception for the day.
3. Produce a useful front-desk handover.
4. Track unresolved enquiries and incidents to the correct next action.
5. Review daily reception information for recurring operational problems.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Reception depends on continuity. A perfect interaction can still fail if the next shift knows nothing about an expected visitor, unresolved complaint or parcel awaiting collection. Daily controls and handovers convert front-desk activity into an organised administrative process.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Opening control** — Check completed before or at the start of reception operations.
- **Closing control** — End-of-day check confirming records, outstanding actions and security items.
- **Handover log** — Record of information another staff member needs to continue work.
- **Outstanding item** — Task or enquiry not yet completed.
- **Exception** — Situation outside normal expected process requiring attention.
- **Daily control sheet** — Checklist or register used to confirm routine reception controls.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Opening controls
At the start of the day, confirm that reception systems, phones and approved directories are available. Review expected visitors, meetings, deliveries and any handover notes from the previous shift.

Check that the waiting area is safe and presentable and that confidential information has not been left exposed.

### 2. Visitor records
Visitor information should be recorded accurately according to procedure. Required fields may include arrival time, visitor name, host, badge number and departure time. Avoid collecting unnecessary information.

### 3. Message records
Messages should contain enough detail for action. Update status when delivered, acknowledged or completed where the process requires follow-up.

### 4. Delivery logs
Record important incoming and outgoing items according to the organisation's process. Include useful evidence such as recipient, sender/courier, time, reference and signature where required.

### 5. Incident records
Security, safety or significant customer-service incidents may require a separate incident process. Record facts, actions and escalation rather than emotional opinions.

### 6. Outstanding-action control
Keep unresolved work visible. Examples include a visitor waiting for rescheduling, a callback still due, a parcel not collected or a complaint awaiting manager response.

A status such as “waiting for Finance; follow up at 14:00” is more useful than “pending.”

### 7. Handover content
A good handover states what is outstanding, current status, who owns the next action, deadline, relevant reference and any risk or special instruction.

Do not overload the handover with completed routine work that no longer affects the next person.

### 8. Closing controls
Before closing reception, reconcile visitor badges where required, confirm visitors have signed out, secure registers and documents, check unresolved messages and deliveries, and prepare handover information.

### 9. Daily review
Look for exceptions: repeated missed callbacks, visitors waiting too long, uncollected deliveries, access issues or recurring room conflicts. These patterns can indicate weaknesses in wider office processes.

### 10. PA application
A PA may not sit at reception, but the same control mindset applies to executive support: expected stakeholders, outstanding communication, action ownership, handover and exception tracking.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
At 16:45 the receptionist notices a confidential courier envelope received at 11:00 is still awaiting collection, a supplier expects a callback before 09:00 tomorrow and one visitor badge has not been returned. A strong closing process does not simply lock the desk. The receptionist follows the appropriate badge/security procedure, secures the envelope, records its status and includes the callback in the handover with owner and deadline.

## Practical Activity
Create a one-page reception daily control sheet with sections for opening, expected visitors, deliveries, outstanding enquiries, incidents, badge control and closing. Then prepare a sample handover containing five unresolved items. Make every item include status, owner, next action and deadline.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Systems, expected activity, prior handover, reception readiness and relevant security controls.
2. To support the operational purpose without unnecessary information collection or exposure.
3. It indicates what has happened, what is still required and when follow-up should occur.
4. Factually, with actions and escalation according to the incident process.
5. So tasks do not disappear between interruptions or shifts.
6. Outstanding issue, status, owner, next action, deadline/reference and material risk where relevant.
7. Examples include badge reconciliation, visitor sign-out, securing registers, unresolved message review and handover preparation.
8. Repeated exceptions can reveal wider process weaknesses.

## Key Takeaways
- Reception needs daily operational controls, not only interpersonal skills.
- Records should support follow-up, continuity and accountability.
- Handovers should focus on unfinished work and next actions.
- Closing controls protect information and prevent tasks from disappearing overnight.
- Exception trends can improve office systems.
