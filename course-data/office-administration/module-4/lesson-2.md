# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 4: Customer & Office Front Desk
### Lesson 2: Receiving Visitors & Access Procedures

**Estimated study time:** 45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Receive visitors professionally and efficiently.
2. Apply appointment, identity and access procedures consistently.
3. Handle unexpected or unverified visitors without unnecessary conflict.
4. Protect staff, visitor and organisational information.
5. Record visitor movement and departures according to procedure.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Visitors may be clients, suppliers, job applicants, contractors, delivery personnel or officials. A receptionist must create a welcoming experience without assuming that every person who enters the building is authorised to move freely. Good visitor handling combines courtesy, verification and clear routing.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Visitor verification** — Confirming identity, appointment or access authority according to procedure.
- **Host** — Employee or department responsible for receiving a visitor.
- **Visitor badge/pass** — Temporary identification or access credential issued where required.
- **Restricted area** — Workplace area not open to general visitor access.
- **Escort requirement** — Rule that a visitor must be accompanied in specified areas.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Prepare for expected visitors
Review the authorised visitor schedule where available. Know the host, expected time, meeting location and any special access requirements. Preparation reduces delays and avoids asking visitors unnecessary questions in public.

### 2. Greet and establish purpose
Welcome the visitor, identify who they are meeting and verify relevant details. If identification is required, follow the approved process consistently rather than applying it selectively based on appearance or status.

### 3. Confirm the appointment
Where procedures require confirmation, notify the host or check the approved system. Do not reveal the host's private schedule while verifying availability.

### 4. Sign-in and visitor passes
Capture only the information required by organisational procedure. Visitor registers should not expose previous visitors' personal details unnecessarily. Issue badges or passes according to access rules and explain return requirements.

### 5. Unexpected visitors
An unexpected visitor should not automatically be rejected or admitted. Establish their purpose, identify the relevant contact and use the authorised referral or escalation route.

A person claiming to know the CEO personally still follows the applicable access process unless an authorised exception is confirmed.

### 6. Contractors and service providers
Contractors may require additional checks such as work orders, permits, safety induction or escorting. Reception should know which department verifies these requirements rather than improvising technical or safety approval.

### 7. Protect confidential information
Avoid saying, “The HR manager is interviewing another candidate” or “The director is at the bank.” Provide only information necessary to manage the visit.

### 8. Waiting and delays
If the host is delayed, give the visitor an appropriate update and check whether the host wants an alternative arrangement. Do not leave visitors uncertain for long periods without communication.

### 9. Access beyond reception
Follow badge, escort, door, lift or restricted-area rules. Never lend staff access cards to visitors unless a specific authorised procedure permits it.

### 10. Departure
Where required, record the visitor's departure, recover temporary passes and notify relevant personnel of any unresolved issue. Visitor records may support emergency accountability and security review.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A man arrives saying he is a technician sent to repair a server and asks to be shown directly to the equipment room. The receptionist confirms the service request through the authorised contact, checks contractor access requirements and waits for the designated escort. The technician's plausible explanation is not treated as sufficient access authority.

## Practical Activity
Create a visitor-handling flow for: expected client, unexpected supplier, job applicant, courier and contractor. For each, identify verification, host contact, access level, record required and departure step.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should expected visitor information be reviewed in advance?
2. Should identification requirements be applied selectively according to appearance?
3. How should an unexpected visitor be handled?
4. Why should visitor registers be protected from unnecessary exposure?
5. What additional controls may apply to contractors?
6. What should reception say when a host is delayed?
7. Why should staff access cards not normally be lent to visitors?
8. What may be required when a visitor leaves?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Preparation reduces delays and supports controlled access.
2. No. Follow approved rules consistently.
3. Establish purpose and use the authorised referral or escalation process.
4. They may contain personal information about other visitors.
5. Work-order confirmation, permits, induction or escort requirements depending on procedure.
6. Provide an appropriate update without exposing private details and clarify the next step.
7. Staff credentials can grant broader access than the visitor is authorised to have.
8. Sign-out, pass return and closure of any outstanding reception record.

## Key Takeaways
- Welcoming service and access control should operate together.
- Verify visitors according to approved procedures.
- Unexpected visitors require controlled routing, not assumptions.
- Protect staff and visitor information during the process.
- Departure controls complete the visitor-management cycle.
