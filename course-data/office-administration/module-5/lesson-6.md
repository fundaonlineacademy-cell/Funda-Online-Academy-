# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 5: Diaries, Meetings & Travel
### Lesson 6: Meeting Follow-up & Action Tracking

**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Convert meeting decisions into trackable actions.
2. Build and maintain an action register.
3. Follow up before deadlines become failures.
4. Escalate overdue or blocked actions appropriately.
5. Close completed actions with clear evidence.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Meetings create value only when decisions turn into action. Without follow-up, the same issues reappear meeting after meeting. Administrators and PAs help convert discussion into accountable work by maintaining action records, reminders and escalation points.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Action register** — Structured list of outstanding meeting actions.
- **Action owner** — Person responsible for completing an action.
- **Target date** — Agreed date by which the action should be completed.
- **Dependency** — Input or event needed before the action can progress.
- **Escalation trigger** — Condition indicating higher-level attention is required.
- **Closure evidence** — Information confirming the action has been completed appropriately.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Transfer actions accurately
After minutes are drafted, move each action into the approved action tracker. Preserve the wording, owner and deadline accurately so the tracker does not contradict the meeting record.

### 2. Make actions specific
An action should answer: what must happen, who owns it, by when, and what completion looks like. Vague actions are difficult to follow up.

### 3. Track status meaningfully
Useful statuses might include Not Started, In Progress, Waiting for Input, Blocked, Completed or Escalated. Avoid a single “pending” label for everything.

### 4. Follow up before due dates
A reminder before the deadline gives the owner time to recover. Waiting until an action is already overdue may be too late if other work depends on it.

### 5. Understand dependencies
If an owner is waiting for Finance approval, record that dependency. Follow-up can then target the real bottleneck rather than repeatedly chasing the same person.

### 6. Escalate with context
An overdue action is not automatically misconduct. Before escalating, establish status, attempts made, impact and support required. Escalate when the deadline or business outcome is genuinely at risk.

### 7. Close correctly
Do not mark an action complete merely because the owner says “done.” Where appropriate, confirm the expected output was delivered, approved or communicated.

### 8. Prepare for the next meeting
Before the next meeting, update the action register and highlight overdue or unresolved items. This allows the chair to focus on genuine exceptions instead of reviewing every completed task.

### 9. Communicate closure
Notify people who depend on the completed action if required. An action can be operationally finished but still cause delay if the outcome is not communicated.

### 10. PA-level follow-up
A PA may follow up actions assigned to senior staff without having authority over them. Professional influence comes from accurate records, agreed deadlines, respectful reminders and appropriate escalation—not from pretending to supervise.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
At a meeting, HR agrees to send an updated staff list by Wednesday so Finance can finalise payroll changes on Thursday. The PA records HR as owner, Wednesday as the target date and Finance completion as a dependency. A reminder is sent Tuesday. When HR reports a system issue, the PA records the blocker and escalates early because the Thursday payroll deadline is now at risk.

## Practical Activity
Create an action register with eight actions. Include Owner, Due Date, Status, Dependency, Follow-up Date, Escalation Trigger and Closure Evidence. Simulate two overdue actions and draft concise escalation notes showing status, impact and decision/support required.

## Knowledge Check
1. What makes an action trackable?
2. Why are meaningful statuses better than “pending”?
3. Why should follow-up happen before deadlines?
4. What is a dependency?
5. What should be checked before escalating an overdue action?
6. What is closure evidence?
7. Why prepare the action register before the next meeting?
8. How can a PA follow up senior staff without supervisory authority?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Clear action, owner, deadline and expected completion.
2. They show what is actually happening and where the bottleneck is.
3. Early follow-up leaves time to recover if something is wrong.
4. Something required before an action can progress.
5. Current status, attempts made, impact, deadline and support needed.
6. Information showing the expected result has actually been delivered or completed.
7. So unresolved exceptions can be addressed efficiently.
8. Through accurate records, agreed deadlines, respectful reminders and authorised escalation.

## Key Takeaways
- Meeting actions require owners, deadlines and clear completion criteria.
- Follow up early enough to protect the final outcome.
- Track dependencies, not only owners.
- Escalation should be factual and purposeful.
- Closure requires confirmation, not assumptions.
