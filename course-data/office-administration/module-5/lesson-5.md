# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 5: Diaries, Meetings & Travel
### Lesson 5: Minute-taking & Action Records

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Distinguish minutes from a word-for-word transcript.
2. Capture decisions, key discussion points and actions accurately.
3. Record action owners and deadlines clearly.
4. Maintain neutrality and confidentiality in meeting records.
5. Review and distribute minutes through the approved process.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Minutes create organisational memory. They help people remember what was decided, who accepted responsibility and what must happen next. Poor minutes create arguments about decisions or leave actions without ownership.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Minutes** — Formal or approved record of a meeting's key proceedings, decisions and actions.
- **Action item** — Specific task arising from the meeting.
- **Resolution** — Formal decision adopted by a meeting where applicable.
- **Action owner** — Person responsible for completing an action.
- **Due date** — Agreed deadline for completing an action.
- **Neutral record** — Factual record avoiding unnecessary personal judgement.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Understand the required minute style
Organisations differ. Some require concise action minutes; others require fuller summaries. Learn the approved template and level of detail before the meeting.

### 2. Prepare before the meeting
Review the agenda, previous minutes and attendee list. Create a note-taking structure aligned to agenda items so important information is easier to capture.

### 3. Capture attendance
Record attendees, apologies and relevant guests according to procedure. For formal meetings, attendance may affect quorum or governance requirements.

### 4. Focus on decisions and actions
Do not attempt to transcribe every sentence unless specifically required. Capture key facts, decisions, unresolved issues and actions.

### 5. Write actions precisely
“Finance to investigate” is weak. “Finance Manager to confirm disputed invoice balance by 12 September” identifies owner, action and deadline.

### 6. Remain neutral
Minutes should not describe people as “angry,” “lazy” or “confused” unless a formal process requires a relevant factual record. Focus on what was discussed, decided or assigned.

### 7. Clarify during the meeting
If an important decision or deadline is unclear and the role permits, confirm it before the meeting moves on. It is easier to clarify at the source than guess while drafting minutes later.

### 8. Draft promptly
Prepare minutes while notes and context are still fresh. Check names, dates, figures, resolutions and action details.

### 9. Approval and distribution
Follow the organisation's process for chair review, approval or confirmation at the next meeting. Distribute only to authorised recipients and protect confidential sections appropriately.

### 10. Link minutes to follow-up
An action register can make follow-up easier than repeatedly reading full minutes. Transfer actions accurately and preserve the connection to the original meeting record.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
During a meeting, the team agrees that Operations will obtain two courier quotations before Friday. A weak minute says “Courier discussed.” A useful minute says: “Decision: obtain alternative courier pricing. Action: Operations Manager to submit two comparable quotations to the GM by 16:00 Friday.”

## Practical Activity
Listen to or imagine a 20-minute team meeting with five agenda items. Produce concise minutes containing attendance, decisions, actions, owners and deadlines. Then create a separate action register and cross-check every action against the minutes.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why are minutes not normally a transcript?
2. What should be prepared before the meeting?
3. What makes an action item strong?
4. Why must minutes remain neutral?
5. When may clarification be needed during a meeting?
6. Why should minutes be drafted promptly?
7. What should happen before distribution if approval is required?
8. Why use an action register alongside minutes?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Their purpose is usually to record important proceedings, decisions and actions rather than every word.
2. Agenda, attendee structure, previous minutes and an appropriate note-taking template.
3. Clear action, owner and deadline.
4. Minutes are organisational records and should avoid unnecessary personal judgement.
5. When a material decision, owner or deadline is unclear.
6. Context and details are easier to verify while fresh.
7. Complete the authorised review or approval process.
8. It makes outstanding actions easier to monitor and close.

## Key Takeaways
- Minutes record organisational decisions and actions.
- Prepare a note structure before the meeting.
- Capture action, owner and deadline precisely.
- Remain factual and neutral.
- Approved minutes and action registers support reliable follow-through.
