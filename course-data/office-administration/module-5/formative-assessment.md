# Module 5 Formative Assessment Bank
## Diaries, Meetings & Travel

**Bank size:** 32 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 15 random questions per attempt  
**Pass standard:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Prerequisite:** Complete all eight Module 5 lessons.

## Lesson 1 — Diary and Calendar Management

1. What is the BEST description of professional diary management?  
A. Recording meetings only  
B. Controlling time, commitments, preparation and availability  
C. Keeping every time slot full  
D. Avoiding flexible work blocks  
**Answer: B**

2. Which calendar entry is MOST useful?  
A. “Meeting”  
B. “Call”  
C. “Client quarterly review — 10:00–11:00 — Boardroom 2 — pack attached”  
D. “Busy”  
**Answer: C**

3. Why are buffers important in a diary?  
A. They make meetings longer  
B. They replace travel planning  
C. They remove the need for priorities  
D. They allow for preparation, travel, transition and reasonable overruns  
**Answer: D**

4. What is a tentative hold?  
A. A provisional time block awaiting confirmation  
B. A permanently confirmed meeting  
C. A cancelled appointment  
D. A travel expense  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 2 — Scheduling, Priorities & Conflict Resolution

5. Before accepting a new meeting, what should be checked?  
A. Only whether the visual slot is empty  
B. Existing commitments, travel, preparation and attendee constraints  
C. Only the requester's job title  
D. Whether the meeting has catering  
**Answer: B**

6. Which factor BEST helps assess priority?  
A. Who sent the shortest email  
B. Which meeting was requested most recently  
C. Purpose, deadline, consequences and movability  
D. Which meeting has the longest title  
**Answer: C**

7. Two high-impact commitments conflict and the PA lacks authority to choose. What should happen?  
A. Cancel both  
B. Choose secretly  
C. Double-book the executive  
D. Escalate the conflict with concise options and consequences  
**Answer: D**

8. Why should preparation time be protected?  
A. A meeting can occur on time but still fail if the participant is unprepared  
B. Preparation is optional  
C. It always replaces meetings  
D. It removes the need for materials  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 3 — Meeting Preparation & Invitations

9. What should be clarified BEFORE sending invitations?  
A. Refreshment preference only  
B. Meeting purpose and required outcome  
C. Number of pages in the agenda  
D. Who has the largest office  
**Answer: B**

10. What is a required attendee?  
A. Anyone copied on an email  
B. Every employee in the department  
C. A person whose presence or authority is necessary for the meeting purpose  
D. Any visitor in reception  
**Answer: C**

11. A required attendee declines an invitation. What should the administrator do?  
A. Ignore it  
B. Keep the meeting unchanged automatically  
C. Remove all other attendees  
D. Resolve the impact through rescheduling, substitute authority or escalation as appropriate  
**Answer: D**

12. Why might a confidential meeting use a discreet title?  
A. To reduce unnecessary exposure of sensitive subject matter  
B. To hide it from all authorised participants  
C. To make the diary difficult to use  
D. To avoid confirming the date  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 4 — Agendas, Meeting Packs & Logistics

13. Which agenda item is MOST useful?  
A. “Finance”  
B. “Approve Q3 budget adjustment”  
C. “Other”  
D. “Discussion”  
**Answer: B**

14. Why should agenda items have realistic time allocations?  
A. To make every meeting exactly one hour  
B. To reduce preparation  
C. To ensure the agenda fits the available time and protects important discussion  
D. To remove decision items  
**Answer: C**

15. What is a key meeting-pack quality check?  
A. Include every draft available  
B. Remove all labels  
C. Send packs only after the meeting  
D. Confirm documents are current and approved where required  
**Answer: D**

16. Why should pack documents be ordered logically?  
A. So participants can find supporting information efficiently  
B. To increase page count  
C. To avoid using an agenda  
D. To make printing slower  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 5 — Minute-taking & Action Records

17. What are meeting minutes?  
A. A transcript of every spoken word in all cases  
B. A record of key proceedings, decisions and actions according to the required style  
C. A personal diary  
D. A visitor register  
**Answer: B**

18. Which action record is strongest?  
A. “Finance to investigate.”  
B. “Someone to check.”  
C. “Finance Manager to confirm invoice balance by 12 September.”  
D. “Discuss next time.”  
**Answer: C**

19. Why should minutes remain neutral?  
A. Opinions are always required  
B. Neutrality prevents decisions  
C. Minutes should never name actions  
D. They are organisational records and should avoid unnecessary personal judgement  
**Answer: D**

20. Why should minutes be drafted promptly?  
A. Context and details are easier to verify while still fresh  
B. Delays improve accuracy  
C. Approval is unnecessary if they are fast  
D. It eliminates review  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 6 — Meeting Follow-up & Action Tracking

21. What makes an action trackable?  
A. A vague description  
B. Clear action, owner, deadline and expected completion  
C. No due date  
D. A status of “pending” only  
**Answer: B**

22. Why should reminders often be sent before a deadline?  
A. To create pressure  
B. To reduce action ownership  
C. To leave recovery time if the action is blocked or incomplete  
D. To replace escalation  
**Answer: C**

23. What is a dependency?  
A. A completed action  
B. A meeting room  
C. A travel itinerary  
D. Something required before an action can progress  
**Answer: D**

24. When should an action be marked complete?  
A. When the expected result or closure evidence has been confirmed where appropriate  
B. As soon as the owner says “done,” regardless of output  
C. Before the deadline  
D. When the next meeting starts  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 7 — Business Travel Planning & Itineraries

25. What should be confirmed before business travel is booked?  
A. Only the cheapest fare  
B. Purpose, dates, destination, meetings and required arrival/departure times  
C. Only hotel breakfast options  
D. The traveller's favourite seat only  
**Answer: B**

26. Why is the lowest airfare not always the best business option?  
A. Expensive flights are always better  
B. Cheap fares are not allowed  
C. Timing, transfers, change terms and disruption risk may create greater total cost or failure  
D. Fare price never matters  
**Answer: C**

27. What should an itinerary include?  
A. Only flight numbers  
B. Only meeting names  
C. Personal social plans  
D. Chronological travel, accommodation, meetings, transfers, references and contacts  
**Answer: D**

28. Why can an executive itinerary be sensitive?  
A. It may reveal exact movements, times and accommodation details  
B. It is always public information  
C. It contains no useful information  
D. It is only a travel receipt  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 8 — Travel Changes, Expenses & Administrative Control

29. What should be protected FIRST during a travel disruption?  
A. The original booking at all costs  
B. The underlying business commitment and traveller requirements  
C. The cheapest available option  
D. The hotel loyalty points  
**Answer: B**

30. Why must the whole itinerary be reviewed after one booking changes?  
A. Changes never affect anything else  
B. It makes the itinerary longer  
C. Other travel, meetings and accommodation may depend on that segment  
D. Only finance needs updated details  
**Answer: C**

31. Why should company-paid expenses be separated from employee-paid costs?  
A. To increase claims  
B. To remove receipts  
C. To avoid approval  
D. To avoid duplicate reimbursement  
**Answer: D**

32. What should happen when a receipt is missing?  
A. Follow the organisation's approved exception process  
B. Create a fake receipt  
C. Hide the cost  
D. Change the category  
**Answer: A**
