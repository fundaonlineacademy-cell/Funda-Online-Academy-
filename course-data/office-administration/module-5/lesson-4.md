# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 5: Diaries, Meetings & Travel
### Lesson 4: Agendas, Meeting Packs & Logistics

**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Build a clear meeting agenda from the meeting purpose.
2. Compile meeting packs using current approved documents.
3. Coordinate rooms, equipment and supporting logistics.
4. Control versions and distribution of meeting materials.
5. Perform a final meeting-readiness check.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Participants cannot prepare effectively if agenda items are unclear or supporting documents arrive late, incomplete or in the wrong version. Administrators create structure by connecting agenda items to objectives, owners and supporting information.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Agenda** — Ordered list of items to be discussed or decided in a meeting.
- **Agenda item** — Specific topic, decision or update included in the agenda.
- **Meeting pack** — Collection of documents needed for participants to prepare or participate.
- **Presenter/owner** — Person responsible for introducing or leading an agenda item.
- **Decision item** — Agenda item requiring an authorised decision.
- **Information item** — Agenda item primarily intended to inform participants.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Build the agenda from purpose
Each agenda item should support the reason for the meeting. Avoid adding unrelated topics simply because participants will already be together.

### 2. Use clear item labels
“Finance” is vague. “Approve Q3 marketing budget adjustment” tells participants what action is expected. Where useful, indicate whether an item is for information, discussion or decision.

### 3. Allocate realistic time
Estimate how much discussion or decision time each item requires. A ten-item agenda cannot realistically fit into thirty minutes if every item needs detailed debate.

### 4. Identify owners
Assign presenters or owners so participants know who is responsible for introducing information or a decision request.

### 5. Collect meeting-pack material
Request documents early enough to review them before distribution. Track missing contributions and escalate if late material threatens the meeting purpose.

### 6. Check versions and quality
Confirm that each document is current, approved where required and labelled clearly. Remove obsolete drafts and avoid including two conflicting versions of the same document.

### 7. Order the pack logically
Arrange documents in agenda order or use clear indexing so participants can move from an agenda item to its supporting material quickly.

### 8. Distribution
Send packs through the approved channel and according to the expected lead time. Restrict confidential materials to authorised participants.

### 9. Logistics
Confirm room capacity, layout, equipment, connectivity, refreshments, accessibility, signage and visitor access as required. Online/hybrid meetings require link, audio, screen-sharing and host readiness.

### 10. Final readiness
Before the meeting, verify the agenda, pack, attendees, room/link, equipment and unresolved issues. Have an authorised backup plan for likely problems where appropriate.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A management meeting requires approval of a revised budget. The administrator places “Approve Revised Q3 Budget” as a timed agenda item, confirms Finance owns the item, verifies the current spreadsheet and summary are approved for circulation, places them directly behind the relevant agenda section and checks that the meeting room display can open the file.

## Practical Activity
Create a 60-minute agenda with five items for a monthly operations meeting. Label each item Information, Discussion or Decision, assign an owner and realistic time. Then design a meeting-pack index and a final logistics checklist.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should agenda items connect to the meeting purpose?
2. What is wrong with an agenda item called only “Finance”?
3. Why should item time be estimated?
4. What is the purpose of naming an agenda-item owner?
5. Why must meeting-pack versions be checked?
6. How should the pack normally be ordered?
7. What confidentiality issue can meeting-pack distribution create?
8. Name four logistics to confirm before the meeting.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. The meeting should use participant time to achieve its intended outcome.
2. It does not show the topic, expected action or decision clearly.
3. To keep the agenda realistic and prioritise important discussion.
4. It clarifies who is responsible for presenting or leading the item.
5. Participants need the current authoritative information.
6. In agenda order or with a clear index linking documents to items.
7. Sensitive material may be shared with people who are not authorised to receive it.
8. Examples include room/link, equipment, seating, connectivity, accessibility, visitor access and refreshments.

## Key Takeaways
- Agendas should make purpose and expected action visible.
- Meeting packs must use current controlled documents.
- Track missing inputs before they become meeting-day failures.
- Arrange information so participants can navigate it easily.
- Logistics are part of meeting quality, not an afterthought.
