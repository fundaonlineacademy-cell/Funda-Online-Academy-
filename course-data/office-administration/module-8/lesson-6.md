# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 8: Practical Admin-to-PA Project
### Lesson 6: Diary, Meeting & Travel Coordination Project

**Estimated study time:** 75 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Coordinate a realistic executive diary containing competing commitments.
2. Prepare a meeting from invitation through action follow-up.
3. Build a practical business travel itinerary.
4. Respond to a simulated schedule or travel disruption.
5. Demonstrate integrated calendar, meeting and travel control.

## Project Task
Coordinate one demanding week for the Operations Director at Ubuntu Business Services. The week must include diary conflicts, an external client meeting, an internal management meeting and domestic business travel.

## Required Evidence
Create:
- **E17 — Executive Weekly Diary** with meetings, preparation, travel and buffers;
- **E18 — Full Meeting Pack & Action Register**;
- **E19 — Business Travel Itinerary & Change Record**.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Build the weekly diary
Include at least eight commitments. Add realistic preparation blocks and travel/transition time. Include one tentative hold and one conflict requiring a scheduling decision.

### 2. Resolve a priority conflict
Document the conflicting commitments, business impact, options and final authorised decision. Show how all affected stakeholders and dependencies were updated.

### 3. Prepare a management meeting
Create a clear meeting purpose, attendee list, invitation, five-item agenda and meeting pack index. Identify items as Information, Discussion or Decision where useful.

### 4. Produce minutes and actions
Write concise simulated minutes with at least four decisions/actions. Transfer actions into a tracker with owner, deadline, dependency, follow-up and status.

### 5. Plan domestic travel
Prepare an itinerary for the director travelling between two South African cities for a client meeting. Include approved-style flight/transport, accommodation if needed, meeting details, buffers and booking references using fictional data.

### 6. Apply policy and approval thinking
Include a travel-approval checkpoint and explain what information would be checked against organisational policy before committing funds.

### 7. Simulate disruption
Add a flight delay or cancellation. Reassess the business commitment, compare two alternatives, identify additional cost/approval implications and update the itinerary.

### 8. Update the diary
Ensure the changed travel arrangements also update executive calendar commitments, transfers and stakeholder communications.

### 9. Record costs and changes
Maintain a simple change log showing original booking, revised arrangement, reason, cost impact and approval reference.

### 10. Close the workflow
After the simulated trip and meeting, close relevant actions, store the final meeting and travel records, and update the evidence index.

## Practical Activity
Complete E17–E19 as one integrated scenario. Then perform an audit asking: Are all times realistic? Are meeting documents current? Are action owners clear? Does travel protect the business commitment? Are changes traceable?

## Knowledge Check
1. Why must preparation and buffers appear in the weekly diary?
2. What should a scheduling-conflict record contain?
3. What makes a meeting pack complete?
4. What should minutes and action records prove?
5. What makes a business itinerary usable?
6. Why is a travel approval checkpoint required?
7. What should happen after a simulated flight cancellation?
8. Why must travel changes update the diary too?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. A visually free slot is not enough; commitments need realistic preparation, travel and transition time.
2. Conflict, impact, options, authority and final decision.
3. Purpose, agenda, current supporting documents, attendee/logistics and controlled versioning.
4. What was decided, who owns each action and by when.
5. Chronological travel, meetings, transfers, accommodation, references and buffers.
6. Cost and booking choices must remain within organisational authority and policy.
7. Confirm facts, protect the business purpose, compare options, obtain required approval and communicate updates.
8. The calendar must reflect the actual commitment and all affected dependencies.

## Key Takeaways
- Diary, meetings and travel are interconnected controls.
- Conflicts should be solved using impact and authority, not guesswork.
- Meeting preparation continues through action follow-up.
- Travel plans should protect the underlying business commitment.
- Changes must be recorded and reflected across all affected systems.
