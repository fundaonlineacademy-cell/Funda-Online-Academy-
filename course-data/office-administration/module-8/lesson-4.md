# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 8: Practical Admin-to-PA Project
### Lesson 4: Records & Document Management Portfolio

**Estimated study time:** 60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Build a controlled filing and document-management structure.
2. Apply naming, version and approval rules consistently.
3. Demonstrate retrieval, retention and access thinking.
4. Perform quality checks and reconciliation on administrative records.
5. Produce a traceable records-management portfolio.

## Project Task
Build the records and document-management evidence for Ubuntu Business Services. Your evidence should show how records move from creation or receipt through controlled storage, use and eventual retention review.

## Required Evidence
Create:
- **E09 — Filing & Classification Structure** with at least six record categories;
- **E10 — Naming & Version Control Standard** with sample filenames and change history;
- **E11 — Records Register** showing owner, location, access level and retention-rule source field;
- **E12 — Data Quality & Reconciliation Exercise** demonstrating identification and correction/escalation of at least three discrepancies.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Create a predictable filing structure
Use business functions and processes rather than personal names. Your structure should be understandable to another administrator.

### 2. Define naming rules
Choose a consistent convention using appropriate elements such as date, subject, document type, project/client and version/status.

### 3. Demonstrate version control
Show at least one document moving from Draft 1 to reviewed draft to approved final. Include a change-history record and remove or clearly control superseded copies.

### 4. Define access
Mark which folders or records are General Internal, Restricted or Executive/Confidential in the simulation. Explain who should have access and why.

### 5. Include retention awareness
Do not invent legal retention periods. Instead, include a field for the approved retention source/rule and show that records would be reviewed against organisational/legal requirements before disposal.

### 6. Demonstrate retrieval
Choose three records and describe how an authorised user would find them using the classification and naming structure.

### 7. Perform a reconciliation
Create a fictional supplier register and related source list with three differences: duplicate invoice, missing record and incorrect amount. Investigate each discrepancy rather than forcing totals to match.

### 8. Preserve traceability
Show how corrections are documented. Do not erase the evidence of a discrepancy merely to make the final spreadsheet look clean.

### 9. Apply secure disposal thinking
For one sensitive record category, explain the approval, hold check and secure disposal method that would be required when the approved retention period expires.

### 10. Audit your records system
Ask whether another authorised administrator can identify the authoritative file, current version, owner and storage location quickly.

## Practical Activity
Complete E09–E12 and test them by asking a peer or using a self-audit scenario: locate an approved supplier contract, retrieve current meeting minutes and identify who may access a fictional employee record. Record any weaknesses and correct them.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should filing structures use stable business functions?
2. What makes a naming convention useful?
3. What must version control make visible?
4. Why should access levels be defined?
5. Why should the project not invent legal retention periods?
6. What should happen when reconciliation finds a discrepancy?
7. Why preserve correction traceability?
8. What makes a records system auditable?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. They remain understandable even when individual staff change.
2. It consistently helps users identify and retrieve records.
3. Draft, current approved and superseded status.
4. Sensitive records should be limited to legitimate authorised users.
5. Actual requirements depend on record type, law and organisational policy.
6. Investigate against source records and escalate if unresolved.
7. It shows what changed and supports accountability.
8. Authoritative records, ownership, versions, access and storage can be traced reliably.

## Key Takeaways
- Build filing and version controls as one connected system.
- Define access according to legitimate business need.
- Retention must refer to approved rules rather than guesses.
- Reconciliation should investigate differences transparently.
- The final portfolio must make authoritative records easy to identify.
