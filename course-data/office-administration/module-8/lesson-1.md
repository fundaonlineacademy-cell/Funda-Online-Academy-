# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 8: Practical Admin-to-PA Project
### Lesson 1: Capstone Brief & Simulated Office Setup

**Estimated study time:** 60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Interpret the capstone project brief and required evidence.
2. Set up a realistic simulated office environment for the project.
3. Identify stakeholders, workflows, records and authority levels.
4. Build a project task plan with milestones and quality controls.
5. Apply professional file, version and evidence management from the start.

## Why This Lesson Matters
The capstone is where separate skills become one office-support system. Instead of completing isolated exercises, you will manage a simulated workplace in which diary changes affect meetings, customer enquiries create records, documents require approval and executive priorities compete for time.

The goal is to demonstrate reliable administrative judgement, not simply produce attractive documents.

## Capstone Scenario
You are the Administrator/Junior PA for **Ubuntu Business Services (Pty) Ltd**, a fictional South African professional-services company with approximately 30 employees. You support the Operations Director and coordinate routine reception, documents, meetings and executive administration.

Your simulated stakeholders include:
- Operations Director — primary manager/executive;
- Finance Manager — approvals and supplier/payment information;
- HR Coordinator — employee administration;
- Client Service Manager — customer issues;
- Reception/Office Assistant — front-desk support;
- external clients, suppliers and visitors.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Capstone** — Final integrated project demonstrating learning across the course.
- **Portfolio** — Organised evidence showing completed work and competence.
- **Project milestone** — Defined checkpoint indicating progress toward completion.
- **Evidence item** — Document, register, scenario response or other output demonstrating a skill.
- **Simulation** — Realistic practice environment used without acting on a live organisation.
- **Quality criterion** — Standard used to assess whether work is acceptable.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Understand the project outcomes
The final portfolio will demonstrate office control, communication, records management, customer/front-desk work, diary/meeting/travel coordination, PA-level executive support and technology/confidentiality awareness.

### 2. Create the simulated office structure
Set up approved-style folders for:
1. Administration Controls
2. Communication
3. Records
4. Front Desk
5. Meetings & Travel
6. Executive Support
7. Technology & Confidentiality
8. Final Handover

Use meaningful filenames and clear draft/final status.

### 3. Define authority
In the simulation, you may coordinate routine administrative actions. Manager approval is required for material spending, significant customer remedies, confidential executive decisions and policy exceptions. This forces you to demonstrate escalation rather than simply deciding everything yourself.

### 4. Identify recurring workflows
Include incoming enquiries, visitor handling, document approval, meeting preparation, action tracking, diary requests and travel planning. Map where records are created and who owns decisions.

### 5. Build the project plan
Create milestones for each remaining lesson. Include evidence required, owner (you or simulated stakeholder), target date, dependency and quality check.

### 6. Establish evidence control
Every portfolio item should have a clear title, purpose and final version. Avoid producing multiple uncontrolled copies. Keep a project evidence index.

### 7. Apply confidentiality
Use fictional names and data only. Do not insert real identity numbers, banking data, passwords or confidential workplace information into the capstone.

### 8. Quality over volume
The portfolio should show usable work. Ten well-controlled records are stronger than fifty repetitive documents with no clear purpose.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
For “Meeting Pack,” the evidence index might state: `E05 — Monthly Operations Meeting Pack — final approved simulation — demonstrates agenda, version control, distribution and meeting logistics.` The evidence index allows an assessor to trace which course outcomes each item demonstrates.

## Practical Activity — Project Setup
Create:
1. the eight-folder portfolio structure;
2. a stakeholder and authority matrix;
3. a workflow map containing at least six administrative workflows;
4. a project milestone tracker;
5. an evidence index with planned evidence IDs E01–E20.

5. Why should filenames and version status be controlled from the start?
6. What type of data should not be inserted into the simulation?
7. Why are milestones useful?
8. Is portfolio quality measured mainly by document quantity?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. To demonstrate integrated application of the course skills.
2. It allows realistic practice without exposing live organisational information or making real-world decisions.
3. To demonstrate judgement, delegation and escalation accurately.
4. Evidence ID, title, purpose/status and relevant outcome or description.
5. The final portfolio must remain traceable and free from version confusion.
6. Real confidential personal, banking, password or other sensitive data.
7. They make progress and dependencies visible.
8. No. Usability, accuracy, control and relevance matter more.

## Key Takeaways
- The capstone integrates the entire course.
- Use a realistic but fictional office scenario.
- Define stakeholders, authority and workflows before producing evidence.
- Control portfolio files and versions from the beginning.
- Demonstrate judgement and usable outputs, not volume alone.
