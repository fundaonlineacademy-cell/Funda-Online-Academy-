# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 8: Practical Admin-to-PA Project
### Lesson 3: Professional Communication Portfolio

**Estimated study time:** 60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Produce professional written and telephone communication evidence.
2. Adapt messages for managers, colleagues, clients and suppliers.
3. Record commitments and follow-up actions accurately.
4. Demonstrate professional complaint and difficult-conversation handling.
5. Apply confidentiality and authority limits throughout communication.

## Project Task
Build a communication portfolio for Ubuntu Business Services using realistic fictional scenarios. Each item should demonstrate a different communication purpose and show what action followed.

## Required Evidence
Create:
- **E05 — Business Email Set**: meeting confirmation, information request and overdue follow-up;
- **E06 — Memo and Notice Set**;
- **E07 — Telephone/Enquiry Message Log** with at least six entries;
- **E08 — Difficult Conversation & Complaint Response Record** containing two scenarios and escalation decisions.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Write for purpose and audience
Every communication item should show a clear purpose, correct audience, required action and professional tone.

### 2. Demonstrate channel choice
Explain briefly why email, telephone, memo, notice or another approved channel was selected for at least three portfolio items.

### 3. Include accurate commitments
Where a message promises an update or response, add that commitment to the task register from Lesson 2. Communication should connect to follow-through.

### 4. Show recipient control
For the email set, identify To and Cc decisions. Avoid copying unnecessary stakeholders or exposing confidential information.

### 5. Demonstrate clarification
Include one example where an initial instruction is vague and you ask focused questions before acting.

### 6. Record telephone information
Your message log should capture caller, contact details, purpose/reference, urgency, routing, follow-up and status.

### 7. Handle a complaint
Use the sequence: listen, acknowledge, record, verify, assess authority, explain next step, follow up and close/escalate.

### 8. Show a difficult internal conversation
Write a factual response to a colleague who missed an important deadline. Focus on the work impact and required next action rather than personal criticism.

### 9. Apply confidentiality
Use fictional data and limit sensitive information in communication. Show at least one case where you decline or refer an information request because the requester is not authorised.

### 10. Quality review
Proofread dates, names, deadlines, references, attachments and tone. Confirm each item's follow-up action is visible in the control system.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A client emails saying nobody has responded about invoice 4521. The portfolio evidence includes a professional acknowledgement, a message record, referral to Finance, follow-up time and final closure note. This demonstrates communication as a complete administrative workflow rather than a single email.

## Practical Activity
Complete E05–E08. Then use a quality checklist to verify purpose, recipient, tone, action, deadline, confidentiality, authority and follow-up for every item.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should communication portfolio items show what happened after the message?
2. What should determine channel choice?
3. Why should To and Cc decisions be reviewed?
4. What information belongs in a telephone message log?
5. What is a useful complaint-handling sequence?
6. How should an internal missed deadline be addressed?
7. Why include one information-refusal or referral example?
8. What should the final communication quality review check?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Professional communication supports action and follow-through, not just wording.
2. Purpose, urgency, complexity, sensitivity and record needs.
3. To avoid unnecessary disclosure and communication noise.
4. Caller, contact, purpose/reference, urgency, routing and follow-up status.
5. Listen, acknowledge, record, verify, assess authority, act/escalate, update and close.
6. Focus factually on the impact, required action and timing.
7. It demonstrates confidentiality and authority judgement.
8. Facts, recipients, attachments, tone, deadlines, authority and follow-up.

## Key Takeaways
- Communication evidence should connect words to action.
- Adapt channel and detail to the stakeholder.
- Keep promises visible in the task-control system.
- Demonstrate complaint handling, clarification and confidentiality.
- Quality-check every communication before finalising the portfolio.
