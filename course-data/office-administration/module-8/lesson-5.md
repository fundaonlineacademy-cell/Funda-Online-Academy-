# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 8: Practical Admin-to-PA Project
### Lesson 5: Front-desk and Customer-service Simulation

**Estimated study time:** 60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Run a simulated front-desk workflow from arrival to closure.
2. Handle visitor, enquiry and delivery records accurately.
3. Apply access, privacy and confidentiality controls.
4. Demonstrate complaint handling and service recovery.
5. Produce a complete front-desk handover.

## Project Task
Run one simulated front-desk day for Ubuntu Business Services. Your evidence must show both service quality and operational control.

## Required Evidence
Create:
- **E13 — Front-desk Daily Control Sheet**;
- **E14 — Visitor & Delivery Register** with at least eight fictional entries;
- **E15 — Customer Enquiry/Complaint Log** with at least five cases;
- **E16 — Front-desk Handover** containing at least four unresolved or next-day items.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Begin with opening controls
Review expected visitors, phone readiness, access procedures, meeting rooms, deliveries and previous handover items.

### 2. Simulate visitor handling
Include an expected client, unexpected supplier, contractor and early visitor. Show verification, host contact, access decision, waiting status and departure record.

### 3. Include a security exception
Add one visitor who attempts to bypass procedure through urgency or claimed seniority. Demonstrate polite refusal, verification and escalation rather than unrestricted access.

### 4. Handle deliveries
Record at least two parcels or documents with sender/courier, recipient, time and collection/receipt status. Include one confidential item requiring secure custody.

### 5. Simulate enquiries
Include routine information, a repeated unresolved enquiry and a sensitive request that requires referral or restricted disclosure.

### 6. Simulate a complaint
A fictional client should complain about a missed callback or service delay. Record acknowledgement, facts, owner, update commitment, escalation and final outcome.

### 7. Protect personal information
Do not place real identities or contact details in your register. Make the evidence realistic but entirely fictional.

### 8. Manage waiting and delays
Show one host delay and how the visitor is updated without exposing the host's private schedule.

### 9. Complete closing controls
Reconcile visitor passes, unresolved deliveries, outstanding messages and security items. Update your task register.

### 10. Produce the handover
Each unresolved item should include current status, owner, next action, deadline/reference and risk where relevant.

## Practical Activity
Complete E13–E16 as one connected simulated day. At the end, identify two service problems and two control risks. Recommend one process improvement for each.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should the simulation begin with opening controls?
2. What should visitor evidence show beyond a name and arrival time?
3. How should a claimed-seniority access request be handled?
4. What makes a delivery record useful?
5. How should a sensitive enquiry be handled?
6. What should a complaint record demonstrate?
7. Why should visitor-pass reconciliation occur at closing?
8. What makes a handover actionable?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Reception performance depends on preparation, expected activity and prior outstanding work.
2. Verification, host, access, status and departure where applicable.
3. Apply the same authorised verification/escalation procedure respectfully.
4. Sender/courier, recipient, time, reference and custody/collection status.
5. Use authorised disclosure and referral controls.
6. Acknowledgement, facts, owner, action, update, escalation and closure.
7. Missing passes may represent an unresolved access risk.
8. Clear status, owner, next action and timing.

## Key Takeaways
- Front-desk service must remain controlled from opening to closing.
- Visitor access, deliveries and enquiries all require usable records.
- Security controls should remain professional and consistent.
- Complaints should connect to service recovery and follow-up.
- A good handover protects continuity into the next day.
