# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 2: Professional Communication
### Lesson 2: Professional Telephone Skills

**Estimated study time:** 40–45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Answer and place business calls professionally.
2. Identify caller needs before transferring or referring a call.
3. Take complete, accurate telephone messages.
4. Manage holds, transfers and unavailable colleagues appropriately.
5. Respond to difficult or sensitive calls within authority limits.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Telephone calls are immediate. The caller forms an impression of the organisation while the administrator is still gathering information. Strong telephone skills combine courtesy with control: listen carefully, capture accurate details, route the call appropriately and avoid promises or disclosures outside your authority.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Call screening** — Establishing relevant caller information and purpose before routing a call.
- **Transfer** — Connecting a caller to another extension or person.
- **Warm transfer** — Speaking to the receiving person or team before connecting the caller where systems and procedures allow.
- **Hold** — Temporarily keeping a caller connected while another action is taken.
- **Callback** — A commitment that a person will return the call.
- **Message log** — An approved record of telephone messages requiring follow-up.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Answer consistently
Follow the organisation's required greeting. A typical professional answer identifies the organisation or department and the employee where appropriate, followed by an offer to assist.

Speak clearly and at a reasonable pace. Avoid eating, carrying on another conversation or sounding irritated while answering.

### 2. Establish the caller's need
Before transferring, understand who is calling and why. This reduces unnecessary transfers and helps protect colleagues' time.

Do not interrogate callers aggressively. Ask only information relevant to routing, service and procedure.

### 3. Listen and confirm details
Names, telephone numbers, reference numbers and dates are easy to mishear. Repeat critical information back when appropriate. If a name is unfamiliar, politely ask for spelling.

For complex messages, summarise the request before ending the call.

### 4. Taking a complete message
A useful message normally includes:
- caller's name and organisation where relevant;
- contact number or other approved contact detail;
- date and time;
- person requested;
- clear reason or message;
- urgency or deadline if stated;
- administrator's identity or system record where required.

Avoid adding personal assumptions such as “caller sounded dishonest” unless an authorised incident process genuinely requires relevant factual observations.

### 5. Holds
Ask or explain before placing a caller on hold according to workplace practice. If the wait becomes long, return with an update and offer an appropriate alternative.

A caller should not remain forgotten on hold.

### 6. Transfers
Confirm the destination before transferring. Where possible and appropriate, provide the receiving person with enough context so the caller does not need to repeat everything.

If the transfer fails, the process should not simply abandon the caller. Know how your telephone system handles failed transfers.

### 7. Unavailable colleagues
Do not disclose private details about why an employee is unavailable. “She is away from her desk” or an approved alternative may be sufficient. Offer to take a message or route the caller appropriately.

Never guarantee a callback time unless authorised and realistic.

### 8. Difficult callers
Remain calm and allow the caller to explain the issue. Clarify facts, acknowledge the concern without automatically admitting liability or making unauthorised promises, and identify the next appropriate action.

If the caller becomes abusive, threatening or discriminatory, follow organisational escalation and safety procedures. Professional service does not require accepting threatening behaviour.

### 9. Confidentiality and identity
Do not disclose employee schedules, personal contact details, customer information or internal matters merely because a caller requests them. Follow verification procedures where information is restricted.

A confident voice does not prove identity.

### 10. Outgoing calls
Prepare before calling. Know whom you need, the purpose, relevant reference details and required outcome. Introduce yourself and organisation clearly, respect the other person's time and record commitments or follow-up actions.

### 11. PA-level call handling
A PA may screen calls for an executive. The goal is not to block everyone; it is to understand priorities and route calls intelligently within agreed instructions. The PA should never falsely claim executive authority or misrepresent information to avoid a caller.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A caller says, “Put me through to the CEO immediately. It's personal.” The administrator should remain courteous, establish the caller's identity and enough purpose to apply the executive's call-handling instructions, avoid revealing the CEO's private schedule, and offer the authorised route such as taking a message or contacting the PA. The caller's insistence does not automatically override procedure.

## Practical Activity
Create a telephone message template with fields for caller, organisation, contact details, person requested, date/time, message, urgency/deadline and follow-up status. Then role-play or write responses for: a routine enquiry, failed transfer, angry customer, caller requesting private employee details and urgent executive call.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should a caller's purpose be established before transfer?
2. Which details should be confirmed carefully during a call?
3. What makes a telephone message actionable?
4. What should happen if a caller is left on hold longer than expected?
5. Why should private reasons for a colleague's absence not be disclosed casually?
6. Does an angry customer automatically justify promising a refund?
7. Why may identity verification be necessary by telephone?
8. What is the purpose of executive call screening?

1. It improves routing and reduces unnecessary transfers.
2. Names, numbers, references, dates, times and other critical information.
3. It contains accurate caller details, the request, timing and information needed for follow-up.
4. Provide an update and an appropriate alternative where possible.
5. Availability does not remove the employee's privacy or confidentiality rights.
6. No. Follow authority and complaint procedures.
7. A caller's voice or confidence does not prove they are authorised to receive restricted information.
8. To route communication according to executive priorities and agreed instructions.

## Key Takeaways
- Telephone professionalism combines courtesy, accuracy and control.
- Understand the request before routing the caller.
- Record messages so another person can act without guessing.
- Holds and transfers require follow-through.
- Protect private information and respect authority limits.
- Difficult callers should be handled calmly and escalated when required.
