# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 7: Office Technology, Information & Confidentiality
### Lesson 7: POPIA Awareness, Personal Information & Confidentiality

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Recognise common forms of personal information handled in office work.
2. Explain why personal information should be collected, used, shared and stored only through authorised business processes.
3. Apply practical privacy and confidentiality controls to routine administration.
4. Recognise situations that require escalation to a manager, information officer, privacy lead or other authorised function.
5. Respond appropriately to accidental disclosure or loss of personal information.

## Why This Lesson Matters
South African office staff routinely handle information about employees, customers, suppliers and visitors. The Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) establishes a framework for the lawful processing and protection of personal information. Administrators do not need to act as lawyers, but they do need to understand that personal information cannot be treated casually simply because it is available inside the organisation.

This lesson provides practical workplace awareness and does not replace organisation-specific legal, privacy or compliance guidance.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Personal information** — Information relating to an identifiable person or, in certain circumstances, an identifiable juristic person under POPIA.
- **Processing** — Broad term covering activities such as collecting, recording, storing, using, sharing, updating or deleting information.
- **Data subject** — Person or entity to whom personal information relates in the context provided by law.
- **Responsible party** — Person or organisation that determines the purpose and means of processing personal information.
- **Operator** — Person or organisation processing personal information for a responsible party under a mandate or contract.
- **Confidentiality** — Restricting information to appropriate authorised people and purposes.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Recognise personal information in routine work
Examples may include names, identity details, contact information, employment records, banking details, customer account information, photographs, location information and other information linked to an identifiable person.

Some categories can be particularly sensitive and may require stronger controls under organisational policy and applicable law.

### 2. Use information for authorised purposes
Just because an employee can see information does not mean they may use it for any purpose. A customer telephone number collected for service delivery should not be copied into a private marketing list or personal phone without an authorised basis and process.

### 3. Collect only what the process requires
Administrative forms and registers should request information that serves a defined business purpose. Avoid collecting extra personal details merely because they might be useful later.

### 4. Keep information accurate where the role requires it
Incorrect personal information can cause service failures, payments to wrong accounts or correspondence to the wrong address. Follow approved processes for corrections and updates.

### 5. Control access and sharing
Share personal information only with authorised people who require it for legitimate work. Use approved systems, correct recipients and appropriate permission settings.

A broad team chat or open shared folder is not automatically suitable simply because it is convenient.

### 6. Protect physical information
Paper forms, visitor registers and employee documents should not be left where unauthorised people can read them. Use secure storage and disposal practices according to organisational requirements.

### 7. Protect digital information
Use access controls, screen locks, approved cloud storage, secure sharing methods and organisational devices/services. Avoid copying sensitive records into personal email or cloud accounts.

### 8. Requests about personal information
Customers or employees may ask to access, correct or otherwise exercise rights concerning their information. Do not invent a process or promise an immediate result. Follow the organisation's authorised privacy procedure and refer the request to the responsible function where required.

### 9. Accidental disclosure or loss
If personal information is sent to the wrong person, lost, exposed or accessed unexpectedly, report it promptly through the organisation's incident or privacy process. Do not try to hide the event or privately “fix” it without reporting where reporting is required.

### 10. POPIA awareness and role boundaries
POPIA compliance can involve legal and organisational judgement beyond an administrator's role. Follow approved policies, training and instructions. Escalate uncertainty rather than guessing about complex legal requirements.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
An administrator prepares a spreadsheet of employee banking details for payroll support. Instead of emailing it to a broad departmental mailing list, the administrator uses the authorised restricted system and shares it only with the designated payroll personnel. When a recipient outside the payroll process is accidentally included, the administrator immediately follows the organisation's privacy/security incident procedure rather than simply asking the person to delete the message and keeping quiet.

## Practical Activity
Review these office records: visitor register, employee emergency contacts, customer complaint log, supplier contact list and payroll spreadsheet. For each, identify the legitimate business purpose, minimum useful fields, authorised users, storage location, sharing risk and escalation point if exposed.

## Key Takeaways
- Personal information is part of everyday office administration.
- Access does not mean unlimited permission to use or share information.
- Collect, store and share information through defined authorised processes.
- Protect both physical and digital records.
- Privacy incidents and uncertain legal questions should be escalated through the correct organisational channel.
