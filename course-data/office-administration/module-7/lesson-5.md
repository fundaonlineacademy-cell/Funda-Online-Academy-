# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 7: Office Technology, Information & Confidentiality
### Lesson 5: Cloud Collaboration & File Sharing

**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the benefits and risks of approved cloud collaboration.
2. Apply appropriate file ownership, permissions and sharing settings.
3. Reduce duplicate files through controlled collaboration.
4. Manage external sharing and link access carefully.
5. Maintain continuity when staff roles or access needs change.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Cloud collaboration allows teams to work on the same files from different locations, but convenience can lead to uncontrolled sharing. Administrators need to understand who owns a file, who can access it and how access should change when work is completed or staff responsibilities change.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Cloud storage** — Remote digital storage accessed through approved online services.
- **File owner** — Account or organisational role responsible for a digital file or workspace.
- **Permission** — Authorised access level such as view, comment, edit or manage.
- **Shared workspace** — Organisational digital area used by multiple authorised users.
- **External collaborator** — Authorised user outside the organisation.
- **Access review** — Periodic check that existing permissions remain appropriate.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Use approved platforms
Business records should be stored in organisationally approved services. Personal cloud drives create continuity, ownership and security risks.

### 2. Understand ownership
Files should remain under organisational control rather than depending on a departing employee's personal account. Shared drives or team workspaces can support continuity when configured correctly.

### 3. Match access to role
Use least-necessary access. A reviewer may need Comment, while a final recipient may need View only. Edit or Manage rights should be limited to people who genuinely need them.

### 4. Share intentionally
Before sharing, check the correct file, recipient, access level and duration. Avoid sending broad links when individual authorised access is more appropriate.

### 5. External collaboration
External stakeholders may need temporary access to a project folder or document. Confirm approval, limit access scope, and remove access when the work ends according to procedure.

### 6. Avoid duplicate download cycles
Downloading, editing and re-uploading shared files can create parallel versions. Use online collaboration features where appropriate and keep one authoritative working location.

### 7. Link management
Links can be forwarded. Understand whether a link is restricted to named users, organisation-only or broader access. Choose the setting required by the sensitivity and purpose of the information.

### 8. Access changes
When employees change roles, leave projects or exit the organisation, permissions should be reviewed. Do not assume access disappears automatically in every system.

### 9. Offline access
Offline copies can help during travel or outages but may become outdated. Synchronise changes and remove temporary sensitive copies when authorised and no longer required.

### 10. Audit and review
Where systems provide sharing history or access reports, use them when appropriate to verify unusual or sensitive sharing. Periodic access reviews help remove permissions that are no longer needed.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A consultant is given Edit access to an entire department folder to work on one policy document. The administrator corrects the setup by placing the relevant document in an approved collaboration area, limiting the consultant to the necessary file and permission, and scheduling access removal when the project ends.

## Practical Activity
Create an access matrix for a shared project workspace containing Drafts, Approved Documents, Finance, Client Materials and Meeting Records. Assign View, Comment, Edit or No Access to PA, Manager, Finance, Client and External Consultant. Explain three choices.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should personal cloud storage be avoided for business records?
2. What does least-necessary access mean?
3. Why should external access have a defined scope?
4. What risk comes from broad sharing links?
5. Why can download/edit/upload cycles create problems?
6. When should permissions be reviewed?
7. What risk comes from offline copies?
8. What is the purpose of an access review?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. It creates ownership, continuity and security risks outside organisational control.
2. Give users only the permissions needed for their legitimate role.
3. External collaborators should not receive unrelated organisational information.
4. Links may be forwarded to unintended people.
5. Multiple competing versions may be created.
6. During role changes, project closure, staff departures and periodic reviews.
7. They may become outdated or remain on devices after they are no longer needed.
8. To confirm that current access still matches legitimate business requirements.

## Key Takeaways
- Use approved organisational cloud services.
- Keep ownership under organisational control.
- Share only the required files with the required permissions.
- Review external and old access regularly.
- Controlled collaboration should reduce, not multiply, versions.
