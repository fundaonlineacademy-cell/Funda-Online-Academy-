# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 7: Office Technology, Information & Confidentiality
### Lesson 8: Digital Housekeeping, Backups & Technology Problem Escalation

**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Perform routine digital housekeeping without deleting required records.
2. Explain the role of approved backups and synchronisation.
3. Identify common signs of storage, access and version problems.
4. Gather useful information before escalating technology incidents.
5. Maintain continuity when systems or devices are temporarily unavailable.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Digital workplaces deteriorate when folders fill with duplicates, devices hold uncontrolled downloads and nobody knows whether important information is backed up. Housekeeping improves usability, but careless deletion can be just as dangerous as clutter. Administrators need controlled cleanup and clear escalation habits.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Digital housekeeping** — Routine organisation and cleanup of digital workspaces according to approved rules.
- **Backup** — Additional protected copy of data used for recovery after loss or damage.
- **Synchronisation** — Process keeping authorised copies or devices aligned with a central system.
- **Restore** — Recover data from a backup or prior state.
- **Storage quota** — Limit on available system storage.
- **Business continuity** — Ability to maintain essential work during disruption.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Housekeeping is controlled maintenance
Review temporary downloads, duplicate working copies, obsolete drafts and unused folders. Do not delete business records solely because they look old. Retention, legal hold and records procedures still apply.

### 2. Keep active areas clear
Move completed records into their approved final or archive location. Remove obsolete shortcuts and use consistent folder names. A clean active workspace helps users identify what is current.

### 3. Understand backups
A backup is not the same as manually saving a second copy to a random USB drive. Approved backup systems should be managed by the organisation or authorised service and tested for recovery.

### 4. Cloud synchronisation is not always a backup
Synchronisation can copy accidental deletion or corruption across devices. Follow organisational IT guidance on what services provide true backup and recovery.

### 5. Avoid unapproved storage devices
Personal USB drives or external disks can create malware, loss and confidentiality risks. Use only approved media and encryption controls where required.

### 6. Recognise warning signs
Repeated sync errors, missing permissions, low storage, corrupted files, unexpected login prompts or rapidly changing filenames may indicate a technology issue needing support.

### 7. Prepare a useful support request
Record the system/device, error message, time, affected file or process, steps already tried, screenshot where authorised, and business impact. Avoid sending sensitive information unnecessarily in the support ticket.

### 8. Know what not to troubleshoot
Do not disable antivirus, bypass access controls, install unauthorised software or make high-risk system changes simply to restore convenience. Escalate beyond your competence or authority.

### 9. Continuity during outages
Know approved alternatives for essential work: contact lists, meeting details, offline procedures or manual logs. Temporary workarounds should later be reconciled into the official system.

### 10. Review after recovery
Once service returns, confirm missing transactions, offline changes and outstanding tasks have been captured. A system being online again does not automatically mean the business process is complete.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A shared drive becomes unavailable before a client meeting. The PA uses the approved offline copy of the final presentation, records two notes manually during the outage and reports the incident with the error and business impact. After recovery, the PA uploads or records the authorised notes in the official system and removes the temporary copy according to procedure.

## Practical Activity
Create a monthly digital-housekeeping checklist with sections for active folders, downloads, duplicates, permissions, storage warnings, offline copies and support issues. Then draft a model IT support ticket for a shared-drive access failure.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Retention, legal hold or organisational requirements may still apply.
2. A protected additional copy used to recover information after loss or damage.
3. Sync can reproduce deletion or corruption rather than preserve an independent recovery copy.
4. Malware, loss, unauthorised copies and confidentiality exposure.
5. Examples include sync errors, missing access, low storage, corruption or unusual login prompts.
6. System/device, error, time, process affected, checks tried and business impact.
7. Disabling security tools, bypassing controls or installing unauthorised software.
8. Reconcile offline work, confirm missing records and close outstanding process actions.

## Key Takeaways
- Digital housekeeping must respect records and retention controls.
- Approved backups support recovery; random copies do not equal backup.
- Do not bypass security controls during troubleshooting.
- Escalate technology problems with useful diagnostic details.
- Reconcile temporary offline work after recovery.
