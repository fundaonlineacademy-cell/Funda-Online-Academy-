# Office Administration Certificate: From Admin to PA
## Module 6: Personal Assistant Skills
### Lesson 3: Inbox, Correspondence & Information Triage

**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Triage executive inbox items by urgency, importance and required action.
2. Separate information, delegation, decision and follow-up items.
3. Draft or prepare routine correspondence within authority.
4. Protect confidential information and avoid unsafe email practices.
5. Maintain visibility of outstanding decisions and commitments.

## Why This Lesson Matters
An executive inbox can contain customer requests, approvals, meeting changes, confidential documents and routine newsletters at the same time. A PA adds value by making the important items easier to see and act on without hiding information or making decisions beyond delegated authority.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Inbox triage** — Systematic review and classification of incoming messages.
- **Decision item** — Message requiring an authorised choice or approval.
- **Delegation item** — Message that can be routed to another responsible person.
- **Information item** — Message requiring awareness but no immediate action.
- **Action item** — Message requiring a specific task or response.
- **Flag/follow-up marker** — Tool used to keep an outstanding message visible.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Triage by business need
Do not process messages only in arrival order. Identify time-sensitive commitments, high-impact stakeholders, approvals, risks and deadlines.

### 2. Classify the required action
Useful categories include: executive decision, PA action, delegate/refer, information only, calendar update, file/record and follow-up required.

### 3. Protect decision items
Summarise decision items so the executive sees what is needed, by when and with what consequences. Attach or link the relevant evidence rather than forcing the executive to search across a long thread.

### 4. Draft routine correspondence
Where authorised, a PA may draft acknowledgements, scheduling responses, confirmations and routine letters. Drafting does not equal final authority. Follow the manager's approval rules and ensure wording accurately represents the organisation.

### 5. Manage email threads
Use clear subjects and keep related communication together where useful. Do not add unrelated sensitive matters to a broad existing thread merely for convenience.

### 6. Use folders, labels and flags carefully
Tools should support retrieval and action, not create hundreds of categories nobody can maintain. Keep the system simple enough to use consistently.

### 7. Prevent inbox disappearance
Moving an email into a folder does not complete its action. Transfer commitments into the diary, task register or appropriate workflow where necessary.

### 8. Protect confidential information
Check recipients, forwarding history and attachments before sending. Do not forward entire threads when the new recipient needs only a limited part. Use authorised secure channels for sensitive material.

### 9. Watch for phishing and unusual requests
Executive offices can be targets for fraudulent payment or credential requests. Treat unusual urgency, changed banking details or requests for passwords as verification triggers according to organisational security procedures.

### 10. End-of-day review
Review flagged decisions, unread high-priority items and promised responses. Ensure urgent commitments are not buried beneath new messages.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A PA finds an email marked urgent requesting payment to a new bank account, apparently from a familiar supplier. Instead of forwarding it immediately as routine, the PA recognises the unusual banking change, follows the organisation's verification process and alerts the appropriate finance/security function. Urgency does not replace verification.

## Practical Activity
Create an inbox-triage matrix with categories: Executive Decision, PA Action, Delegate, Information, Calendar, Record and Follow-up. Classify 15 sample messages. For three decision items, write a one-sentence decision summary stating issue, deadline and required choice.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should inboxes not be handled only in arrival order?
2. Name four useful inbox categories.
3. What makes a decision item easy for an executive to process?
4. Does drafting correspondence give the PA authority to approve it?
5. Why can moving email to a folder be misleading?
6. What should be checked before forwarding sensitive email?
7. What type of message should trigger extra verification?
8. What should an end-of-day inbox review focus on?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Business impact, deadlines and risk differ across messages.
2. Examples include decision, PA action, delegate, information, calendar, record and follow-up.
3. A concise summary of the issue, relevant facts, timing and decision required.
4. No. Approval remains with the authorised person unless delegated.
5. The message may be organised but its underlying action may still be outstanding.
6. Recipients, attachment, thread content, authority and appropriate secure channel.
7. Unusual payment, credential, banking or high-pressure requests are examples.
8. Outstanding urgent items, promised responses, decisions and time-sensitive follow-up.

## Key Takeaways
- Triage inboxes by business value, not merely chronology.
- Distinguish decision, action, delegation, information and follow-up items.
- Make executive decisions easy to identify and process.
- Email organisation must connect to real task control.
- Unusual financial or credential requests require verification.
