# Module 1 Summative Assessment Bank — South African Retail Management Foundations

**Bank size:** 52 MCQs | **Delivery:** 25 | **Pass:** 70% | **Maximum attempts:** 3

1. A retail manager creates most value by: A coordinating people, stock, service and controls toward store objectives B doing every task personally C avoiding reports D focusing only on appearance. **Answer: A**
2. When a supervisor is assigned replenishment, the store manager: A has no further responsibility B should retain appropriate oversight and accountability C must redo all work D should not set a standard. **Answer: B**
3. Which is a management KPI? A wall colour B employee shoe size C sales conversion rate D supplier logo. **Answer: C**
4. After checking execution and finding a gap, the manager should: A forget it B advertise C close the store D take appropriate corrective action. **Answer: D**
5. Evidence-based management uses: A reliable observations and records B rumours only C instinct only D no measures. **Answer: A**
6. A customer shopping mission is: A a staff roster B the purpose of the customer's shopping interaction C a supplier contract D a till float. **Answer: B**
7. A convenience store and a fashion store may need different priorities because: A all retail is identical B customers never differ C formats and shopping missions differ D policies do not matter. **Answer: C**
8. A physical store can compete with an online seller because: A online sellers cannot sell goods B customers never compare channels C only price matters D customers may choose among alternative channels for similar needs. **Answer: D**
9. Store-specific demand planning should use: A historical and current local evidence B national assumptions only C guesses D no customer information. **Answer: A**
10. If unexpected traffic rises sharply, the manager should first: A ignore it B assess service, staffing, stock and queue priorities C cancel all breaks permanently D close tills. **Answer: B**
11. A reporting line helps clarify: A product colour B rent value C who has authority and accountability D weather. **Answer: C**
12. Escalation is appropriate when: A every small task occurs B a manager wants to avoid work C no risk exists D the issue exceeds authority or specialist competence. **Answer: D**
13. A clear role description helps: A reduce ambiguity about responsibilities B determine customer income C replace training D remove all supervision. **Answer: A**
14. When two supervisors believe the other owns a task, the management problem is mainly: A pricing B unclear accountability C marketing D customer segmentation. **Answer: B**
15. Good delegation includes: A no deadline B no authority C clear outcome, owner, authority and follow-up D no checking. **Answer: C**
16. Accountability is strongest when: A nobody records results B several people assume someone else will act C standards are optional D ownership and expected results are clear. **Answer: D**
17. Product availability is commercially important because: A customers cannot buy products they cannot access B it eliminates competition C it removes pricing D it guarantees profit. **Answer: A**
18. An omnichannel customer may: A use one channel only B order online and collect in-store C never use a phone D avoid returns. **Answer: B**
19. A manager analysing customer missions should consider: A only staff preference B only supplier preference C why customers visit and what outcome they seek D only store size. **Answer: C**
20. When a customer researches online before entering a store, the team should: A refuse service B ignore online information C hide product information D support a consistent cross-channel experience within policy. **Answer: D**
21. A retail standard specifies: A the required condition or performance level B an employee's opinion C a rumour D a competitor's procedure. **Answer: A**
22. An SOP is useful because it: A removes all judgement B provides an approved repeatable method for a process C guarantees no errors D replaces management. **Answer: B**
23. When a policy does not cover an unusual high-risk exception, the manager should: A invent evidence B ignore controls C use authorised escalation D conceal the issue. **Answer: C**
24. A checklist is most effective when: A completed without looking B used only after failure C never reviewed D linked to real verification and follow-up. **Answer: D**
25. Repeated failure of the same standard suggests the manager should: A investigate causes and strengthen corrective action B simply rewrite the date C stop measuring D blame customers. **Answer: A**
26. A morning management plan should reflect: A no trading information B priorities, resources, risks and ownership C employee preferences only D yesterday's weather only. **Answer: B**
27. A store walk is a form of: A advertising B payroll C operational observation and verification D taxation. **Answer: C**
28. A blocked emergency route observed during a store walk should generally receive: A low priority B next-month attention C no action D immediate appropriate attention because of safety risk. **Answer: D**
29. During peak trading, a manager should: A monitor changing conditions and redeploy within policy where needed B remain in the office regardless of conditions C stop all replenishment automatically D ignore queues. **Answer: A**
30. A useful priority decision asks: A which issue is newest B what has the greatest relevant customer, safety, financial or operational impact C who complained loudest only D which task has the shortest name. **Answer: B**
31. Ethical management includes: A changing results to hit target B hiding stock losses C accurate reporting even when results are disappointing D favouring friends. **Answer: C**
32. A manager offered a personal benefit by a supplier should: A accept secretly B change the order C delete the record D follow conflict-of-interest and gift policies. **Answer: D**
33. Confidential business information should be: A accessed and shared only for authorised business purposes B posted publicly C discussed with customers D stored without controls. **Answer: A**
34. Fair decision-making means: A identical outcomes in every circumstance B applying relevant standards consistently while considering legitimate facts C favouring senior employees D ignoring policy. **Answer: B**
35. If an employee admits a serious till error, the manager should: A alter records secretly B promise no consequences C follow evidence, control and authorised process requirements D announce it publicly. **Answer: C**
36. Professional conduct supports trust because it: A guarantees sales B removes competition C eliminates mistakes D promotes reliable, respectful and accountable behaviour. **Answer: D**
37. The first step in a management control cycle is usually to: A plan B punish C advertise D count cash. **Answer: A**
38. A briefing should clarify: A private gossip B priorities, standards and ownership C competitor salaries D personal opinions. **Answer: B**
39. Checking is necessary because: A plans always fail B staff cannot work independently C managers need evidence that execution matches requirements D every task must be repeated. **Answer: C**
40. Corrective action without follow-up may fail because: A reports are illegal B customers dislike action C standards disappear D the manager cannot confirm whether the gap was resolved. **Answer: D**
41. A root cause is: A an underlying factor contributing to a recurring problem B a product barcode C a sales target D a shift start time. **Answer: A**
42. If a promotion repeatedly has missing tickets, a useful response is to: A print random prices B investigate the process that causes the recurring failure C stop promotions permanently D ignore customer complaints. **Answer: B**
43. A practical store review should compare: A employee personalities B competitors only C actual performance against plans, standards and indicators D only total sales. **Answer: C**
44. The review stage helps management: A avoid learning B remove accountability C stop measurement D capture lessons and improve the next cycle. **Answer: D**
45. Sales are on target but complaints and shrinkage are worsening. The manager should: A treat performance as multidimensional rather than relying on sales alone B ignore complaints C stop stock counts D assume the store is healthy. **Answer: A**
46. Cutting labour hours without considering traffic may: A always improve service B reduce cost but damage queues, replenishment and sales C eliminate shrinkage D increase stock automatically. **Answer: B**
47. A store manager notices two popular lines unavailable on the floor although stock exists in the backroom. The immediate management issue is: A supplier bankruptcy B national demand C execution/replenishment availability D customer fraud. **Answer: C**
48. When an operational problem involves possible legal or specialist implications, the manager should: A create a legal rule B make a public statement C destroy records D operate within authority and escalate appropriately. **Answer: D**
49. A strong manager develops supervisors because: A capability enables effective delegation and stronger execution B managers should avoid staff C training removes accountability D supervisors replace policies. **Answer: A**
50. The best response to an unexpected service queue is to: A blame customers B assess demand and available trained capacity, then redeploy appropriately C close the store D stop monitoring. **Answer: B**
51. The strongest sign of management control is: A manager is always busy B many meetings occur C standards are translated into ownership, execution, verification and improvement D employees never ask questions. **Answer: C**
52. The overall purpose of Module 1 is to prepare a learner to: A memorise store slogans B avoid commercial data C focus only on customer greetings D manage a retail store through structured, ethical and evidence-based control. **Answer: D**
