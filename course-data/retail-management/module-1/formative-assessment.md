# Module 1 Formative Assessment — South African Retail Management Foundations

**Bank size:** 32 MCQs | **Delivery:** 15 | **Pass:** 70% | **Maximum attempts:** 3

## Lesson 1 — The Role of the Retail Manager
1. Which best describes a retail manager's core role?
A. Coordinate resources and execution to achieve customer and business objectives
B. Personally perform every store task
C. Focus only on sales
D. Avoid delegating work
**Answer: A**

2. Delegation means that the manager:
A. loses all accountability
B. assigns task responsibility while retaining appropriate accountability
C. must never check results
D. transfers company ownership
**Answer: B**

3. Which belongs in the management control cycle after execution?
A. Ignore
B. Advertise
C. Check
D. Recruit
**Answer: C**

4. Which is evidence-based management?
A. Guessing customer traffic
B. Relying only on instinct
C. Avoiding reports
D. Combining observation with sales, stock and operational records
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 2 — The South African Retail Environment
5. A retail format is:
A. a type or model of retail operation
B. a staff roster
C. a tax invoice
D. a stock count
**Answer: A**

6. Why should a manager use store-specific trading data?
A. Every branch trades identically
B. Demand patterns differ by location and period
C. Historical information is never useful
D. It replaces all judgement
**Answer: B**

7. Which can influence store demand?
A. Only national advertising
B. Only the store manager
C. Pay cycles, promotions, holidays and local events
D. None of these
**Answer: C**

8. Omnichannel retail means customers may:
A. use cash only
B. shop in one department only
C. never return online purchases
D. interact across connected physical and digital channels
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 3 — Store Structures, Roles & Management Accountability
9. An organisation structure mainly clarifies:
A. roles, reporting lines and authority
B. customer prices
C. weather forecasts
D. supplier ownership
**Answer: A**

10. A useful escalation principle is to escalate when:
A. the issue is easy and within authority
B. risk or required authority exceeds the manager's mandate
C. the manager dislikes the task
D. a customer enters the store
**Answer: B**

11. A RACI-style responsibility approach helps teams clarify:
A. product colour
B. customer age
C. who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed
D. store rent
**Answer: C**

12. Why are clear reporting lines important?
A. They eliminate all problems
B. They reduce customer demand
C. They replace policies
D. They support timely decisions and accountability
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 4 — Customer, Product & Channel Fundamentals
13. A customer mission refers to:
A. the purpose behind the customer's shopping interaction
B. a staff disciplinary process
C. a stock write-off
D. a supplier invoice
**Answer: A**

14. Product availability means:
A. all stock is in the backroom
B. customers can obtain the required product when and where expected
C. every product has the same price
D. the store never sells out
**Answer: B**

15. Which illustrates cross-channel behaviour?
A. Employee clocks in
B. Supplier sends invoice
C. Customer researches online and buys in-store
D. Manager counts cash
**Answer: C**

16. A manager should align product and service execution primarily with:
A. personal preferences
B. competitor rumours
C. employee hobbies
D. customer missions and approved business strategy
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 5 — Retail Standards, Policies & Operating Procedures
17. A standard defines:
A. the required level or condition of performance
B. an optional rumour
C. a customer complaint
D. a salary payment
**Answer: A**

18. A procedure primarily explains:
A. why competitors exist
B. how an approved process should be performed
C. who owns the shopping centre
D. national GDP
**Answer: B**

19. If a procedure is unclear in a high-risk situation, a manager should:
A. invent a permanent rule
B. ignore the risk
C. escalate through the appropriate authority
D. ask a customer to decide
**Answer: C**

20. Compliance checks are most useful when they:
A. happen once a year only
B. focus on blame
C. contain no evidence
D. identify gaps and lead to corrective action
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 6 — Management Priorities, Daily Planning & Store Walks
21. A store walk should primarily help the manager:
A. observe conditions, identify priorities and verify execution
B. avoid employees
C. replace every report
D. perform advertising design
**Answer: A**

22. When several issues occur simultaneously, priority should consider:
A. alphabetical order
B. customer, safety, financial and operational impact
C. employee age
D. which task is easiest
**Answer: B**

23. A good daily plan includes:
A. no owners
B. only yesterday's problems
C. priorities, owners, timing and follow-up
D. no measurable expectations
**Answer: C**

24. Why should managers revisit the floor during trading?
A. Conditions never change
B. It is legally required every minute
C. Reports are prohibited
D. Actual trading conditions may differ from the opening plan
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 7 — Retail Ethics, Professional Conduct & Decision-Making
25. Ethical retail management requires:
A. honest, fair and responsible decisions within authority
B. hiding material errors
C. favouring friends in controls
D. changing records without approval
**Answer: A**

26. A conflict of interest should generally be:
A. concealed
B. disclosed and managed according to policy
C. rewarded
D. ignored if profitable
**Answer: B**

27. If a manager discovers a significant cash-control irregularity, the best first principle is to:
A. destroy evidence
B. accuse someone publicly
C. secure evidence and follow authorised escalation procedures
D. change the figures
**Answer: C**

28. Professional judgement should operate:
A. outside all company rules
B. without evidence
C. only when customers complain
D. within authority, policy and appropriate escalation boundaries
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 8 — Management Control Cycle & Practical Store Review
29. A store review compares:
A. actual conditions and results with required standards and plans
B. employee birthdays
C. supplier logos
D. product colours only
**Answer: A**

30. Corrective action should ideally address:
A. symptoms only
B. the identified gap and, where possible, its underlying cause
C. unrelated departments
D. customer demographics only
**Answer: B**

31. Which sequence best reflects a control cycle?
A. Sell–close–forget
B. Recruit–advertise–close
C. Plan–brief–execute–check–correct–review
D. Count–guess–ignore
**Answer: C**

32. The purpose of reviewing corrective action is to determine whether:
A. the manager looked busy
B. a report was printed
C. employees agreed with everything
D. the action solved the problem and reduced recurrence risk
**Answer: D**
