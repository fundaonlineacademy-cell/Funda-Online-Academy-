# Module 8 — Retail Management Capstone & Career Leadership
## Lesson 5 — Build the Commercial & Stock Action Plan

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- connect sales performance with margin, customer and stock indicators;
- identify commercial priorities from a simulated scorecard;
- design actions that improve availability while protecting stock control;
- evaluate promotion and markdown decisions; and
- assign measurable commercial and inventory actions.

## Why This Matters
Retail managers are responsible for more than achieving a sales number. Strong performance requires profitable sales, available products, disciplined stock movement and controlled costs. Chasing revenue without understanding margin, shrinkage or availability can damage the business.

### Key Terms
**Commercial driver:** Factor influencing revenue, margin or cost performance.
**Availability:** Ability to provide the product when and where the customer wants to buy it.
**Markdown:** Reduction in selling price, normally controlled by approved policy.
**Shrinkage:** Inventory loss not explained by legitimate recorded sales or authorised movements.
**Scorecard:** Selected measures used to review business performance.

## Main Lesson Content

### 1. Read measures together
Consider sales, traffic, conversion, ATV, UPT, gross margin, labour productivity, stock availability, variance and shrinkage indicators together where relevant.

Example: sales may be below target because traffic is low. But if traffic is healthy and conversion is weak, investigate availability, service, price and customer experience. If sales are strong but margin is poor, examine discounting, product mix and markdown activity.

### 2. Protect availability
Availability depends on the entire stock flow:
**order/supply → receive → record → store → replenish → sell/fulfil → adjust/count**.

A breakdown at any stage can create a false out-of-stock or incorrect system balance.

### 3. Investigate stock variance
Do not automatically classify unexplained variance as theft. Examine:
- receiving differences;
- transfer errors;
- incorrect adjustments;
- damages/write-offs;
- scanning or unit-of-measure errors;
- picking/fulfilment errors;
- process non-compliance; and
- possible internal/external loss where evidence supports investigation.

Use approved loss-prevention and HR/security processes for sensitive cases.

### 4. Promotion and markdown decisions
A promotion should be operationally executable. Check stock, ticketing, system price, display, staffing and customer communication. A markdown can improve sell-through but reduces unit margin, so decisions should use approved authority and commercial reasoning.

### 5. Build the action plan
For each commercial or stock issue define:
- baseline;
- desired result;
- action;
- owner;
- timing;
- cost/resource implication;
- risk/control;
- measure and review date.

### Worked Example
Ubuntu Retail Hub's promotional category shows good traffic but weak conversion. Shelf checks reveal repeated gaps while backroom stock exists. The action plan should focus first on replenishment discipline and ownership, not on ordering more stock automatically. The manager sets priority-line checks, assigns ownership, measures shelf availability and reviews sales/conversion after implementation.

## Practical Activity — Commercial & Stock Plan
Using the simulated store, create at least six actions covering:
1. sales/conversion;
2. ATV or UPT;
3. margin/markdown;
4. availability;
5. stock variance/shrinkage control; and
6. receiving/replenishment accuracy.

Include a measure and review date for every action.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should sales not be reviewed in isolation?
2. Name the major stages in stock flow.
3. Why is stock variance not automatically proof of theft?
4. What commercial risk accompanies markdowns?
5. What should every action include besides the action itself?

### Feedback
1. Because traffic, conversion, transaction value, margin, stock and cost measures may explain the result.
2. Supply/order, receive, record, store, replenish, sell/fulfil and adjust/count.
3. Many process or recording failures can also create variance.
4. Reduced unit margin and possible profitability impact.
5. Ownership, timing, controls/resources, measure and follow-up.

## Key Takeaways
- Manage profitable sales, not sales alone.
- Treat availability as an end-to-end stock-flow outcome.
- Investigate variance objectively.
- Control promotions and markdowns through approved authority.
- Connect every commercial action to measurable evidence.
