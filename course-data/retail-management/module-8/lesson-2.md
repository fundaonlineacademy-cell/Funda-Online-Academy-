# Module 8 — Retail Management Capstone & Career Leadership
## Lesson 2 — Diagnose Store Performance & Priorities

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- diagnose a simulated store using multiple sources of management evidence;
- distinguish symptoms from likely root causes;
- rank priorities by risk, impact and urgency;
- create a concise store diagnostic; and
- define measurable outcomes for priority problems.

## Why This Matters
Managers lose time when they attack symptoms without understanding causes. Low sales may be linked to weak traffic, poor conversion, unavailable stock, pricing errors, service failure or an unsuitable product mix. High stock variance may come from receiving errors, process failures, damage, incorrect adjustments or theft risk. Diagnosis prevents random action.

### Key Terms
**Symptom:** Visible evidence that a problem exists.
**Root cause:** Underlying factor contributing materially to the problem.
**Variance:** Difference between expected and actual performance.
**Triangulation:** Comparing several evidence sources before reaching a conclusion.
**Priority matrix:** Method for ranking issues using impact and urgency.

## Main Lesson Content

### 1. Start with the whole store
Use six diagnostic lenses:
1. **Customer** — complaints, queues, returns, service measures.
2. **People** — coverage, capability, expectations, engagement and attendance patterns.
3. **Operations** — opening/closing, housekeeping, pricing, promotion and execution.
4. **Commercial** — sales, conversion, ATV, UPT, margin, costs and targets.
5. **Stock** — availability, receiving, variance, damages, shrinkage indicators.
6. **Technology/information** — POS exceptions, dashboard signals, outages and digital fulfilment.

No single measure tells the entire story.

### 2. Diagnose variance
Use a simple sequence:
**What happened? → Where/when? → How large? → What evidence agrees? → What may explain it? → What must be verified?**

If footwear sales are 12% below target, do not immediately order more stock. Check traffic, conversion, availability, price, promotion execution, product mix and service observations.

### 3. Root-cause questioning
Repeatedly ask “why?” while testing each answer against evidence. Avoid forcing a predetermined conclusion.

Example:
- Symptom: click-and-collect orders are late.
- Why? Orders are not ready at promised time.
- Why? Picking often starts late.
- Why? No clear ownership exists during afternoon peak.
- Why? Shift plan does not assign digital fulfilment coverage.

Possible root cause: weak work allocation rather than simply “slow staff.”

### 4. Prioritise
Create a matrix using **impact** and **urgency**. High-impact/high-urgency matters move first. Then consider control risk and dependencies.

Priority examples:
- immediate: serious safety hazard, major cash-control exposure, significant information-security incident;
- urgent operational: unavailable promotion lines, severe queue failure, recurring receiving discrepancy;
- planned improvement: coaching gaps, layout optimisation, reporting refinement.

### 5. Define desired outcomes
Replace vague goals such as “improve service” with measurable outcomes such as:
- reduce average peak queue waiting time from the simulated baseline to an agreed target;
- achieve 98% promotional ticket accuracy during daily checks;
- complete all high-risk receiving checks with documented sign-off;
- reduce unexplained stock variance through identified controls.

### Worked Example
Ubuntu Retail Hub shows these signals:
- sales 7% below target;
- traffic close to plan;
- conversion below target;
- top promotional lines frequently unavailable;
- customer comments mention long queues;
- POS shows one till unavailable for repeated periods.

The diagnosis should not say “customers are not spending.” Traffic is present. A stronger hypothesis is that availability and service capacity may be reducing conversion. The manager should verify stock flow, till uptime, peak deployment and customer-service observations.

## Practical Activity — Diagnostic Dashboard
Create a table with columns:
**Area | Evidence | Variance/problem | Likely causes | Verification required | Priority | Desired outcome**.

Add at least eight issues across the six diagnostic lenses. Rank the top five.

### Feedback
1. Comparing several evidence sources before concluding.
2. Treating only the symptom may leave the underlying problem unchanged.
3. Customer, people, operations, commercial, stock and technology/information.
4. Impact and urgency.
5. So progress and effectiveness can be reviewed objectively.

## Key Takeaways
- Diagnose the store as a connected system.
- Compare data with observation and process evidence.
- Test likely causes instead of jumping to blame.
- Rank issues according to business risk and urgency.
- Define measurable outcomes before building the action plan.
