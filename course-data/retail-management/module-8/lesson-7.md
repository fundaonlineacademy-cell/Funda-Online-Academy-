# Module 8 — Retail Management Capstone & Career Leadership
## Lesson 7 — Retail Manager Career Readiness & Professional Portfolio

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- describe retail-management capability using evidence rather than vague claims;
- build a professional portfolio for supervisory and management opportunities;
- translate course activities into CV and interview examples;
- identify personal development priorities; and
- create a practical 90-day career-development plan.

## Why This Matters
Completing a course is valuable, but career progression also depends on demonstrating what you can do. Employers want evidence of leadership, customer management, operational discipline, commercial understanding, stock control, problem solving and communication. A professional portfolio helps you organise that evidence.

### Key Terms
**Competency:** Demonstrated ability to apply knowledge and skill effectively.
**Portfolio:** Organised collection of evidence showing capability and achievement.
**STAR:** Situation, Task, Action, Result — a structure for behavioural examples.
**Development gap:** Capability requiring further improvement.
**Career narrative:** Clear explanation of your experience, strengths and direction.

## Main Lesson Content

### 1. Build evidence, not adjectives
“Hard-working leader” is difficult to verify. Stronger evidence sounds like:
- coordinated peak-period deployment and reduced simulated queue delay;
- investigated stock variance using receiving, adjustment and count evidence;
- built a commercial action plan using conversion, ATV, UPT and margin measures;
- coached employees against observable service standards;
- prepared an evidence-based management report and action tracker.

Where examples come from this course simulation, label them honestly as simulated/capstone work. Never present fictional experience as paid employment.

### 2. Portfolio sections
A useful portfolio may include:
- professional profile;
- CV;
- course/certificate evidence;
- capstone store diagnosis;
- operations/service plan;
- shift/leadership plan;
- commercial/stock plan;
- dashboard/reporting example;
- development plan;
- references or genuine work evidence where available and appropriate.

Remove or protect confidential employer/customer information.

### 3. Use STAR examples
**Situation:** Brief context.
**Task:** What responsibility or problem existed?
**Action:** What did you personally do?
**Result:** What changed or what did you learn?

For a simulated assessment, say so. Example: “In my retail-management capstone simulation, I diagnosed weak conversion despite stable traffic...” This remains credible because it demonstrates reasoning without misrepresenting employment history.

### 4. Interview readiness
Prepare examples for:
- difficult customer/service recovery;
- leading a busy shift;
- coaching performance;
- resolving conflict;
- stock variance;
- commercial underperformance;
- POS/system incident;
- ethical decision or escalation.

Interviewers often test judgement. Explain why you chose an action, not only what you did.

### 5. Personal development
Rate yourself from 1–5 against course capabilities. Select three priorities. For each define:
- current gap;
- development activity;
- workplace/practice opportunity;
- evidence of improvement;
- target date.

### 6. First 90 days in a management role
A practical new-manager approach:
**Days 1–30:** learn standards, people, customers, routines, risks and measures.
**Days 31–60:** strengthen execution, coaching, controls and priority improvements.
**Days 61–90:** review results, standardise gains and agree next development priorities.

Avoid arriving and changing everything before understanding the operation.

### Worked Example
Weak CV statement: “Responsible for stock.”

Stronger genuine-work version: “Supported cycle counts, investigated documented discrepancies and followed approved stock-adjustment controls.”

Capstone version: “Completed a simulated stock-control diagnostic and improvement plan covering receiving, replenishment, variance investigation and shrinkage controls.”

## Practical Activity — Career Portfolio
Prepare:
1. 80–120 word professional profile;
2. six evidence-based competency statements;
3. four STAR examples;
4. self-rating across eight course modules;
5. three development priorities; and
6. 90-day development plan.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why are evidence-based statements stronger than adjectives?
2. Should simulated course work be presented as real employment experience?
3. What does STAR stand for?
4. Name three useful portfolio items.
5. What is the purpose of a 90-day plan?

### Feedback
1. They show what capability looks like in action.
2. No. It should be labelled honestly as simulation/capstone evidence.
3. Situation, Task, Action, Result.
4. Examples include CV, capstone plans, reports, course evidence and development plan.
5. To structure learning, execution and improvement during early management responsibility.

## Key Takeaways
- Career readiness requires credible evidence of capability.
- Never misrepresent simulations as employment experience.
- Prepare examples that demonstrate judgement and results.
- Protect confidential information in your portfolio.
- Continue developing after certification.
