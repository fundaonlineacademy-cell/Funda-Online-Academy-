# Module 8 — Retail Management Capstone & Career Leadership
## Lesson 3 — Build the Operations & Service Improvement Plan

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- convert diagnostic findings into an operations and service plan;
- define practical controls for trading readiness, availability and customer flow;
- allocate actions with owners, deadlines and measures;
- integrate service recovery with operational correction; and
- monitor whether interventions are working.

## Why This Matters
Diagnosis has little value if it does not produce controlled action. Retail improvement plans must be usable during real trading. They should tell the team what will change, who is responsible, when it must happen, what evidence proves completion and how results will be reviewed.

### Key Terms
**Corrective action:** Action taken to address an identified failure or cause.
**Preventive control:** Control designed to reduce the chance of a problem occurring.
**Service recovery:** Action to resolve a service failure and restore customer confidence where possible.
**Owner:** Person accountable for completing an assigned action.
**Measure:** Evidence used to assess whether an action achieved its intended result.

## Main Lesson Content

### 1. Convert priorities into actions
For each priority, define:
**Problem → cause/hypothesis → action → owner → deadline → measure → follow-up.**

Example: peak queues are high because till coverage is below demand. Action might include restoring an unavailable till, revising deployment, introducing active queue monitoring and reviewing hourly waiting times.

### 2. Trading readiness
Your plan should consider:
- opening controls completed before customers enter;
- POS/till readiness;
- safe and clean customer areas;
- promotional and ticket accuracy;
- high-priority product availability;
- service-point coverage; and
- closing/handover controls.

A short daily readiness checklist can create stronger discipline than a long report nobody uses.

### 3. Customer flow and capacity
Use demand patterns to deploy resources. During peak periods, management may need to move capable employees between tasks while protecting essential controls. Breaks, replenishment, digital fulfilment and service points should be planned around predictable demand where operationally possible and within approved employment arrangements.

### 4. Service standards
Translate broad promises into observable behaviours. For example:
- acknowledge waiting customers;
- verify customer needs before recommending a solution;
- explain delays accurately;
- own complaints until properly handed over;
- follow approved return/refund processes;
- escalate when authority is insufficient.

### 5. Service recovery
When failure occurs:
1. listen and establish facts;
2. acknowledge the customer's concern professionally;
3. explain what can be done within policy/authority;
4. resolve or escalate promptly;
5. record recurring causes where required;
6. correct the operational cause where possible.

The goal is not only to settle one complaint. Managers should learn from patterns.

### 6. Monitor results
Select measures that correspond to the problem: queue time, ticket accuracy, availability, complaint recurrence, order readiness, opening-check completion or mystery-shop/service observations. Avoid collecting measures with no management purpose.

### Worked Example
Problem: promotional products are advertised but unavailable on shelf even when backroom stock exists.

Plan:
- verify promotion list before opening;
- assign replenishment owner;
- conduct checks at 10:00, 14:00 and before peak period;
- record exceptions and root causes;
- measure on-shelf availability for priority lines;
- review after seven trading days.

This links the customer promise to operational execution.

## Practical Activity — Operations & Service Plan
Build a plan for Ubuntu Retail Hub with at least six actions. Include:
**Priority | Action | Owner | Start/deadline | Required resource | Measure | Review point | Escalation trigger**.

Your actions must cover at least trading readiness, queue/service capacity, promotion/availability, complaints/service recovery and digital-order handover.

2. So employees and managers can recognise and coach the required behaviour.
3. Controlled action to resolve a service failure and restore confidence where possible.
4. Otherwise data is collected without showing whether the intervention worked.
5. It defines when the issue exceeds normal control or authority and needs higher support.

## Key Takeaways
- Turn diagnosis into assigned, measurable action.
- Protect readiness, availability and service capacity.
- Use service failures as operational learning opportunities.
- Make plans simple enough to execute during trading.
- Review outcomes and correct again when results do not improve.
