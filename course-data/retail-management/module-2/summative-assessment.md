# Module 2 Summative Assessment — Store Operations & Execution Excellence

**Bank size:** 52 questions | **Delivery:** 25 questions | **Pass:** 70% | **Maximum attempts:** 3

1. A manager's strongest opening decision is to: A. Verify critical readiness before trading B. Assume yesterday's condition remains C. Focus only on sales target D. Wait for complaints
**Answer: A**
2. The main purpose of store layout management is to: A. Increase paperwork B. support customer navigation, merchandising and safe operations C. Eliminate staff D. Replace stock control
**Answer: B**
3. When system stock exists but the shelf is empty, the first management concern is: A. Recruitment B. Advertising C. availability execution D. Payroll
**Answer: C**
4. A delivery shortage should be handled by: A. Accepting the invoice unchanged B. Guessing the quantity C. Hiding the variance D. documenting and escalating through approved receiving controls
**Answer: D**
5. Opening controls should be risk-based because: A. Critical failures can affect customers, staff and business controls immediately B. Every task has identical impact C. Managers should avoid checklists D. Opening has no relationship to trading
**Answer: A**
6. Poor customer flow can result in: A. Automatic margin growth B. congestion, frustration and lost sales C. Perfect replenishment D. Reduced need for staff
**Answer: B**
7. The most useful response to recurring shelf gaps is to: A. Remove the shelf B. Blame customers C. investigate root causes across stock records, backroom and replenishment D. Ignore low availability
**Answer: C**
8. Pricing execution should align: A. Staff preferences B. Competitor rumours C. Handwritten estimates D. approved system price, tickets/signage and transaction outcome
**Answer: D**
9. A strong manager assigns opening tasks but also: A. verifies critical outcomes B. Delegates accountability completely C. Avoids the shop floor D. Waits until week-end
**Answer: A**
10. A planogram supports: A. Payroll control B. consistent approved product placement C. Employee discipline D. Banking
**Answer: B**
11. Backroom stock should be organised to support: A. Maximum hidden inventory B. Random storage C. identification, access, safe handling and replenishment D. Customer checkout
**Answer: C**
12. Promotional signs that no longer match the active offer should be: A. Left until stocktake B. Covered by boxes C. Ignored if customers are quiet D. corrected promptly under approved procedures
**Answer: D**
13. A store walk should convert observations into: A. priorities and accountable actions B. Informal gossip C. Marketing slogans D. Supplier contracts
**Answer: A**
14. FIFO means: A. Final item, final order B. first in, first out C. Fast inventory, fast ordering D. Fixed item, fixed output
**Answer: B**
15. When checkout demand rises sharply, the manager should use: A. Random staffing B. No intervention C. an approved queue/capacity response based on actual conditions D. Fewer available tills automatically
**Answer: C**
16. An immediate hazard in a customer aisle requires: A. A note for next month B. Sales analysis first C. No action if nobody has fallen D. immediate control of exposure plus approved corrective action
**Answer: D**
17. Closing controls should confirm: A. critical cash, stock, security, premises and handover requirements B. Only total sales C. Only staff departure D. Only cleaning
**Answer: A**
18. Stock may be unavailable to customers despite positive inventory because it is: A. Always stolen B. misplaced, in the backroom or not replenished C. Automatically sold D. Always damaged
**Answer: B**
19. Receiving accuracy protects: A. Advertising quality B. Staff leave C. inventory records and supplier/store accountability D. Customer loyalty points only
**Answer: C**
20. A promotional display with correct signage but no stock represents: A. Perfect execution B. A staffing policy C. A financial statement D. an availability/execution failure
**Answer: D**
21. Clean, unobstructed trading areas support: A. safety and customer experience B. Shrinkage C. Payroll D. Supplier payment
**Answer: A**
22. During peak trading, redeployment should consider: A. Job titles only B. customer demand, staff capability and operational risk C. Staff friendship groups D. Who arrived first
**Answer: B**
23. An exception log is valuable because it: A. Replaces management B. Guarantees no failures C. records significant deviations, ownership and follow-up D. Eliminates escalation
**Answer: C**
24. After corrective action, the manager should: A. Assume success B. Delete records C. Avoid the area D. verify whether the required standard has been restored
**Answer: D**
25. Replenishment during trading should be managed to protect: A. availability while maintaining safe customer access B. Only backroom appearance C. Only supplier convenience D. Only staff productivity
**Answer: A**
26. Effective merchandising helps translate assortment into: A. Payroll B. a clear, shoppable customer offer C. Security footage D. Employment contracts
**Answer: B**
27. Signing a delivery without checking creates risk because: A. It speeds up receiving B. Suppliers dislike checks C. discrepancies may enter inventory and financial records D. Customers see the signature
**Answer: C**
28. A good closing handover identifies: A. Every employee's personal plans B. Only sales C. Only cleaning D. unresolved issues, risk, ownership and next actions
**Answer: D**
29. Operational readiness is best viewed as: A. a combined state of people, stock, systems, premises and controls B. A sales figure C. A marketing campaign D. A supplier meeting
**Answer: A**
30. Persistent floor-stock gaps should trigger: A. More advertising B. root-cause analysis C. Less reporting D. Removal of stock records
**Answer: B**
31. If an employee reports damaged electrical equipment, the manager should: A. Tell customers to avoid it casually B. Continue use C. apply approved safety isolation/escalation procedures D. Repair it personally without authority
**Answer: C**
32. Daily operations review should compare: A. Competitors only B. Employee personalities C. Weather only D. actual performance against plan and standards
**Answer: D**
33. Which is the strongest receiving control? A. Independent verification appropriate to the risk and procedure B. Accepting sealed cartons without process C. Counting later from memory D. Ignoring damaged packaging
**Answer: A**
34. A high-traffic aisle should generally be managed to: A. Hold excess cartons B. remain navigable and support the intended customer journey C. Store damaged goods D. Replace the backroom
**Answer: B**
35. Product availability is commercially important because unavailable products can: A. Increase conversion B. Improve service C. cause missed sales and customer frustration D. Reduce the need for replenishment
**Answer: C**
36. If a ticket price and approved system price appear inconsistent, staff should: A. Choose the higher amount automatically B. Ask the customer to decide C. Ignore the discrepancy D. follow approved price-verification and correction/escalation procedures
**Answer: D**
37. An opening brief should communicate: A. priorities, risks, responsibilities and relevant targets B. Personal gossip C. Only yesterday's sales D. Supplier bank details
**Answer: A**
38. The backroom should not become: A. A controlled storage area B. an unmanaged holding area that hides availability problems C. Part of stock flow D. A replenishment source
**Answer: B**
39. A store manager sees promotion stock selling faster than expected. Best response: A. Stop sales B. Remove signage immediately C. review remaining stock, replenishment options and customer communication D. Ignore it
**Answer: C**
40. Housekeeping checks should be: A. Annual only B. Optional during busy periods C. Performed only after incidents D. integrated into routine operational control
**Answer: D**
41. A queue KPI alone is insufficient because a manager should also: A. observe causes, capacity and customer conditions B. Ignore customers C. Remove tills D. Stop measuring
**Answer: A**
42. When demand changes unexpectedly, a static plan should: A. Never change B. be adjusted using evidence and approved management judgement C. Be discarded without replacement D. Be hidden from staff
**Answer: B**
43. Root-cause analysis asks: A. Who can be blamed fastest B. Which report looks best C. why the gap occurred and what system/process factors contributed D. How to avoid recording it
**Answer: C**
44. A corrective action without an owner or deadline is: A. Always effective B. A complete control C. Better than verification D. less likely to be executed and followed through
**Answer: D**
45. The best daily operating rhythm includes: A. planning, execution, monitoring, correction and review B. Opening only C. Closing only D. Sales reporting only
**Answer: A**
46. Merchandise placed attractively but blocking required access is: A. Excellent execution B. unacceptable because presentation must coexist with operational/safety requirements C. Automatically profitable D. A supplier issue only
**Answer: B**
47. Receiving damages should be: A. Hidden in backroom B. Sold immediately C. identified and processed through approved damage/discrepancy controls D. Added to available stock without review
**Answer: C**
48. If promotional demand exceeds forecast, the manager should not: A. Monitor availability B. Communicate appropriately C. Seek approved replenishment D. invent unapproved customer promises
**Answer: D**
49. A closing review improves the next trading period by: A. carrying forward learning, risks and unfinished actions B. Eliminating opening checks C. Replacing supervisors D. Avoiding documentation
**Answer: A**
50. The relationship between merchandising and stock control is that: A. They are unrelated B. presentation depends on accurate availability and stock flow C. Merchandising replaces inventory D. Stock never affects customers
**Answer: B**
51. When an operational issue exceeds the manager's authority, the manager should: A. Conceal it B. Invent a policy C. contain immediate risk where appropriate and escalate through the approved route D. Wait indefinitely
**Answer: C**
52. Store execution excellence is best demonstrated by: A. A single strong sales day B. Busy employees C. Attractive posters only D. consistent achievement of customer, operational, safety and commercial standards
**Answer: D**
