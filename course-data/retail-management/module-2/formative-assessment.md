# Module 2 Formative Assessment — Store Operations & Execution Excellence

**Bank size:** 32 questions | **Delivery:** 15 questions | **Pass:** 70% | **Maximum attempts:** 3

1. What is the strongest purpose of an opening control? A. Confirm the store is ready for safe, controlled trading B. Replace all manager judgement C. Count only promotional stock D. Delay customer entry unnecessarily
**Answer: A**
2. Customer flow refers primarily to: A. Supplier payment timing B. How customers move through and use the store C. Staff payroll processing D. Bank deposits
**Answer: B**
3. A product shown on the system but unavailable to customers is mainly a failure of: A. Advertising design B. Staff uniforms C. On-shelf availability/execution D. Recruitment
**Answer: C**
4. A receiving discrepancy should normally be: A. Ignored if delivery is busy B. Corrected by guessing C. Hidden until stocktake D. Recorded and handled through the approved discrepancy process
**Answer: D**
5. The best opening checklist is one that: A. Focuses on critical readiness controls and assigned ownership B. Contains every possible task regardless of risk C. Is completed after closing D. Is never updated
**Answer: A**
6. A fixture that blocks an important aisle creates primarily: A. Better merchandising B. Customer-flow and safety problems C. Higher margin D. Better stock accuracy
**Answer: B**
7. Replenishment should prioritise: A. Products easiest to carry B. Items with attractive packaging only C. Customer demand, availability gaps and business priorities D. Products selected randomly
**Answer: C**
8. Price-ticket accuracy is important because it supports: A. Staff leave planning B. Supplier recruitment C. Store music D. customer trust and transaction accuracy
**Answer: D**
9. Before opening, a manager should verify that critical systems and areas are: A. Operational and ready B. Decorated differently each day C. Unstaffed D. Locked throughout trading
**Answer: A**
10. A planogram or approved merchandising guide mainly helps a team: A. Calculate payroll B. Execute agreed product presentation consistently C. Replace inventory records D. Approve refunds
**Answer: B**
11. Backroom congestion can damage store performance because it can: A. Increase customer loyalty automatically B. Improve picking speed C. Obstruct access, replenishment and safe handling D. Eliminate shrinkage
**Answer: C**
12. When a promotion ends, the manager should ensure: A. Old signage remains indefinitely B. All promoted stock is destroyed C. Prices are guessed D. Signage, ticketing and system execution are correctly transitioned
**Answer: D**
13. A store walk is most useful when it: A. Produces observations, ownership and follow-up B. Is only a social activity C. Avoids customer areas D. Replaces all reports
**Answer: A**
14. FIFO is especially useful where stock: A. Never changes B. Has age, freshness or date considerations C. Is only digital D. Has no storage requirements
**Answer: B**
15. A queue problem should first be managed by: A. Blaming customers B. Closing tills C. Assessing demand and deploying approved capacity D. Ignoring it until closing
**Answer: C**
16. The safest response to a serious spill in a customer area is to: A. Leave it for cleaners tomorrow B. Put stock over it C. Continue trading around it without control D. Protect the area and initiate approved cleaning/escalation controls
**Answer: D**
17. Effective closing controls help protect: A. Cash, stock, premises and records B. Only advertising materials C. Only staff uniforms D. Competitor information
**Answer: A**
18. A shelf gap does not always mean the store owns no stock because: A. All system records are false B. Stock may be in the backroom or incorrectly located C. Customers cannot buy shelf stock D. Suppliers own all inventory
**Answer: B**
19. A delivery should be checked against: A. Social media comments B. Staff schedules only C. Approved delivery/order documentation and physical goods D. Competitor prices
**Answer: C**
20. Promotional execution should be checked for: A. Employee birthdays B. Store paint colour only C. Supplier uniforms D. availability, price, signage and placement
**Answer: D**
21. Good housekeeping contributes to: A. Safety, customer experience and operational efficiency B. Lower stock accuracy C. More queueing D. Fewer controls
**Answer: A**
22. During unexpected peak traffic, the manager should: A. Keep the original allocation regardless of conditions B. Reassess priorities and redeploy trained staff within approved rules C. Stop monitoring the floor D. Send all staff to the backroom
**Answer: B**
23. An operational exception is: A. Every normal task B. A staff member arriving on time C. A condition that differs materially from expected standard or plan D. A completed checklist
**Answer: C**
24. Corrective action is complete when: A. Someone is blamed B. An email is sent C. The problem is mentioned at lunch D. action is implemented and effectiveness is appropriately checked
**Answer: D**
25. The best replenishment approach during trading is to: A. Balance availability with customer flow and safe execution B. Block aisles with cartons C. Replenish only after customers complain D. Ignore high-selling lines
**Answer: A**
26. A merchandising standard should help customers: A. Avoid products B. Find, understand and select products more easily C. Bypass payment D. Change system prices
**Answer: B**
27. If physical received quantity differs from documentation, staff should: A. Change the physical quantity B. Sign without checking C. Record the discrepancy according to procedure D. Discard the paperwork
**Answer: C**
28. A manager reviewing closing results should ask: A. Who worked the longest only B. Which competitor closed first C. Whether tomorrow is a public holiday only D. What exceptions remain and who owns follow-up
**Answer: D**
29. Operational readiness includes: A. People, premises, stock, systems and safety controls B. Sales only C. Marketing only D. Supplier contracts only
**Answer: A**
30. A high-selling product repeatedly missing from the floor requires: A. Less monitoring B. investigation of replenishment and stock-flow causes C. hiding the sales report D. removing its shelf label
**Answer: B**
31. A manager should treat an unresolved safety hazard as: A. A merchandising opportunity B. A minor sales variance C. A priority requiring control and escalation D. A customer preference
**Answer: C**
32. The purpose of a daily operations review is to: A. Repeat the opening brief word-for-word B. Avoid accountability C. Focus only on sales D. compare results with standards, identify gaps and set corrective actions
**Answer: D**
