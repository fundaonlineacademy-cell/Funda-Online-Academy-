# Professional Certificate in Retail Management & Team Leadership
## Module 2: Store Operations & Execution Excellence
### Lesson 5: Pricing, Promotions & Ticketing Accuracy

**Estimated study time:** 50–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain why pricing execution is a management control.
2. Identify common promotional and ticketing failures.
3. Describe a controlled promotion-change process.
4. Respond appropriately to price discrepancies.
5. Verify promotional readiness before trading peaks.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Customers make buying decisions using the information presented by the retailer. When shelf tickets, promotional signs, displays and point-of-sale information do not align, the store creates confusion and disputes and may damage customer trust.

Managers may not determine every price, but they are responsible for disciplined store-level execution within company systems, policies and applicable requirements.

## Key Terms
- **Ticketing** — price/product information displayed to customers.
- **Promotion** — time-bound or conditional offer intended to influence sales.
- **Price discrepancy** — inconsistency between displayed, communicated or system price information.
- **Promotional compliance** — correct execution of approved promotional instructions.
- **Effective date** — date/time from which an approved price or promotion applies.
- **Exception report** — information identifying transactions or conditions requiring review.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Pricing accuracy affects trust and control
A price is not only a number in the POS system. It may also appear on shelves, products, promotional boards, digital channels or other customer communications. Store execution should keep relevant information aligned.

### 2. Common causes of discrepancies
Problems can arise from late ticket changes, old promotional material, products placed under the wrong ticket, incomplete system updates, misunderstood offer conditions or rushed execution.

### 3. Plan promotion changes
A manager should understand what changes, when it becomes effective, what materials are required, who owns each area and how completion will be checked. Major promotion launches may require preparation before opening or controlled changes at a specified time.

### 4. Check the whole customer journey
Do not inspect only the promotional display. Consider whether the correct product is present, stock is available, signs are clear, offer conditions are understandable and the POS processes the approved offer correctly.

### 5. Handle discrepancies through approved procedures
When a customer raises a price concern, staff should not invent solutions or argue. Follow retailer policy and applicable consumer requirements, escalate where authority is exceeded, correct the underlying display/system issue and check whether other customers or products are affected.

### 6. Promotions can create operational pressure
A successful promotion may increase demand, queues and replenishment frequency. Promotional planning therefore connects marketing execution with staffing, stock and service capacity.

### 7. Prevent expired material from remaining
At promotion end, obsolete tickets and signage should be removed or replaced according to the approved schedule. Yesterday's offer can become today's dispute.

### 8. Verify using samples and exceptions
Managers can sample high-risk promotional lines, scan selected products where appropriate, inspect ticket/product alignment and review exception or complaint information to detect problems early.

## Worked Example
A weekend promotion begins Friday morning. The POS is updated, but one aisle still carries the previous week's promotional shelf strips. Customers begin questioning prices at checkout.

The manager follows the approved discrepancy procedure for affected customers, immediately assigns removal/correction of obsolete material, checks the rest of the store for the same failure and investigates why the promotion-change checklist was signed off incorrectly.

## Practical Activity
Design a promotion-readiness checklist containing at least ten checks across Stock, Display, Ticketing, POS, Staff Briefing and Verification.

Then identify the three checks you would classify as highest risk and explain why.

3. What should a promotion-change plan specify?
4. Why should managers check POS execution as well as signage?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Store-level execution determines what customers see and experience.
2. Examples: old tickets, misplaced products, incomplete updates or misunderstood conditions.
3. What changes, timing, ownership, materials and verification.
4. The customer must receive consistent information through the transaction.
5. Follow approved procedure and escalate beyond authority.
6. Higher demand can increase queues, service workload and replenishment.
7. It can mislead/confuse customers and create disputes.
8. Correct it and check whether the issue is wider or systemic.

## Key Takeaways
- Accurate pricing requires alignment between product, communication and transaction systems.
- Promotion execution should be planned, owned and verified.
- Discrepancies require controlled customer handling and root-cause correction.
- Promotions affect stock and service capacity as well as displays.
- Managers should sample high-risk areas instead of assuming sign-off guarantees accuracy.
