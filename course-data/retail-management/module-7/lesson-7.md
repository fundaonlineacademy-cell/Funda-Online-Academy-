# Module 7 — POS, Digital Retail & Management Information
## Lesson 7: Technology Incidents, Continuity & Escalation

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- identify common technology incidents in retail;
- distinguish immediate containment from permanent resolution;
- apply approved business-continuity procedures;
- communicate appropriately during service disruption; and
- document and review incidents for improvement.

## Why This Matters
Retail operations depend on technology, but systems can fail. POS terminals may go offline, payment services may be disrupted, scanners may stop working, connectivity may fail or digital-order systems may become unavailable.

A manager's job is not necessarily to repair the technology. It is to protect people, transactions, information and customer service while the authorised technical response takes place.

### Key Terms
**Incident:** An event that disrupts or threatens normal technology-supported operations.  
**Business continuity:** Approved arrangements that help essential operations continue during disruption.  
**Containment:** Immediate action to limit impact.  
**Escalation:** Referring an issue to the authorised level or specialist.  
**Recovery:** Returning operations to a controlled normal state.

## 1. Common Retail Technology Incidents
Examples include:
- POS outage;
- payment terminal failure;
- network disruption;
- printer or scanner failure;
- digital-order system outage;
- suspected cyber or account compromise;
- power-related disruption; and
- inaccurate system synchronisation.

Different incidents require different approved responses.

## 2. First Management Priorities
The manager should establish:
1. What is affected?
2. Is there a safety, security or information risk?
3. Which transactions or services can continue under approved procedures?
4. Who must be notified?
5. What customer communication is appropriate?
6. What evidence must be preserved?

## 3. Avoid Improvised Workarounds
Pressure can lead staff to bypass controls. Examples include writing down sensitive payment information, sharing passwords, using personal devices without authorisation or processing unapproved manual transactions.

Continuity procedures should be defined by the organisation. If no authorised alternative exists, managers should escalate rather than invent a risky one.

## 4. Communication
During an outage, teams need clear instructions. Customers need accurate information without unsupported promises.

A useful message explains:
- what service is currently affected;
- what approved alternatives are available; and
- where the customer can obtain further assistance.

Avoid guessing recovery times unless they are confirmed.

## 5. Recovery Is Not the End
After systems return, managers may need to:
- reconcile transactions;
- verify stock movements;
- complete delayed records;
- investigate failed orders;
- check exception logs; and
- confirm that temporary procedures are closed.

## 6. Incident Review
A short review should ask:
- What happened?
- What was the impact?
- What worked?
- What failed?
- Were controls bypassed?
- What should change before the next incident?

## Worked Example
The store's primary POS connection fails during a busy Saturday. Staff suggest recording customer card details manually for later processing. The manager refuses because this is not an approved continuity method. The manager follows the authorised outage procedure, communicates service limitations and escalates the incident.

This protects customers and the business even though some sales may be delayed.

## Practical Activity — Outage Drill
Create a response plan for a 45-minute POS outage. Include:
- first five actions;
- staff communication;
- customer communication;
- approved continuity checks;
- escalation contacts by role; and
- recovery reconciliation.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should managers avoid unapproved workarounds?  
2. What is containment?  
3. Why is reconciliation needed after recovery?  
4. What should an incident review achieve?

### Feedback
Unapproved workarounds can create security and financial risk. Containment limits immediate impact. Recovery may leave incomplete or inconsistent records requiring reconciliation. Reviews improve future resilience.

## Key Takeaways
- Technology incidents require controlled operational leadership.
- Protect safety, information and transaction integrity first.
- Follow approved continuity procedures.
- Communicate facts rather than guesses.
- Reconcile and learn after recovery.
