# Module 7 — POS, Digital Retail & Management Information
## Lesson 5: Digital Retail, E-commerce & Omnichannel Operations

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- explain the operational relationship between stores and digital channels;
- identify common omnichannel fulfilment processes;
- manage inventory and handoff risks;
- recognise customer-experience failures across channels; and
- coordinate store responsibilities in digital retail.

## Why This Matters
Customers increasingly experience a retailer as one business rather than separate physical and digital channels. A customer may research online, buy through an app, collect in store and return through another channel. Weak coordination creates broken promises.

### Key Terms
**E-commerce:** Buying and selling through digital channels.  
**Omnichannel:** Coordinated customer and operational experience across channels.  
**Click-and-collect:** Online order collected at a physical location.  
**Fulfilment:** Preparing and completing an order for the customer.  
**Handoff:** Transfer of responsibility between people, systems or channels.

## 1. The Store's Digital Role
Depending on the retailer, stores may:
- fulfil online orders;
- prepare click-and-collect orders;
- support returns;
- answer digital-order queries;
- maintain stock accuracy that feeds online availability; or
- act as pickup or dispatch locations.

This means digital retail can create store workload even when the original sale occurred online.

## 2. Inventory Accuracy Becomes More Important
If an online channel promises an item based on inaccurate store inventory, the customer may travel to collect an order that cannot be fulfilled.

Stock accuracy therefore becomes a customer promise, not merely a backroom measure.

## 3. Fulfilment Control
A controlled fulfilment process may include:
1. receive the order;
2. locate the correct item;
3. verify product and quantity;
4. update status accurately;
5. stage the order securely;
6. verify customer/order details at handoff; and
7. complete the system transaction.

The exact process should follow the retailer's authorised systems and procedures.

## 4. Handoff Risk
Failures often occur between steps rather than within one step. Examples:
- order marked ready before it is physically staged;
- item picked but not system-confirmed;
- customer arrives but staff cannot locate the parcel;
- return accepted physically but not processed correctly.

Managers should therefore define ownership at every handoff.

## 5. Customer Communication
Digital customers need accurate status information. Staff should not promise completion times or outcomes they cannot verify. Where an order is delayed, communication should follow authorised escalation and service-recovery procedures.

## Worked Example
A customer receives a “ready for collection” message. At the store, staff cannot find the parcel. Investigation shows the order was system-confirmed before it reached the collection area.

The improvement is not simply to apologise. Management must correct the handoff rule: an order should only reach the relevant status when the required physical and system steps are complete.

## Practical Activity — Omnichannel Journey Map
Map a click-and-collect order from online purchase to customer collection. Identify:
- system steps;
- physical stock steps;
- responsible roles;
- customer communication points; and
- three possible failure points.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why does store inventory accuracy affect online customers?  
2. What is a handoff risk?  
3. Why should digital order status reflect physical reality?  
4. Give two store responsibilities in omnichannel retail.

### Feedback
Online availability may depend on store stock records. Handoffs transfer responsibility and can create gaps. Status messages are customer promises and should reflect actual progress. Stores may fulfil, stage, collect or return digital orders.

## Key Takeaways
- Physical and digital retail are operationally connected.
- Inventory accuracy supports digital promises.
- Fulfilment requires controlled physical and system steps.
- Clear ownership reduces handoff failures.
- Customer communication must be accurate and authorised.
