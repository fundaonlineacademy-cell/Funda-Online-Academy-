# Module 7 — POS, Digital Retail & Management Information
## Lesson 8: Data-led Store Management Practical

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- integrate POS, sales, stock, service and labour information;
- diagnose a simulated store performance problem;
- prioritise management actions using evidence;
- create a concise data-led action plan; and
- present decisions with appropriate limitations and follow-up measures.

## Why This Matters
Retail managers rarely receive a problem labelled with its correct cause. They receive a mixture of symptoms: sales below target, stock gaps, refunds increasing, queues getting longer or labour cost rising. Management skill is the ability to combine evidence and decide what matters first.

### Key Terms
**Diagnosis:** Evidence-based identification of likely causes.  
**Correlation:** Two measures changing together; this does not automatically prove one caused the other.  
**Priority:** An issue selected for earlier or greater management attention.  
**Action owner:** Person accountable for completing an agreed action.  
**Verification measure:** Evidence used to determine whether action worked.

## Simulation — Ubuntu Retail Store
Ubuntu Retail Store is a fictional medium-sized general merchandise store.

This week:
- sales are 6% below target;
- customer traffic is approximately on plan;
- conversion is below the previous four-week average;
- average transaction value is slightly above target;
- five top-selling lines had repeated shelf gaps;
- system stock for two of those lines was positive during the gaps;
- refunds are normal;
- price overrides increased sharply on one promoted category;
- queue complaints increased during 16:00–18:00;
- labour hours are on budget overall;
- several online collection orders were not ready when customers arrived.

## 1. Separate Symptoms from Causes
Possible symptoms:
- sales below target;
- conversion decline;
- queue complaints;
- failed collection promises.

Possible causes requiring verification:
- poor shelf replenishment;
- incorrect promotional price setup;
- labour scheduled at the wrong times;
- weak digital-order handoff.

Do not confuse a plausible cause with a proven cause.

## 2. Build an Evidence Chain
For each issue, connect evidence:

**Sales gap** → traffic stable → conversion down → top sellers unavailable → investigate availability and selling execution.

**Overrides up** → concentrated in one promoted category → verify promotional price setup.

**Queue complaints** → labour total on budget → check hourly deployment rather than simply adding total labour.

**Collection failures** → inspect pick, stage and ready-status handoffs.

## 3. Prioritise
A practical order might be:
1. correct high-impact availability failures;
2. resolve promotion pricing issue;
3. rebalance peak-hour deployment;
4. correct digital-order handoff;
5. monitor results.

The precise order should reflect customer, financial and control impact.

## 4. Action Plan
A strong action includes:
- issue;
- evidence;
- immediate containment;
- root-cause investigation;
- owner;
- deadline;
- KPI; and
- review date.

Example:
**Issue:** Top-five shelf gaps.  
**Action:** Verify system-to-physical stock and replenish; investigate backroom-to-shelf process.  
**Owner:** Duty manager and stock lead.  
**Measure:** Availability checks at 12:00, 15:00 and 18:00.  
**Review:** Next three trading days.

## 5. Present Management Information Clearly
A manager briefing should distinguish:
- facts;
- interpretation;
- assumptions requiring verification;
- actions; and
- decisions requiring higher authority.

This prevents opinion from being presented as evidence.

## Practical Activity — Full Management Pack
Using the simulation, prepare:
1. a six-KPI dashboard;
2. a one-page diagnosis;
3. four priority actions;
4. an exception/escalation list; and
5. a 100-word manager briefing.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why does stable traffic plus lower conversion matter?  
2. Why might labour be on budget while queues remain poor?  
3. Why should override data be analysed by category?  
4. What is a verification measure?

### Feedback
Stable traffic suggests the sales problem may sit later in the customer journey. Total labour can be adequate but poorly timed. Category concentration can reveal a pricing/setup problem. Verification measures test whether corrective action actually improved the result.

## Key Takeaways
- Combine multiple data sources before deciding.
- Distinguish symptoms, hypotheses and verified causes.
- Prioritise by customer, commercial and control impact.
- Assign actions with owners and measures.
- Present facts separately from assumptions.
