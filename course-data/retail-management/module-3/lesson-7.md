# Professional Certificate in Retail Management & Team Leadership
## Module 3: Customer Experience & Service Leadership
### Lesson 7: Coaching Teams for Consistent Service

**Estimated study time:** 50–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Translate service standards into observable employee behaviours.
2. Coach service performance during real trading conditions.
3. Give specific, balanced and actionable feedback.
4. Distinguish capability gaps from motivation or process problems.
5. Build follow-up into service coaching.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A service standard has little value if employees interpret it differently or use it only when the manager is watching. Retail managers create consistency through clear expectations, observation, coaching, reinforcement and follow-up.

## Key Terms
- **Coaching** — structured support that helps an employee improve performance or capability.
- **Observable behaviour** — action that can be seen or heard rather than a vague personality judgement.
- **Feedback** — information about performance intended to reinforce or improve behaviour.
- **Capability gap** — difference between required skill/knowledge and current ability.
- **Follow-up** — later verification that agreed improvement has occurred.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Convert values into behaviours
“Give excellent service” is too vague. Observable standards might include acknowledging waiting customers, confirming needs before recommending products, explaining delays, keeping service areas ready and closing interactions professionally.

### 2. Observe before coaching
Managers should collect enough evidence to understand what happened. One interaction may not represent normal performance. Observe patterns while intervening immediately where serious risk or misconduct requires it.

### 3. Use specific feedback
Effective feedback explains the situation, observed behaviour, impact and expected next action. “Your service is bad” gives little direction. “During the last two collections, customers waited while you searched without updating them; give an update if the search takes longer than the normal process” is actionable.

### 4. Diagnose the cause
Poor performance may result from lack of knowledge, insufficient practice, unclear standards, broken systems, workload, low motivation or inappropriate conduct. The intervention should match the cause.

### 5. Coach in manageable pieces
Do not overload an employee with ten improvements at once. Prioritise the behaviour with the greatest customer or operational impact, demonstrate where appropriate, allow practice and check understanding.

### 6. Reinforce good performance
Coaching is not only correction. Recognising specific good behaviour shows the team what should be repeated. Recognition should be genuine and linked to the standard.

### 7. Use team learning carefully
A recurring process issue can be discussed in a team huddle without humiliating an individual. Personal performance discussions should normally be handled with appropriate privacy.

### 8. Follow up
Without follow-up, coaching becomes a conversation rather than a management process. Set a reasonable check point and observe whether the agreed behaviour changed.

## Worked Example
A new employee repeatedly gives customers directions to products instead of accompanying them where the store's service standard expects active assistance when practical. The supervisor first confirms the standard was taught, demonstrates the expected behaviour, lets the employee practise, and observes later interactions. Improvement is recognised; continued gaps trigger further support.

## Practical Activity
Choose three service behaviours for a fictional store. For each, write: the expected behaviour, how a manager can observe it, a coaching statement for poor execution and a recognition statement for strong execution.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why is “give good service” an incomplete performance standard?
2. What makes feedback actionable?
3. Name three possible causes of poor performance.
4. Why should coaching focus on observable behaviour?
5. Is coaching only for correcting mistakes?
6. Why should personal performance feedback usually be private?
7. What is a capability gap?
8. Why is follow-up essential?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. It does not define observable expected behaviour.
2. It is specific about what happened, impact and expected next action.
3. Examples: knowledge, practice, unclear standards, system failure, workload, motivation or conduct.
4. It reduces vague personality judgement and clarifies what can change.
5. No; it also reinforces strengths and develops capability.
6. To preserve dignity and enable a focused discussion.
7. A difference between required and current skill/knowledge.
8. It verifies whether improvement actually occurred.

## Key Takeaways
- Service consistency is a management outcome.
- Translate service values into observable behaviours.
- Diagnose before choosing a coaching response.
- Give specific feedback and recognise strong execution.
- Follow up every important coaching commitment.
