# Professional Certificate in Retail Management & Team Leadership
## Module 3: Customer Experience & Service Leadership
### Lesson 3: Customer Communication & Needs Discovery

**Estimated study time:** 45–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Use structured listening and questioning to understand customer needs.
2. Explain products and options without misleading the customer.
3. Adapt communication while maintaining respect and professionalism.
4. Manage uncertainty without guessing.
5. Coach employees on customer communication quality.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Retail employees often try to solve a problem before understanding it. Good communication reduces errors, improves recommendations and prevents avoidable conflict. Managers must create a culture in which employees listen, verify and communicate accurately rather than saying whatever ends the interaction fastest.

## Key Terms
- **Needs discovery** — identifying what the customer is trying to achieve and relevant constraints.
- **Open question** — question encouraging explanation rather than a one-word answer.
- **Closed question** — question used to confirm a specific fact.
- **Active listening** — deliberately listening, checking understanding and responding to what was said.
- **Misrepresentation** — presenting materially inaccurate or misleading information.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start with the customer's purpose
A customer asking for “the cheapest” product may actually need a temporary solution, a specific size or compatibility with equipment they already own. Staff should understand the need before recommending.

### 2. Use a questioning funnel
Begin with open questions: “What will you use it for?” Then narrow with relevant closed questions: size, budget, timing, compatibility or quantity. Do not interrogate customers with irrelevant questions.

### 3. Listen for constraints
Important constraints may include budget, urgency, accessibility, product specifications or intended use. Employees should avoid assumptions based on appearance, accent or other personal characteristics.

### 4. Explain accurately
Product benefits should be based on approved information and reasonable knowledge. If an employee does not know, a professional response is to verify the information or find an authorised colleague rather than invent an answer.

### 5. Use clear language
Avoid unexplained internal jargon. Explain policies and next steps in language the customer can understand. Where communication barriers arise, use available approved assistance respectfully.

### 6. Confirm understanding
For complex requests, summarise: “So you need X for Y, within Z range—is that correct?” This prevents the employee and customer from solving different problems.

### 7. Do not pressure through misinformation
Sales performance matters, but sustainable retail leadership protects trust. Employees should not claim scarcity, guarantees, features or rights that are not supported by approved information.

### 8. Coach through observation
Managers can observe whether employees greet appropriately, ask relevant questions, listen, explain clearly, confirm understanding and close the interaction professionally. Coaching should focus on specific behaviours.

## Worked Example
A customer asks for a laptop “for school.” Instead of immediately recommending a product, the employee asks what software is required, whether the learner needs portability, the approximate budget and whether accessories are already available. When asked about a technical feature the employee cannot confirm, they check the approved product information rather than guessing.

## Practical Activity
Write a short needs-discovery conversation for one of these scenarios: school shoes, a mobile accessory, a household appliance or a grocery product for a specific dietary requirement. Include two open questions, three closed questions, a summary and an accurate next step if information is uncertain.

6. What is active listening?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Understanding the customer's purpose and relevant constraints before proposing a solution.
2. Early in the conversation when explanation is needed.
3. Assumptions can be inaccurate, disrespectful and lead to poor recommendations.
4. Verify using approved information or an authorised colleague.
5. To confirm both parties understand the same requirement.
6. Listening deliberately, checking understanding and responding to the information provided.
7. It damages trust and may create compliance and reputational risk.
8. Examples: questioning, listening, clarity, verification, confirmation and professional closure.

## Key Takeaways
- Understand before recommending.
- Use open questions, then narrow appropriately.
- Accuracy is more important than pretending to know.
- Clear communication reduces errors and conflict.
- Managers should coach observable communication behaviours.
