# Module 6 Summative Assessment Bank — Stock, Shrinkage & Supply Chain Control

**Question bank:** 52 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 25 questions per attempt  
**Pass mark:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3

1. A store receives 48 units while the approved delivery record indicates 50. What is the strongest control response?  
A. Verify, record and resolve the two-unit discrepancy before finalising receiving  
B. Record 50 because that was ordered  
C. Ignore the difference  
D. Add two units to the next delivery  
**Answer: A**

2. Why must receiving combine document checking with physical verification?  
A. Documents are never useful  
B. The business needs evidence that recorded receipt agrees with goods actually received  
C. It eliminates supplier relationships  
D. It guarantees no future variance  
**Answer: B**

3. A shelf is empty but the system says 20 units are available. What should management investigate first?  
A. Advertising spend only  
B. Employee uniforms  
C. Stock location, record accuracy and replenishment execution  
D. Competitor salaries  
**Answer: C**

4. Which control best protects a stock-adjustment process?  
A. Allow everyone to adjust records  
B. Use shared credentials  
C. Delete adjustment history  
D. Require reason, evidence and appropriate authorisation  
**Answer: D**

5. Which statement best explains stock availability?  
A. Customers can obtain the right saleable product at the required place and time  
B. All inventory is stored in the backroom  
C. Every item has maximum stock  
D. Every supplier delivers daily  
**Answer: A**

6. Why use cycle counting between major stocktakes?  
A. To replace all other controls  
B. To test selected inventory regularly and identify variances earlier  
C. To count customers  
D. To calculate selling price  
**Answer: B**

7. A variance persists after a recount. What should happen next?  
A. Hide it  
B. Change the target  
C. Trace transactions, movements, adjustments and process exceptions  
D. Automatically charge an employee  
**Answer: C**

8. Which situation most clearly represents weak segregation of duties?  
A. A receiver counts stock with a colleague  
B. A manager reviews variance reports  
C. A supplier sends an invoice  
D. One employee can receive, adjust and approve the same stock discrepancy without independent review  
**Answer: D**

9. What is the main commercial consequence of chronic out-of-stocks?  
A. Lost sales and weakened customer trust  
B. Guaranteed higher margin  
C. Lower need for controls  
D. Automatic supplier discounts  
**Answer: A**

10. What should a manager do before signing a delivery as correct?  
A. Ask a customer  
B. Complete the required quantity, condition and documentation checks  
C. Wait until month-end  
D. Assume sealed cartons are correct  
**Answer: B**

11. Why is backroom discipline important?  
A. It replaces replenishment  
B. It makes stock invisible  
C. It improves location control, safety, counting and movement efficiency  
D. It eliminates supplier errors  
**Answer: C**

12. Which response to damaged goods creates the greatest control risk?  
A. Isolate and record them  
B. Follow authorised disposal or return procedure  
C. Obtain required approval  
D. Remove them informally without a recorded stock movement  
**Answer: D**

13. What is shrinkage?  
A. Loss reflected by differences between expected inventory and actual accountable stock  
B. A planned promotion  
C. Shelf-space reduction only  
D. A supplier lead time  
**Answer: A**

14. Which statement about shrinkage is most accurate?  
A. It is always caused by customers  
B. It can result from theft, fraud, process errors, damage and weak controls  
C. It is identical to markdown  
D. It cannot be managed  
**Answer: B**

15. Which is the best starting point for shrinkage reduction?  
A. Accuse the team  
B. Stop reporting losses  
C. Analyse evidence to identify where and how losses occur  
D. Increase stock levels indiscriminately  
**Answer: C**

16. A manager suspects internal theft. What should the manager avoid?  
A. Protecting evidence  
B. Following company procedure  
C. Escalating appropriately  
D. Making unsupported public accusations  
**Answer: D**

17. Why are count instructions important?  
A. They create consistent counting methods and reduce avoidable count errors  
B. They guarantee the expected quantity  
C. They eliminate recounts  
D. They replace supervision  
**Answer: A**

18. What is the purpose of a recount?  
A. To force the result to match the system  
B. To verify whether the original physical count was reliable  
C. To alter selling price  
D. To delay reporting  
**Answer: B**

19. A high-value category shows repeated negative variances. Which management response is strongest?  
A. Write off every variance automatically  
B. Stop selling the category  
C. Prioritise targeted investigation and strengthened controls based on evidence  
D. Ignore it until annual stocktake  
**Answer: C**

20. Why should stock movements between locations be recorded?  
A. To increase administrative workload  
B. To avoid replenishment  
C. To increase shrinkage  
D. To preserve accurate location and quantity records  
**Answer: D**

21. What is a likely result of inaccurate inventory records?  
A. Poor ordering and replenishment decisions  
B. Guaranteed sales growth  
C. Perfect customer service  
D. Lower control risk  
**Answer: A**

22. Which is the strongest receiving exception record?  
A. “Delivery bad”  
B. Specific product, expected quantity, actual quantity, condition, date and action taken  
C. A verbal complaint only  
D. No record until month-end  
**Answer: B**

23. Why investigate supplier shortages systematically?  
A. To end all supplier relationships  
B. To hide internal errors  
C. To distinguish genuine supplier exceptions from internal receiving or recording failures  
D. To avoid documentation  
**Answer: C**

24. A product repeatedly arrives damaged from distribution. What is the best long-term action?  
A. Keep writing it off indefinitely  
B. Sell damaged units without disclosure  
C. Stop recording damage  
D. Analyse the pattern, escalate evidence and address the source of damage  
**Answer: D**

25. Which action best supports replenishment effectiveness?  
A. Prioritise shelf gaps and fast-moving lines using reliable stock information  
B. Fill shelves randomly  
C. Keep fast sellers in the backroom  
D. Replenish only once a week regardless of demand  
**Answer: A**

26. What is the key difference between a stock count and variance investigation?  
A. There is no difference  
B. Counting establishes physical quantity; investigation seeks reasons for differences  
C. Investigation establishes selling price  
D. Counting identifies employee guilt  
**Answer: B**

27. Which control would best reduce unauthorised write-offs?  
A. Verbal approval after disposal  
B. No transaction log  
C. Restricted access plus documented authorisation and review  
D. Shared manager passwords  
**Answer: C**

28. Why should high-risk stock receive proportionate attention?  
A. Every product must receive identical control effort  
B. High-risk stock should never be sold  
C. It guarantees zero loss  
D. Control effort should reflect value, loss exposure and identified risk  
**Answer: D**

29. What is the strongest evidence that a corrective stock action worked?  
A. Follow-up results show the targeted variance or failure has reduced sustainably  
B. The action was discussed  
C. A memo was written  
D. The manager intended to improve  
**Answer: A**

30. Which is a useful stock-control trend?  
A. One isolated number with no comparison  
B. Variance or availability performance over comparable periods  
C. Staff birthdays  
D. Store music volume  
**Answer: B**

31. Why should stock exceptions be prioritised?  
A. Every exception has identical impact  
B. Managers should investigate only easy issues  
C. Value, customer impact, recurrence and control risk differ across exceptions  
D. Prioritisation eliminates accountability  
**Answer: C**

32. A supplier invoice reflects items not physically received. What should happen?  
A. Pay automatically  
B. Receive them into stock anyway  
C. Delete the invoice  
D. Raise and track the discrepancy under approved supplier/receiving procedures  
**Answer: D**

33. Why must managers protect stock-count integrity?  
A. Count results influence financial, replenishment and control decisions  
B. Counts are only ceremonial  
C. Stock has no relationship to profit  
D. Only auditors use stock information  
**Answer: A**

34. Which practice creates a stronger audit trail?  
A. Unrecorded verbal decisions  
B. Timestamped, authorised records of movements, adjustments and exceptions  
C. Deleted histories  
D. Shared accounts  
**Answer: B**

35. What does root-cause analysis add to stock management?  
A. It removes the need for action  
B. It guarantees supplier performance  
C. It helps management correct the underlying process rather than repeatedly treating symptoms  
D. It replaces stock counts  
**Answer: C**

36. Which is the weakest response to recurring receiving errors?  
A. Review receiving practice  
B. Coach staff  
C. Strengthen verification  
D. Correct each error but leave the failing process unchanged  
**Answer: D**

37. Why are damages and write-offs commercial issues?  
A. They consume inventory value and may reduce margin and availability  
B. They affect only housekeeping  
C. They increase revenue automatically  
D. They are unrelated to controls  
**Answer: A**

38. What should happen to saleable and non-saleable stock?  
A. Always mix them together  
B. Identify and control them according to status and procedure  
C. Remove all labels  
D. Record only saleable stock  
**Answer: B**

39. A manager sees unusually high refund-related stock adjustments. What is the best action?  
A. Ignore them because refunds are customer service  
B. Disable all refunds  
C. Analyse transaction and stock evidence for process errors or abuse  
D. Delete the adjustment report  
**Answer: C**

40. Why is stock-control coaching important?  
A. Policies are unnecessary  
B. It eliminates all mistakes  
C. Only new staff need controls  
D. Teams must understand both required procedures and why accurate execution matters  
**Answer: D**

41. Which situation requires urgent escalation?  
A. Evidence of a potentially material fraud or control breach  
B. A normal planned cycle count  
C. Routine shelf replenishment  
D. A correctly completed delivery  
**Answer: A**

42. What is the best way to manage stock exceptions across multiple days?  
A. Rely on memory  
B. Maintain an exception log with ownership, status and follow-up  
C. Discuss them informally only  
D. Close every case immediately without evidence  
**Answer: B**

43. Why compare physical stock with system stock?  
A. To change sales targets  
B. To assess customer satisfaction  
C. To test whether recorded inventory reflects reality  
D. To set employee schedules  
**Answer: C**

44. Which action is most likely to create false stock accuracy?  
A. Independent recount  
B. Investigating movements  
C. Recording valid adjustments  
D. Changing system quantities merely to force agreement without establishing the reason  
**Answer: D**

45. What should a manager include in a stock-control dashboard?  
A. A focused set of measures such as availability, variances, shrinkage and unresolved exceptions  
B. Every possible number regardless of relevance  
C. Only sales revenue  
D. Only supplier names  
**Answer: A**

46. Why connect stock data to customer-service data?  
A. Stock has no customer effect  
B. Availability failures can explain lost sales, substitutions and customer complaints  
C. It eliminates the need for service coaching  
D. It guarantees conversion  
**Answer: B**

47. What is the manager's role in supply-chain exception management?  
A. Personally drive every delivery  
B. Ignore upstream problems  
C. Identify store impact, document evidence, escalate appropriately and track resolution  
D. Change supplier contracts without authority  
**Answer: C**

48. Which improvement plan is most actionable?  
A. “Reduce shrinkage someday”  
B. “Team must improve”  
C. “Watch stock carefully”  
D. “Investigate top five variance lines by Tuesday; stock controller owns recounts; manager reviews causes Wednesday”  
**Answer: D**

49. Why should management distinguish controllable and external causes of stock problems?  
A. Different causes require different actions and escalation  
B. External causes never matter  
C. Internal causes never matter  
D. It avoids accountability  
**Answer: A**

50. What is the strongest stock-management mindset?  
A. Focus only on month-end counts  
B. Treat stock as a continuous flow requiring daily control and periodic verification  
C. Assume the system is always correct  
D. Assume physical counts are always correct  
**Answer: B**

51. A corrective action reduced variance for one week but the problem returned. What should management conclude?  
A. The problem is solved  
B. Stop measuring it  
C. The control may not be sustainable and requires further root-cause review  
D. Delete historical data  
**Answer: C**

52. What is the best final step in a stock-control review?  
A. Count stock and stop  
B. Write off all differences  
C. Assign blame  
D. Confirm actions, owners, measures and follow-up dates so improvement can be verified  
**Answer: D**
