# Module 6 Formative Assessment — Stock, Shrinkage & Supply Chain Control

**Question bank:** 32 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 15 questions per attempt  
**Pass mark:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3

1. What is the main purpose of controlling stock flow?  
A. Ensure stock moves accurately and remains available for customers  
B. Increase paperwork regardless of need  
C. Eliminate all supplier communication  
D. Keep all inventory permanently in the backroom  
**Answer: A**

2. At receiving, what should normally be checked against the delivery?  
A. Only the driver's name  
B. Quantity, condition and relevant delivery documentation  
C. Only shelf price  
D. Yesterday's sales target  
**Answer: B**

3. Why is inventory-record accuracy important?  
A. It guarantees every promotion succeeds  
B. It replaces physical controls  
C. Replenishment and availability decisions depend on reliable stock information  
D. It eliminates the need for investigation  
**Answer: C**

4. What is a cycle count?  
A. A customer traffic count  
B. A weekly sales forecast  
C. A staff attendance check  
D. A focused physical count of selected inventory  
**Answer: D**

5. A delivery contains visibly damaged cartons. What should the manager do first?  
A. Follow receiving procedure, record the exception and verify the affected goods  
B. Put everything directly on sale  
C. Ignore the damage if the supplier is busy  
D. Change the system quantity without evidence  
**Answer: A**

6. What does replenishment primarily aim to achieve?  
A. Maximum backroom stock  
B. Appropriate product availability at the selling location  
C. Fewer customer choices  
D. Higher shrinkage  
**Answer: B**

7. A system shows 12 units but only 8 can be physically located. What is this?  
A. A guaranteed supplier error  
B. A merchandising promotion  
C. A stock variance requiring investigation  
D. A pricing strategy  
**Answer: C**

8. Which is the best response to repeated stock variances?  
A. Automatically blame one employee  
B. Stop counting stock  
C. Delete the product record  
D. Analyse patterns, evidence and process weaknesses before corrective action  
**Answer: D**

9. Which practice best supports receiving accuracy?  
A. Count and inspect goods systematically before completing the receiving process  
B. Sign all deliveries before checking them  
C. Let suppliers change internal records directly  
D. Estimate quantities from carton size  
**Answer: A**

10. Shrinkage is best described as:  
A. A planned reduction in selling space  
B. Inventory loss or unexplained difference between expected and actual stock  
C. A customer-service measure  
D. A normal sales discount  
**Answer: B**

11. Which may contribute to shrinkage?  
A. Only external theft  
B. Only supplier delays  
C. Theft, fraud, damage, errors and weak controls  
D. Only markdowns  
**Answer: C**

12. Why should stock adjustments require evidence and authorisation?  
A. To make every adjustment slower  
B. To hide variances  
C. To avoid stock counts  
D. To protect record integrity and create accountability  
**Answer: D**

13. What should happen when received quantity differs from approved documentation?  
A. Record and resolve the discrepancy through the authorised process  
B. Change the supplier's document secretly  
C. Ignore small differences automatically  
D. Increase shelf prices  
**Answer: A**

14. What is the strongest reason for maintaining an organised backroom?  
A. It looks impressive to visitors  
B. It supports safe handling, stock visibility and efficient replenishment  
C. It removes the need for records  
D. It guarantees no stock loss  
**Answer: B**

15. What should a manager do with recurring out-of-stocks despite apparent system stock?  
A. Order unlimited stock immediately  
B. Blame customers  
C. Investigate record accuracy, location, replenishment and process causes  
D. Remove the item from reports  
**Answer: C**

16. Which is an appropriate loss-prevention principle?  
A. Treat every employee as guilty  
B. Eliminate all customer access  
C. Hide procedures from authorised staff  
D. Use proportionate controls, evidence, segregation and escalation  
**Answer: D**

17. What is a stock count intended to establish?  
A. The physical quantity actually present at a defined point or area  
B. Employee popularity  
C. Customer satisfaction only  
D. Future selling price  
**Answer: A**

18. Why should count teams follow consistent counting rules?  
A. To increase paperwork  
B. To improve reliability and comparability of count results  
C. To make variances disappear  
D. To prevent recounts in all circumstances  
**Answer: B**

19. What is a good first response to a major count variance?  
A. Immediately dismiss a staff member  
B. Write it off without review  
C. Recheck the count and investigate records and movements  
D. Change the target  
**Answer: C**

20. Damaged stock should normally be:  
A. Mixed with saleable stock  
B. Given away informally  
C. Left unrecorded  
D. Identified, controlled and processed under authorised damage procedures  
**Answer: D**

21. What does good supplier exception management require?  
A. Clear evidence, timely communication and tracked resolution  
B. Verbal complaints only  
C. Automatic payment of disputed quantities  
D. Ignoring repeated failures  
**Answer: A**

22. What is the purpose of segregation of duties in stock control?  
A. Reduce customer service  
B. Reduce the risk that one person can initiate, approve and conceal an improper action  
C. Remove accountability  
D. Eliminate management oversight  
**Answer: B**

23. A fast-selling line is repeatedly empty on shelf while stock remains in the backroom. The likely control problem is:  
A. Product design  
B. Supplier invoicing only  
C. Replenishment execution  
D. Customer returns  
**Answer: C**

24. Which action best supports stock traceability?  
A. Unrecorded transfers  
B. Shared passwords  
C. Informal write-offs  
D. Recording authorised movements and adjustments accurately  
**Answer: D**

25. What should a stock-control improvement plan contain?  
A. Specific issues, causes, actions, owners, deadlines and measures  
B. Only general intentions  
C. Only a shrinkage target  
D. No review date  
**Answer: A**

26. Why analyse variance patterns by product, location or process?  
A. To make reports longer  
B. To identify where control failures may be concentrated  
C. To eliminate physical counts  
D. To avoid corrective action  
**Answer: B**

27. Which is the best management approach to suspected stock fraud?  
A. Public accusation  
B. Immediate social-media disclosure  
C. Preserve evidence and follow authorised investigation/escalation procedures  
D. Delete transaction history  
**Answer: C**

28. Why should returns to suppliers be documented?  
A. To increase receiving volume  
B. To avoid communication  
C. To increase selling stock  
D. To maintain an auditable record of stock leaving the business  
**Answer: D**

29. Which measure is most directly linked to customer availability?  
A. Whether required products are present and saleable when customers want them  
B. Number of emails sent  
C. Size of the manager's office  
D. Number of supplier meetings  
**Answer: A**

30. What is the best response when a process weakness repeatedly causes stock errors?  
A. Continue correcting individual records only  
B. Correct the root process as well as the affected records  
C. Stop reporting errors  
D. Increase prices  
**Answer: B**

31. Why are stock controls a management responsibility rather than only a stockroom task?  
A. Managers must personally count every unit  
B. Stock has no financial impact  
C. Availability, cash, margin, service and loss are affected by inventory performance  
D. Suppliers manage all store inventory  
**Answer: C**

32. What completes the stock-control management cycle?  
A. Counting once  
B. Writing a report only  
C. Setting a target only  
D. Reviewing results, verifying actions and adjusting controls where necessary  
**Answer: D**
