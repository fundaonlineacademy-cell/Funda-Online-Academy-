# Carpentry Premium Course — Module 9

## Quality Control, Defects & Corrective Practice

**Module purpose:** Develop disciplined quality-control habits so learners can compare completed work with a brief or specification, detect defects systematically, identify likely causes, choose proportionate corrective action, prevent repeated errors and document handover. Quality is treated as measurable conformance and fitness for purpose rather than personal opinion.

### Lesson 1 — Understanding Specifications & Acceptance Criteria
Learners distinguish requirements, dimensions, tolerances, finish expectations and acceptance criteria from preferences. They learn that inspection begins with knowing what the work is supposed to achieve and that unclear requirements should be clarified before manufacture or handover.

### Lesson 2 — Dimensional Inspection & Tolerance
Learners check critical dimensions using appropriate measuring tools, understand nominal versus actual measurement, compare results with stated tolerances and record findings. No universal carpentry tolerance is invented: acceptable variation comes from the drawing, job specification, product system or competent authority.

### Lesson 3 — Checking Square, Level, Plumb & Alignment
Learners use appropriate checks for geometry and alignment, including diagonals where applicable, and interpret errors without confusing square, level and plumb. The worked example should show how a small early error can create cumulative fitting problems later.

### Lesson 4 — Joint & Fixing Inspection
Learners inspect joint fit, seating, gaps, alignment, splitting, fastener condition, adhesive evidence and workmanship against the intended joint/fixing method. Cosmetic appearance must not be used to hide an unsafe, loose or structurally unsuitable connection.

### Lesson 5 — Surface & Finish Inspection
Learners inspect preparation and finishing for scratches, contamination, uneven colour, runs/sags, missed areas, roughness, edge damage and other visible defects. They distinguish defects that can be corrected locally from conditions requiring wider rework or product-specific advice.

### Lesson 6 — Diagnosing Common Carpentry Defects
Learners use evidence to separate symptom from cause. Scenarios include poor fit, out-of-square assemblies, tear-out, splitting, misaligned components, movement-related problems and finishing defects. Diagnosis should trace backward through material, measurement, setup, sequence, tool condition and technique before corrective work begins.

### Lesson 7 — Corrective Action, Rework & Waste Prevention
Learners decide whether to accept, adjust, repair, remake, replace or escalate work based on specification, safety, cost and quality. They learn to avoid repeated trimming or improvised correction without measurement and to capture lessons that reduce future material and time waste.

### Lesson 8 — Final Inspection, Documentation & Handover
Learners complete a final systematic inspection, clean/protect the work, record defects/corrections, confirm completion against the brief and communicate appropriate use/care information at handover. A practical activity should create a reusable quality-control checklist and defect record suitable for a learner portfolio.

### Entry 9 — Formative Assessment
- 15 MCQs randomly selected from a bank of at least 30.
- Covers Lessons 1–8.
- Pass mark: 70%.
- Include specification interpretation, measurement, geometry, joint/finish inspection, root-cause diagnosis, rework decisions and handover evidence.
- Maximum formal attempts in production policy: 3.
- Feedback explains the inspection/correction reasoning.

### Entry 10 — Summative Assessment
- 25 MCQs randomly selected from a bank of at least 50.
- Pass mark: 70%.
- Scenario-led assessment requiring learners to judge whether work meets stated requirements, identify likely defect causes and select appropriate corrective/escalation actions.
- Questions must be broader and more applied than formative items and must not be simple reworded duplicates.
- Maximum formal attempts in production policy: 3.

## Premium lesson requirements
Every teaching lesson must contain lesson identification, estimated study time, 3–5 measurable outcomes, Why This Lesson Matters, 5–8 meaningful bold-defined terms, at least 500 words substantive main content (normally 700–1,000), worked example/demonstration, practical activity, informal knowledge check, explanatory feedback, key takeaways and progression notes where supported.

## Visual learning plan
Use instructional visuals with meaningful captions: specification-versus-result comparison, measuring/inspection examples, diagonal square check, level/plumb/alignment diagram, acceptable-versus-defective joint examples, finish-defect examples, root-cause decision flow and final inspection checklist. Visuals must support identification and judgement rather than decoration.

## Accuracy and quality controls
- Do not invent universal tolerances, load capacities or acceptance limits. Use the applicable drawing, specification, manufacturer requirements or authorised workplace criteria.
- Quality correction must never compromise safety to save material or time.
- If a defect may affect structural integrity, regulated installation, fire performance or another specialist requirement, learners should stop and escalate to an appropriately competent person.
- Measurement tools themselves must be suitable, undamaged and used correctly; apparent product defects can result from poor inspection technique.
- Corrective action should address root cause where practicable, not merely conceal the visible symptom.
- Assessment questions may only test content taught or reasonably developed in Lessons 1–8.
