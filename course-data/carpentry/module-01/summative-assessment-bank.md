# Carpentry — Module 1 Summative Assessment Bank

## Module
Carpentry Practice, Workshop Safety & Professional Standards

## Assessment Configuration
- Entry: 10 — Summative Assessment
- Questions presented per attempt: 25
- Question bank: 52 MCQs
- Pass mark: 70%
- Maximum attempts: 3
- Prerequisite: Module 1 Formative Assessment passed
- Coverage: Lessons 1–8
- Level: application, judgement and integrated workplace decision-making
- Format: one best answer from A–D

> Assessment integrity: Questions test knowledge and judgement developed in the eight teaching lessons. Safety questions require learners to favour safe systems of work, risk control, competent supervision, manufacturer/workplace instructions, and stopping/escalating where conditions are unsafe or outside competence.

## Lesson 1 — The Carpentry Profession & Workplace Environment

### Q1
A carpenter receives a drawing for a small timber storage frame. What should happen before cutting begins?
A. Cut the longest pieces first and check the drawing later
B. Clarify the job requirements, review dimensions and plan the work sequence
C. Select whichever timber is closest to the saw
D. Assemble the frame and then measure it

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Professional carpentry starts with understanding the brief/specification and planning before material is irreversibly processed.
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — workflow from brief to handover.

### Q2
Which statement best describes professional carpentry?
A. It is mainly the ability to cut timber quickly
B. It is a complete process combining planning, safe work, accurate making, checking and handover
C. It is limited to working with solid hardwood
D. It is only work performed on construction sites

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Carpentry is an end-to-end work process, not a single manual skill.
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — scope of carpentry.

### Q3
A learner is asked to perform a task using equipment they have never been trained to operate. What is the most professional response?
A. Try the equipment slowly without telling anyone
B. Ask another learner to explain it while the machine is running
C. Stop and seek appropriate instruction or competent supervision
D. Use the equipment only if PPE is available

**Correct answer:** C
**Rationale:** Recognising limits of competence and escalating appropriately protects people, equipment and quality.
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — competence and professional boundaries.

### Q4
Which action belongs most clearly to the handover stage of a carpentry job?
A. Confirming that the completed item meets the agreed requirements and communicating relevant information to the recipient
B. Choosing a saw blade before the first cut
C. Identifying hazards before work starts
D. Preparing the initial cut list

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Handover follows completion and final checking and includes professional communication about the finished work.
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — handover.

### Q5
A carpenter finishes an item that looks attractive but does not match the dimensions in the approved drawing. Which conclusion is best?
A. The work is acceptable because appearance is more important than dimensions
B. The work may fail the specification despite looking good
C. The dimensions matter only if the customer complains
D. The drawing becomes irrelevant after assembly

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Quality includes conformance to requirements, not appearance alone.
**Traceability:** Lessons 1 and 8 — specification and quality.

### Q6
Why should a carpenter understand how their task connects with other workers or trades?
A. To take responsibility for every other trade's work
B. To coordinate work, avoid interference and support safe, correct sequencing
C. To eliminate the need for job instructions
D. To decide independently which site rules to ignore

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Carpentry often occurs within a wider workplace where coordination affects safety, timing and quality.
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — workplace interfaces.

## Lesson 2 — Roles, Responsibilities & Professional Conduct

### Q7
A supervisor gives a learner an instruction that the learner believes may create an immediate safety risk. What should the learner do?
A. Continue because supervisors are always responsible for consequences
B. Stop, communicate the concern and seek clarification or escalation through the workplace process
C. Secretly change the job specification
D. Ask a customer to decide

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Professional conduct includes communicating hazards and not proceeding blindly with unsafe work.
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — responsibility and professional communication.

### Q8
Which behaviour best demonstrates accountability?
A. Hiding a measurement error so the job stays on schedule
B. Reporting the error promptly and helping determine an appropriate corrective action
C. Blaming the previous shift before checking the facts
D. Discarding the defective component without recording it

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Accountability means taking responsibility, communicating accurately and supporting correction.
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — accountability.

### Q9
A learner notices that a co-worker has removed a machine guard. Which response is most appropriate?
A. Ignore it because the learner did not remove the guard
B. Use the machine only for small pieces
C. Avoid using the unsafe equipment and report/escalate the condition according to workplace procedure
D. Replace the guard with a piece of scrap timber

**Correct answer:** C
**Rationale:** Professional responsibility includes acting on hazards rather than ignoring them or improvising safety devices.
**Traceability:** Lessons 2 and 5 — responsibilities and tool safety.

### Q10
Which is the best example of professional communication during a carpentry job?
A. Assuming an unclear dimension rather than delaying the work
B. Asking for clarification before proceeding where an important requirement is ambiguous
C. Changing materials without informing anyone
D. Waiting until final handover to mention a known defect

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Clarifying ambiguity early prevents unsafe work, waste and quality failures.
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — communication.

### Q11
A worker is under pressure to finish quickly. Which approach best reflects professional conduct?
A. Skip inspections to recover lost time
B. Maintain required safety and quality controls while communicating realistic constraints
C. Remove PPE if it slows the task
D. Accept defects that are difficult to see

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Schedule pressure does not justify abandoning required safety or quality practices.
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — professional conduct.

### Q12
Why is accurate reporting important after a workplace mistake or incident?
A. It helps establish facts, supports correction and reduces the chance of recurrence
B. It guarantees nobody will be held accountable
C. It removes the need for investigation
D. It is useful only when property is damaged

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Accurate information supports effective corrective and preventive action.
**Traceability:** Lessons 2 and 7 — reporting.

## Lesson 3 — Workshop Hazards & Risk Awareness

### Q13
Which statement correctly distinguishes a hazard from risk?
A. A hazard is the potential source of harm; risk considers the likelihood and consequences of that harm in the circumstances
B. A hazard and risk are exactly the same thing
C. Risk is a machine and a hazard is an injury
D. A hazard exists only after an accident

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Effective risk thinking begins by distinguishing the source of harm from the risk arising from exposure and circumstances.
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — hazard and risk.

### Q14
A cutting area has offcuts on the floor, a power lead across the walkway and poor dust extraction. What is the best response before starting work?
A. Wear safety glasses and continue
B. Address the hazards and required controls before beginning the operation
C. Work faster to reduce exposure time
D. Ask people to step over the lead

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** PPE alone does not correct avoidable housekeeping, access and extraction problems.
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — risk controls.

### Q15
Which control is generally higher in the hierarchy of controls than PPE?
A. Engineering control
B. Personal preference
C. Warning from a co-worker only
D. Working more quickly

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Engineering controls act on the hazard/exposure before reliance on personal protective equipment.
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — hierarchy of controls.

### Q16
A machine begins making an abnormal sound during use. What should the operator do?
A. Increase feed speed until the sound stops
B. Continue until the current piece is finished regardless of risk
C. Stop safely and investigate/report the abnormal condition according to procedure
D. Wear hearing protection and ignore it

**Correct answer:** C
**Rationale:** Changes in equipment behaviour are warning signs requiring safe stopping and appropriate assessment.
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — dynamic risk awareness.

### Q17
Which is the strongest example of dynamic risk awareness?
A. Completing one risk check at the start and ignoring all later changes
B. Continually noticing relevant changes in people, materials, equipment and the environment during the task
C. Assuming a familiar task cannot change
D. Relying only on warning signs

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Risk conditions can change while work is underway.
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — dynamic risk awareness.

### Q18
A learner finds a damaged electrical lead on a portable tool. The correct immediate judgement is to:
A. Wrap the damage with any available tape and continue
B. Remove the tool from normal use and report it for appropriate action
C. Use it only for short jobs
D. Ask another learner to test it while powered

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Damaged equipment should not remain in normal service until appropriately assessed and made safe.
**Traceability:** Lessons 3 and 5 — hazard recognition and defect reporting.

### Q19
Why is asking “what has changed?” useful during a task?
A. It helps identify new or increased risks that were not present at the start
B. It replaces all formal workplace procedures
C. It allows the worker to ignore the original risk assessment
D. It is only relevant after an accident

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Changes can invalidate earlier assumptions and controls.
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — dynamic risk awareness.

## Lesson 4 — Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

### Q20
What is the best description of PPE's role in risk control?
A. PPE always removes the hazard itself
B. PPE is one layer of protection and should not be used to justify leaving reasonably controllable hazards uncontrolled
C. PPE makes machine guards unnecessary
D. PPE is required only after an accident

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** PPE protects the wearer but usually does not eliminate the underlying hazard.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — role and limitations of PPE.

### Q21
A sanding task produces significant dust and the extraction system has stopped working. What is the best response?
A. Put on a disposable mask and continue automatically
B. Stop and address/report the failed control before deciding whether work may safely continue
C. Open a door and ignore workplace procedures
D. Sand faster

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** PPE should not automatically be used to compensate for a failed engineering control.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — PPE and hierarchy of controls.

### Q22
Why must PPE be selected for the specific task and hazard?
A. All PPE provides identical protection
B. Different hazards require different forms and levels of protection and may have different limitations
C. The most expensive PPE is suitable for every task
D. PPE colour determines its protection

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Appropriate selection depends on assessed hazards, workplace requirements and product/manufacturer information.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — PPE selection.

### Q23
Which statement about gloves in carpentry is most accurate?
A. Gloves must always be worn around every rotating machine
B. Gloves are task-specific and may create entanglement risk around some rotating equipment
C. Gloves replace machine guards
D. Any glove is suitable for chemical finishing products

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Hand protection must be selected with the actual task and entanglement risks in mind.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — hand protection.

### Q24
A worker must wear eye, hearing and respiratory protection together. What additional issue should be checked?
A. Whether the items are the same colour
B. Whether the items fit and remain compatible without compromising each other's protective function
C. Whether the worker can remove one item to improve comfort
D. Whether all items came from the same shop

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Multiple PPE items can interfere with fit or seals and must function together.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — PPE compatibility.

### Q25
A tight-fitting respirator does not seal correctly on a worker's face. What should happen?
A. Use it anyway because some protection is always enough
B. Address the fit/selection problem according to the respiratory protection requirements before relying on it
C. Hold it against the face by hand during work
D. Wear two loose masks instead

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Respiratory protection that depends on a face seal must fit as required to provide intended protection.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — respiratory protection and fit.

### Q26
What should a worker do when PPE is damaged or has reached a condition where it may no longer provide intended protection?
A. Continue until the end of the month
B. Report it and arrange appropriate replacement or action before relying on it
C. Lend it to another worker
D. Hide the damage with paint

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** PPE must be maintained in serviceable condition.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — inspection and serviceability.

## Lesson 5 — Safe Tool Handling & Pre-use Checks

### Q27
What is the main purpose of a pre-use tool check?
A. To make the tool look clean for inspection
B. To identify conditions that could make the tool unsafe or unsuitable before operation
C. To replace manufacturer instructions
D. To guarantee that no accident can occur

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Pre-use checks help identify defects, missing safeguards and unsuitable setup before exposure begins.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — pre-use checks.

### Q28
A circular saw guard does not return correctly after a check. What should the operator do?
A. Hold it open manually while cutting
B. Remove the saw from use and report the defect for appropriate action
C. Use the saw only on thin timber
D. Ask a co-worker to watch the guard

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** A defective safety device is not something an operator should bypass to continue production.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — guards and defect reporting.

### Q29
Why is secure workholding important?
A. It helps control the workpiece and reduces unexpected movement during an operation
B. It allows hands to be placed closer to cutting edges
C. It makes pre-use checks unnecessary
D. It is needed only for decorative work

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Appropriate clamping, vices or other workholding methods improve control and safety.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — workholding.

### Q30
Before fitting or changing an accessory on a power tool, the worker should first:
A. Ensure the tool cannot start unexpectedly and follow the manufacturer's/workplace isolation procedure
B. Increase the tool speed
C. Hold the trigger down
D. Ask someone to watch the switch

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Preventing unexpected energisation is fundamental when changing accessories or performing relevant adjustments.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — isolation and accessories.

### Q31
A drill bit is visibly damaged. What is the best decision?
A. Use lower speed and continue
B. Do not use the damaged accessory; select a suitable serviceable replacement
C. Sharpen it while fitted to a running drill
D. Use extra force

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Damaged accessories can fail, reduce control and damage the work.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — accessory inspection.

### Q32
Why should the operator check that the selected tool and accessory are suitable for the intended material and operation?
A. Suitability affects control, performance, quality and safety
B. Any cutting accessory works on any material
C. Suitability matters only to experienced carpenters
D. It is relevant only when the tool is new

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Correct selection is part of safe and professional tool use.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — tool selection and checks.

### Q33
Which action is least acceptable when a safety-related tool defect is discovered?
A. Tagging or identifying the tool according to workplace procedure
B. Reporting the defect
C. Preventing normal use until appropriate action is taken
D. Quietly returning the defective tool to storage for the next person

**Correct answer:** D
**Rationale:** Returning defective equipment without warning transfers the hazard to another person.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — defect reporting.

## Lesson 6 — Workshop Housekeeping & Material Storage

### Q34
Why should walkways and access routes be kept clear?
A. Mainly to improve the workshop's appearance
B. To reduce trip/collision risks and preserve safe movement and emergency access
C. To create more space for offcuts
D. Only because customers may visit

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Clear circulation areas support normal work and emergency movement.
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — access and housekeeping.

### Q35
Long timber is leaning unsecured where it can slide or fall into a walkway. What is the best action?
A. Leave it because timber is not machinery
B. Move/store it using an appropriate stable method that prevents uncontrolled movement and obstruction
C. Place a small offcut in front of it
D. Ask workers to walk around it

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Materials must be stored to remain stable and not create avoidable hazards.
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — timber storage.

### Q36
Why should useful stock, reusable offcuts and waste be separated?
A. It supports material control, reduces clutter and waste, and makes disposal/storage decisions clearer
B. It guarantees every offcut will be reused
C. It removes the need for cleaning
D. It allows waste to be stored in walkways

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Organised material management improves safety, efficiency and waste reduction.
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — material and waste organisation.

### Q37
A pile of sawdust is accumulating near an active work area. Which response is best?
A. Wait until the end of the week
B. Manage the accumulation using the workplace's appropriate dust/housekeeping controls
C. Blow it into a walkway
D. Cover it with a board

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Dust accumulation can contribute to slip, respiratory, visibility and fire concerns depending on circumstances.
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — dust and housekeeping.

### Q38
Why should tools be returned to designated storage after use?
A. It supports inspection, accessibility, damage prevention and an orderly workspace
B. It prevents anyone else from using them
C. It eliminates the need for maintenance
D. It is required only for hand tools

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Controlled storage contributes to both efficiency and safety.
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — tool organisation.

### Q39
A container of finishing product has lost its label. What is the safest professional response?
A. Smell it to identify the contents
B. Do not guess; isolate/report it and follow workplace chemical-identification procedures
C. Pour it into a smaller unlabelled container
D. Use a small amount to test it on scrap

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Unknown substances should not be identified through unsafe experimentation or used without reliable information.
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — hazardous-product storage awareness.

## Lesson 7 — Emergency, Fire & Incident Procedures

### Q40
When an emergency alarm or evacuation instruction is given, what is normally the priority?
A. Finish the current workpiece first
B. Follow the site's emergency procedure and move to the designated safe/assembly area as instructed
C. Collect all personal tools
D. Return to find the cause of the alarm

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Emergency procedures depend on prompt, orderly action rather than protecting production or possessions.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — evacuation.

### Q41
When should a worker attempt to fight a workplace fire?
A. Whenever an extinguisher is visible
B. Only where the workplace emergency procedure, training, conditions and safe escape make such action appropriate
C. Whenever the fire is near timber
D. Before raising the alarm

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Firefighting is not automatically appropriate; personal safety, training and emergency procedures govern the response.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — fire response.

### Q42
Why is incident reporting important even when the immediate injury appears minor?
A. It supports required response, fact gathering, corrective action and prevention of recurrence
B. It automatically proves who caused the incident
C. It replaces medical assessment
D. It is useful only for insurance claims

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Reporting enables the organisation to respond appropriately and learn from events.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — incident reporting.

### Q43
What is a near miss?
A. An event that caused major injury
B. An event in which harm did not occur but circumstances had the potential to cause harm
C. A planned emergency drill
D. A quality defect with no safety relevance under any circumstances

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Near misses are valuable warning events because they reveal hazards before serious harm occurs.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — near misses.

### Q44
After a serious incident, why might the scene need to be preserved except where action is necessary to rescue people or prevent further danger?
A. To support appropriate investigation and reliable fact finding
B. To stop employees from returning to work permanently
C. To hide damaged equipment
D. To avoid reporting the incident

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Unnecessary disturbance can remove evidence needed to understand what happened; emergency safety needs remain the priority.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — incident scene and investigation.

### Q45
A worker sees smoke from an electrical tool. What is the best immediate principle?
A. Continue using it until flames appear
B. Stop work, raise the alarm/communicate the emergency as required and follow the workplace emergency/isolation procedure without taking unsafe action
C. Pour water on the tool automatically
D. Carry the powered tool through the workshop

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Electrical/fire situations require controlled emergency action according to training and procedure; improvisation can increase danger.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — emergency response.

### Q46
What is the main purpose of corrective action after an incident investigation?
A. To assign blame as quickly as possible
B. To address identified causes or contributing conditions and reduce recurrence
C. To remove all records of the incident
D. To guarantee that no future incident can ever occur

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Effective corrective action focuses on reducing risk by addressing what contributed to the event.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — investigation and corrective action.

## Lesson 8 — Quality Workmanship & Professional Standards

### Q47
Which statement best defines quality workmanship?
A. Work that looks acceptable from a distance
B. Work that conforms to relevant requirements, functions as intended and is completed with appropriate accuracy, finish and professional care
C. Work completed in the shortest possible time
D. Work made only from expensive materials

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Quality combines conformance, function, accuracy, finish and professional execution.
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — quality workmanship.

### Q48
A frame is assembled but measures differently across its two diagonals when the design requires it to be square. What should the carpenter conclude?
A. Equal diagonals are never relevant to frame geometry
B. The frame should be checked and corrected as appropriate before accepting the work
C. Paint will correct the geometry
D. The defect matters only after handover

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Dimensional and geometric inspection should occur before defects are hidden or passed forward.
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — inspection and accuracy.

### Q49
Why is checking quality during the job generally better than waiting only until the end?
A. Early checks can identify errors before additional work, material and time are invested
B. Final inspection is illegal
C. Early checks remove the need for specifications
D. Quality cannot be checked after assembly

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** In-process quality control helps prevent defects from becoming more costly or difficult to correct.
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — quality control.

### Q50
A small visible defect is found before handover. The carpenter believes the customer may not notice it. What is the most professional approach?
A. Hide it and say nothing
B. Assess it against the required quality/specification and correct or escalate it appropriately
C. Add another coating without inspection
D. Ask a learner to decide whether it matters

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Professional quality decisions are based on requirements and integrity, not on whether a defect might escape notice.
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — defects and professional standards.

### Q51
What is rework?
A. Planned work completed correctly the first time
B. Additional work required to correct work that does not meet the required condition or specification
C. Routine cleaning after a task
D. A type of timber seasoning

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Rework consumes additional resources and is often reduced through planning, control and timely inspection.
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — defects and rework.

### Q52
Which final-handover practice best demonstrates professional workmanship?
A. Delivering immediately after the last operation without inspection
B. Completing final checks, ensuring the work is suitably presented, and communicating relevant use/care or outstanding information
C. Leaving waste and offcuts with the customer
D. Avoiding documentation because the job is finished

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Professional handover confirms completion and communicates relevant information rather than simply transferring the item.
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — final inspection and handover.
