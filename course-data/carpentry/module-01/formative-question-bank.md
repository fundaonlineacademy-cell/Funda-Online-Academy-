# Carpentry — Professional Skills Short Course
## Module 1: Carpentry Practice, Workshop Safety & Professional Standards
### Entry 9 — Formative Assessment Question Bank

**Assessment type:** Formative Assessment  
**Questions presented per attempt:** 15  
**Question bank:** 32 MCQs  
**Pass mark:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Progression rule:** A learner must pass this formative assessment before Module 1 Summative Assessment is unlocked.  

## Assessment design

This bank assesses knowledge and applied judgement developed in Lessons 1–8. Questions are designed for random selection. Every question has four options (A–D), one best answer, a correct-answer rationale, and lesson traceability.

---

### Q01 — The carpentry work process
**Lesson:** 1 — The Carpentry Profession & Workplace Environment  
**Question:** Which sequence best represents a professional carpentry work process?

A. Cut materials → ask for the brief → inspect tools → hand over  
B. Clarify the brief → plan the work → control risks → prepare and make → inspect → hand over  
C. Choose the fastest tool → make the item → measure it at the end  
D. Start work immediately → correct all problems during handover

**Correct answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Professional work begins by understanding requirements, planning and controlling risks before irreversible operations. Quality is checked during and after production before handover.

### Q02 — Understanding a specification
**Lesson:** 1  
**Question:** In carpentry, what is a specification primarily used for?

A. To describe required dimensions, materials, quality or other job requirements  
B. To record only the worker's attendance  
C. To replace all workplace safety procedures  
D. To identify the cheapest available tool

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** A specification communicates requirements against which the work can be planned, produced and checked.

### Q03 — Competence and boundaries
**Lesson:** 1  
**Question:** A learner is asked to operate unfamiliar workshop machinery without having been trained or authorised. What is the most appropriate response?

A. Operate it slowly and learn by trial and error  
B. Ask another learner to demonstrate while the machine is running  
C. Stop and seek instruction or competent supervision before operating it  
D. Operate it only if the job is urgent

**Correct answer:** C  
**Rationale:** Workers should not misrepresent competence or undertake unfamiliar hazardous operations without appropriate instruction, authorisation and supervision.

### Q04 — Handover
**Lesson:** 1  
**Question:** What is the main purpose of professional handover?

A. To transfer completed work after confirming its condition and relevant requirements  
B. To allow the worker to avoid final inspection  
C. To transfer responsibility for defects that were already known  
D. To remove the need for customer communication

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Handover is the controlled transfer of completed work and should follow appropriate inspection and communication.

### Q05 — Responsibility and accountability
**Lesson:** 2 — Roles, Responsibilities & Professional Conduct  
**Question:** Which statement best distinguishes responsibility from accountability?

A. Responsibility concerns assigned duties; accountability concerns being answerable for decisions or results within one's role  
B. Responsibility applies only to managers and accountability only to customers  
C. They both mean permission to ignore instructions  
D. There is no practical difference in workplace conduct

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** A person may be responsible for performing a task while accountability describes the obligation to answer for decisions, actions or outcomes within the person's authority.

### Q06 — Unclear instruction
**Lesson:** 2  
**Question:** A supervisor tells a learner to trim a fitted door but the amount to remove is unclear. What should the learner do before cutting?

A. Estimate the amount and cut immediately  
B. Clarify the required dimension and confirm the instruction  
C. Remove the maximum amount to avoid a second cut  
D. Ask a customer to choose an amount without consulting the supervisor

**Correct answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Irreversible work should not proceed from ambiguous instructions. Clarification protects safety, material, quality and customer requirements.

### Q07 — Professional conduct
**Lesson:** 2  
**Question:** Which behaviour best demonstrates professional conduct in a carpentry workplace?

A. Hiding a mistake so that the job appears complete  
B. Arriving reliably, communicating respectfully and reporting problems promptly  
C. Borrowing tools without permission if they are returned later  
D. Continuing unsafe work to avoid delaying the team

**Correct answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Reliability, respectful communication, honesty, care of property and timely reporting are important elements of professional conduct.

### Q08 — Escalation
**Lesson:** 2  
**Question:** When should a learner escalate a problem to an appropriate supervisor or competent person?

A. Only after damage has occurred  
B. When the problem exceeds the learner's authority, competence or ability to resolve it safely  
C. Only when a customer complains  
D. Never; every worker must solve every problem alone

**Correct answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Escalation is appropriate where authority, competence, safety or specification decisions exceed the learner's role.

### Q09 — Hazard versus risk
**Lesson:** 3 — Workshop Hazards & Risk Awareness  
**Question:** Which statement correctly distinguishes a hazard from risk?

A. A hazard is a source or situation with potential to cause harm; risk considers the likelihood and consequences of that harm  
B. A hazard is an injury that has already occurred; risk is the medical treatment  
C. Hazard and risk always mean exactly the same thing  
D. Risk refers only to financial loss

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Identifying the hazard is the starting point; risk assessment considers how likely harm is and how serious its consequences could be.

### Q10 — Dynamic risk awareness
**Lesson:** 3  
**Question:** A power lead is suddenly found crossing a busy workshop walkway. What is the best immediate approach?

A. Ignore it because the job has already started  
B. Recognise the changed condition, prevent exposure where possible and have the hazard controlled  
C. Cover it with sawdust so people notice it  
D. Wait until the end of the week to mention it

**Correct answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Risk awareness continues throughout work. Changed conditions should trigger reassessment and appropriate control.

### Q11 — Hierarchy of controls
**Lesson:** 3  
**Question:** Which control generally addresses a hazard more directly than relying only on PPE?

A. Eliminating the hazard from the task where reasonably practicable  
B. Wearing two unrelated items of PPE  
C. Working faster to reduce exposure time without planning  
D. Asking workers to be more careful without changing the hazard

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** The hierarchy of controls prioritises removing or controlling hazards at source before relying primarily on personal protective equipment.

### Q12 — Near miss
**Lesson:** 3  
**Question:** Why should a near miss be reported according to workplace procedures?

A. Because it may reveal a hazard or control failure before someone is injured  
B. Only so that a worker can be blamed  
C. Because every near miss automatically requires dismissal  
D. It should not be reported unless equipment is destroyed

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Near misses provide valuable warning information and can support preventive corrective action.

### Q13 — Purpose of PPE
**Lesson:** 4 — Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)  
**Question:** Which statement about PPE is most accurate?

A. PPE can be selected without considering the task  
B. PPE is one layer of protection and does not automatically replace stronger hazard controls  
C. PPE eliminates every workshop hazard  
D. Any PPE item is suitable if it looks protective

**Correct answer:** B  
**Rationale:** PPE should be appropriate to the identified hazard and is normally part of a wider risk-control approach.

### Q14 — Eye protection
**Lesson:** 4  
**Question:** A task can produce flying wood particles. Which consideration is most relevant when selecting eye protection?

A. Whether the protection is suitable for the identified eye hazard and fits correctly  
B. Whether it matches the worker's clothing  
C. Whether it is the oldest PPE available  
D. Whether another worker is wearing gloves

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** PPE selection must be hazard-specific, suitable and correctly fitted.

### Q15 — PPE inspection
**Lesson:** 4  
**Question:** A learner notices that required eye protection is badly damaged and no longer provides reliable protection. What should happen?

A. Continue using it until the end of the month  
B. Repair it with any available material while working  
C. Remove it from use and obtain suitable serviceable protection according to workplace procedure  
D. Work without eye protection if the task is short

**Correct answer:** C  
**Rationale:** Defective protective equipment should not be relied upon. It should be reported, replaced or otherwise handled according to the workplace system.

### Q16 — PPE compatibility
**Lesson:** 4  
**Question:** Why must different items of PPE be checked for compatibility when worn together?

A. One item can interfere with the fit or protective function of another  
B. Compatible PPE always costs less  
C. Compatibility matters only for uniforms  
D. It allows workers to ignore equipment guards

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Multiple PPE items must work together without reducing fit, visibility, seal or other protective performance.

### Q17 — Pre-use inspection
**Lesson:** 5 — Safe Tool Handling & Pre-use Checks  
**Question:** What is the main purpose of a pre-use tool check?

A. To identify visible defects, incorrect setup or unsafe conditions before operation  
B. To prove that maintenance is never required  
C. To increase the speed of every tool  
D. To replace training and manufacturer instructions

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Pre-use checks help identify conditions that could make the planned operation unsafe or unreliable.

### Q18 — Defective tool
**Lesson:** 5  
**Question:** During a pre-use check, a worker finds a damaged electrical cable on a portable power tool. What is the best response?

A. Hold the damaged section away from the workpiece and continue  
B. Cover the damage temporarily and continue without reporting it  
C. Do not use the tool; isolate or remove it from service as required and report the defect  
D. Use the tool only for a short cut

**Correct answer:** C  
**Rationale:** A defective tool should not remain in service where the defect may create danger. Workplace isolation and reporting procedures should be followed.

### Q19 — Guards
**Lesson:** 5  
**Question:** A machine guard interferes with a worker's preferred cutting technique. What should the worker do?

A. Remove the guard and continue  
B. Bypass the guard only for narrow workpieces  
C. Use the equipment only in its safe intended configuration and seek guidance if the task cannot be performed safely  
D. Ask another worker to hold the guard open

**Correct answer:** C  
**Rationale:** Safety devices should not be defeated for convenience. The task, equipment or method must be reconsidered if safe operation is not possible.

### Q20 — Workholding
**Lesson:** 5  
**Question:** Why is secure workholding important during many carpentry operations?

A. It helps prevent uncontrolled workpiece movement and supports accuracy  
B. It makes PPE unnecessary  
C. It guarantees that every cut will be perfect  
D. It allows damaged tools to be used safely

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Suitable workholding can improve stability, control and accuracy while reducing exposure created by a moving workpiece.

### Q21 — Clear walkways
**Lesson:** 6 — Workshop Housekeeping & Material Storage  
**Question:** Why should workshop walkways and access routes be kept clear?

A. To reduce trip and obstruction hazards and support safe movement, including during emergencies  
B. Only to improve the appearance of the workshop  
C. So that timber can be stored in every passage  
D. To create more space for waste piles

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Good housekeeping protects normal movement and emergency access and reduces preventable hazards.

### Q22 — Timber storage
**Lesson:** 6  
**Question:** Which is the best general principle for storing timber and sheet materials?

A. Store them securely and stably using an appropriate storage method that prevents uncontrolled movement or collapse  
B. Lean all materials loosely against the nearest machine  
C. Store heavy boards in front of emergency equipment  
D. Stack materials wherever they were last used

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Materials should be stored in a stable, organised way appropriate to their size, mass and workplace system.

### Q23 — Sawdust and offcuts
**Lesson:** 6  
**Question:** Why should sawdust and offcuts be controlled and removed appropriately?

A. They can contribute to slips, trips, fire load, dust exposure and poor access  
B. They always improve floor grip  
C. They protect electrical equipment from damage  
D. They should be left in place to show that work is progressing

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Waste accumulation can introduce several hazards and interfere with safe, efficient work.

### Q24 — Tool storage
**Lesson:** 6  
**Question:** What is a key benefit of returning tools to designated storage after use?

A. It supports accountability, protects tools and reduces clutter and search time  
B. It removes the need for pre-use checks  
C. It guarantees tools never require maintenance  
D. It allows damaged tools to be hidden

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Organised storage supports housekeeping, asset care and efficient preparation for future work.

### Q25 — Emergency alarm
**Lesson:** 7 — Emergency, Fire & Incident Procedures  
**Question:** When an emergency alarm or evacuation instruction is given, what should a learner generally do?

A. Finish the current cut before responding  
B. Follow the workplace emergency procedure and proceed by the designated safe route as instructed  
C. Return to collect personal belongings first  
D. Hide until the alarm stops

**Correct answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Workers should know and follow site-specific emergency and evacuation procedures without creating unnecessary delay or additional exposure.

### Q26 — Fire response
**Lesson:** 7  
**Question:** A small fire starts in a workshop. Which principle is most appropriate for an untrained learner?

A. Attempt firefighting regardless of the fire size or personal risk  
B. Raise the alarm and follow the emergency procedure; only use firefighting equipment if trained, authorised and it is safe to do so  
C. Carry the burning material through the workshop  
D. Ignore it until a supervisor notices

**Correct answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Life safety and alarm/evacuation procedures take priority. Firefighting should not expose an untrained person to unnecessary danger.

### Q27 — Incident reporting
**Lesson:** 7  
**Question:** Why is prompt workplace incident reporting important?

A. It supports appropriate response, investigation, records and corrective action  
B. It guarantees that nobody will ask questions  
C. It is useful only when equipment is destroyed  
D. It replaces emergency medical response

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Reporting helps the workplace respond correctly, preserve relevant information and prevent recurrence.

### Q28 — Scene preservation
**Lesson:** 7  
**Question:** After a serious workplace incident, why might the area need to be preserved and access controlled, subject to emergency needs and workplace/legal requirements?

A. To protect people and avoid unnecessarily disturbing evidence relevant to investigation  
B. To allow normal production to restart immediately  
C. To hide damaged equipment  
D. To prevent the incident from being reported

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Once immediate life-safety needs are addressed, controlled access can protect others and support proper investigation.

### Q29 — Quality workmanship
**Lesson:** 8 — Quality Workmanship & Professional Standards  
**Question:** Which description best reflects quality workmanship?

A. Work that meets relevant requirements for dimensions, function, fit, finish and safe professional execution  
B. Work that is completed fastest regardless of defects  
C. Work that looks acceptable from a distance even if it does not function  
D. Work that uses the most expensive material available

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Quality is measured against requirements and fitness for purpose, not speed or price alone.

### Q30 — In-process quality checks
**Lesson:** 8  
**Question:** Why are quality checks useful during production instead of only at the end?

A. They can identify errors before more material, labour and time are committed  
B. They remove the need for final inspection  
C. They guarantee there will never be rework  
D. They allow specifications to be ignored

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Detecting deviations early can reduce waste and prevent errors from being carried into later stages.

### Q31 — Known defect before handover
**Lesson:** 8  
**Question:** A worker identifies a significant defect before handing the item to the customer. What is the most professional action?

A. Hide the defect with a quick cosmetic treatment  
B. Assess and report it, then correct or escalate it according to requirements before handover  
C. Hand it over immediately and wait for the customer to find it  
D. Change the specification without approval

**Correct answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Professional quality practice requires honest inspection, appropriate corrective action and communication rather than concealing known defects.

### Q32 — Continuous improvement
**Lesson:** 8  
**Question:** After completing a job, the team notices that repeated measuring errors caused unnecessary rework. What best supports continuous improvement?

A. Identify the cause and improve the measuring/checking process for future work  
B. Accept the rework as unavoidable and make no changes  
C. Stop measuring altogether  
D. Reduce inspection so fewer errors are recorded

**Correct answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Continuous improvement uses evidence from defects, rework and work experience to strengthen future processes and quality.
