# Carpentry — Professional Skills Short Course
## Module 4: Measurement, Marking & Technical Accuracy
### Entry 10 — Summative Assessment Bank

**Assessment type:** Summative Assessment  
**Questions displayed per attempt:** 25  
**Question bank size:** 52  
**Pass mark:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Prerequisite:** Module 4 Formative Assessment passed  
**Format:** Multiple choice, A–D, one best answer

## Assessment purpose
This summative assessment evaluates whether the learner can apply measurement, marking, geometry, alignment, tolerance and setting-out principles in realistic carpentry decisions. It deliberately goes beyond simple recall. Questions require the learner to select reliable references, diagnose measurement and layout errors, protect specified dimensions, verify work before irreversible operations and make quality-conscious decisions.

The bank is traceable to all eight teaching lessons in Module 4. Numerical tolerances must come from the applicable drawing, specification, approved procedure or other authorised requirement; learners are not expected to invent tolerances.

## Lesson 1 — Measurement Systems & Reading Dimensions

### Q1
A drawing gives the shelf length as 0.92 m, while the workshop cutting list records dimensions in millimetres. What should the carpenter record?

A. 92 mm  
B. 920 mm  
C. 9 200 mm  
D. 0.092 mm

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** 1 metre equals 1 000 millimetres, so 0.92 m equals 920 mm.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — metric dimension interpretation and conversion.

### Q2
A learner measures the position of every shelf divider from the previous divider instead of from one common datum. What is the main quality risk?

A. The timber becomes harder  
B. Small errors can accumulate across the project  
C. The pencil becomes blunt  
D. Every dimension automatically becomes equal

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Chaining dimensions from one feature to the next can accumulate small errors. A reliable common datum reduces this risk.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — reference points, datums and cumulative error.

### Q3
A component dimension on a drawing is unclear and could reasonably be read in two different ways. What is the most professional action before cutting?

A. Choose the larger value  
B. Estimate the value from memory  
C. Clarify the specification before proceeding  
D. Cut both possibilities from good stock

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Ambiguous dimensions should be clarified rather than guessed, especially before an irreversible operation.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — stated dimensions versus assumptions.

### Q4
A worker has marked a board at 650 mm but has not yet cut it. Which sequence best reflects professional measurement control?

A. Cut immediately because the mark already exists  
B. Verify the unit, reference point and marked dimension before cutting  
C. Add another mark beside it without measuring  
D. Remove the reference mark to avoid confusion

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** A pre-cut verification should confirm the unit, datum/reference and transferred dimension before material is removed.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — measure, mark and verify workflow.

### Q5
A drawing states 72 cm for a component, but the workshop works primarily in millimetres. Which value is equivalent?

A. 7.2 mm  
B. 72 mm  
C. 720 mm  
D. 7 200 mm

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** 1 cm equals 10 mm, therefore 72 cm equals 720 mm.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — metric conversion.

### Q6
Why is a consistent reference point important when transferring several dimensions to one workpiece?

A. It makes every component decorative  
B. It reduces the chance that positioning errors accumulate  
C. It eliminates the need to read the drawing  
D. It makes tolerance irrelevant

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** A consistent datum creates a stable origin for dimensions and reduces cumulative layout error.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — datum-based measurement.

## Lesson 2 — Tape Rules, Steel Rules & Measuring Accuracy

### Q7
A carpenter must check a short, flat joint component accurately at the bench. Which tool is generally the better starting choice when its range is suitable?

A. A steel rule in sound condition  
B. A long flexible tape held in a curve  
C. A damaged folding rule  
D. A spirit level used as a scale

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** A sound steel rule is well suited to controlled shorter measurements on flat work when its range is appropriate.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — selecting a measuring tool for the task.

### Q8
A tape blade is twisted and sagging while a long board is being measured. What should the learner do?

A. Read the value anyway and compensate mentally  
B. Straighten and correctly align the tape before accepting the reading  
C. Add 5 mm automatically  
D. Mark the nearest visible number

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Twist, sag and poor alignment can create measurement error. The tool should be positioned correctly before the reading is accepted.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — tape alignment and reading accuracy.

### Q9
A tape measure has a visibly damaged end hook and is giving inconsistent results on repeated checks. What is the best response?

A. Continue because all tape hooks are identical  
B. Use it only for expensive components  
C. Stop relying on it for precision work and follow the workplace process for checking or replacing the tool  
D. Bend the hook until it looks approximately correct

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** A visibly damaged or inconsistent measuring tool should not be trusted for precision work. Unauthorised adjustment can make the problem worse.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — measuring-tool inspection and defect response.

### Q10
A learner reads a steel rule while looking sharply from one side rather than directly over the graduation. What type of problem may result?

A. Parallax or line-of-sight reading error  
B. Timber seasoning  
C. Kickback  
D. Adhesive failure

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Reading a scale from an angle can shift the apparent relationship between the graduation and the feature being measured.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — reading graduations accurately.

### Q11
Two repeated measurements of the same unchanged component differ noticeably. What should happen before machining?

A. Average the values and cut immediately  
B. Investigate the reference, tool condition, alignment and reading method  
C. Select the smaller reading automatically  
D. Ignore the difference because repeat readings are never expected to agree

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Inconsistent repeated measurements are a warning to diagnose the measuring process before committing material.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — repeatability and measurement verification.

### Q12
A carpenter needs to measure a long piece of stock that exceeds the practical length of the available steel rule. Which choice is most appropriate?

A. Use a suitable tape measure correctly aligned with the reference points  
B. Estimate the remaining distance after the steel rule ends  
C. Stretch a piece of string and call it the final dimension  
D. Measure only half and assume the stock is symmetrical

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** A suitable tape measure is appropriate for longer dimensions when used straight, aligned and from reliable reference points.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — tape versus steel-rule selection.

## Lesson 3 — Squares, Straightedges & Checking Geometry

### Q13
A rectangular frame has opposite sides of the correct specified length, but its two diagonal measurements are different. What does this most strongly indicate?

A. The frame may be racked and not square  
B. The timber is automatically too short  
C. The finish is too glossy  
D. The frame must be level

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** For a true rectangular frame, equal diagonals are a useful geometric check. Unequal diagonals indicate racking or another geometry problem that should be investigated.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — diagonal checking and racking.

### Q14
A try square is placed against an edge that is damaged and not the approved reference edge. Why is the check unreliable?

A. A square can only be used outdoors  
B. The tool is referencing an unsuitable surface, so the result may not represent true geometry  
C. A square measures only length  
D. Damaged timber always expands by the same amount

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Geometry checks depend on reliable reference faces and edges. A defective reference can transfer its error into the inspection.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — reference geometry.

### Q15
What is the main purpose of a straightedge in a geometry check?

A. To measure electrical voltage  
B. To provide a reference for checking straightness or alignment  
C. To replace every measuring tool  
D. To sharpen a marking knife

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** A sound straightedge provides a reference against which straightness, gaps or alignment can be observed.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — straightedge use.

### Q16
During dry assembly, a frame is found to be out of square. What is the best time to correct the geometry?

A. Before final fastening or irreversible fixing, where the assembly method permits adjustment  
B. Only after the finish has cured  
C. After handing the item to the customer  
D. Never; square is only cosmetic

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Progressive checking allows geometry errors to be corrected while adjustment is still practical, reducing rework.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — checking during assembly.

### Q17
Which combination gives the strongest basic evidence that a rectangular assembly is geometrically correct?

A. Correct specified dimensions together with appropriate square/diagonal checks  
B. A smooth surface only  
C. Equal nail spacing only  
D. A dark pencil line around the frame

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Geometry should be judged against dimensions and appropriate squareness/alignment checks, not appearance alone.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — integrated geometry verification.

### Q18
A learner checks a corner with a try square and sees a gap, but the square itself has been dropped and is visibly damaged. What should happen next?

A. Condemn the workpiece immediately  
B. Verify the checking tool before using its result to judge the workpiece  
C. File the workpiece until the gap disappears  
D. Ignore all future square checks

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Inspection equipment must itself be serviceable. A suspect square can produce a false diagnosis.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — reliable inspection tools.

## Lesson 4 — Marking Gauges, Knives, Pencils & Reference Faces

### Q19
Why should a marking gauge fence remain firmly against the chosen reference face while a parallel line is marked?

A. To keep the marked line at a consistent offset from that reference  
B. To increase timber moisture  
C. To make the line wider  
D. To replace the need for a specified dimension

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** The fence follows the reference face so the cutter or pin marks a consistent parallel distance.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — marking-gauge use.

### Q20
A component has one face and one edge already established as reliable references. What is the best layout practice?

A. Mark important dimensions consistently from the approved reference face/edge where the job requires it  
B. Change reference faces after every mark  
C. Measure from whichever rough edge is nearest  
D. Remove all reference-face marks before layout

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Consistent reference faces and edges help control relationships between dimensions and reduce transferred error.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — reference faces and edges.

### Q21
A carpenter uses a very thick, fuzzy pencil line for a precision joint and later cannot tell which part of the line is the true dimension. What is the main problem?

A. The marking method introduced unnecessary ambiguity  
B. The timber became structurally weaker  
C. The pencil changed the specified tolerance  
D. The joint is automatically correct

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Precision layout requires clear, controlled marks whose intended position is unambiguous.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — marking quality.

### Q22
When is a marking knife especially useful in accurate layout?

A. When a fine, precise reference line is appropriate for the operation and material  
B. When measuring a three-metre room  
C. When checking whether a shelf is level  
D. When tightening a screw

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** A marking knife can create a fine, precise layout line where that marking method is appropriate.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — pencil versus knife marking.

### Q23
A learner sets a marking gauge correctly, then allows the fence to lift away from the reference face halfway through the stroke. What is the likely result?

A. The line may no longer remain consistently parallel to the reference  
B. The timber becomes perfectly square  
C. The gauge automatically resets itself  
D. The line becomes a level datum

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Loss of fence contact can alter the offset and spoil the intended parallel layout.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — controlled gauge technique.

### Q24
Why is the waste side of a cut commonly identified during layout?

A. To help preserve the required finished dimension when cutting  
B. To make all components longer  
C. To eliminate the kerf  
D. To remove the need for checking the drawing

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Clearly identifying the retained and waste sides helps the operator position the cut so the required component dimension is preserved.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — waste-side marking and dimension control.

### Q25
A marking gauge pin is loose and changes position during use. What is the professional response?

A. Continue and correct the lines by eye  
B. Stop, correct or report the tool condition according to procedure, then verify the setting before layout  
C. Press harder so the pin cannot move  
D. Add an arbitrary allowance to every mark

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** A tool that cannot hold its setting cannot reliably transfer a specified dimension.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — tool inspection and reliable marking.

## Lesson 5 — Angles, Bevels & Accurate Layout

### Q26
A sliding bevel has been set to an angle from an approved template. What is its main role during layout?

A. Transfer that angle consistently to the workpiece  
B. Measure timber moisture  
C. Check electrical continuity  
D. Determine coating thickness

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** A sliding bevel is commonly used to set and transfer an angle rather than to create an arbitrary angle by guesswork.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — sliding-bevel use.

### Q27
Two frame components are mirror images. Why must the learner identify component orientation before marking the angles?

A. Because the same angle can face the wrong direction on a mirrored component  
B. Because mirrored timber has no grain  
C. Because a bevel cannot mark timber  
D. Because all mirrored parts must have different lengths

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Orientation matters. A correctly sized angle transferred in the wrong direction can ruin a mirrored component.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — mirrored components and orientation.

### Q28
A mitred component has clearly identified long and short points. Why is this useful?

A. It helps relate the specified dimension to the correct point of the angled cut  
B. It changes the angle to 90 degrees  
C. It eliminates the need for a cutting line  
D. It makes the material wider

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Angled components can be dimensioned to particular points. Identifying them prevents measuring to the wrong end of the mitre.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — long/short points and mitre layout.

### Q29
A bevel setting is accidentally knocked after the first of six identical components is marked. What is the best action?

A. Continue because the first component proves the bevel is still correct  
B. Re-establish and verify the required angle before marking the remaining components  
C. Estimate the angle by eye  
D. Change all remaining dimensions to match the new setting

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Repeated work depends on preserving a verified setting. A disturbed setting must be rechecked before it is propagated.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — repeated layout and error prevention.

### Q30
Why should an angle be transferred from an approved drawing, template or verified setting rather than guessed?

A. Because professional layout must reflect the specified geometry  
B. Because guessed angles are always 45 degrees  
C. Because timber cannot be marked twice  
D. Because a drawing changes after cutting

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Layout should reproduce the required geometry, not the operator's visual estimate.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — specified angle control.

### Q31
Before cutting an angled component, which check is most useful?

A. Confirm orientation, angle line, finished side/waste side and the dimension reference  
B. Count the knots only  
C. Check the workshop clock  
D. Remove the layout marks

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** A final layout check should verify both geometry and which material must remain after the cut.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — pre-cut angle verification.

### Q32
A learner accurately transfers an angle but starts from the wrong datum. What is the likely outcome?

A. The angle may be correct while the feature is still in the wrong position  
B. The work must automatically be correct  
C. The timber will become plumb  
D. The bevel will become a steel rule

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Correct angle and correct location are separate requirements. Both depend on the specified reference system.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — angle layout from reliable datums.

## Lesson 6 — Levels, Plumb & Alignment

### Q33
What does it mean when a vertical component is plumb?

A. It is truly vertical relative to the applicable reference  
B. It is necessarily square in every dimension  
C. It is exactly one metre long  
D. It has been sanded smooth

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Plumb describes vertical orientation; it is not the same as level, square or dimensional accuracy.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — level and plumb concepts.

### Q34
A shelf is level, but its front edge does not align with the other shelves in the unit. What does this show?

A. Level and alignment are different checks  
B. A level shelf is automatically aligned  
C. Alignment matters only after painting  
D. The spirit level must be wrong

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** A component can be level yet incorrectly positioned relative to neighbouring components or a layout datum.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — distinguishing level from alignment.

### Q35
A spirit level has been dropped and gives suspicious results. What should the carpenter do before relying on it for installation?

A. Verify the tool's condition and reliability according to workplace practice  
B. Assume the bubble cannot be affected  
C. Add 10 mm to every installation mark  
D. Use the longest component as a replacement vial

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Inspection and layout tools must be trustworthy before their readings are used to accept or reject work.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — checking levelling equipment.

### Q36
Why should alignment be checked progressively during an installation rather than only at the end?

A. Early checks can identify and correct developing error before it affects later components  
B. Final checks are prohibited  
C. Alignment always improves by itself  
D. Progressive checks remove the need for dimensions

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Progressive verification limits error propagation and reduces rework.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — progressive alignment checks.

### Q37
A cabinet is installed against an existing wall that is visibly uneven. What is the most professional approach?

A. Treat the wall automatically as a perfect datum  
B. Establish and verify the appropriate installation references, then follow the specification for fitting to the actual condition  
C. Force the cabinet to copy every irregularity without checking  
D. Ignore level, plumb and alignment

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Existing surfaces are not automatically reliable references. Installation should use verified datums and the applicable specification or approved method.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — reliable installation references.

### Q38
Which statement is correct?

A. Level describes horizontal orientation; plumb describes vertical orientation  
B. Level and plumb both mean 45 degrees  
C. Plumb means equal diagonals  
D. Level means within any tolerance chosen by the worker

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** These terms describe different orientation conditions and should not be used interchangeably.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — terminology and checking orientation.

## Lesson 7 — Tolerances, Allowances & Fit

### Q39
A drawing specifies a finished width and an allowable tolerance. What should determine whether the component is acceptable?

A. Whether the measured result falls within the specified acceptable limits  
B. Whether it looks close from across the room  
C. Whether the worker prefers the dimension  
D. Whether extra material is available

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Acceptance must be judged against the stated specification and tolerance, not personal preference.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — tolerances and acceptance limits.

### Q40
A drawing gives a nominal dimension but no numerical tolerance, and the learner is unsure what variation is permitted. What should the learner do?

A. Invent a ±2 mm tolerance  
B. Clarify the applicable specification, drawing requirement or authorised standard before deciding acceptance  
C. Assume any result is acceptable  
D. Use the tolerance from an unrelated project

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Numerical tolerances should not be invented. The applicable requirement must come from authorised project information or standards.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — specified versus invented tolerances.

### Q41
What is an allowance in a carpentry fit context?

A. An intentional dimensional difference provided for the required fit, movement or process where specified  
B. Any accidental measuring error  
C. A decorative pencil line  
D. A replacement for a cutting list

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** An allowance is intentional; an error is not. Its value and purpose must come from the applicable design or procedure.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — allowances and fit.

### Q42
A door component binds after assembly. The learner immediately removes material without measuring or checking alignment. Why is this poor practice?

A. The cause may be dimensional, geometric, alignment-related or another specified condition, so diagnosis should precede rework  
B. Removing material always increases strength  
C. Binding proves the door is too wide in every case  
D. Measurements are unnecessary after assembly

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Rework should address the actual cause. Unmeasured material removal can create a second defect.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — fit diagnosis and corrective action.

### Q43
Four small layout errors occur in the same direction across a multi-component assembly. What concept should the carpenter consider?

A. Cumulative error  
B. Surface finishing  
C. Fire triangle  
D. Abrasive loading

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Multiple small errors can combine and produce a significant final misfit or misalignment.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — cumulative dimensional error.

### Q44
A component measures slightly above its nominal dimension. Can the learner decide from that fact alone that it has failed?

A. Yes, every dimension above nominal fails  
B. No; acceptance depends on the applicable specified limits or fit requirement  
C. Yes, but only for timber  
D. No, because dimensions never matter

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Nominal dimension alone does not define acceptance when a tolerance or other fit criterion applies.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — nominal dimensions and tolerances.

### Q45
Why are tolerances important in assemblies containing several interacting components?

A. They help define acceptable dimensional variation so components can meet the required fit and function  
B. They eliminate the need for measurement  
C. They guarantee timber will never move  
D. They allow workers to ignore drawings

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Tolerances establish acceptable limits linked to function, fit and specification.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — tolerance and functional fit.

## Lesson 8 — Setting Out a Simple Carpentry Project & Cut List

### Q46
A cutting list shows four identical rails. What should the learner confirm before marking stock?

A. Quantity, specified dimensions, material requirements and any relevant orientation/reference information  
B. Only the colour of the pencil  
C. Only the total project price  
D. Nothing, because identical components cannot be marked incorrectly

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** A cutting list must be interpreted as a complete production instruction, not merely a length list.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — cutting-list interpretation.

### Q47
Why should a cutting list be checked against the working drawing or approved brief before material is cut?

A. To detect omissions, transcription errors or misunderstandings before they become wasted material  
B. To make the drawing unnecessary  
C. To increase the kerf  
D. To change all dimensions to standard stock length

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Cross-checking protects the link between design information and production information.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — drawing-to-cutting-list workflow.

### Q48
A board can provide several components. Which layout approach best supports cost-effective material use without compromising specifications?

A. Plan component positions and cuts before machining, while preserving required dimensions and material quality  
B. Cut components randomly from the centre  
C. Ignore defects and grain considerations  
D. Make every component oversized by an arbitrary amount

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Planned set-out can reduce avoidable waste while still respecting specifications, defects, process needs and component quality.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — material planning and waste reduction.

### Q49
A learner marks the correct finished length but places the saw kerf on the retained side of the line. What is the likely quality consequence?

A. The finished component may become undersize  
B. The component automatically becomes square  
C. The kerf disappears after cutting  
D. The board becomes longer

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Cutting on the wrong side of the layout line can remove material from the required finished dimension.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — kerf and waste-side planning.

### Q50
A project has several related components that must align in the final assembly. Which set-out strategy is strongest?

A. Use clearly established references and verify related dimensions before cutting  
B. Give every component a different datum without reason  
C. Estimate repeated dimensions by eye  
D. Mark each part from the previous unfinished cut only

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Consistent references and verification improve dimensional relationships between components.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — project set-out and common references.

### Q51
A learner discovers that the cutting list quantity conflicts with the drawing. What should happen?

A. Cut the larger quantity to be safe  
B. Clarify the discrepancy before committing material  
C. Ignore the drawing  
D. Average the two quantities

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Conflicting production information must be resolved before cutting to avoid waste and incorrect manufacture.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — checking production information.

### Q52
Which final pre-cut review best integrates the skills of Module 4?

A. Confirm drawing/cutting-list information, units, quantities, datum/reference faces, dimensions, geometry or angles, waste side, tool suitability and any specified tolerances  
B. Check only whether the pencil is sharp  
C. Check only the longest dimension  
D. Cut first and inspect all information afterwards

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Professional set-out integrates measurement, references, geometry, marking, fit requirements and verification before an irreversible operation.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — integrated set-out and pre-cut verification.

13. This summative bank is intentionally more application- and judgement-focused than the formative bank.
