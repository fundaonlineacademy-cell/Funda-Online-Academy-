# Carpentry Module 6 — Carpentry Joints & Assembly Techniques

## Module purpose
Develop the learner's ability to select, prepare, assemble and inspect common carpentry joints and fastening systems while working accurately, safely and to a defined quality standard.

## Teaching entries
1. Joint Selection & Principles
2. Butt Joints & Reinforcement
3. Lap and Halving Joints
4. Housing/Dado & Rebate/Rabbet Joints
5. Mortise-and-Tenon Principles
6. Dowel, Biscuit & Mechanical Joining
7. Adhesives, Screws, Nails & Fixings
8. Dry Assembly, Squareness, Clamping & Joint Quality
9. Formative Assessment
10. Summative Assessment

## Locked lesson standard (Lessons 1–8)
Each lesson must contain: identification; estimated study time; 3–5 measurable learning outcomes; Why This Lesson Matters; 5–8 bold key terms with clear definitions; at least 500 words substantive main teaching content (normally 700–1,000); worked example/demonstration; practical activity; informal knowledge check; answer feedback/explanation; key takeaways; relevant educational visual with caption/alt text where useful.

## Lesson-specific scope
### 6.1 Joint Selection & Principles
Function of joints; load direction; appearance; material thickness; grain direction; accuracy; reference faces/edges; selecting a joint according to purpose rather than habit.

### 6.2 Butt Joints & Reinforcement
Plain butt joints; end-grain limitations; glue blocks/cleats where appropriate; screws, nails, dowels or other reinforcement; pilot holes; alignment; common failure causes.

### 6.3 Lap and Halving Joints
Full/half laps; marking shoulder and depth lines; waste identification; controlled cutting and paring; checking flushness, square and fit before assembly.

### 6.4 Housing/Dado & Rebate/Rabbet Joints
Terminology differences; stopped/through housings; rebates at edges; applications in shelving, frames and casework; setting-out; fit; avoiding over-depth cutting and breakout.

### 6.5 Mortise-and-Tenon Principles
Mortise, tenon, shoulders and cheeks; proportions as job-dependent rather than universal; marking from reference faces; maintaining alignment; safe hand-tool preparation; trial fitting without forcing.

### 6.6 Dowel, Biscuit & Mechanical Joining
Alignment principles; dowel location and drilling accuracy; biscuit-slot concept; proprietary/mechanical connectors; manufacturer instructions; suitability, limitations and repeatability.

### 6.7 Adhesives, Screws, Nails & Fixings
Selecting fastening/adhesive systems for material and service conditions; open/working time; surface preparation; correct quantity; pilot/clearance/countersink concepts; corrosion/service considerations; safe chemical handling according to label/SDS and workplace controls.

### 6.8 Dry Assembly, Squareness, Clamping & Joint Quality
Dry fitting; assembly sequence; clamp selection and pressure; protecting surfaces; checking diagonals/square; squeeze-out management; curing before loading; inspection against specification; correcting defects before handover.

## Visual-learning plan
Use clear labelled visuals where they improve identification: joint anatomy; butt-joint reinforcement; lap-joint waste; housing vs rebate; mortise/tenon components; dowel alignment; screw pilot/clearance/countersink concept; clamp placement and diagonal checking. Visuals must teach a point and include meaningful captions, not act as decoration.

## Safety and accuracy controls
- Safety guidance must be consistent with South African occupational health and safety duties and applicable machinery/workplace requirements.
- Do not teach one fixed joint proportion, screw size, adhesive curing time or clamp pressure as universally correct; dimensions and procedures depend on material, product, design and manufacturer specifications.
- Adhesive and chemical-finish handling must refer learners to current product labels/SDS and workplace controls.
- Power-tool or machinery operations that require training/authorisation must not be presented as unsupervised beginner tasks.
- Learners must be taught to isolate/remove energy before adjustments or accessory changes where applicable and to follow manufacturer instructions.

## Formative assessment specification
- Entry 9.
- 15 MCQs delivered from a bank of at least 30 unique questions.
- Four options A–D.
- Pass mark: 70%.
- Maximum 3 formal attempts when platform attempt policy is implemented.
- Questions distributed across Lessons 1–8 and traceable to taught outcomes.
- Include identification, selection, sequence, defect diagnosis and simple application scenarios.

## Summative assessment specification
- Entry 10; unlocked only after formative pass.
- 25 MCQs delivered from a bank of at least 50 unique questions.
- Four options A–D.
- Pass mark: 70%.
- Maximum 3 formal attempts when platform attempt policy is implemented.
- Broader and more demanding than formative; test integrated selection, setting-out, assembly, quality and safety decisions.
- No question may require knowledge not taught/developed in Lessons 1–8.
