# Carpentry Fundamentals — Module 8 Formative Assessment Bank
## Surface Preparation, Finishing & Protection

**Delivery configuration:** 32-question bank; platform randomly selects 15 questions per attempt; A–D multiple choice; pass mark 70%; maximum 3 attempts; passing unlocks Module 8 Summative Assessment. Each question is traceable to Lessons 1–8. Questions assess taught knowledge, application and judgement.

### Lesson 1 — Surface Assessment & Preparation
1. A clear-finish panel has visible glue beside a joint. What should happen first? A Coat it heavily B Remove/prepare contamination using the approved method C Ignore it D Wet the entire panel. **Answer: B.** Explanation: glue can interfere with appearance and colour/finish uptake.
2. Why should a surface be inspected before sanding? A To decide what defects and preparation are actually required B To avoid all tools C To change timber grade D To cure paint. **Answer: A.** Explanation: preparation must respond to actual condition.
3. Which condition should normally be referred rather than cosmetically hidden? A Loose dust B Serious delamination C Pencil line D Light sanding scratch. **Answer: B.** Explanation: delamination is a material failure, not merely a surface blemish.
4. Why use side/raking light during inspection? A To heat the coating B To reveal scratches and unevenness C To change colour D To increase moisture. **Answer: B.** Explanation: low-angle light makes surface defects easier to see.

### Lesson 2 — Abrasives & Correct Sanding Sequences
5. What is the best starting abrasive? A Always the coarsest available B Least aggressive abrasive that efficiently corrects the condition C Finest available regardless of condition D Any used disc. **Answer: B.** Explanation: unnecessary coarseness creates extra scratches/removal.
6. Why use progressive sanding stages? A Each suitable stage removes significant scratches from the previous one B It waterproofs timber C It replaces finishing D It increases dimensions. **Answer: A.** Explanation: controlled progression refines the scratch pattern.
7. Why is veneer vulnerable to aggressive sanding? A It cannot be sanded B Its decorative layer may be thin and can be cut through C It has no surface D It is always wet. **Answer: B.** Explanation: over-sanding can expose the substrate.
8. What should determine final abrasive preparation? A One universal grit number B Material, process, finish specification and product requirements C Worker age D Paint colour alone. **Answer: B.** Explanation: no universal sequence suits all systems.

### Lesson 3 — Filling, Repairing & Preparing Surface Defects
9. A loose structural joint has a gap. Best response? A Fill and hide it B Correct/refer the joint problem before cosmetic finishing C Add wax D Apply stain. **Answer: B.** Explanation: filler should not conceal structural failure.
10. Why test filler beneath a transparent finish? A Filler can differ in colour/absorption from timber B Filler always becomes invisible C Clear finish removes filler D Testing strengthens wood. **Answer: A.** Explanation: repair areas may remain visually obvious.
11. When should a repair be sanded? A Immediately B When the repair product reaches the manufacturer-required condition C Before application D Only after topcoat. **Answer: B.** Explanation: drying/curing requirements are product-specific.
12. Why avoid excessive overfilling? A It can require unnecessary sanding and damage surrounding geometry B It prevents all adhesion C It changes hardwood to softwood D It stops curing. **Answer: A.** Explanation: controlled local repair protects the component.

### Lesson 4 — Stains, Dyes & Colour Preparation
13. Which statement about stain is safest? A Every stain is a complete protective finish B Verify whether the specific stain/system provides protection C Stain cannot colour timber D All stains are waterproof. **Answer: B.** Explanation: product functions vary.
14. What can make end grain appear darker after staining? A Different absorption B It contains paint C It is always defective D It has no fibres. **Answer: A.** Explanation: end grain often absorbs liquids differently.
15. Why prepare a representative colour sample? A To verify appearance using the intended material/preparation/system B To avoid specifications C To eliminate drying D To change the product. **Answer: A.** Explanation: timber and preparation affect colour.
16. What can glue residue cause under stain? A Uniform absorption B Pale/uneven areas where uptake is blocked C Stronger joints D Faster cure. **Answer: B.** Explanation: contamination interferes with colour penetration.

### Lesson 5 — Clear Finishes & Protective Coatings
17. What should primarily guide clear-finish selection? A Service conditions and specification B Tin shape C Cheapest product only D Smell. **Answer: A.** Explanation: performance requirements determine suitability.
18. Is all varnish automatically suitable for exterior exposure? A Yes B No; verify product suitability C Only if glossy D Only on pine. **Answer: B.** Explanation: product categories do not guarantee identical performance.
19. Why can incompatible layers fail? A Chemical/system incompatibility can cause adhesion or film problems B They always dry faster C They become structural D They remove dust. **Answer: A.** Explanation: coatings must form a compatible system.
20. Touch-dry means: A Always fully cured B One stage of drying, not necessarily service cure C Waterproof D Ready for any solvent. **Answer: B.** Explanation: cure can continue after touch dryness.

### Lesson 6 — Paint Systems for Timber
21. Why remove loose flaking paint before recoating? A Unsound coating is a poor foundation for the new system B Flakes improve adhesion C It changes colour D It increases thickness. **Answer: A.** Explanation: failures must be diagnosed and prepared.
22. A previously coated surface has unknown coating chemistry. Best action? A Apply any new coating B Follow approved compatibility/testing/referral procedure C Add oil D Ignore it. **Answer: B.** Explanation: incompatible systems can fail.
23. Is the thickest possible paint coat desirable? A Always B No; apply within specified product requirements C Only indoors D Only on doors. **Answer: B.** Explanation: excessive film can create defects.
24. What is spot priming? A Priming selected bare/repaired areas where the system permits B Painting only corners C Sanding primer D Applying topcoat first. **Answer: A.** Explanation: spot primer is localised preparation within an approved system.

### Lesson 7 — Application Methods, Drying, Curing & Work Environment
25. Why is spray finishing not chosen solely for speed? A It introduces atomisation/overspray and additional control requirements B It never works C It uses no coating D It cannot finish timber. **Answer: A.** Explanation: spray application requires competence and safety controls.
26. What should a worker do when a recoat window is missed? A Ignore it B Follow the product-required preparation/recoat process C Add solvent randomly D Increase pressure. **Answer: B.** Explanation: recoat requirements are product-specific.
27. Why protect a coated component while curing? A Pressure, dust and handling can damage a vulnerable film B Cure makes wood softer forever C It prevents measuring D It changes grain. **Answer: A.** Explanation: handling damage can occur before service cure.
28. What can excessive dust in a finishing area cause? A Dust nibs and contamination B Stronger coating C Faster cure D Better sheen. **Answer: A.** Explanation: airborne particles can settle in wet films.

### Lesson 8 — Finish Defects, Inspection, Care & Protection
29. A run is found in a coating. Best response? A Add another wet coat immediately B Allow the system to reach the appropriate repair stage, correct and recoat as instructed C Ignore every run D Scrape while wet regardless of product. **Answer: B.** Explanation: correction must respect coating readiness.
30. Why is peeling a symptom rather than a complete diagnosis? A Several causes can produce adhesion loss B It has no cause C It always means bad timber D It is only colour. **Answer: A.** Explanation: contamination, moisture or incompatibility may contribute.
31. What should guide cleaning advice for a finished item? A Strongest cleaner available B Finish manufacturer/system guidance C Any solvent D Water in every case. **Answer: B.** Explanation: chemical resistance differs between finishes.
32. Why record recurring finish defects? A To identify process trends and support corrective improvement B To hide them C To increase coating thickness D To avoid inspection. **Answer: A.** Explanation: records support traceability and quality control.
