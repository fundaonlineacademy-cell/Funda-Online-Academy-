# Carpentry Fundamentals — Module 8, Lesson 6
## Paint Systems for Timber

**Estimated study time:** 40–45 minutes

### Learning Outcomes
Learners should be able to explain the stages of a timber paint system; distinguish primer, undercoat/intermediate and finish functions where applicable; prepare new or previously coated timber appropriately; apply specification and manufacturer requirements; and recognise common causes of paint failure.

### Why This Lesson Matters
Paint can provide colour, protection and a uniform appearance, but performance depends on the entire system beneath the final coat. Painting over contamination, moisture, unstable old coating or unsuitable primer often creates expensive rework.

### Key Terms / Vocabulary
**Primer** — A preparatory coating intended to promote or provide specified substrate functions before subsequent coats.

**Undercoat** — An intermediate coating used in some paint systems to build, level or provide a suitable base for finishing coats.

**Topcoat** — The final exposed coating layer or layers providing specified appearance and service performance.

**Previously coated surface** — A substrate carrying an existing paint or coating system that must be assessed before recoating.

**Flaking** — Loss of coating adhesion resulting in pieces lifting or detaching from the substrate or underlying coat.

**Feather edge** — A smoothly tapered transition between an existing coating edge and adjacent prepared area.

**Spot priming** — Application of primer to selected bare or repaired areas when permitted by the specified system.

### Main Lesson Content
#### 1. Paint is a system
A professional paint job is not simply a final colour coat. Depending on product and specification, it can involve substrate preparation, filler, primer, intermediate/undercoat and topcoat. Modern products may combine functions, so do not assume every system requires separate products. Follow the specified manufacturer's system.

#### 2. New timber preparation
Inspect new timber for moisture condition, resinous areas, defects, contamination, machining marks, glue and damage. Complete repairs and sanding. Remove dust. Apply the specified primer or preparation coat according to instructions. End grain and exposed edges may require particular attention under the selected system.

#### 3. Previously coated timber requires diagnosis
Determine whether the existing coating is sound, compatible and sufficiently adhered. Look for peeling, flaking, blistering, cracking, chalking, contamination and moisture-related damage. Loose coating must not simply be buried beneath new paint. If the previous coating identity or compatibility is uncertain, use the approved testing/referral procedure.

#### 4. Repairs and transitions
Remove loose material using an appropriate safe method. Feather sound coating edges where required. Fill defects with compatible material, allow readiness, sand and clean. Prime bare areas or the full surface as required by the specified system. Do not create a visible ridge between old and repaired areas.

#### 5. Apply coats correctly
Mix paint as instructed. Use the specified brush, roller or spray method. Maintain coverage and film thickness according to product requirements. Avoid runs, sags and missed areas. Respect recoat conditions and intercoat preparation. A thicker-than-specified coat is not automatically better; excessive thickness can create drying and film problems.

#### 6. Protect surroundings and hardware
Remove or mask hardware where appropriate. Protect glass, floors and adjacent surfaces. Keep the work area organised. Good masking should not substitute for controlled application, and tape must be removed according to the product/process requirements to avoid damaging the finish.

#### 7. Environmental and safety control
Paint systems can contain hazardous substances and, depending on chemistry, flammable solvents. Use specified ventilation, PPE and ignition controls. Store and dispose of products, solvent-contaminated materials and waste according to product safety information and workplace/environmental requirements.

#### 8. Inspect the completed system
Check colour, coverage, sheen, runs, misses, contamination, edge quality and damage. Confirm that hardware and adjacent surfaces are clean. Do not place the item into service until the coating has reached the required handling/use condition.

### Worked Example / Demonstration
An old painted timber door has local flaking around the bottom edge. Instead of painting over it, the worker investigates the condition, removes unsound coating safely, checks the timber for moisture damage, prepares the transition to sound paint, repairs where approved, primes according to the selected system and applies the subsequent coats within product requirements. The cause is addressed rather than hidden.

### Practical Activity
Prepare a **Paint System Planning Sheet** for one new-timber sample and one previously coated sample. For each, record inspection findings, preparation, repairs, primer requirement, subsequent coats, recoat conditions, safety controls and final inspection criteria.

### Knowledge Check
1. Why is paint described as a system? A) Final colour alone determines performance B) Preparation and compatible coat stages work together C) Timber does not need preparation D) All paints are identical. **Answer: B.**
2. What should happen to loose flaking coating before repainting? A) It should simply be covered B) It should be assessed and appropriately removed/prepared C) It should be waxed D) Nothing. **Answer: B.**
3. Is a thicker coat always better? A) Yes B) No; follow specified application thickness/coverage C) Only outdoors D) Only on MDF. **Answer: B.**
4. Why might a previously coated surface require compatibility checking? A) New and old systems may not perform correctly together B) Paint changes timber species C) It eliminates sanding D) It changes dimensions. **Answer: A.**

### Feedback / Explanation
Paint performance depends on preparation, compatible layers and controlled application. Existing coating failures must be diagnosed rather than concealed. Manufacturer and project requirements determine the actual system.

### Key Takeaways
- Treat painting as a complete system.
- Inspect and prepare new timber.
- Diagnose old coating condition before recoating.
- Repair and feather transitions carefully.
- Follow specified film and recoat requirements.
- Protect hardware and surroundings.
- Control chemical and fire hazards.
- Inspect before handover or service.
