# Carpentry Premium Course — Module 8

## Surface Preparation, Finishing & Protection

**Module purpose:** Teach learners how to assess timber surfaces, prepare them systematically, select and apply appropriate finishing systems at an introductory workplace level, identify common finish defects, and protect completed work. The module treats finishing as a controlled process involving surface condition, product compatibility, environmental conditions, safe handling and quality inspection—not simply decoration.

### Lesson 1 — Surface Assessment & Preparation
Learners inspect a workpiece before finishing and identify machining marks, dents, scratches, glue contamination, raised fibres, gaps, sharp edges and other conditions that can affect the final result. They learn to plan preparation rather than automatically sanding every surface in the same way.

### Lesson 2 — Abrasives & Sanding Sequence
Learners interpret abrasive grit, distinguish cutting from polishing action, choose an appropriate sanding sequence, sand with regard to grain direction and surface condition, and recognise the risks of over-sanding, rounding edges or creating uneven surfaces. Dust control and suitable respiratory/eye protection must be integrated into the workflow.

### Lesson 3 — Filling, Repairing & Preparing Defects
Learners distinguish defects that can be prepared or filled from defects that require replacement, rework or escalation. Topics include compatible fillers, small surface repairs, glue squeeze-out, dents/scratches, drying, re-sanding and colour/finish implications. Cosmetic repair must never be presented as a substitute for correcting a structural or unsafe defect.

### Lesson 4 — Stains, Dyes & Colour Preparation
Learners understand the purpose of colouring timber, the difference between colouring and protective topcoating, sample testing, grain/absorption variation, blotching risk, compatibility and colour consistency. Product-specific mixing, application and drying instructions must come from the manufacturer rather than invented universal ratios.

### Lesson 5 — Clear Finishes & Protective Coatings
Learners compare broad finish families and purposes, such as oils, varnish-type coatings and other clear protective systems, without treating product names as interchangeable. Emphasis: intended use, compatibility, ventilation, PPE, manufacturer instructions, coat preparation and safe handling.

### Lesson 6 — Paint Systems for Timber
Learners study surface preparation for painted timber, suitable primer/undercoat/topcoat concepts, coating compatibility, edge/end-grain attention and inspection between coats. The lesson must avoid universal drying times or coverage rates because these vary by product and conditions.

### Lesson 7 — Application Methods, Drying/Curing & Environment
Learners compare brushing, rolling, wiping and other appropriate introductory application methods; control dust and contamination; understand wet film versus dry/cured coating; and recognise how temperature, humidity, airflow and product instructions affect the process. Spray application involving hazardous substances or specialist equipment is not taught as an unsupervised beginner task.

### Lesson 8 — Finish Defects, Inspection, Care & Protection
Learners identify likely causes of common problems such as runs/sags, brush marks, dust inclusions, uneven colour, poor adhesion, roughness and incomplete coverage. They practise diagnose-before-correct reasoning, final inspection, protection during transport/installation and basic aftercare communication.

### Entry 9 — Formative Assessment
- 15 MCQs randomly selected from a bank of at least 30.
- Covers Lessons 1–8.
- Pass mark: 70%.
- Include surface-diagnosis, abrasive-sequence, compatibility, environmental-control, safe-handling and defect-correction scenarios.
- Maximum formal attempts in production policy: 3.
- Feedback explains why the answer is appropriate.

### Entry 10 — Summative Assessment
- 25 MCQs randomly selected from a bank of at least 50.
- Pass mark: 70%.
- Uses broader job scenarios requiring learners to select a sensible preparation-to-handover finishing process.
- Must test judgement and application, not merely finish-name memorisation.
- Maximum formal attempts in production policy: 3.

## Premium lesson requirements
Each teaching lesson must contain lesson identification, estimated study time, 3–5 measurable learning outcomes, Why This Lesson Matters, 5–8 meaningful bold-defined key terms, at least 500 words of substantive main teaching content (normally 700–1,000), worked example/demonstration, practical activity, informal knowledge check, explanatory feedback, key takeaways and learner progression notes where supported.

## Visual learning plan
Use teaching visuals with captions: surface-defect examples, abrasive/grit progression graphic, grain-direction sanding diagram, before/after preparation example, finish sample board, coating-layer concept diagram, application-method comparison and finish-defect identification examples. Images must help the learner identify a material, defect, process or result rather than merely decorate the lesson.

## Accuracy, health and safety controls
- Product labels, safety data sheets and manufacturer instructions govern product-specific mixing, application, drying, curing, ventilation and PPE requirements.
- Never invent universal ratios, coat counts, drying times, coverage rates or exposure limits.
- Dust generation must be controlled and appropriate extraction/housekeeping/PPE principles reinforced.
- Solvent-containing or otherwise hazardous finishing products require suitable ventilation, ignition-source control and workplace chemical-safety procedures.
- Contaminated rags and finishing waste must be handled according to the product SDS/manufacturer instructions and workplace disposal/fire-safety procedures; do not provide a single disposal method for every finish chemistry.
- Learners must not be encouraged to spray hazardous coatings without suitable training, equipment, controls and authorisation.
- Assessment questions may test only knowledge taught or reasonably developed in Lessons 1–8.
