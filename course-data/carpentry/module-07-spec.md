# Carpentry Premium Course — Module 7

## Construction Methods & Practical Application

**Module purpose:** Develop the learner's ability to translate drawings and job briefs into safe, sequenced, accurate carpentry construction work. This module integrates measurement, material selection, jointing, fastening, assembly, installation, quality checks and workplace problem-solving without duplicating the detailed tool instruction taught earlier.

### Lesson 1 — Reading a Simple Job Brief & Drawing
Learners interpret purpose, dimensions, notes, symbols, views, material requirements and acceptance criteria from a simple carpentry brief or drawing. Emphasis: verify unclear information before cutting material.

### Lesson 2 — Planning Sequence, Materials & Cut Lists
Learners convert the brief into a logical work sequence and material/cut list, identify dependencies, plan safe work areas and reduce avoidable waste. Worked examples must distinguish finished dimensions from cutting allowances.

### Lesson 3 — Building Frames & Simple Structures
Learners study reference faces, component preparation, frame layout, joint/fixing selection, dry assembly, diagonal checks, squareness, clamping and final assembly. Examples should use a simple non-structural frame so learners do not infer professional structural design competence from this short course.

### Lesson 4 — Shelving & Basic Storage Construction
Learners plan and assemble a basic shelving/storage unit, considering load path at an introductory level, spacing, support, alignment, edge quality, fixing choice and final inspection. Heavy-load or building-integrated systems must defer to appropriate specifications and competent persons.

### Lesson 5 — Doors, Frames & Fittings: Basic Principles
Learners identify the parts of a basic door/frame arrangement and explain clearance, alignment, hinge positioning, hardware preparation and checking operation. The lesson must clearly distinguish introductory workshop practice from fire-door, security-door and regulated installation work.

### Lesson 6 — Installing Components & Fixing to Substrates Safely
Learners plan positioning, check level/plumb/alignment, identify substrate uncertainty, select fixings according to manufacturer data and job requirements, and recognise when hidden electrical/plumbing services or uncertain substrate capacity require escalation rather than drilling or fixing blindly.

### Lesson 7 — On-site Adjustments & Problem Solving
Learners diagnose common fit and alignment problems using measurement and evidence before reworking components. Topics include cumulative error, out-of-square openings, minor fitting adjustments, protecting finished surfaces, controlling rework and knowing when not to improvise.

### Lesson 8 — Practical Build Workflow: Set-out to Handover
Learners integrate the module into a complete workflow: confirm brief, risk check, prepare materials, set out, cut/prepare, dry assemble, assemble, inspect, correct, clean and hand over. The activity should produce a portfolio-ready planning/inspection record even where a learner cannot access a workshop.

### Entry 9 — Formative Assessment
- 15 MCQs randomly selected from a bank of at least 30.
- Covers Lessons 1–8 and their taught outcomes.
- Pass mark: 70%.
- Questions must include sequencing, drawing interpretation, cut-list reasoning, alignment, installation judgement, defect diagnosis and safe escalation scenarios.
- Maximum formal attempts in production policy: 3.
- Feedback should explain the reasoning, not merely reveal the correct option.

### Entry 10 — Summative Assessment
- 25 MCQs randomly selected from a bank of at least 50.
- Pass mark: 70%.
- Broader and more applied than the formative assessment.
- Scenario questions must test whether the learner can choose a safe and technically sensible construction workflow from brief to handover.
- Maximum formal attempts in production policy: 3.

## Premium lesson requirements
Every teaching lesson must contain: identification, estimated study time, 3–5 measurable outcomes, Why This Lesson Matters, 5–8 meaningful bold-defined terms, at least 500 words of substantive main teaching content (normally 700–1,000), worked example/demonstration, practical activity, informal knowledge check, explanatory feedback, key takeaways and progression notes where supported.

## Visual learning plan
Use relevant instructional visuals with meaningful captions: simple dimensioned drawing/job brief, annotated cut list, frame squareness/diagonal-check diagram, shelving component layout, basic door/frame terminology diagram, level/plumb/alignment visual, defect/fit diagnosis examples, and end-to-end workflow graphic. Visuals must teach identification or process rather than decorate the page.

## Accuracy and safety controls
- Never imply that a short online lesson authorises a learner to perform regulated, structural, electrical-adjacent or otherwise specialist work beyond their competence.
- Fixing selection must depend on substrate, load, manufacturer instructions and job specification; do not present a universal anchor/fixing rule.
- Before drilling/cutting into existing construction, hidden-service risk must be considered and workplace procedures followed.
- Machine and power-tool use must remain subject to training, guarding, pre-use checks, manufacturer instructions and workplace procedures established in earlier modules.
- Structural dimensions, load capacities and installation tolerances must not be invented; where relevant they come from approved drawings/specifications/manufacturer requirements.
- Assessment questions may only test knowledge taught or reasonably developed in Lessons 1–8.
