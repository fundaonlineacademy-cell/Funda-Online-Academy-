# Module 6 — Formative Assessment Bank
## Carpentry Joints & Assembly Techniques

**Delivery:** 15 randomized MCQs per attempt | **Bank:** 32 | **Pass:** 70% | **Maximum attempts:** 3 | Passing unlocks Module 6 Summative Assessment.

Questions are traceable to Lessons 1–8, four questions per lesson. Randomisation should preserve broad lesson coverage.

### Lesson 1 — Joint Selection & Principles
1. An approved drawing specifies a mortise-and-tenon joint. What should a carpenter do first? A Substitute screws B Follow the drawing/specification C Use the fastest joint D Ask a customer to choose. **Answer: B.** Explanation: specified joinery controls unless an approved change is made.
2. What is the main purpose of dry assembly? A Apply finish B Check fit and geometry before permanent fixing C Increase moisture D Sharpen tools. **Answer: B.** Explanation: dry assembly exposes fit/orientation/dimensional errors while correction is possible.
3. A joint requires destructive force to close. Best action? A Hammer harder B Diagnose the interference C Add more glue D Add nails. **Answer: B.** Explanation: excessive force can split or distort components.
4. Why can a fixing method differ between solid timber and board products? A Board products have no dimensions B Material and edge behaviour differ C Solid timber needs no fixings D Boards cannot be drilled. **Answer: B.** Explanation: substrate behaviour affects fastener performance.

### Lesson 2 — Butt Joints & Reinforcement
5. A basic butt joint has limited mechanical interlock. Which is an appropriate response when the design requires reinforcement? A Ignore it B Use the specified reinforcement C Paint the joint D Reduce contact. **Answer: B.** Explanation: screws, nails, dowels, biscuits or other systems may be specified.
6. Why may a pilot hole be used? A To colour timber B Guide a screw and reduce splitting risk where required C Replace a screw D Increase board thickness. **Answer: B.**
7. Matching dowel holes are offset. What is the best response? A Enlarge randomly B Diagnose and correct set-out C Force assembly D Hide gap with glue. **Answer: B.**
8. What should be checked after clamping a butt-jointed box? A Only colour B Flushness, dimensions and square C Supplier logo D Tool price. **Answer: B.**

### Lesson 3 — Lap & Halving
9. What is removed in a halving joint? A Finish only B Controlled material so members overlap C All end grain D Fasteners. **Answer: B.**
10. Why mark waste clearly? A Decoration B Prevent cutting the retained side C Increase strength automatically D Replace a square. **Answer: B.**
11. A cross-halving recess has a high spot. Best fitting method? A Deepen everything B Locate and pare the high spot gradually C Hammer joint closed D Fill with adhesive. **Answer: B.**
12. Why verify the first joint from a repeated jig setup? A Catch systematic error before batch work B Make tools blunt C Avoid measurements D Change species. **Answer: A.**

### Lesson 4 — Housing, Dado & Rebate
13. A stopped housing is: A a failed groove B a housing ending before an exposed edge C a nail recess D a finish defect. **Answer: B.**
14. Why is excessive housing depth undesirable? A It removes supporting material B It changes timber species C It eliminates grain D It makes shelves longer. **Answer: A.**
15. Before routing several identical housings, the best quality control is: A Remove guard B Verify setup with first/test cut C Ignore board thickness D Cut all at once. **Answer: B.**
16. A shelf will not seat fully in its housing. First action? A Force it B Inspect width, depth, debris and fit C Add a longer screw D Sand the whole cabinet. **Answer: B.**

### Lesson 5 — Mortise-and-Tenon
17. The projecting part is the: A mortise B tenon C housing D rebate. **Answer: B.**
18. Why mark mortise and tenon from consistent reference faces? A Control alignment B Increase moisture C Avoid drawings D Make glue dry faster. **Answer: A.**
19. One shoulder stays open in a dry fit. Best action? A Fill with glue B Diagnose shoulder/cheek/mortise alignment C Add paint D Over-clamp. **Answer: B.**
20. A tenon is extremely tight. Why avoid heavy hammering? A It may split the mortised component B It changes colour C It removes grain D It sharpens tools. **Answer: A.**

### Lesson 6 — Dowel, Biscuit & Mechanical Joining
21. Which must match accurately in a dowel joint? A Hole position and depth B Paint colour only C Timber price D Workshop temperature only. **Answer: A.**
22. Biscuit slots should be registered consistently to: A control mating-face alignment B enlarge panels C remove all marking D increase moisture. **Answer: A.**
23. Exact drilling for a proprietary connector should come from: A Guessing B Manufacturer/approved production data C Another unrelated fitting D Visual estimate. **Answer: B.**
24. Why test a jig? A A wrong jig can repeat an error B Jigs need no verification C It replaces drawings D It makes every board identical. **Answer: A.**

### Lesson 7 — Adhesives & Fixings
25. Why is there no universal adhesive clamp time? A Product systems and conditions differ B Clamps are decorative C Timber never bonds D Time is not measurable. **Answer: A.**
26. A screw begins splitting an edge. Best action? A Continue driving B Stop and diagnose placement/preparation/selection C Add a second screw beside it D Hide split. **Answer: B.**
27. Which statement about clamp pressure is correct? A Maximum pressure is always best B Controlled pressure should close the joint without distortion C Pressure is never needed D Pressure replaces fit. **Answer: B.**
28. Structural/proprietary anchor capacity should be taken from: A Appearance B Approved design/manufacturer information C Any old project D Fastener colour. **Answer: B.**

### Lesson 8 — Dry Assembly & Quality
29. A rectangular frame has correct side lengths but unequal diagonals. What does this suggest? A Geometry requires adjustment/checking B Timber species is wrong C Adhesive is cured D Nails are too shiny. **Answer: A.**
30. Can equal diagonals alone prove every aspect of an assembly is correct? A Yes B No. **Answer: B.** Explanation: side dimensions, plane, joint closure and other criteria also matter.
31. What can poor clamp placement cause? A Distortion/twist B Automatic accuracy C Stronger species D Longer rails. **Answer: A.**
32. When should final assembly geometry be rechecked? A During clamping while correction remains possible and again at inspection B Never C Only after painting D Only before cutting. **Answer: A.**
