# Module 6 — Formative Assessment Bank
## Carpentry Joints & Assembly Techniques

**Delivery:** 15 randomized MCQs per attempt | **Bank:** 32 | **Pass:** 70% | **Maximum attempts:** 3 | Passing unlocks Module 6 Summative Assessment.

Questions are traceable to Lessons 1–8, four questions per lesson. Every item has four options A–D, a keyed answer and remediation explanation.

### Lesson 1 — Joint Selection & Principles
1. An approved drawing specifies a mortise-and-tenon joint. What should a carpenter do first? A Substitute screws B Follow the drawing/specification C Use the fastest joint D Ask a customer to choose. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The approved design controls unless an authorised change is made.
2. What is the main purpose of dry assembly? A Apply finish B Check fit and geometry before permanent fixing C Increase moisture D Sharpen tools. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Dry assembly exposes fit, orientation and dimensional errors while they can still be corrected.
3. A joint requires destructive force to close. Best action? A Hammer harder B Diagnose the interference C Add more glue D Add nails. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Excessive force can split, bruise or distort components and does not correct inaccurate fit.
4. Why can a fixing method differ between solid timber and board products? A Board products have no dimensions B Material and edge behaviour differ C Solid timber needs no fixings D Boards cannot be drilled. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Substrate structure and edge behaviour influence fastener holding and installation method.

### Lesson 2 — Butt Joints & Reinforcement
5. A basic butt joint has limited mechanical interlock. Which is appropriate when the design requires reinforcement? A Ignore it B Use the specified reinforcement C Paint the joint D Reduce contact. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Reinforcement must suit the approved design and material rather than being improvised.
6. Why may a pilot hole be used? A To colour timber B Guide a screw and reduce splitting risk where required C Replace a screw D Increase board thickness. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Correct pilot preparation can improve screw guidance and reduce splitting in suitable applications.
7. Matching dowel holes are offset. What is the best response? A Enlarge randomly B Diagnose and correct set-out C Force assembly D Hide the gap with glue. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Misalignment is a set-out or jig problem that should be corrected before permanent assembly.
8. What should be checked after clamping a butt-jointed box? A Only colour B Flushness, dimensions and square C Supplier logo D Tool price. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Joint closure is only one quality criterion; overall geometry and dimensions must also comply.

### Lesson 3 — Lap & Halving
9. What is removed in a halving joint? A Finish only B Controlled material so members overlap C All end grain D Fasteners. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The joint is formed by removing a specified portion so members overlap to the required geometry.
10. Why mark waste clearly? A Decoration B Prevent cutting the retained side C Increase strength automatically D Replace a square. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Clear waste marking reduces the risk of removing material from the component portion that must remain.
11. A cross-halving recess has a high spot. Best fitting method? A Deepen everything B Locate and pare the high spot gradually C Hammer joint closed D Fill with adhesive. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Controlled local correction preserves fit and avoids unnecessarily weakening the whole recess.
12. Why verify the first joint from a repeated jig setup? A Catch systematic error before batch work B Make tools blunt C Avoid measurements D Change species. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** A wrong jig or stop can repeat the same error across many components.

### Lesson 4 — Housing, Dado & Rebate
13. A stopped housing is: A A failed groove B A housing ending before an exposed edge C A nail recess D A finish defect. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** A stopped housing terminates before the component edge so the recess is not visible through that edge.
14. Why is excessive housing depth undesirable? A It removes supporting material B It changes timber species C It eliminates grain D It makes shelves longer. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Removing too much material can unnecessarily reduce the remaining section and alter the designed joint.
15. Before routing several identical housings, the best quality control is: A Remove guard B Verify setup with a first/test cut C Ignore board thickness D Cut all at once. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** A verified first-off confirms position, width and depth before an error is repeated.
16. A shelf will not seat fully in its housing. First action? A Force it B Inspect width, depth, debris and fit C Add a longer screw D Sand the whole cabinet. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The cause should be identified before material is removed or force is applied.

### Lesson 5 — Mortise-and-Tenon
17. The projecting part is the: A Mortise B Tenon C Housing D Rebate. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The tenon is the projecting member that fits into the mortise.
18. Why mark mortise and tenon from consistent reference faces? A Control alignment B Increase moisture C Avoid drawings D Make glue dry faster. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Consistent references help mating components align to the intended faces and dimensions.
19. One shoulder stays open in a dry fit. Best action? A Fill with glue B Diagnose shoulder, cheek or mortise alignment C Add paint D Over-clamp. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** An open shoulder indicates a fit or alignment issue that should be identified before bonding.
20. A tenon is extremely tight. Why avoid heavy hammering? A It may split the mortised component B It changes colour C It removes grain D It sharpens tools. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Excessive interference can damage fibres, split the member or distort the assembly.

### Lesson 6 — Dowel, Biscuit & Mechanical Joining
21. Which must match accurately in a dowel joint? A Hole position and depth B Paint colour only C Timber price D Workshop temperature only. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Dowel holes must register correctly so the joint closes and aligns as intended.
22. Biscuit slots should be registered consistently to: A Control mating-face alignment B Enlarge panels C Remove all marking D Increase moisture. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Consistent registration helps mating faces line up during assembly.
23. Exact drilling for a proprietary connector should come from: A Guessing B Manufacturer or approved production data C Another unrelated fitting D Visual estimate. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Proprietary systems depend on controlled dimensions supplied by the approved source.
24. Why test a jig? A A wrong jig can repeat an error B Jigs need no verification C It replaces drawings D It makes every board identical. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Jig verification protects a batch from systematic positioning errors.

### Lesson 7 — Adhesives & Fixings
25. Why is there no universal adhesive clamp time? A Product systems and conditions differ B Clamps are decorative C Timber never bonds D Time is not measurable. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Open time, clamp time and cure depend on the actual adhesive, substrate and conditions.
26. A screw begins splitting an edge. Best action? A Continue driving B Stop and diagnose placement, preparation and selection C Add a second screw beside it D Hide the split. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Continuing can worsen the defect; the cause must be corrected first.
27. Which statement about clamp pressure is correct? A Maximum pressure is always best B Controlled pressure should close the joint without distortion C Pressure is never needed D Pressure replaces fit. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Clamping should bring mating surfaces together without racking, crushing or bowing the work.
28. Structural or proprietary anchor capacity should be taken from: A Appearance B Approved design or manufacturer information C Any old project D Fastener colour. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Capacity depends on the complete fixing system, substrate and specified installation conditions.

### Lesson 8 — Dry Assembly & Quality
29. A rectangular frame has correct side lengths but unequal diagonals. What does this suggest? A Geometry requires adjustment/checking B Timber species is wrong C Adhesive is cured D Nails are too shiny. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Unequal diagonals can indicate racking or lack of square in a rectangular assembly.
30. Can equal diagonals alone prove every aspect of an assembly is correct? A Yes, diagonals replace all other checks B No, dimensions, plane, joint closure and other criteria also matter C Yes, if the timber is hardwood D Yes, if clamps are tight. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Equal diagonals are useful for checking rectangular geometry, but they do not prove overall dimensions, twist, joint closure or specification compliance.
31. What can poor clamp placement cause? A Distortion or twist B Automatic accuracy C Stronger species D Longer rails. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Uneven or badly positioned pressure can rack, bow or twist an assembly.
32. When should final assembly geometry be rechecked? A During clamping while correction remains possible and again at inspection B Never C Only after painting D Only before cutting. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Geometry can shift during assembly, so it should be verified while adjustable and again before acceptance.
