## Lesson 1 — Wood Structure, Grain & Basic Properties

### Q1
A carpenter is preparing a visible timber component. Why should grain direction be considered before machining?

A. Grain direction determines the legal selling price of every board.
B. Grain direction can influence cutting behaviour, surface quality and the risk of tear-out.
C. Grain direction removes the need to inspect knots.
D. Grain direction guarantees that timber will not move with moisture changes.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Wood has directional structure. Grain orientation can affect how a cutting edge interacts with the fibres and therefore influences machining behaviour and surface quality.

**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — grain direction and machining behaviour.

### Q2
Which statement best describes heartwood and sapwood?

A. They are names for manufactured-board layers.
B. They are regions of a tree stem that can differ in function and characteristics.
C. Heartwood is always structurally acceptable and sapwood is always defective.
D. Sapwood is another name for end grain.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Heartwood and sapwood are natural regions within the tree stem. Their presence alone does not provide a universal accept/reject decision; suitability depends on species, condition and specification.

**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — wood structure.

### Q3
A learner notices a knot close to the intended joint location. What is the best first response?

A. Ignore it because knots are only visual features.
B. Paint over it before cutting.
C. Assess its position and condition against the component's purpose and specification before proceeding.
D. Assume every knot makes the entire board unusable.

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** A knot can influence machining, appearance and performance depending on its size, condition, position and the job requirements. It should be assessed rather than automatically ignored or universally rejected.

**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — natural features, inspection and material suitability.

### Q4
Why can end grain behave differently from a long-grain face during cutting or finishing?

A. The wood fibres are exposed in a different orientation.
B. End grain contains no wood fibres.
C. End grain is always manufactured material.
D. Long grain cannot absorb finishes.

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** End grain exposes fibre ends rather than predominantly longitudinal fibre surfaces, which can change cutting and absorption behaviour.

**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — grain orientation and basic properties.

## Lesson 2 — Softwoods, Hardwoods & Common Applications

### Q5
Which statement about hardwoods and softwoods is correct?

A. Every hardwood is physically harder than every softwood.
B. The terms are botanical classifications and do not by themselves guarantee actual hardness.
C. Softwoods cannot be used in carpentry.
D. Hardwoods never contain defects.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Hardwood and softwood are botanical groupings. Actual density, hardness, durability and workability vary by species and product.

**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — botanical classification.

### Q6
A job specification calls for a particular timber species and grade. A cheaper species is available in the workshop. What should the carpenter do?

A. Substitute it automatically if it looks similar.
B. Use the cheaper species and hide the change during finishing.
C. Follow the specification or obtain authorised approval for any proposed substitution.
D. Choose whichever board is easiest to cut.

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Material selection must satisfy controlled job requirements. Cost alone does not authorise substitution.

**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — timber selection and specification.

### Q7
Which combination gives the strongest basis for selecting a timber for a job?

A. Colour alone
B. Botanical group alone
C. Intended use, service conditions, required properties, specification, condition and workability
D. The shortest board in the rack

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Professional selection considers the complete job requirement rather than one characteristic.

**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — practical timber selection.

### Q8
Why should a carpenter avoid saying that one timber species is suitable for every application?

A. Timber cannot be measured.
B. Suitability depends on job requirements, properties, condition, treatment, service environment and specification.
C. All species have identical properties.
D. Only manufactured boards can be specified.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Material suitability is application-specific. A material appropriate for one component or environment may be unsuitable for another.

**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — applications and selection limits.

## Lesson 3 — Moisture Content, Seasoning & Timber Movement

### Q9
What does moisture content describe in timber?

A. The amount of metal contained in a board
B. The amount of moisture present in the wood relative to its dry wood mass under the applicable measurement method
C. The number of growth rings
D. The timber's selling price

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Moisture content is a measure of moisture present in wood. It is important because moisture change is connected to dimensional movement and processing behaviour.

**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — moisture content.

### Q10
Why is timber seasoned or dried in a controlled way before many carpentry applications?

A. To make every species the same colour
B. To reduce and control moisture so the material is more suitable for processing and service
C. To remove all grain direction
D. To turn solid timber into plywood

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Drying reduces moisture and helps prepare timber for intended processing and use. The required condition depends on the job and applicable specification.

**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — seasoning.

### Q11
A board is moved from one environment to another and later changes dimension. Which wood behaviour best explains this possibility?

A. Timber is hygroscopic and can exchange moisture with the surrounding environment.
B. Timber becomes metal after installation.
C. Every board permanently stops moving once planed.
D. Only painted timber changes dimension.

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Wood can gain or lose moisture in response to environmental conditions, which can lead to swelling or shrinkage.

**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — hygroscopic behaviour and timber movement.

### Q12
A learner asks for the one universal moisture-content percentage that is correct for every carpentry project. What is the best answer?

A. One percentage applies to every species, product and environment.
B. The correct requirement must be taken from the applicable material, project, workplace or technical specification rather than invented as a universal value.
C. Moisture content is never relevant to carpentry.
D. The wettest board is always best.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Appropriate moisture condition depends on material, intended service environment and controlled requirements. The course deliberately avoids inventing one universal percentage.

**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — moisture verification and specification.

## Lesson 4 — Timber Defects & Grading Awareness

### Q13
Which of the following is a form of timber distortion?

A. Twist
B. Datum
C. Kerf
D. Countersink

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Twist is a type of warp/distortion in which the board does not remain in one flat plane.

**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — timber defects.

### Q14
A board has a pronounced split extending from one end into the intended component area. What is the professional response?

A. Ignore it because splits cannot affect a component.
B. Assess the defect against the required component, grade/specification and intended use before deciding whether the material can be used or must be rejected/replanned.
C. Cover the split with a pencil line.
D. Always use the split as the joint centre.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Defects require assessment in context. Their position, extent and the component requirement determine the appropriate decision.

**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — inspection and acceptance/rejection.

### Q15
What is wane?

A. A missing or rounded portion along an edge or corner associated with the original log surface
B. A manufactured-board adhesive
C. A type of screwdriver
D. A moisture meter setting

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Wane is a timber feature where part of the normal rectangular edge/corner is absent, often leaving bark or the original log contour.

**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — timber defects and grading features.

### Q16
Why does this course distinguish defect awareness from formal structural grading?

A. Because learners should assign any structural grade they want.
B. Because recognising visible features does not automatically qualify a person to certify structural grade; formal grading follows applicable standards and authorised systems.
C. Because structural timber never has visible features.
D. Because grading only means measuring board length.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Learners need to recognise features and make safe workplace decisions within their competence, while formal grading and certification must follow the applicable grading system and authorised requirements.

**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — grading awareness and professional boundaries.

## Lesson 5 — Manufactured Boards: Plywood, MDF, Particleboard & OSB

### Q17
Which description best matches plywood?

A. A panel made from layers of wood veneer bonded together, commonly with adjacent layers oriented differently
B. A solid log with bark removed
C. A board made only from metal fibres
D. A timber board containing no adhesive or layers

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Plywood is a manufactured panel built from bonded veneer layers. Its construction influences its properties and applications.

**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — plywood construction.

### Q18
A project drawing specifies a particular moisture-resistant or exterior-rated board product. What should the learner do?

A. Assume every board with the same colour has the same rating.
B. Confirm the product identification/specification and use material that meets the required rating.
C. Use ordinary particleboard automatically.
D. Ignore the service environment.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Manufactured boards vary by product and rating. Selection must follow the approved specification and intended service conditions.

**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — board selection and moisture considerations.

### Q19
Why can exposed edges of some manufactured boards require special attention during finishing?

A. Their edge structure can differ from the factory face and may absorb finish or reveal internal material differently.
B. Manufactured boards have no edges.
C. Every edge is already waterproof.
D. Edge preparation is illegal.

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** MDF, particleboard, plywood and OSB have different edge structures. Edge treatment must suit the particular product and finish system.

**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — face/edge characteristics and finishing.

### Q20
What is the best approach when cutting or sanding a manufactured wood board that produces dust?

A. Treat dust as harmless because the material is manufactured.
B. Apply suitable source dust controls, safe work procedures and task-appropriate PPE as required by the workplace and material information.
C. Blow dust into another person's work area.
D. Disable extraction because it makes noise.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Cutting and sanding wood and wood-composite materials can generate airborne dust. Professional practice uses effective controls and follows workplace/material safety requirements.

**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — dust control and safe processing.

## Lesson 6 — Selecting Materials for Purpose & Service Conditions

### Q21
A component will be exposed to conditions different from those assumed in the original material choice. What should happen before manufacture continues?

A. Ignore the change.
B. Reassess material suitability against the actual service conditions and controlled specification, and escalate any required change for approval.
C. Select material only by colour.
D. Reduce all component dimensions.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Service conditions are central to material suitability. A changed environment can change the material requirement.

**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — service conditions and material selection.

### Q22
Which is the best example of whole-job cost thinking?

A. Buying the lowest-priced material without considering waste or rework
B. Considering purchase price together with yield, machining, finishing, defects, waste, durability requirements and rework risk
C. Ignoring the cutting list
D. Choosing the heaviest board regardless of specification

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Economical selection considers the full effect of a material choice, not only its initial purchase price.

**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — cost and waste considerations.

### Q23
A board meets the species requirement but has damage in the exact area needed for a critical visible component. What is the best decision?

A. Use it automatically because species is the only requirement.
B. Inspect whether the usable portion can meet the component's quality and specification; otherwise reallocate, replace or report as appropriate.
C. Hide the damage from quality control.
D. Make the component smaller without approval.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Correct species does not remove the need to inspect actual stock condition and usable yield.

**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — stock inspection and purpose-based selection.

### Q24
When is a material substitution professionally acceptable?

A. Whenever the substitute is cheaper
B. Whenever the carpenter personally prefers it
C. When it satisfies the applicable requirements and the substitution is approved through the required job/workplace process
D. Whenever the original material is farther away in the store

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Substitution must be controlled. The alternative needs to meet the job requirements and be authorised where approval is required.

**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — controlled substitution.

## Lesson 7 — Timber Storage, Handling & Conditioning

### Q25
Why should long timber generally be supported adequately during storage?

A. To encourage distortion
B. To reduce the risk of sagging, instability and avoidable damage
C. To make moisture irrelevant
D. To remove all knots

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Stable, adequate support helps protect stock condition and makes storage safer.

**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — stable timber storage.

### Q26
What is the main purpose of stickers/spacers in a correctly planned timber stack where airflow is required?

A. To create controlled separation between layers and support airflow while maintaining suitable support
B. To replace every structural support
C. To permanently glue boards together
D. To identify hardwood botanically

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Properly positioned stickers can separate layers and permit airflow. They must be used as part of a stable, correctly supported stack.

**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — stickered stacking and airflow.

### Q27
A heavy sheet must be moved through a congested workshop. What is the best first approach?

A. Carry it immediately even if the route is blocked.
B. Plan the route and handling method, clear hazards and obtain suitable assistance or handling equipment where needed.
C. Drag it across electrical leads.
D. Balance it upright in a doorway while deciding what to do.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Safe material handling begins with planning the load, route, space and assistance/equipment needed rather than improvising during the lift.

**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — safe handling.

### Q28
Why might timber need time to condition after being moved into a different work or service environment?

A. To allow its moisture condition to move toward the new environment before critical processing or installation where required
B. To change hardwood into softwood
C. To remove every natural defect
D. To make measurement unnecessary

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Conditioning can help timber adjust toward the environment in which it will be processed or used. The required procedure depends on the job/material specification.

**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — conditioning.

## Lesson 8 — Sustainable Sourcing, Waste Reduction & Material Planning

### Q29
What is the difference between a cutting list and a cutting plan?

A. There is never any difference.
B. A cutting list identifies required components and dimensions, while a cutting plan organises how those components may be obtained efficiently from available stock.
C. A cutting list is only for metal.
D. A cutting plan replaces the approved drawing.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** The cutting list records what is required; the cutting plan helps organise efficient material use while still meeting the controlled requirements.

**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — cutting lists and cutting plans.

### Q30
A proposed cutting layout saves material but would place a serious defect inside a required component. What should the learner do?

A. Use the layout because waste reduction is always more important than quality.
B. Reject or revise the layout so specification, quality and safety are maintained while seeking the best practical yield.
C. Hide the defect with a label.
D. Change the drawing without approval.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Waste reduction must never override safety, component quality or specification. Efficient planning works within those controls.

**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — material yield and responsible waste reduction.

### Q31
Which practice best supports responsible material traceability?

A. Removing all product identification before checking it
B. Retaining or recording relevant supplier, product, certification or specification information required by the job or workplace
C. Guessing the timber source from colour
D. Mixing unidentified stock and assuming it is equivalent

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Traceability depends on reliable information and records, not visual guessing. Required sourcing or certification claims should be supported by appropriate evidence.

**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — responsible sourcing and traceability.

### Q32
A workshop has reusable offcuts from previous jobs. What is the best sustainability and cost-control practice?

A. Throw every offcut away immediately.
B. Keep every scrap indefinitely regardless of condition or usefulness.
C. Identify, store and reuse suitable offcuts where they meet the new job requirements, while disposing or recycling unsuitable waste through approved processes.
D. Use offcuts even when they fail the specification.

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Responsible waste reduction includes retaining genuinely usable material without compromising the new job's specification, quality or safety.

**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — reusable offcuts, waste reduction and material planning.
