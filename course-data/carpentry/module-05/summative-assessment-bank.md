## Lesson 1 — Wood Structure, Grain & Basic Properties

### Q1
A carpenter must plane a visible solid-timber edge. Why should grain direction be considered before choosing the working direction?
A. Grain direction determines the legal ownership of the timber.
B. Working against difficult grain can increase tearing and reduce surface quality.
C. Grain direction determines the exact moisture content without measurement.
D. Grain direction makes all species equally easy to machine.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Grain orientation influences cutting behaviour and surface quality. A professional carpenter observes the material and adapts the process rather than assuming every board machines identically.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q2
A board contains a knot exactly where a narrow, highly stressed component must be cut. What is the best first response?
A. Ignore the knot because knots are always decorative only.
B. Paint over the knot before cutting.
C. Assess the knot, component requirement and specification before deciding whether that section is suitable.
D. Cut directly through it because every knot has the same effect.

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Natural features must be assessed in relation to location, size, condition and the intended component. Their significance is application-dependent.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q3
Which statement best describes heartwood and sapwood?
A. They are names for plywood face grades.
B. They are regions within a tree stem with different biological roles and potentially different characteristics.
C. Heartwood is always structurally perfect and sapwood is always defective.
D. Sapwood exists only in softwoods.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Heartwood and sapwood are parts of the wood structure. Their properties vary by species and condition, so neither should be assigned universal performance claims.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q4
Two boards of the same species behave differently during machining. Which explanation is most professionally reasonable?
A. Same species guarantees identical grain and defects.
B. Individual boards can differ in grain orientation, knots, growth characteristics, moisture condition and defects.
C. The board that machines poorly must be counterfeit.
D. Species has no relationship to material behaviour.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Timber is a natural material. Variation within a species is normal and should be inspected rather than ignored.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q5
Why is end grain often treated differently from a long-grain face during cutting or finishing?
A. End grain exposes the ends of wood fibres and can respond differently to tools and finishes.
B. End grain is manufactured board.
C. End grain cannot absorb any liquid.
D. End grain has no relationship to fibre orientation.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Fibre orientation changes the way a surface cuts, absorbs and finishes. Recognising end grain supports better process selection and quality control.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q6
A learner chooses a board only because its colour is attractive. What important professional step is missing?
A. Checking whether the material's condition and properties suit the job specification and service requirements.
B. Immediately applying a darker stain.
C. Cutting all components before inspecting the board.
D. Assuming appearance proves structural suitability.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Appearance can matter, but material selection must also consider condition, properties, specification and intended use.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q7
What is the strongest reason for learning basic wood structure in practical carpentry?
A. To identify every tree botanically from memory.
B. To predict material behaviour more intelligently during selection, machining, joining and finishing.
C. To eliminate the need for drawings.
D. To avoid inspecting timber before use.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Understanding structure helps the carpenter connect grain, growth features and material condition with practical behaviour and quality.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

## Lesson 2 — Softwoods, Hardwoods & Common Applications

### Q8
Which statement about hardwood and softwood is correct?
A. Every hardwood is physically harder than every softwood.
B. The terms are botanical groupings and do not guarantee actual hardness.
C. Softwood means defective timber.
D. Hardwood means timber can be used outdoors without verification.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Hardwood and softwood classifications are based primarily on botanical origin, not a universal hardness ranking.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q9
A supplier offers South African pine for a project that specifies a particular structural grade. What should the carpenter verify?
A. Only the species name.
B. Only the colour.
C. The required grade and other specified properties or treatment, not merely the species.
D. Nothing, because all pine has identical performance.

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Species identity does not replace project requirements for grade, treatment, dimensions, condition or certification where applicable.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q10
A clear-finished cabinet has highly visible doors and concealed internal support blocks. What is a sensible material-allocation principle?
A. Allocate suitable visually consistent stock to prominent surfaces while ensuring every component still meets its technical requirement.
B. Put the most defective stock into load-bearing parts because they are hidden.
C. Ignore grain and appearance everywhere.
D. Use unsuitable timber in concealed positions to reduce cost.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Professional material allocation balances appearance, technical suitability and waste reduction without lowering required quality.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q11
A hardwood board is difficult to machine cleanly. What is the best response?
A. Force the tool harder.
B. Diagnose grain, tool condition, setup and process before continuing.
C. Assume all hardwood must produce a rough surface.
D. Add water to the cutting edge.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Workability depends on material and process. Forcing a poor setup can damage the workpiece and create safety risks.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q12
Why is the statement “hardwood is always best for furniture” too simplistic?
A. Furniture can never be made from solid timber.
B. Material suitability depends on design, specification, properties, appearance, service conditions, workability, availability and cost.
C. Hardwood has no grain.
D. Softwoods cannot be finished.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Material selection is a multi-factor decision. No broad timber group is automatically best for every application.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q13
Two boards are labelled as the same species, but one is straight and sound while the other is distorted and damaged. Which should guide selection?
A. The label alone.
B. Actual condition plus the job specification.
C. The heavier board automatically.
D. The board closest to the workshop door.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Stock must be inspected individually. Species identification alone cannot establish suitability.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

## Lesson 3 — Moisture Content, Seasoning & Timber Movement

### Q14
Why can timber dimensions change after it moves between environments?
A. Wood can exchange moisture with surrounding air and may shrink or swell as its moisture condition changes.
B. Timber permanently stops interacting with moisture after sawing.
C. Paint always changes timber length by a fixed amount.
D. Only manufactured boards respond to humidity.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Wood is hygroscopic. Its moisture condition can change with the surrounding environment, contributing to dimensional movement.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q15
Boards arrive from damp storage for an indoor cabinet job. The job specification requires moisture verification before manufacture. What is the best response?
A. Machine and assemble immediately to save time.
B. Allow appropriate conditioning and verify moisture using the approved method before proceeding.
C. Dry the boards with an open flame.
D. Assume that a dry-looking surface proves compliance.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Where the specification requires moisture verification, conditioning and an approved measurement method should precede critical manufacture.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q16
Which statement about “seasoned timber” is most accurate?
A. Seasoned timber can never absorb moisture again.
B. Seasoning reduces moisture under controlled or managed conditions, but timber can later gain or lose moisture as conditions change.
C. Seasoning means coating timber with varnish.
D. Seasoned timber cannot move dimensionally.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Seasoning does not make timber permanently moisture-inert. Subsequent environmental exposure still matters.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q17
Why should a training course avoid teaching one moisture-content percentage as universally correct for every carpentry job?
A. Moisture has no importance in carpentry.
B. Appropriate moisture condition depends on species, product, intended service environment, specification and measurement method.
C. Moisture meters cannot measure timber.
D. All timber is supplied at exactly the same moisture content.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Required moisture condition is context-specific. The relevant specification, approved procedure and instrument guidance must govern acceptance.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q18
A wide board becomes curved across its width after an environmental change. Which term most closely describes this form of distortion?
A. Cup
B. Kerf
C. Datum
D. Veneer

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Cupping is curvature across the width of a board and may be associated with uneven or directional moisture-related movement.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q19
What is a major purpose of stickers in a properly designed timber drying or conditioning stack?
A. To glue the boards together.
B. To separate layers so air can circulate while boards remain appropriately supported.
C. To replace all structural supports.
D. To make moisture measurement unnecessary.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Correctly positioned spacers can support airflow and orderly stacking. Their arrangement must also avoid introducing distortion through poor support.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q20
A moisture meter gives an unexpected reading. What is the best professional response?
A. Change the reading manually to the expected value.
B. Check the instrument, measurement method, material factors and manufacturer guidance, then verify against the applicable specification.
C. Ignore all further moisture checks.
D. Reject every board automatically.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Moisture readings require correct instrument use and interpretation. Unexpected results should be investigated rather than guessed away.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

## Lesson 4 — Timber Defects & Grading Awareness

### Q21
What is the best general definition of a timber defect in carpentry?
A. Any visible grain pattern.
B. A feature or condition that may reduce suitability, appearance, machining quality or performance for the intended use.
C. Only a crack caused by a saw.
D. Any feature found in hardwood.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The significance of a feature depends on the job, its location and the governing specification.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4

### Q22
A board is curved along its length on its wide face. Which defect is most consistent with that description?
A. Bow
B. Cup
C. Wane
D. Knot

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Bow describes longitudinal curvature visible along the face of a board.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4

### Q23
A board rotates along its length so its corners no longer lie in one plane. What is this commonly called?
A. Twist
B. Sapwood
C. Kerf
D. Allowance

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Twist is a form of warp in which the board rotates along its length.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4

### Q24
A learner identifies a knot in structural timber and declares the board “Grade X” without using the required grading rules or authority. What is wrong with this?
A. Nothing; one visible knot determines every grade.
B. Defect awareness is not the same as formal grading, which must follow the applicable standard, grading system or authorised process.
C. Structural timber never has knots.
D. Grades are determined only by colour.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Learners should recognise features and understand their possible significance, but formal grade assignment requires the appropriate grading criteria and authority.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4

### Q25
A board has wane along an edge needed for a visible, dimension-critical component. What should happen first?
A. Ignore it and assemble the component.
B. Determine whether enough compliant material remains to produce the specified finished component; otherwise select different stock.
C. Fill the missing edge with paint.
D. Change the drawing without approval.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The defect must be evaluated against finished dimensions, appearance and specification before material is committed.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4

### Q26
Why should defects be identified before cutting a full set of components?
A. Early inspection supports material allocation, quality control and waste prevention.
B. Defects disappear after cutting.
C. Inspection is only required after finishing.
D. It guarantees every offcut can be sold.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Early inspection prevents unsuitable sections from being committed to critical components and can reduce avoidable rework and waste.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4

## Lesson 5 — Manufactured Boards: Plywood, MDF, Particleboard & OSB

### Q27
What distinguishes plywood from MDF most clearly?
A. Plywood is built from bonded veneer layers, while MDF is made from wood fibres bonded into a panel.
B. MDF is made from whole solid boards nailed together.
C. Plywood contains no wood.
D. They are identical products with different names.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Their different construction influences machining, edges, fastening, finishing and suitable applications.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

### Q28
Why should a carpenter not assume every sheet of plywood is suitable for exterior exposure?
A. All plywood dissolves outdoors immediately.
B. Plywood products differ in construction, bond, grade and intended service conditions; the specified product must be verified.
C. Plywood has no grades.
D. Exterior work can use any board if it is painted once.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The product specification and manufacturer information determine suitability; the generic name “plywood” is not enough.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

### Q29
Which board is generally characterised by a fine, relatively uniform fibre structure and smooth faces useful for many painted interior applications?
A. MDF
B. Solid log
C. Rough-sawn pole
D. Steel plate

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** MDF's fibre-based construction commonly provides smooth faces, although the exact grade and service suitability must still be verified.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

### Q30
Why do exposed edges of some manufactured boards need special planning?
A. Their edge structure can differ from the face and may affect appearance, finishing and fixing performance.
B. Board edges are always stronger than solid timber.
C. Edges never absorb coatings.
D. Edges cannot be cut.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Edge treatment is part of product quality. It may involve lipping, edging, sealing or other specified methods depending on the board and design.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

### Q31
A drawing specifies a particular moisture-resistant board grade. The workshop has standard board of the same thickness. What is the correct action?
A. Substitute automatically because thickness is the only relevant property.
B. Use the specified grade or obtain authorised approval for a technically suitable substitution.
C. Use two layers of standard board without approval.
D. Change the service environment.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Matching thickness does not prove equivalent performance. Material substitutions require specification-based approval.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

### Q32
What is a key safety concern when machining MDF, particleboard or other wood-based panels?
A. Dust generation must be controlled using appropriate extraction/workplace controls and required PPE.
B. Manufactured boards cannot produce dust.
C. Only visible chips matter.
D. Dust controls are unnecessary if the cut is short.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Machining wood and composite boards can generate dust. Source control and applicable workplace safety measures are important.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

### Q33
Before cutting a large manufactured-board sheet, what combination best supports quality and safety?
A. Confirm specification and orientation, inspect the sheet, plan cuts, provide stable support and use suitable tools/processes.
B. Balance the sheet on one corner.
C. Cut first and check dimensions later.
D. Ignore damaged edges because they are part of every board.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Sheet work requires planning, support, material inspection and correct process selection before the first cut.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

## Lesson 6 — Selecting Materials for Purpose & Service Conditions

### Q34
What should drive professional material selection first?
A. The cheapest available material regardless of specification.
B. The job requirements, service conditions and applicable specification.
C. The carpenter's favourite species.
D. The colour of the supplier's packaging.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Material selection begins with what the finished product must do and the conditions it must withstand.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q35
A component will be used where moisture exposure is expected. What is the best selection approach?
A. Assume every timber and board performs equally when wet.
B. Verify that the specified material, grade, treatment, finish and detailing are suitable for the intended service conditions.
C. Select only by colour.
D. Use indoor material and rely on luck.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Service exposure must be matched to verified material and system suitability.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q36
A cheaper substitute requires more machining, produces more waste and may not meet the finish specification. Why can it be a false economy?
A. Purchase price is only one part of whole-job cost and performance.
B. Cheap materials are illegal.
C. More waste always improves quality.
D. Labour and rework never affect cost.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Whole-job decisions consider purchase cost, labour, waste, finishing, risk, rework and expected performance.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q37
A supplier cannot confirm the required structural grade of timber. What should the carpenter do?
A. Guess the grade from colour.
B. Use it in the critical structural component anyway.
C. Obtain compliant verified material or escalate according to the project procedure.
D. Sand the timber until the grade becomes visible.

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Required grades must be verified. Uncertainty in a specification-critical property should be resolved before use.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q38
Which combination is most complete when comparing two materials for a cabinet project?
A. Appearance only.
B. Price only.
C. Specification, service conditions, dimensions, condition, workability, joining/finishing compatibility, appearance, availability, cost and waste implications.
D. Supplier distance only.

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Professional selection integrates technical, production, aesthetic and commercial factors.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q39
When is a material substitution acceptable?
A. Whenever the substitute is physically nearby.
B. When it has been checked against the requirements and approved through the applicable authority or workplace process.
C. Whenever it is cheaper.
D. Whenever the carpenter has used it once before.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Substitution is a controlled decision. Equivalent appearance or size alone does not prove equivalent suitability.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

## Lesson 7 — Timber Storage, Handling & Conditioning

### Q40
Why should long timber be adequately supported in storage?
A. To reduce avoidable distortion, instability and damage.
B. To increase moisture absorption deliberately.
C. To make boards harder.
D. To eliminate the need for inspection.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Appropriate support helps preserve stock condition and keeps storage safer and more orderly.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q41
What is a major problem with storing timber directly where it is exposed to standing water or persistent dampness?
A. It can change moisture condition and increase risks of staining, deterioration or distortion.
B. It guarantees improved seasoning.
C. It automatically upgrades the timber.
D. It has no effect on wood.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Storage should protect material from inappropriate moisture exposure and contamination.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q42
A sheet panel is too large for one worker to handle safely under the workplace procedure. What should happen?
A. The worker should carry it alone to prove competence.
B. Use the approved team-handling or mechanical-handling method.
C. Drag it by a corner through the workshop.
D. Cut it while carrying it.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Handling methods should match the load, environment and workplace controls. Safety takes priority over speed.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q43
What does conditioning timber for a job generally aim to achieve?
A. Allow material to approach an appropriate moisture relationship with its intended environment before critical manufacture or installation where required.
B. Make timber permanently waterproof.
C. Change hardwood into softwood.
D. Replace grading.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Conditioning can reduce the risk of major post-manufacture movement when material has come from significantly different environmental conditions.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q44
Why should timber storage racks and stacks remain accessible and clearly organised?
A. It supports safer retrieval, inspection, stock identification and rotation.
B. It makes defects invisible.
C. It allows workers to climb on unstable stacks.
D. It removes the need for housekeeping.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Organised storage contributes to safety, material protection, inventory control and efficient work.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q45
A newly delivered batch is placed in front of older suitable stock, which is then repeatedly forgotten. What business problem can result?
A. Poor stock rotation can increase deterioration, obsolete stock and waste.
B. Older stock automatically improves forever.
C. Stock age never affects inventory decisions.
D. It eliminates material costs.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Appropriate stock rotation and identification help reduce avoidable loss while still respecting job-specific material requirements.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q46
A stored board looks distorted when removed from a stack. What is the best next step?
A. Machine it immediately without checking.
B. Inspect the board and storage conditions, compare it with the job requirements and decide whether it can be safely and economically used, reallocated or rejected.
C. Hide the distortion inside the finished product.
D. Force it straight during final installation regardless of specification.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Material condition should be assessed before processing. Forced correction without diagnosis can create poor fit, stress, rework or failure.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

## Lesson 8 — Sustainable Sourcing, Waste Reduction & Material Planning

### Q47
What is the difference between a cutting list and a cutting plan?
A. A cutting list identifies required components and dimensions; a cutting plan organises how those components will be obtained efficiently from available stock.
B. They are always exactly the same document.
C. A cutting plan replaces the drawing and specification.
D. A cutting list is only a waste record.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** The cutting list defines what is needed, while the cutting plan helps determine how to obtain it from stock with controlled waste.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q48
A cutting plan reduces waste but places a serious defect inside a critical component. Is the plan acceptable?
A. Yes, because lowest waste always has priority.
B. No. Waste reduction must not override specification, safety or required quality.
C. Yes, if the defect will be painted.
D. Yes, if the material is expensive.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Efficient use of material is important, but compliance and product performance come first.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q49
Which action best represents responsible use of reusable offcuts?
A. Keep every scrap forever without identification.
B. Retain useful pieces in an organised system where their dimensions, material type and condition make future use practical.
C. Mix reusable timber with contaminated waste.
D. Use offcuts in any project regardless of specification.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Reuse works best when offcuts are safe, suitable, identifiable and efficiently stored.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q50
Why is accurate setting out a sustainability practice as well as a quality practice?
A. Accurate work can reduce avoidable miscuts, rework and wasted material.
B. Accuracy increases waste deliberately.
C. Sustainability is unrelated to workshop practice.
D. Accurate work eliminates all offcuts.

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Preventing errors is often more effective than trying to recover material after an avoidable mistake.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q51
A client requires evidence that timber comes from an approved responsible source. What should the carpenter do?
A. Make an unsupported verbal claim.
B. Preserve and verify the required supplier or chain-of-custody documentation according to the job requirement.
C. Remove all product labels.
D. Substitute unverified timber because it looks similar.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Where sourcing evidence is specified, traceable documentation is part of compliance and professional procurement.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q52
A workshop repeatedly produces the same large avoidable offcut from a standard product. What is the best continuous-improvement response?
A. Accept the waste as unavoidable without investigation.
B. Review cutting lists, stock sizes, nesting/cutting plans, purchasing and design constraints to identify a compliant way to improve yield.
C. Reduce finished dimensions without approval.
D. Hide the offcuts before stocktaking.

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Waste data can reveal opportunities to improve purchasing and production planning while preserving specifications and quality.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

## Summative Assessment Quality Controls

This bank deliberately goes beyond simple recall. Questions require learners to apply material knowledge to selection, inspection, storage, moisture, defects, board products, specification compliance, waste control and workplace decisions.

The assessment must remain traceable to the eight teaching lessons. It must not assess specialist structural design, formal timber grading authority, laboratory wood science, certification claims or numerical moisture limits that were not taught.

Where a question involves a specified timber grade, board grade, treatment, moisture requirement or responsible-sourcing requirement, the learner is expected to follow the supplied project specification, approved documentation and workplace procedure rather than inventing a universal rule.

## Academic/Vocational Alignment Note
South African carpentry occupational materials used as alignment references emphasise accurate cutting lists from working drawings, material selection according to specification and properties, timber of specified grade where required, correct setting out to specified dimensions, cost-effective working methods and minimising material waste. Some referenced legacy SAQA unit standards have passed their registration end date; they are used here only as occupational-content references and are not represented as current enrolment qualifications.
