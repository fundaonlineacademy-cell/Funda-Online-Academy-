# Module 10 — Formative Assessment Bank
## Applied Carpentry Practice & Workplace Readiness

**Bank size:** 32 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 15 questions randomly selected per attempt  
**Pass mark:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Progression:** A pass is required before the Module 10 Summative Assessment unlocks.  
**Coverage:** 4 questions per teaching lesson. Every item has four options A–D, a keyed answer, explanation and traceability.

### Lesson 1 — Interpreting a Client or Workplace Brief
**Q1.** A drawing gives cabinet width but the client message gives a different width. What is the best first action? A Use the larger value B Use the drawing automatically C Clarify the conflict before affected work proceeds D Average them. **Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Conflicting controlled information must be resolved, not guessed. **Trace:** L1.

**Q2.** Which item is an assumption rather than a confirmed requirement? A Material named in approved specification B Dimension on current approved drawing C Carpenter believes the client probably wants satin finish D Hardware listed in schedule. **Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Belief without supporting approved information is an assumption. **Trace:** L1.

**Q3.** Why identify acceptance criteria before manufacture? A To remove inspection B To align production and final judgement with the same requirements C To permit substitutions D To avoid drawings. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Quality should be planned and checked against known requirements from the beginning. **Trace:** L1.

**Q4.** A client verbally changes a specified material. The change affects price and durability. Best response? A Substitute immediately B Record and obtain required approval before affected work C Ignore client D Mix materials. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Significant changes require controlled variation or revision before work proceeds. **Trace:** L1.

### Lesson 2 — Planning an End-to-End Carpentry Job
**Q5.** Which is a dependency? A Final finish colour B Site measurement that must be confirmed before final cutting C Brand logo D Invoice number. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** A dependency is a condition or activity that must be completed before a later task can proceed reliably. **Trace:** L2.

**Q6.** Why should dry-fit inspection occur before final adhesive where appropriate? A Adhesive is expensive B Fit and geometry can be corrected before becoming difficult to reverse C To eliminate measurement D To increase waste. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Dry fitting reveals alignment, orientation and dimensional errors while correction is still practical. **Trace:** L2.

**Q7.** A specified hinge has a long lead time. When should this normally be identified? A At handover B During resource and planning review C After doors are installed D Never. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Procurement constraints should be identified early so they do not disrupt production or installation. **Trace:** L2.

**Q8.** A contingency plan should allow a carpenter to silently replace unavailable specified material. A True in all cases B False; substitution still requires appropriate approval C True only for expensive material D True whenever delivery is late. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Contingency planning does not override the approved specification or change-control process. **Trace:** L2.

### Lesson 3 — Basic Costing, Material Quantities & Waste Estimation
**Q9.** Quantity take-off should primarily come from: A Guesswork B Controlled drawings and cutting information C Competitor advertising D Final invoice. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Quantities should be derived from approved job information so purchasing and costing reflect the actual scope. **Trace:** L3.

**Q10.** Why can purchasing quantity exceed net component quantity? A Because all waste is mandatory B Stock sizes, cutting layout, actual process requirements and suitable allowances affect purchasing C Because drawings are irrelevant D To increase price. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Real stock formats and production processes can require more material than the sum of finished component sizes. **Trace:** L3.

**Q11.** Which approach to waste allowance is strongest? A Always add 10% B Always add 20% C Base it on actual material, stock format, cutting plan, defects and process D Never allow waste. **Answer: C.** **Explanation:** There is no universal waste percentage that suits every material and job. **Trace:** L3.

**Q12.** Why compare estimated cost with actual cost after completion? A To improve future estimating and waste control B To change completed dimensions C To avoid records D To remove labour. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Comparing estimate to actual results shows where quantities, waste, labour or assumptions can be improved. **Trace:** L3.

### Lesson 4 — Safe Work Method & Risk Planning
**Q13.** A circular saw's required guard is defective. Best action? A Wear extra PPE and continue B Remove tool from use and report according to procedure C Cut faster D Ask customer. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** PPE does not compensate for a defective required safety control. **Trace:** L4.

**Q14.** Before drilling into an unfamiliar installation substrate, the carpenter should first: A Choose the longest screw B Verify substrate, relevant concealed-service risk and approved fixing method C Drill trial holes everywhere D Use glue only. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Fixing choice and drilling safety depend on the actual substrate and hidden-service risk. **Trace:** L4.

**Q15.** An SDS is especially useful for: A Choosing timber dimensions B Chemical product hazard and handling information C Measuring square D Pricing labour. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Safety Data Sheets communicate product-specific hazards, handling and control information. **Trace:** L4.

**Q16.** Which is a valid stop-work trigger? A Essential safety control is absent B Customer says hello C Tool is clean D Drawing is clear. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Work should not continue when an essential control needed for safe compliant operation is missing. **Trace:** L4.

### Lesson 5 — Executing & Sequencing a Practical Project
**Q17.** A first-off component is outside the controlled dimension. What should happen before batch production? A Continue and correct later B Correct the cause or setup and verify a compliant first-off C Change drawing D Hide component. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** A wrong first-off can reveal a setup error that would otherwise be repeated across the batch. **Trace:** L5.

**Q18.** Why maintain reference faces and edges? A Decoration B To maintain consistent measurement and machining relationships C To increase material use D To replace drawings. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Consistent references reduce cumulative positioning errors and support repeatable machining. **Trace:** L5.

**Q19.** A joint does not fit during dry assembly. Best response? A Force it B Identify the cause and make a controlled correction C Apply more finish D Ignore it. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Fit problems should be diagnosed before permanent fixing or bonding. **Trace:** L5.

**Q20.** Protecting components between operations is part of quality control. A True because handling damage can create new defects B False because only final inspection matters C True only for hardwood D False once machining starts. **Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Storage and handling between operations can damage prepared surfaces, edges and dimensions. **Trace:** L5.

### Lesson 6 — Quality Control During Project Execution
**Q21.** Quality inspection should compare work mainly against: A Personal preference B Controlled requirements C Fastest competitor D Random sample online. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Inspection decisions must be based on the approved drawing, specification and applicable acceptance criteria. **Trace:** L6.

**Q22.** When is a strong time to check cabinet squareness? A Only after handover B During dry or final assembly before geometry is irreversibly locked C Never D After invoicing. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Geometry should be checked while correction remains possible. **Trace:** L6.

**Q23.** After authorised rework, what should occur? A No further check B Re-inspect the affected requirement C Change specification D Hide record. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Rework must be verified because the original defect may remain or a secondary defect may have been introduced. **Trace:** L6.

**Q24.** What is a hold point? A Lunch break B Stage where work does not proceed until required verification or approval occurs C Storage shelf D Customer complaint. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** A hold point prevents costly or irreversible work from continuing before a critical check is completed. **Trace:** L6.

### Lesson 7 — Customer Communication, Handover & Aftercare
**Q25.** A customer requests an extra shelf outside agreed scope. Best response? A Always do it without discussion B Follow the variation or scope approval process C Refuse all changes D Remove existing shelf. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Scope changes can affect time, material, price and design and therefore require controlled agreement. **Trace:** L7.

**Q26.** Before handover, the carpenter should: A Rely on customer to find defects B Conduct final inspection against approved scope and requirements C Hide snags D Discard records. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Final inspection confirms that completed work meets the agreed scope before release. **Trace:** L7.

**Q27.** Aftercare instructions for a coating should come from: A Guessed universal rule B Relevant manufacturer or product and approved job information C Neighbour's opinion D Unrelated product. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Cleaning, curing and service advice can differ between finish systems. **Trace:** L7.

**Q28.** A complaint is best initially handled by: A Immediate blame B Listening, recording facts and comparing the issue with requirements C Deleting messages D Promising anything. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** A professional complaint process starts with evidence and objective comparison rather than assumptions. **Trace:** L7.

### Lesson 8 — Employability, Portfolio, Entrepreneurship & Continuous Development
**Q29.** Which portfolio evidence is strongest? A Only one final photo B Process evidence linked to drawings, planning, checks and final outcome C Unverified claims D Another person's project. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Credible evidence shows how the learner planned, produced, checked and completed the work. **Trace:** L8.

**Q30.** A graduate should describe credentials: A More impressively than earned B Exactly and truthfully C As a trade test even when not one D Without evidence. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Professional credibility requires accurate representation of training and qualifications. **Trace:** L8.

**Q31.** Work requiring capability or authorisation beyond the carpenter's competence should normally be: A Guessed B Referred or escalated to an appropriately competent or authorised person C Hidden D Accepted regardless. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Recognising limits of competence protects safety, compliance and quality. **Trace:** L8.

**Q32.** Which habit best supports continuous development? A Never review completed work B Record lessons, seek feedback and set specific skill goals C Avoid manufacturer instructions D Repeat errors. **Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Structured reflection and targeted practice turn project experience into measurable improvement. **Trace:** L8.
