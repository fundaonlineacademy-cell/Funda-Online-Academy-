# Carpentry — Module 10: Applied Carpentry Practice & Workplace Readiness
## Lesson 7 — Customer Communication, Handover & Aftercare
**Estimated study time:** 45 minutes

### Learning Outcomes
Learners should be able to communicate professionally during a carpentry job; manage changes and expectations; conduct a structured handover; explain appropriate care information; and record unresolved items without making unsupported promises.

### Why This Lesson Matters
The customer experiences more than the finished object. Reliability, communication, cleanliness, respect for property, change control and clear handover all influence professional reputation. Good communication also prevents technical misunderstandings from becoming commercial disputes.

### Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Expectation** — result or service a customer believes will be delivered.
- **Variation** — approved change to the agreed scope or requirement.
- **Snag** — minor outstanding defect or incomplete item identified near handover.
- **Handover record** — evidence documenting completion, inspection and transfer of work.
- **Aftercare** — appropriate guidance or service following completion.
- **Warranty** — commitment whose exact meaning depends on the supplier/manufacturer/agreement; it must not be invented.
- **Care instruction** — guidance for cleaning, use or maintenance of the completed work.
- **Escalation** — referring an issue to a person authorised to decide or resolve it.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Communicate before problems grow
Confirm scope, access, timing and important decisions early. If a delay or technical issue emerges, communicate the facts promptly through the appropriate channel. Do not conceal problems until handover.

### 2. Use clear, non-technical explanations when needed
Customers may not know carpentry terminology. Explain choices in terms of function, appearance, maintenance, cost and limitations without misleading them. When technical approval is required, document the exact decision.

### 3. Control variations
A customer may request an extra shelf, different finish or changed dimensions. Determine the effect on material, labour, programme, safety and existing work. Obtain required approval before proceeding. A friendly verbal request should not automatically become uncontrolled extra work.

### 4. Respect the site and customer property
Protect floors, walls and nearby items, control dust, maintain access where agreed, keep the work area safe and remove waste appropriately. Professional conduct includes punctuality, appropriate language, honesty and care with property.

### 5. Prepare for handover
Before inviting acceptance, conduct final inspection. Confirm specified dimensions/fit, operation, hardware, finish, cleanliness and completion of approved scope. Resolve defects where possible and record outstanding items clearly.

### 6. Demonstrate the completed work
Show the customer or responsible person how doors, drawers, locks, adjustable components or other relevant features operate. Explain limitations that are part of the approved design. Do not claim a load capacity or product performance that has not been specified or verified.

### 7. Provide accurate aftercare
Use manufacturer/product instructions for coatings, hardware and special materials. Explain cleaning and maintenance only within supported information. If a finish requires a defined cure period before heavy use, communicate the manufacturer's actual requirement rather than a guessed time.

### 8. Record handover
A handover record can identify the project, date, completed scope, inspected items, documents/care information provided, snags, agreed follow-up and acknowledgement. This does not replace any legally required commercial document but creates useful operational evidence.

### 9. Respond to complaints professionally
Listen, record the issue, inspect evidence, compare against agreed requirements and avoid immediate blame. If the matter is outside your authority, escalate it. A complaint can reveal a workmanship, communication, material or expectation-management failure and should be used for improvement.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
After installing a cabinet, the carpenter notices one door requires adjustment. Instead of asking the customer to sign immediately, the hinge is adjusted, operation is rechecked and the unit is cleaned. The carpenter demonstrates the doors/drawers, provides the finish manufacturer's care guidance and records completion. A later request for an additional shelf is documented as new/changed scope rather than quietly treated as part of the original work.

## Practical Activity
Role-play a five-minute handover. Use a checklist covering final inspection, operation demonstration, care guidance, snags, variations and customer acknowledgement. A partner should introduce one complaint or change request for you to handle professionally.

## Knowledge Check
1. A requested extra item should automatically be done free of charge. **Answer: False.** Scope/variation procedures should be followed.
2. Care instructions should be based on: A) guesswork B) product/manufacturer and approved job information C) social media comments D) habit. **Answer: B.**
3. Why record snags? **Answer:** To make outstanding work visible, assigned and traceable.
4. A complaint should first be met with blame. **Answer: False.** Gather facts and compare with requirements.

### Key Takeaways
Communicate early; explain clearly; control changes; protect customer property; inspect before handover; demonstrate operation; give evidence-based aftercare; and record completion and outstanding items.
