# Carpentry — Module 10: Applied Carpentry Practice & Workplace Readiness
## Lesson 6 — Quality Control During Project Execution
**Estimated study time:** 45 minutes

### Learning Outcomes
Learners should be able to plan stage-specific quality checks; verify dimensions and geometry; inspect joints, hardware, surfaces and finishes; record nonconformity and corrective action; and decide when work should proceed, be reworked or be escalated.

### Why This Lesson Matters
Final inspection cannot undo every mistake. Some defects become hidden or irreversible after assembly, finishing or installation. Quality control during execution prevents defects from moving downstream and gives the carpenter evidence that important requirements were checked.

### Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Inspection** — deliberate examination or measurement against a requirement.
- **Tolerance** — permitted variation from a specified value where a tolerance has been defined.
- **Conformity** — fulfilment of a requirement.
- **Hold point** — stage at which work does not continue until required verification/approval occurs.
- **Rework** — controlled action to bring nonconforming work into conformity where permitted.
- **Root cause** — underlying reason a problem occurred.
- **Verification** — confirmation, using evidence, that requirements have been fulfilled.
- **Traceability** — ability to connect work/results to relevant information, checks or decisions.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Inspect against requirements, not preference
Quality is not simply whether work “looks good.” Use approved dimensions, drawings, material/finish requirements, manufacturer information and workplace acceptance criteria. Do not invent tolerances where none are authorised.

### 2. Inspect incoming material
Check material identity, dimensions where relevant, defects, damage, moisture/conditioning requirements where applicable, sheet faces/edges and hardware. Reject or isolate unsuitable material before investing labour in it.

### 3. Inspect after marking and machining
Check critical dimensions, angles, reference relationships, hole/joint positions and edge quality. Use first-off checks for repeated work. Measuring after every repeated defective cut is too late if the setup itself was wrong.

### 4. Inspect dry assembly
Verify fit, square, alignment, diagonals or other relevant geometry, component orientation and hardware clearances. Correct problems before adhesive or irreversible fixings lock them in.

### 5. Inspect during final assembly
Watch for movement caused by clamping, incorrect fastener placement, damaged edges, adhesive contamination and loss of square. Confirm required dimensions again before the assembly becomes difficult to adjust.

### 6. Inspect surface and finish stages
Before coating, check scratches, glue contamination, sanding defects, dust and repairs. During finishing, follow the specified system and inspect for coverage and defects appropriate to that product. Respect manufacturer drying/recoat/cure instructions rather than using universal times.

### 7. Inspect installation
Check location, level/plumb/alignment where applicable, fixing security under the approved method, clearances, operation of doors/drawers/hardware, surface damage and interface with surrounding work. A workshop-perfect component can become nonconforming during installation.

### 8. Control nonconformity
When a defect is found, identify it, prevent accidental acceptance, determine cause and choose an authorised response: rework, replacement, concession/approval where formally permitted, or escalation. Never disguise a defect. After correction, re-inspect the affected requirement.

### 9. Record meaningful evidence
A checklist should record the requirement, result, status, correction and verification. Evidence may include measurements and photographs where appropriate. Records support learning, customer confidence and continuous improvement.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
During dry assembly of a cabinet, diagonals differ beyond the project's accepted geometry. The learner does not attach the back panel to hide the problem. Clamps and joint seating are checked, the assembly is corrected, dimensions are reverified and only then is the next stage allowed to proceed.

## Practical Activity
Create a quality-control plan with checks at material receipt, first-off machining, dry assembly, final assembly, pre-finish, post-finish and installation/handover. State the requirement, method and action if the check fails.

## Knowledge Check
1. Quality should be judged primarily against: A) personal taste B) controlled requirements C) fastest method D) cheapest material. **Answer: B.**
2. After rework, should the affected requirement be re-inspected? **Answer: Yes.**
3. Why inspect before finishing? **Answer:** Coating can make correction more difficult and may reveal or lock in surface defects.
4. Is hiding a defect acceptable if the customer cannot see it? **Answer: No.**

### Key Takeaways
Inspect throughout the job; use controlled criteria; establish hold points; correct causes rather than symptoms; re-inspect rework; and keep meaningful quality evidence.
