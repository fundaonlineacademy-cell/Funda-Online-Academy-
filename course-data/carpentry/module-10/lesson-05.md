# Carpentry — Module 10: Applied Carpentry Practice & Workplace Readiness
## Lesson 5 — Executing & Sequencing a Practical Project
**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

### Learning Outcomes
Learners should be able to execute a planned project in a controlled sequence; maintain reference surfaces and identification; use first-off and in-process checks; protect work between stages; and respond correctly to nonconformity or changed conditions.

### Why This Lesson Matters
Execution is where drawings, tools, materials, safety and quality become a physical product. Good workmanship is not simply “working carefully.” It is a controlled sequence that protects dimensions and references, verifies each stage before errors multiply, and maintains traceability.

### Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Reference face** — selected face from which related measurements or operations are established.
- **Reference edge** — selected edge used as a reliable datum for marking or machining.
- **First-off check** — verification of the first component or operation before repeating it.
- **Batch** — group of similar components processed together.
- **Dry assembly** — temporary assembly without final adhesive/fixing to verify fit and geometry.
- **In-process inspection** — quality check performed during production.
- **Nonconformity** — failure to meet a specified requirement.
- **Protection** — measures that prevent completed surfaces/components from being damaged before handover.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start only when prerequisites are ready
Confirm current drawings, material, cutting list, tools, work area and safety controls. Check that the material selected matches the job requirement and is suitable. Do not begin simply because some material is available.

### 2. Establish references and identification
Select reference faces/edges where the process requires them and mark components clearly. Consistent reference prevents cumulative error. Identify paired or matched components so that grain, orientation, hardware and machining remain coordinated.

### 3. Measure and mark deliberately
Work from controlled dimensions. Verify the measuring tool and reading, mark clearly, and avoid repeatedly measuring from different uncontrolled edges. When multiple equal components are required, a correctly verified stop, jig or template may improve consistency if it is appropriate and safely used.

### 4. Use first-off checks
Before producing a batch, verify the first component against the drawing/specification. A setup error repeated across ten pieces creates ten defects. Check dimensions, orientation, edge/face selection and machining position before continuing.

### 5. Sequence joints and assembly logically
Prepare components so that later operations remain possible. Dry fit joints where appropriate, check fit and geometry, then make controlled corrections. Confirm square, alignment and dimensions before adhesive cures or irreversible fixings are completed.

### 6. Manage adhesive and fixings correctly
Use the adhesive/fixing system specified or approved for the material and service conditions. Follow manufacturer requirements for preparation, application, open/assembly/clamping and curing conditions where applicable. Do not invent universal clamping times or screw spacing.

### 7. Protect work between stages
A perfect component can be damaged by poor stacking, metal debris, wet surfaces or careless transport. Store and support components appropriately, protect finished faces and maintain clean work areas. This is part of quality control, not housekeeping alone.

### 8. Respond to nonconformity immediately
If a component is wrong, identify and isolate it. Determine whether approved rework is possible without compromising requirements. Do not hide defects or force misfitting components into assembly. Record/report significant nonconformity according to workplace procedure.

### 9. Maintain production evidence
Keep relevant checks, measurements, revision information and changes. For a learner portfolio, photographs of marking, first-off inspection, dry assembly and final product can demonstrate process competence better than a final photo alone.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A learner builds a small frame. After marking all pieces, the first rail is cut and checked. It is 2 mm short of the controlled dimension. Instead of cutting the remaining rails to match the error, the learner stops, checks the setup and reference method, identifies the cause, corrects it and then produces a compliant first-off component before continuing.

## Practical Activity
Execute or simulate a small frame/shelf project and maintain a production record showing prerequisites, reference marks, first-off measurement, dry-fit check, corrective action (if any), final assembly check and protection method.

## Knowledge Check
1. Main purpose of a first-off check? **Answer:** Prevent a setup/error from being repeated across multiple components.
2. A misfitting joint should be forced together to save time. **Answer: False.**
3. Why protect work between stages? **Answer:** To prevent damage that can create rework or rejection.
4. Batch production should begin before verifying the first component. **Answer: False.**

### Key Takeaways
Confirm prerequisites; preserve references; verify first-off work; dry fit before irreversible assembly where appropriate; use approved adhesives/fixings; protect components; and stop nonconformity from propagating.

### Visual Learning Guidance
Recommended visual: reference-face markings, first-off inspection, dry assembly and final protected stack. Caption: **“Control each stage before repeating or locking in the next.”**
