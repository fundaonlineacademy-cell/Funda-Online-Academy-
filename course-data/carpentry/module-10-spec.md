# Carpentry Premium Course — Module 10

## Applied Carpentry Practice & Workplace Readiness

**Module purpose:** Integrate the knowledge and practical reasoning developed across Modules 1–9 into an end-to-end carpentry job workflow. Learners practise interpreting a brief, planning materials and safety, estimating basic costs, sequencing work, controlling quality, communicating with a customer/supervisor and presenting evidence of competence for employment or small-business opportunities. This module reinforces workplace readiness without overstating the scope of an online short-course certificate.

### Lesson 1 — Interpreting a Client or Workplace Brief
Learners extract purpose, dimensions, material/finish expectations, deadline, site constraints and acceptance criteria from a realistic brief. They identify missing information, ask appropriate clarification questions, document agreed changes and avoid beginning work from assumptions.

### Lesson 2 — Planning an End-to-End Carpentry Job
Learners convert the clarified brief into a sequence of tasks, identify tools/materials, dependencies, checkpoints and likely constraints, and prepare a simple job plan. The plan integrates safe setup, measurement, material preparation, assembly, finishing, inspection and handover.

### Lesson 3 — Basic Costing, Material Quantities & Waste Estimation
Learners calculate simple quantities and prepare an introductory material-cost estimate using current supplier prices supplied in an exercise or gathered locally. They distinguish quantity, unit price, material subtotal, allowance for reasonable waste and labour/business considerations. The course must not hard-code market prices or present one markup percentage as universally correct.

### Lesson 4 — Safe Work Method & Risk Planning
Learners review the job step by step, identify hazards, choose controls using the hierarchy of controls, confirm competence/equipment requirements and document a simple pre-job risk plan. PPE is treated as one control layer rather than the first or only response to hazards.

### Lesson 5 — Executing & Sequencing a Practical Project
Learners follow a controlled workflow from set-out through preparation, dry fitting, assembly and finishing. Where a learner lacks workshop access, an evidence-based simulation/planning alternative may assess sequencing and judgement, but it must not be described as equivalent to supervised practical performance where hands-on competence is required.

### Lesson 6 — Quality Control During Project Execution
Learners establish hold/check points before errors become expensive: verify dimensions before cutting, inspect prepared components before assembly, check square/alignment during assembly, inspect surfaces before finishing and compare final work with acceptance criteria. They record corrective action rather than hiding defects.

### Lesson 7 — Customer Communication, Handover & Aftercare
Learners practise professional updates, handling changes, explaining delays or defects accurately, presenting completed work, recording acceptance and giving appropriate care information. They distinguish factual product/manufacturer care instructions from unsupported promises or guarantees.

### Lesson 8 — Employability, Portfolio, Entrepreneurship & Continuous Development
Learners organise evidence of learning into a basic portfolio, describe skills accurately on a CV, prepare for entry-level workplace conversations, understand professional boundaries, plan further supervised skills development and explore small-business basics such as quotations, records, customer service and reputation. The lesson explicitly teaches learners not to claim trade status, licensing, accreditation or competencies they do not hold.

### Entry 9 — Formative Assessment
- 15 MCQs randomly selected from a bank of at least 30.
- Covers Lessons 1–8.
- Pass mark: 70%.
- Questions include brief interpretation, planning, quantity/cost reasoning, risk controls, workflow sequencing, quality checkpoints, communication and ethical competence claims.
- Maximum formal attempts in production policy: 3.
- Feedback explains why the selected action or calculation is appropriate.

### Entry 10 — Summative Assessment
- 25 MCQs randomly selected from a bank of at least 50.
- Pass mark: 70%.
- Capstone-style scenarios integrate knowledge from this module and legitimately draw on prerequisite concepts developed throughout the course where the question clearly relates to the applied job workflow.
- Strong emphasis on safe judgement, accuracy, quality, professional conduct and appropriate escalation.
- Questions must not merely reword formative items.
- Maximum formal attempts in production policy: 3.

## Premium lesson requirements
Every teaching lesson must contain lesson identification, estimated study time, 3–5 measurable outcomes, Why This Lesson Matters, 5–8 meaningful bold-defined terms, at least 500 words of substantive main teaching content (normally 700–1,000), worked example/demonstration, practical activity, informal knowledge check, explanatory feedback, key takeaways and learner progression notes where supported.

## Visual learning plan
Use meaningful instructional visuals: annotated client brief, end-to-end job-plan flow, sample cut/material list, simple costing table, hierarchy-of-controls graphic, project quality checkpoint diagram, handover checklist and example portfolio structure. Visuals should make processes and evidence easier to understand rather than merely decorate the page.

## Accuracy, ethics and workplace-readiness controls
- Do not hard-code supplier prices, wages, markups, tax treatment or legal/business requirements that can change; teach method and direct learners to current applicable requirements.
- A course completion certificate must not be described as a trade qualification, licence or statutory authorisation unless the Academy has separately established that status.
- Learners should accurately represent the scope of their training and practical experience to employers and customers.
- Structural, regulated, specialist or otherwise high-risk work outside the learner's competence must be escalated to an appropriately competent person.
- Safety planning must prioritise elimination/substitution/engineering/administrative controls where practicable before relying on PPE.
- Practical evidence and knowledge assessment serve different purposes; passing MCQs alone should not be described as proof of every hands-on carpentry competency.
- Assessment questions may only test knowledge taught in the course or reasonable application of established prerequisite learning.
