# Module 7 — Construction Methods & Practical Application
## Lesson 5 — Doors, Frames, Hardware & Basic Fitting

**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:
1. identify basic door, frame and hardware components;
2. explain the sequence for checking an opening before fitting a door;
3. describe controlled marking, fitting and adjustment principles;
4. identify quality checks for alignment, clearance and hardware operation; and
5. recognise when fire, security, accessibility or other regulated door requirements require approved specifications.

### Why This Lesson Matters
Doors are high-use components. Poor fitting produces binding, excessive gaps, misaligned locks, damaged finishes and premature hardware wear. South African woodworking assessment and carpentry standards treat doors, frames and ironmongery as core vocational content. Professional fitting depends on measurement and controlled adjustment, not repeatedly removing material until the door happens to close.

### Key Terms / Vocabulary
**Door leaf** — The movable door panel that opens and closes within or against a frame.

**Frame** — The fixed assembly surrounding the door opening and providing locations for hinges and closing hardware.

**Jamb** — A vertical side of a door frame or lining.

**Head** — The horizontal top member of a door frame.

**Ironmongery / hardware** — Functional metal or manufactured fittings such as hinges, locks, handles, catches and bolts.

**Hinge gain / recess** — A prepared recess that allows a hinge leaf to sit at the specified position and depth.

**Clearance** — The designed space between the door and adjacent frame, floor or component.

**Reveal** — A visible edge or spacing relationship around a fitted component; in door fitting, consistent visual spacing is an important quality indicator where specified.

### Main Lesson Content
Door fitting begins with the job information. Confirm door type, opening, handing, frame, hardware schedule, finish and any special performance requirements. Fire-rated, security, acoustic or other specialised door assemblies can have strict tested-system requirements. Do not modify them using ordinary fitting assumptions. Follow the approved system, manufacturer information and applicable project requirements.

Inspect the opening and frame before altering the door leaf. Check frame dimensions, plumb, level, square, twist and condition. A door cannot be fitted accurately to a seriously defective frame by endlessly trimming the leaf. If the frame is outside the project’s acceptable condition, report or correct it using the authorised method first.

Identify door orientation and handing. Confirm which face is intended to be visible from each side, which edge is the hinge edge and where the lock or latch belongs. Decorative grain, panels or factory markings can affect orientation. Mark the door clearly before carrying out irreversible machining.

Hinges must be suitable for the specified door and installed according to the hardware or project requirements. Positioning, number, screw type and recess depth should not be guessed. Mark hinge positions from controlled references. Recesses should allow the hinge to seat correctly without excessive gaps or material removal. Screws should be driven accurately without damaging heads or pulling the hinge out of alignment.

After hanging, inspect the door’s movement. It should operate through the required range without binding and should meet specified clearances. Observe the spacing at the head and jambs, alignment with stops and the relationship to the floor or threshold. Do not create excessive clearance merely to eliminate one binding point. Diagnose the cause: frame geometry, hinge position, loose screws, door distortion or incorrect trimming.

Lock and latch fitting must align the operating hardware with its receiver. Mark positions carefully and follow the hardware template or instructions where supplied. Drill and chisel with controlled depth to avoid breaking through visible faces. Test operation before final tightening and again after all hardware is secured.

Hardware installation includes quality of finish. Screw slots or heads should not be damaged, plates should sit correctly, handles should operate smoothly and exposed surfaces should not be scratched by tools. Protect finished doors during fitting.

Site adjustment must be deliberate. If trimming is permitted, identify exactly where material must be removed and how much, based on measurements and specified clearances. Remove material progressively and recheck. Consider the door construction: excessive trimming may expose cores, weaken edge details or violate manufacturer limits. Factory-made doors may state maximum trimming allowances, which must be followed.

Final inspection includes opening and closing, latch/lock engagement, hinge security, specified clearances, alignment, surface condition and cleanliness. Where door performance is safety-critical, complete the required documentation or inspection process rather than relying only on visual judgement.

### Worked Example / Demonstration
A newly hung door binds at the upper latch-side corner. Instead of immediately planing the door, the carpenter checks the frame and hinges. One top-hinge screw has not seated correctly, allowing the door to sag. Correcting the hinge fastening restores alignment without unnecessary material removal. This demonstrates why diagnosis comes before trimming.

### Practical Activity
Using a training door, mock-up or diagram, create a Door Fitting Inspection Record. Include frame width/height checks, plumb/level/square observations, door orientation, hinge condition, hardware specification, clearance observations, latch alignment, surface protection and final operation. Add a “cause before correction” section for one simulated binding fault.

### Knowledge Check
1. Why should the frame be inspected before trimming the door?
2. What is the danger of guessing hinge recess depth?
3. Why should a carpenter diagnose binding before removing material?
4. What should be done when a door has specialised fire or security requirements?

### Feedback / Explanation
1. Frame defects can cause apparent door-fit problems that trimming will not properly solve.
2. Incorrect depth can misalign the door, prevent the hinge seating correctly or create excessive clearance.
3. The fault may be caused by hardware, frame geometry or sag rather than excess door material.
4. Follow the approved tested system, manufacturer/project information and applicable requirements; do not treat it as an ordinary door.

### Key Takeaways
- Confirm door type, handing, hardware and performance requirements.
- Inspect the opening and frame before altering the leaf.
- Mark irreversible operations from controlled references.
- Fit hardware to its specification or template.
- Diagnose binding before trimming.
- Remove only authorised amounts of material.
- Protect finished surfaces during fitting.
- Test operation and hardware at final inspection.
