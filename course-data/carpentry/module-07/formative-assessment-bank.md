## Lesson 1 — Reading a Simple Job Brief & Working Drawing

**Q1.** A working drawing displayed on screen has been resized. An unstated dimension is needed. What is the best action?
A. Measure the screen image with a ruler
B. Estimate it from a nearby component
C. Obtain the missing dimension from authorised information or clarification
D. Use the most common workshop size
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Screen scaling may not represent real size; unstated critical dimensions require authorised information.

**Q2.** Two dimensions on the current job information contradict each other. What should happen before cutting?
A. Use the larger dimension
B. Use the smaller dimension
C. Average the two
D. Stop the affected work and obtain clarification
**Answer: D.** **Explanation:** Conflicting requirements must be resolved rather than guessed.

**Q3.** Which item is most likely to be part of a specification rather than a graphical view?
A. Required board type
B. Front view
C. Side view
D. Top view
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Specifications commonly control material, grade, finish, hardware and related technical requirements.

**Q4.** Why should drawing revision information be checked?
A. To choose the drawing with the clearest printing
B. To confirm that the authorised current requirements are being used
C. To avoid reading dimensions
D. To decide the selling price
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** An obsolete revision can contain superseded requirements.

## Lesson 2 — Planning Work Sequence, Materials & Cut Lists

**Q5.** What is the main purpose of a cutting list?
A. To replace the working drawing
B. To record confirmed component quantities, materials and dimensions for production
C. To record only offcuts
D. To list workshop employees
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** A cutting list translates confirmed job requirements into controlled component information.

**Q6.** Why should a universal machining allowance not be added to every component?
A. Allowances are illegal
B. Every board is already finished
C. Preparation needs vary with material, process, condition and specification
D. Cutting lists never use dimensions
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Stock/preparation requirements depend on the actual process rather than a single arbitrary allowance.

**Q7.** Which is the best example of a planned inspection point?
A. Checking square after permanent finishing only
B. Checking critical dimensions before irreversible assembly
C. Counting tools after the client leaves
D. Measuring scrap at the end of the month
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Inspection points catch defects while correction is still practical.

**Q8.** A suitable offcut is large enough but its grade cannot be identified. What should the carpenter do?
A. Use it because it reduces waste
B. Paint over it
C. Confirm suitability/identity before use or select compliant material
D. Reduce the component size
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Waste reduction never overrides material specification and traceability.

## Lesson 3 — Building Frames & Simple Timber Structures

**Q9.** A rectangular practice frame has correct width and height but unequal diagonals. What does this suggest?
A. The frame may be racked
B. The timber is automatically too dry
C. The finish is incorrect
D. The cutting list has too many parts
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Unequal diagonals can indicate that a rectangular assembly is out of square.

**Q10.** Why are component identifiers useful during frame construction?
A. They increase timber strength
B. They connect members to the drawing/cutting list and reduce orientation errors
C. They replace measurement
D. They make clamps unnecessary
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Identification supports traceability and correct orientation.

**Q11.** When should a frame normally be checked for square?
A. Only after adhesive has fully cured
B. During dry fit and again during controlled assembly
C. Only after painting
D. Only if a customer complains
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Geometry should be checked while adjustment remains possible.

**Q12.** Which statement about structural framing is correct?
A. Any furniture-frame rule can be used structurally
B. Member sizes can be chosen by appearance
C. Structural work must follow approved design and applicable requirements
D. Equal diagonals are the only requirement
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Structural construction cannot be designed by informal workshop assumptions.

## Lesson 4 — Shelving & Basic Storage Construction

**Q13.** Why is there no single universal maximum shelf span?
A. Shelves are never loaded
B. Suitability depends on material, thickness, load, support and design
C. All boards have equal stiffness
D. Span is determined by colour
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Multiple technical factors influence deflection and suitability.

**Q14.** Adjustable shelf supports on opposite sides are at different heights. What is the likely result?
A. Improved rigidity
B. The shelf may rock or tilt
C. Increased load rating
D. Better ventilation
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Support positions must align for stable seating.

**Q15.** Why should a carcass be checked for square before permanently fixing its back?
A. The back may lock incorrect geometry into the assembly
B. The back changes timber species
C. Square is only decorative
D. It prevents all future dust
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** A fixed back can preserve distortion if the carcass is not corrected first.

**Q16.** Before anchoring a wall cabinet, what must be confirmed?
A. Only paint colour
B. Substrate and approved fixing requirements
C. Shelf count only
D. The carpenter’s favourite screw
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Fixing suitability depends on the supporting substrate and specified system.

## Lesson 5 — Doors, Frames, Hardware & Basic Fitting

**Q17.** A door binds after hanging. What should happen first?
A. Remove material immediately
B. Diagnose frame, hinge and door conditions
C. Increase every clearance
D. Replace the handle
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Binding is a symptom; its cause must be identified before trimming.

**Q18.** Why should the frame be checked before fitting the leaf?
A. Frame geometry can create apparent door-fit problems
B. Door leaves never require measurement
C. Hinges fit themselves
D. Frames are always square
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** A defective opening can make correct leaf fitting impossible without addressing the cause.

**Q19.** What should control hinge positions and recess details?
A. Guesswork from another job
B. Hardware/project requirements and controlled set-out
C. The nearest existing screw hole
D. Door colour
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Hardware installation follows the applicable specification/template.

**Q20.** A fire-rated door system requires adjustment. What is the correct approach?
A. Trim it like any ordinary door
B. Follow the approved system and manufacturer/project requirements
C. Remove labels first
D. Increase all gaps
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Specialised door assemblies may depend on tested system details.

## Lesson 6 — Installing Components & Safe Fixing to Substrates

**Q21.** Why is a painted wall surface insufficient for choosing an anchor?
A. Paint is too smooth
B. The supporting substrate behind it may be unknown
C. Anchors cannot be used indoors
D. Wall colour affects screw length
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The visible finish does not reliably identify the load-supporting construction.

**Q22.** Which is NOT a sound basis for selecting a fixing?
A. Confirmed substrate
B. Manufacturer/project requirements
C. Expected load/application
D. “This screw looks strong enough”
**Answer: D.** **Explanation:** Appearance is not a technical fixing specification.

**Q23.** Why should concealed services be considered before drilling?
A. Drilling can strike hidden electrical or plumbing services
B. Services make walls heavier
C. It changes cabinet colour
D. It determines shelf spacing
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Hidden services create serious installation hazards.

**Q24.** After multiple wall fixings are tightened, what should be rechecked?
A. Only the tool battery
B. Level, plumb, position and stability as applicable
C. Timber species only
D. Packaging
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Tightening can move or distort the component.

## Lesson 7 — On-site Adjustments & Practical Problem Solving

**Q25.** “The cabinet does not fit” is best described as:
A. A complete root cause
B. A symptom requiring measurement and diagnosis
C. An approved repair instruction
D. A material specification
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The statement describes the observed problem but not its cause.

**Q26.** What is the best sequence for a site-fit problem?
A. Cut → guess → measure
B. Observe → measure → compare → diagnose → correct → verify
C. Paint → drill → measure
D. Replace → inspect → read drawing
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Evidence should lead the corrective action.

**Q27.** When is packing inappropriate?
A. When the approved method permits minor adjustment
B. When it would conceal a major unsupported or out-of-tolerance condition
C. When it provides stable designed support
D. When alignment is being checked
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Packing cannot legitimately hide a fundamental defect.

**Q28.** Why should an affected quality check be repeated after rework?
A. Correction can alter another condition
B. Rework automatically guarantees compliance
C. Drawings are no longer relevant
D. It reduces material grade
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Corrections can influence fit, dimensions, alignment or operation.

## Lesson 8 — Practical Build Workflow: Set-out to Handover

**Q29.** What is a hold point?
A. A lunch break
B. A planned pause for inspection/approval before costly or irreversible work proceeds
C. A clamp-storage location
D. A customer complaint
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Hold points prevent defects from being carried beyond a controllable stage.

**Q30.** Which should be used as the final acceptance reference?
A. The carpenter’s memory
B. The original authorised job information and applicable specifications
C. A previous unrelated project
D. The material supplier’s advertisement
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Final inspection must test the work against its actual requirements.

**Q31.** A component becomes non-compliant during machining. What is the best action?
A. Alter every other component to match it
B. Stop, assess and apply an authorised rework or replacement decision
C. Hide the error during assembly
D. Continue because material has already been spent
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** One error should not be propagated through the project.

**Q32.** What is a useful purpose of post-job review?
A. To change completed specifications retrospectively
B. To identify waste, rework, delays and successful methods for improvement
C. To avoid handover
D. To remove inspection records
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Evidence from completed work supports continuous improvement.
