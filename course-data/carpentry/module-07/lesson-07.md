# Module 7 — Construction Methods & Practical Application
## Lesson 7 — On-site Adjustments & Practical Problem Solving

**Estimated study time:** 45 minutes

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:
1. diagnose common fit and alignment problems before making adjustments;
2. separate symptoms from underlying causes;
3. select proportionate corrective action within authorised limits;
4. record and escalate conditions that affect specification, safety or design; and
5. protect completed work while carrying out site adjustments.

### Why This Lesson Matters
Site conditions rarely behave exactly like a clean workshop drawing. Walls can be uneven, openings can vary, components can arrive damaged and earlier trades can create conflicts. Professional carpentry does not mean hiding these conditions with uncontrolled cutting. It means measuring, diagnosing, communicating and making only authorised corrections.

### Key Terms / Vocabulary
**Site tolerance** — An allowed variation in site dimensions or condition where the project documentation defines one.

**Scribing** — Transferring the profile of an irregular adjoining surface onto a component so it can be fitted accurately using an approved method.

**Rework** — Additional work required to correct a component or installation that does not meet requirements.

**Nonconformance** — A condition where work, material or site conditions do not meet a specified requirement.

**Root cause** — The underlying reason a problem occurred rather than the visible symptom alone.

**Remedial action** — An authorised action taken to correct an identified problem.

**Protection** — Measures used to prevent damage to completed work, finishes and surrounding surfaces during adjustment or installation.

**Escalation** — Referring a problem to the authorised supervisor, designer, client representative or other responsible person when it exceeds the carpenter’s authority.

### Main Lesson Content
Problem solving begins by defining the problem accurately. “The cabinet does not fit” is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Measure the cabinet, opening, floor level, wall plumb and any obstruction. Compare the results with the drawing and specified tolerances. Determine whether the issue originates in the manufactured component, the site condition, the installation position or a combination.

Avoid irreversible action before diagnosis. Trimming a cabinet because the wall is uneven may destroy the specified width without solving the installation problem. Planing a door because it binds may be unnecessary if a loose hinge is causing sag. Adding more screws to a moving component may worsen damage if the substrate is unsuitable.

Use a cause-and-evidence approach. First observe the symptom. Second measure the relevant dimensions or alignment. Third compare with the approved requirement. Fourth identify likely causes. Fifth test those causes using safe, non-destructive checks where possible. Sixth choose an authorised correction. Seventh re-inspect after correction.

Scribing is one controlled method for fitting a component to an irregular adjoining surface. The principle is to transfer the irregular profile to the workpiece while preserving the required installed position. The exact technique depends on the component, allowance, finish and installation system. Do not remove material that affects required dimensions, edge strength or factory finish unless the job method permits it.

Packing and adjustment hardware can compensate for some site variation where designed to do so. Packers must be stable and appropriately positioned. They should not be used to hide a major out-of-tolerance condition. Similarly, adjustable hinges or cabinet fittings should be used within their intended adjustment range, not as a substitute for correcting a wrongly installed carcass.

Damage assessment is another common site task. A chipped edge, wet board, split timber or scratched finish should be evaluated against the quality requirement. Some defects may be repairable; others require replacement. Do not conceal damage with filler or trim unless that repair is authorised and appropriate to the finish and service condition.

Communication is essential when work interfaces with other trades. If plumbing, electrical work, tiling or structural elements conflict with the carpentry installation, record the location and condition. Do not move another trade’s work without authority. A photograph, measurement and drawing reference can make an escalation clear and reduce disputes.

Protect the work during adjustment. Use clean supports, protective coverings and controlled tool placement. Dust and chips from site cutting can damage finished floors or hardware. Secure the workpiece before trimming and isolate powered tools according to safe procedures.

After correction, repeat the relevant quality checks. Confirm fit, level, plumb, square, operation, clearance and surface condition as applicable. Record significant adjustments where the workplace or project requires traceability. A correction is not complete merely because the visible symptom disappears; the finished work must meet the required condition.

### Worked Example / Demonstration
A base cabinet rocks after being placed against a wall. Measurement shows the cabinet carcass is square, but the floor varies. The approved installation system permits packing beneath defined support points. The carpenter levels the cabinet using stable approved packers, confirms support and alignment, then fixes it according to the installation specification. Cutting the cabinet side to follow the floor would have been an unnecessary and damaging response.

### Practical Activity
Complete a Problem-Solving Worksheet for three scenarios: a binding door, an uneven wall beside a cabinet and a shelf unit that is not level after fixing. For each, record symptom, measurements required, possible causes, non-destructive checks, authorised correction options, escalation trigger and final verification.

### Knowledge Check
1. Why is “the door binds” not a complete diagnosis?
2. What is the purpose of comparing measurements with a specification?
3. When should packing not be used to solve a site problem?
4. Why should final checks be repeated after a correction?

### Feedback / Explanation
1. Binding is the symptom; the cause could be frame geometry, hinge position, sag, distortion or another condition.
2. It distinguishes actual nonconformance from normal or permitted variation and guides the correct action.
3. When the condition exceeds the permitted method or tolerance, support would be unstable, or packing would conceal a more serious defect.
4. The correction may have changed another dimension, alignment or operating condition.

### Key Takeaways
- Define and measure the problem before correcting it.
- Treat symptoms and root causes separately.
- Avoid irreversible adjustments until evidence supports them.
- Use scribing, packing and adjustment only where authorised.
- Do not conceal damage or nonconformance.
- Communicate conflicts with other trades clearly.
- Protect completed surfaces during rework.
- Verify the complete requirement after correction.
