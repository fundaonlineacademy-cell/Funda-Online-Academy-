## Questions

**Q1 [L1].** A drawing shows an overall cabinet width of 1000 mm, but component dimensions appear to total 980 mm and no allowance is explained. What is the best response?
A. Add 20 mm to the largest part
B. Continue because 20 mm is small
C. Record the conflict and obtain authorised clarification
D. Split 20 mm equally between all parts
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** A dimensional conflict must be resolved before manufacture.

**Q2 [L1].** A carpenter receives two drawing copies with different shelf positions. Which should be used?
A. The cleaner copy
B. The copy with fewer dimensions
C. The authorised current revision confirmed through the project process
D. Either copy if the overall size matches
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Revision control prevents manufacture from superseded information.

**Q3 [L1].** Which action best demonstrates correct interpretation of a job brief?
A. Starting from a similar previous project
B. Recording purpose, quantity, dimensions, material, hardware and unresolved questions
C. Ordering the cheapest available board
D. Cutting all long parts first
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Interpretation captures the complete confirmed requirement and identifies gaps.

**Q4 [L1].** A printed drawing has a scale statement but has clearly been photocopied at a different size. A written dimension is available. Which should control?
A. Ruler measurement from the photocopy
B. Written authorised dimension
C. Visual proportion
D. Previous project size
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Resizing invalidates graphical measurement; stated dimensions control unless otherwise authorised.

**Q5 [L1].** A drawing does not state the fixing type for a heavy wall-mounted unit. What should the carpenter do?
A. Use the longest screw in stock
B. Choose a familiar anchor
C. Confirm substrate and obtain the applicable fixing requirement
D. Double the number of random screws
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Missing safety-critical fixing information cannot be guessed.

**Q6 [L1].** Why should a specification be read alongside graphical views?
A. Specifications may contain material, grade, hardware and finish requirements not evident from shape alone
B. Views never contain dimensions
C. Specifications replace all drawings
D. It makes measurement unnecessary
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Technical requirements are distributed across drawings and notes/specifications.

**Q7 [L2].** A cutting list contains the correct quantities but one component is identified only as “board piece.” What is the main weakness?
A. It is too descriptive
B. Poor component traceability can cause selection/orientation errors
C. It will increase timber moisture
D. It prevents tool sharpening
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Clear component identity connects production to the drawing.

**Q8 [L2].** Which planning decision best reduces rework?
A. Inspecting only after installation
B. Placing dimensional and square checks before final assembly
C. Skipping dry fit to save time
D. Cutting before material inspection
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Early inspection catches defects while correction remains practical.

**Q9 [L2].** A carpenter changes component layout to use a knotty section that the specification excludes. This is:
A. Good optimisation
B. Acceptable if waste falls
C. Poor optimisation because compliance has been sacrificed
D. Required stock rotation
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Waste reduction never overrides quality/specification.

**Q10 [L2].** Why might an installation sequence need to be planned before final workshop assembly?
A. The completed unit may be too large for access or may block required fixing points
B. Assembly never belongs in a workshop
C. Installation does not use drawings
D. Finished dimensions change automatically on site
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Access and fixing dependencies can determine assembly sequence.

**Q11 [L2].** What is the strongest reason to distinguish finished size from stock/preparation size?
A. Finished dimensions are optional
B. Preparation and machining needs can require different starting dimensions
C. Stock is always smaller
D. Cutting lists use no measurements
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The process may require material to start oversize according to the approved method.

**Q12 [L2].** Which work sequence is most controlled?
A. Assemble → read drawing → cut
B. Verify → inspect material → cut/machine → dry fit → inspect → final assemble
C. Finish → cut → mark
D. Install → prepare cutting list
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** It respects information, material and quality dependencies.

**Q13 [L3].** During dry fit, a rectangular frame has equal diagonals but its width is 10 mm oversize. Is it acceptable?
A. Yes, equal diagonals prove full compliance
B. No, geometry and specified dimensions must both comply
C. Yes, if joints are tight
D. Yes, after sanding
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Squareness does not replace dimensional compliance.

**Q14 [L3].** What is the best response to severely twisted stock intended for a precision frame?
A. Force it straight using extra screws
B. Assess suitability and replace/report if it cannot meet requirements
C. Increase adhesive only
D. Reduce all other members
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Fixings should not be used to conceal fundamentally unsuitable material.

**Q15 [L3].** Why should clamp pressure be monitored during frame assembly?
A. Excessive or uneven pressure can rack or distort the assembly
B. Clamps determine timber species
C. Pressure changes drawing scale
D. It eliminates the need for measurement
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Clamping can alter geometry while joints are closing.

**Q16 [L3].** A frame will form part of regulated structural work. Which principle applies?
A. Select member sizes by visual judgement
B. Follow approved design and applicable requirements
C. Copy a furniture frame
D. Equal diagonals are sufficient
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Structural work requires authorised design/specification.

**Q17 [L3].** What is a useful role of temporary bracing?
A. Permanently replace all joints
B. Maintain intended geometry during handling or installation where specified
C. Hide inaccurate dimensions
D. Reduce material grade
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Bracing can stabilise an assembly until permanent support is established.

**Q18 [L3].** A correctly sized rail is jointed on the wrong face. The component is:
A. Correct because length is right
B. Non-compliant because orientation/joint position is wrong
C. Automatically repairable with paint
D. Improved by rotation after glue-up
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Orientation is part of the component requirement.

**Q19 [L4].** A designer specifies a long shelf for heavy books. What should determine whether the proposed board is suitable?
A. Colour only
B. Material properties, thickness, span, support and expected load under the approved design
C. The cheapest available sheet
D. Number of edge strips
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Shelf performance depends on the complete loading/support system.

**Q20 [L4].** A cabinet is out of square before its back is installed. What is the best action?
A. Fix the back immediately to hide the problem
B. Correct and verify carcass geometry first
C. Increase shelf clearances
D. Trim the doors before checking the carcass
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Finalising the back can lock distortion into the unit.

**Q21 [L4].** Why might a template or jig be useful for adjustable shelf holes?
A. It helps produce consistent aligned positions
B. It changes board thickness
C. It replaces the drawing permanently
D. It increases shelf span
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Repeated support positions require consistent spacing and alignment.

**Q22 [L4].** A wall cabinet is square but the wall is uneven. What should the carpenter avoid?
A. Checking site conditions
B. Forcing the cabinet out of square to match the wall
C. Following an approved packing/scribing method
D. Confirming anchorage
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Site adjustment should not destroy correct cabinet geometry.

**Q23 [L4].** Why is the back panel’s function determined from the design rather than assumed?
A. Some backs contribute to alignment/rigidity while others perform different roles
B. All backs are structural
C. No backs affect geometry
D. Backs are always decorative only
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Construction details vary by product design.

**Q24 [L4].** A tall storage unit appears stable before loading. Why may anchorage still require attention?
A. Apparent unloaded stability does not replace the specified safety/installation requirement
B. Tall units never need fixings
C. Shelves act as anchors
D. Paint prevents movement
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Installation must follow the approved design and expected service conditions.

**Q25 [L5].** A door binds at the top latch corner. Which first action is most professional?
A. Plane the latch edge heavily
B. Inspect hinge seating, frame geometry and door alignment
C. Enlarge the lock recess
D. Cut the frame head
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Diagnosis should identify the cause before irreversible trimming.

**Q26 [L5].** A hinge recess is cut too deep. What can result?
A. Door alignment and clearances may be affected
B. Timber becomes a hardwood
C. The frame becomes level
D. Lock handing changes
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Hinge seating influences leaf position in the frame.

**Q27 [L5].** A factory door states a maximum permitted trimming amount. The carpenter needs to remove more. What is the correct response?
A. Continue because the opening must be fitted
B. Obtain an approved alternative/correction rather than exceed the stated limit
C. Trim equally from all edges without checking
D. Hide the exposed core
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Manufacturer limits form part of the product requirements.

**Q28 [L5].** What should be tested after lock and latch hardware is finally secured?
A. Operation and alignment with the receiver
B. Timber moisture only
C. Shelf span
D. Drawing scale
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Final tightening can influence hardware operation.

**Q29 [L5].** Which condition most clearly requires special-system instructions rather than ordinary fitting practice?
A. A decorative untreated practice door
B. A fire-rated door assembly
C. A scrap mock-up
D. An uninstalled loose hinge sample
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Performance-rated assemblies can depend on tested details and approved components.

**Q30 [L5].** Why should surface protection be used while fitting finished doors?
A. Tools and handling can damage visible finishes
B. It changes hinge capacity
C. It replaces PPE
D. It increases clearance
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Quality includes protecting completed surfaces during work.

**Q31 [L6].** A wall appears to be masonry, but a test/approved information indicates a hollow partition. The drawing assumed masonry. What should happen?
A. Use a masonry anchor anyway
B. Escalate the changed substrate condition and obtain an approved fixing solution
C. Add more adhesive
D. Reduce cabinet width
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** A changed substrate changes the load path and fixing requirements.

**Q32 [L6].** Which statement about anchors is most accurate?
A. Anchor performance depends only on screw length
B. Anchors function as systems involving substrate and specified installation details
C. Any anchor works in any wall
D. More anchors always solve an unsuitable substrate
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Product performance depends on multiple controlled conditions.

**Q33 [L6].** Why is temporary support planned before lifting a large component?
A. To maintain safe control while positioning and fixing
B. To avoid checking level
C. To replace permanent anchors
D. To increase board density
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Stable support reduces handling risk and allows controlled installation.

**Q34 [L6].** A drilled anchor hole is accidentally oversized beyond the fixing manufacturer’s requirement. What is the best action?
A. Fill it with random offcuts
B. Do not improvise; use an approved corrective fixing method or relocate as authorised
C. Tighten harder
D. Add paint
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Incorrect holes can invalidate anchor performance.

**Q35 [L6].** Why should fixings be tightened progressively on a multi-point installation?
A. To monitor and maintain component alignment
B. To change substrate type
C. To avoid reading the drawing
D. To make holes deeper
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Uneven tightening can pull a component out of position.

**Q36 [L6].** Before drilling into a wall, the carpenter should consider:
A. Concealed services
B. Only the visible paint
C. The supplier logo
D. The room temperature alone
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Hidden cables or pipes may be present in the drilling path.

**Q37 [L7].** A cabinet side touches an uneven wall at one point and leaves a varying gap elsewhere. What should happen before trimming?
A. Identify installed position, measure the irregularity and confirm the approved scribing/adjustment method
B. Cut the largest gap from the whole side
C. Force the cabinet against the wall
D. Fill every gap before levelling
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Controlled adjustment starts from measurement and required final position.

**Q38 [L7].** A door binds because a hinge screw is loose. Which correction best addresses the root cause?
A. Plane the door edge
B. Correct the hinge fastening using the approved method
C. Increase frame width
D. Shorten the door
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Correct the cause rather than removing unnecessary material.

**Q39 [L7].** When should a carpenter escalate a conflict with another trade?
A. When resolving it would require unauthorised movement/change of their work or affect the specification
B. Never
C. Only after damaging the component
D. Only after handover
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Trade interfaces must be coordinated through authorised project processes.

**Q40 [L7].** Why is a photograph plus measurement useful when reporting a site nonconformance?
A. It provides clearer evidence of the condition and location
B. It automatically approves a design change
C. It replaces all drawings
D. It guarantees payment
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Objective evidence improves communication and traceability.

**Q41 [L7].** A scratch on a visible finished panel is discovered. What should determine whether it is repaired or replaced?
A. Whether the carpenter has filler available
B. The specified quality requirement and authorised repair method
C. The time of day
D. The panel colour alone
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Defect disposition must follow acceptance requirements.

**Q42 [L7].** A cabinet is level after packing, but one packer is loose and carries no stable support. Is the job complete?
A. Yes, the bubble is centred
B. No, support and installation stability must also meet requirements
C. Yes, if hidden
D. Yes, after adding sealant
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Alignment without stable support is not a satisfactory installation.

**Q43 [L8].** Which stage is the best hold point before permanent glue-up?
A. After final delivery
B. After dry fit and dimensional/square inspection
C. Before reading the drawing
D. After all adhesive has cured
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** This is the last practical stage to correct many assembly errors.

**Q44 [L8].** Why should material identity be verified at receipt?
A. Unsuitable material can otherwise enter production and become harder to correct later
B. It changes the drawing revision
C. It removes the need for inspection
D. It determines staff attendance
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Early control prevents non-compliant material from being built into the job.

**Q45 [L8].** What does a “last reversible check” before cutting aim to confirm?
A. Component, orientation, dimension, waste side and process
B. Customer payment only
C. Workshop temperature only
D. Finished advertising photographs
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** These checks prevent common irreversible set-out errors.

**Q46 [L8].** One part is accidentally machined 5 mm short. Which response is least appropriate?
A. Assess authorised rework or replacement
B. Record the nonconformance
C. Shorten all matching components without approval so the mistake looks intentional
D. Check the drawing requirement
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** A single error should not be propagated into a design change.

**Q47 [L8].** What is the main purpose of in-process inspection?
A. To detect deviations before they accumulate into final defects
B. To replace final inspection entirely
C. To reduce drawing accuracy
D. To avoid documentation
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Quality is controlled throughout production.

**Q48 [L8].** What should final inspection compare the completed project against?
A. The authorised brief, drawings, specifications and applicable acceptance requirements
B. The carpenter’s favourite method
C. A competitor’s product
D. Only the project colour
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Acceptance is based on the actual job requirements.

**Q49 [L8].** A snag is corrected immediately before handover. What should happen next?
A. Nothing; correction proves success
B. Repeat the relevant quality check
C. Delete the snag record
D. Change the drawing
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Rework must be verified because it may affect related conditions.

**Q50 [L8].** Which is part of professional handover?
A. Leaving drilling debris because the work is installed
B. Cleaning, checking operation where relevant and communicating required completion/care information
C. Removing all labels from specialised products regardless of requirement
D. Hiding unresolved defects
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Handover includes condition, operation, cleanliness and communication.

**Q51 [Integrated].** Which principle connects drawing interpretation, cutting lists, assembly and installation?
A. Each stage may invent its own dimensions
B. Requirements must remain traceable from authorised information through completed work
C. Installation can ignore workshop information
D. Final appearance overrides safety
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Traceability maintains technical control across the job lifecycle.

**Q52 [Integrated].** Which behaviour best represents workplace-ready carpentry judgement?
A. Proceeding quickly whenever information is incomplete
B. Measuring, checking, following specifications, controlling irreversible steps and escalating conditions outside authority
C. Using the same fixing and material for every job
D. Hiding rework to avoid questions
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Competence combines technical skill with controlled decisions, safety, quality and communication.
