# Carpentry — Module 9: Quality Control, Defects & Corrective Practice
## Summative Assessment Bank

**Delivery configuration:** 52-question bank; platform selects 25 questions randomly per attempt; A–D multiple choice; pass mark 70%; maximum 3 attempts; prerequisite: Module 9 Formative Assessment passed.

This bank assesses integrated application, diagnosis and workplace judgement across Lessons 1–8.

### Questions

**Q1.** A cabinet matches its target width but the drawing specifies a tighter permitted range than the workshop's usual rule. Which requirement applies? A. Workshop habit B. Drawing/project requirement C. Learner preference D. Largest tolerance available
**Answer: B.** The applicable controlled specification governs acceptance. **Traceability:** L1–L2.

**Q2.** An inspector cannot find a tolerance needed to decide acceptance. What is the best action? A. Invent 2 mm B. Ask another learner to guess C. Obtain the applicable controlled requirement D. Accept automatically
**Answer: C.** Missing acceptance information must be clarified. **Traceability:** L1–L2.

**Q3.** A customer-approved sample shows intentional natural colour variation. One finished component varies similarly but has correct coating and workmanship. The inspector should: A. reject all variation automatically B. compare against the approved appearance requirement C. repaint it black D. ignore the sample
**Answer: B.** Natural variation must be judged against the agreed criterion. **Traceability:** L1, L5.

**Q4.** A component fails one critical specified dimension but passes nine other checks. It is: A. conforming because most checks passed B. non-conforming for the failed requirement C. automatically repairable D. acceptable if expensive
**Answer: B.** A critical failed requirement is not cancelled by other passes. **Traceability:** L1–L2.

**Q5.** Why is “looks fine” insufficient as a quality record? A. Appearance never matters B. It lacks objective requirement/finding evidence C. It is too long D. It contains no price
**Answer: B.** Records should be factual and traceable. **Traceability:** L1.

**Q6.** A batch dimension trends steadily toward its lower limit. No item has failed yet. Best action? A. Continue without concern B. Investigate process drift before rejects occur C. Lower the limit D. Stop measuring
**Answer: B.** Trend control prevents defects and waste. **Traceability:** L2, L7.

**Q7.** Which is most likely to create cumulative error? A. Repeatedly setting positions from a common datum B. Setting each new position from the previous mark C. Checking a drawing revision D. Recording actual dimensions
**Answer: B.** Sequential transfer can accumulate small errors. **Traceability:** L2.

**Q8.** A tape hook is damaged. Measurements differ from a verified steel rule. What should happen? A. Average readings B. Use the tape only on important jobs C. Remove it from reliable inspection use according to procedure D. Increase tolerances
**Answer: C.** Suspect equipment must be controlled. **Traceability:** L2.

**Q9.** A finished panel was correct before aggressive sanding but is now below minimum thickness. Which conclusion is strongest? A. Sanding cannot affect dimensions B. Finishing-stage material removal caused a dimensional non-conformity C. The drawing changed automatically D. Thickness no longer matters
**Answer: B.** Quality must be maintained through later processes. **Traceability:** L2, L5.

**Q10.** A cabinet has equal diagonals but one side is longer than specified. Equal diagonals mean: A. all dimensions automatically conform B. geometry evidence must still be combined with dimensional checks C. the long side may be ignored D. tolerance doubles
**Answer: B.** Diagonal checks do not replace dimensional requirements. **Traceability:** L2–L3.

**Q11.** A frame is square on the bench but out of plumb after installation. Which stage should be investigated? A. Installation/reference/support/fixing B. Timber classification only C. Sanding grit only D. Invoice preparation
**Answer: A.** The defect appeared during installation. **Traceability:** L3, L6.

**Q12.** A spirit level gives opposite indications when reversed on the same surface. The inspector should: A. trust the first reading B. suspect instrument condition and verify it C. adjust the cabinet randomly D. ignore level
**Answer: B.** Inspection instruments must be trustworthy. **Traceability:** L3.

**Q13.** A door binds after the cabinet is fixed to an uneven wall. The best first response is: A. plane the door B. inspect carcass distortion, support, plumb and fixing C. enlarge hinge holes D. remove the finish
**Answer: B.** Diagnose installation geometry before altering the door. **Traceability:** L3, L6.

**Q14.** Which condition is specifically vertical relative to gravity? A. Square B. Plumb C. Flush D. Parallel only
**Answer: B.** Plumb is the vertical reference condition. **Traceability:** L3.

**Q15.** Approved shims are used during installation primarily to: A. hide documentation B. adjust/support position under the specified installation method C. replace all fixings D. change timber grade
**Answer: B.** Shimming can support controlled level/plumb/alignment adjustments. **Traceability:** L3.

**Q16.** A mortise-and-tenon shoulder remains open during dry fit. Which response is strongest? A. Add more glue B. Diagnose debris, joint dimensions, damage and alignment C. Paint over it D. Hammer until timber splits
**Answer: B.** Fit defects require cause investigation. **Traceability:** L4, L6.

**Q17.** A screw head is driven far below the surface of particleboard and the material is crushed. The fixing should be: A. accepted because tighter is always stronger B. inspected against fixing/material requirements C. ignored if hidden D. covered with stain only
**Answer: B.** Over-driving can damage material and performance. **Traceability:** L4.

**Q18.** Which is NOT sufficient proof of a complete adhesive bond? A. Correct process evidence B. Appropriate curing C. Visible edge squeeze-out alone D. Suitable joint preparation
**Answer: C.** Edge squeeze-out alone cannot prove internal bond quality. **Traceability:** L4.

**Q19.** Repeated splitting beside screws most logically triggers review of: A. fastener/pilot-hole/edge-distance/material conditions B. final invoice C. stain colour only D. shelf labels
**Answer: A.** These factors directly influence splitting. **Traceability:** L4, L6.

**Q20.** A drawer joint is structurally sound but protrudes enough to interfere with runners. It: A. passes because strength is all that matters B. fails required fit/function C. passes if hidden D. needs no inspection
**Answer: B.** Quality includes interface and function. **Traceability:** L4, L8.

**Q21.** A clear finish reveals sanding scratches invisible before coating. The likely root stage is: A. surface preparation B. packaging C. hardware ordering D. handover signature
**Answer: A.** Finish can magnify preparation defects. **Traceability:** L5–L6.

**Q22.** A learner uses a universal “30-minute cure time” for every coating. Why is this unsafe academically and practically? A. Cure requirements are product/environment/process-specific B. All coatings cure instantly C. Time never matters D. Only colour matters
**Answer: A.** Manufacturer and project instructions govern drying/cure conditions. **Traceability:** L5.

**Q23.** Patchy stain follows glue-contaminated zones. Adding darker stain without addressing contamination is likely to: A. diagnose the cause B. conceal temporarily or worsen inconsistency C. restore dimensional accuracy D. fix hinges
**Answer: B.** Corrective work should address substrate preparation. **Traceability:** L5–L7.

**Q24.** A coating run appears only on vertical faces after application. Best approach? A. Assume timber species caused it B. investigate application amount, viscosity/system and conditions per product guidance C. increase tolerance D. drill the surface
**Answer: B.** Finish diagnosis considers process and product variables. **Traceability:** L5–L6.

**Q25.** Why inspect edges and corners during finishing? A. They can have thin coverage, accumulation or damage B. Only flat centres matter C. Edges cannot fail D. To avoid checking faces
**Answer: A.** Finish quality must include vulnerable transitions. **Traceability:** L5.

**Q26.** A defect is discovered after machining but before assembly. Why identify the stage of detection? A. It helps narrow likely causes and affected process steps B. It changes the customer C. It removes need for measurements D. It guarantees repair
**Answer: A.** Process-stage evidence improves diagnosis. **Traceability:** L6.

**Q27.** Five identical components fail at exactly the same hole position. Which hypothesis deserves early investigation? A. Jig/datum/setup error B. Random finish colour C. Customer misuse D. Packaging damage only
**Answer: A.** Repeated positional defects indicate a common process source. **Traceability:** L2, L6.

**Q28.** Why should diagnosis avoid blame-first thinking? A. People never make mistakes B. Evidence may reveal system, document, equipment or process causes C. Quality has no responsibility D. Records are unnecessary
**Answer: B.** Root-cause analysis should follow evidence. **Traceability:** L6.

**Q29.** A door is planed to stop binding, but later inspection shows the frame was out of square. This illustrates: A. successful root-cause correction B. symptom treatment without diagnosis C. correct tolerance control D. ideal waste prevention
**Answer: B.** Material was removed before finding the actual cause. **Traceability:** L3, L6–L7.

**Q30.** After correcting a machine setup, what should happen before full production resumes? A. Produce/inspect an appropriate verification piece or follow required verification process B. Assume correction worked C. Remove guards D. Skip specifications
**Answer: A.** Process correction must be verified safely. **Traceability:** L6–L7.

**Q31.** A non-conforming component is found among conforming stock. First control priority? A. Contain/identify it according to procedure B. Hide it C. Install it quickly D. Change the drawing
**Answer: A.** Containment prevents unintended use. **Traceability:** L7.

**Q32.** Which best describes rework? A. Any cosmetic disguise B. Controlled processing intended to bring work back to original requirements C. Customer complaint D. Automatic rejection
**Answer: B.** Rework aims at conformity with original requirements. **Traceability:** L7.

**Q33.** Why can rework require additional inspection beyond the original defect? A. Corrective operations may create secondary defects B. Rework makes specifications irrelevant C. Rework always changes species D. Inspection is optional
**Answer: A.** Material removal, drilling or stripping can affect other characteristics. **Traceability:** L7.

**Q34.** Production management wants an out-of-tolerance item accepted because delivery is late. Quality should: A. change criteria secretly B. follow authorised disposition and applicable acceptance requirements C. erase measurements D. accept because schedule overrides quality
**Answer: B.** Production pressure is not an acceptance criterion. **Traceability:** L1, L7.

**Q35.** Which action produces the greatest long-term waste reduction after recurring short rails? A. Repair each rail only B. Identify and correct the process cause C. Stop measuring rails D. Buy more filler
**Answer: B.** Prevention addresses recurrence. **Traceability:** L6–L7.

**Q36.** A repaired component meets dimensional requirements but has new surface damage from the repair. It should: A. pass because original defect is gone B. remain non-conforming until all applicable criteria are satisfied C. be delivered immediately D. have records deleted
**Answer: B.** Verification covers all affected requirements. **Traceability:** L5, L7.

**Q37.** A final inspection checklist should be based primarily on: A. latest approved job scope and requirements B. memory C. only visible faces D. material cost
**Answer: A.** Release must reflect controlled requirements. **Traceability:** L8.

**Q38.** A cabinet door opens correctly but a required handle is missing. The job is: A. complete B. incomplete/non-conforming to scope C. automatically accepted D. finished if clean
**Answer: B.** Completeness is part of handover quality. **Traceability:** L8.

**Q39.** Why should moving components be operated during final inspection? A. Functional defects may not be visible when stationary B. To wear them out C. To avoid measurements D. To change alignment
**Answer: A.** Functional checking confirms service performance. **Traceability:** L8.

**Q40.** A snag item is corrected. What should happen next? A. Close it without checking B. Verify the correction and update status C. Delete all records D. Add a new tolerance
**Answer: B.** Close-out requires evidence. **Traceability:** L7–L8.

**Q41.** Care information should: A. make unsupported guarantees B. reflect approved product/use/maintenance guidance C. promise timber never moves D. replace the handover inspection
**Answer: B.** Handover communication must be accurate. **Traceability:** L8.

**Q42.** Final inspection discovers glue residue that should have been removed before finishing. This demonstrates why: A. in-process checks reduce expensive late correction B. final inspection is unnecessary C. glue residue improves quality D. specifications should be ignored
**Answer: A.** Early detection prevents downstream rework. **Traceability:** L5, L8.

**Q43.** A component is within dimensional tolerance but visibly cracked in a prohibited area. Is dimensional conformity enough? A. Yes B. No, all applicable criteria must be satisfied C. Only if painted D. Only for hardwood
**Answer: B.** Acceptance is multi-criteria. **Traceability:** L1, L4.

**Q44.** Which record best supports traceability? A. “Fixed it” B. Component ID, defect, requirement, action, re-inspection result and date C. No record if passed later D. Verbal memory only
**Answer: B.** Complete factual records preserve quality history. **Traceability:** L1, L7–L8.

**Q45.** An inspector finds knots and wane in timber. Can the inspector assign a formal structural grade purely from casual appearance? A. Yes always B. No; formal grading follows applicable grading rules/authorised process C. Only after sanding D. Only if timber is pine
**Answer: B.** Defect awareness is not equivalent to formal grading authority. **Traceability:** prior material knowledge integrated with L1, L6.

**Q46.** A finish defect and a joint gap appear in the same product. Which should be corrected first? A. Always finish B. Follow diagnosis, dependency and authorised repair sequence; structural/fit issues may need resolution before refinishing C. Whichever is easiest D. Neither
**Answer: B.** Corrective sequencing must prevent wasted rework and protect performance. **Traceability:** L4–L7.

**Q47.** A cabinet is level but adjacent doors have inconsistent top edges. Which separate condition should be checked? A. Alignment B. Moisture name C. Invoice total D. Tool storage only
**Answer: A.** Level does not guarantee component alignment. **Traceability:** L3.

**Q48.** A process produces correct parts only when one worker “adjusts by feel,” with no controlled setup. The quality risk is mainly: A. lack of repeatable process control B. excessive documentation C. too much traceability D. too many specifications
**Answer: A.** Repeatability depends on controlled references and methods. **Traceability:** L1–L2, L6.

**Q49.** Which sequence is strongest after discovering a defect? A. Hide → deliver → record later B. Describe/contain → compare requirement → diagnose → authorised action → verify → record/prevent recurrence C. Sand → paint → guess D. Change tolerance → accept
**Answer: B.** This is the integrated quality-control cycle. **Traceability:** L1–L8.

**Q50.** An item was reworked successfully, but the same root cause remains in the jig. The quality action is incomplete because: A. only the product was corrected, not recurrence risk B. rework is never allowed C. jigs do not affect quality D. records replace prevention
**Answer: A.** Corrective action should address the cause where appropriate. **Traceability:** L6–L7.

**Q51.** Which principle best protects customers and the academy's professional standard? A. Accept anything that can be hidden B. Make acceptance decisions against defined requirements and retain evidence C. Avoid reporting defects D. Use one tolerance for all jobs
**Answer: B.** Objective, traceable conformity is central to professional quality. **Traceability:** L1–L8.

**Q52.** A final handover should occur when: A. the carpenter is tired B. required work is complete, applicable checks pass, outstanding items are controlled/closed as required and release is authorised C. paint is touch-dry regardless of instructions D. the customer has not inspected
**Answer: B.** Handover follows verified completion and authorised release. **Traceability:** L5, L7–L8.
