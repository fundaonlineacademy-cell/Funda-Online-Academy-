# Carpentry — Module 9: Quality Control, Defects & Corrective Practice
## Formative Assessment Bank

**Delivery configuration:** 32-question bank; platform selects 15 questions randomly per attempt; four options A–D; pass mark 70%; maximum 3 attempts; passing unlocks the Module 9 Summative Assessment.

Each teaching lesson contributes exactly four questions. Questions test understanding, application and judgement rather than copying lesson knowledge checks.

### Lesson 1 — Specifications & Acceptance Criteria

**Q1.** A completed shelf looks attractive but its material thickness differs from the approved specification. What should control the acceptance decision?
A. Appearance alone
B. The applicable specification and authorised acceptance process
C. Amount of labour already spent
D. Learner preference
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Quality acceptance is based on controlled requirements, not appearance or sunk cost.
**Traceability:** Lesson 1.

**Q2.** A drawing and cutting list contain conflicting dimensions for the same component. The best action is to:
A. choose the smaller dimension to save material
B. average the dimensions
C. clarify the current controlled requirement before proceeding
D. use whichever document is nearest
**Correct answer: C.**
**Explanation:** Conflicting job information must be resolved by the authorised source.
**Traceability:** Lesson 1.

**Q3.** Which statement best describes a tolerance?
A. A universal amount of error allowed in carpentry
B. Permitted variation defined by the applicable requirement
C. Permission to ignore a drawing
D. A visual preference
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Tolerances are requirement-specific and must not be invented universally.
**Traceability:** Lesson 1.

**Q4.** Which inspection record is strongest?
A. “Bad cabinet”
B. “Looks wrong”
C. “Left stile measured below specified minimum; item contained for review”
D. “Fix later”
**Correct answer: C.**
**Explanation:** Objective requirement, finding and control action provide traceability.
**Traceability:** Lesson 1.

### Lesson 2 — Dimensional Inspection & Tolerance Checking

**Q5.** Why should repeated component measurements use the same defined datum?
A. To increase permitted tolerance
B. To reduce reference inconsistency and cumulative error
C. To eliminate the need for drawings
D. To make parts heavier
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Consistent reference points improve repeatability.
**Traceability:** Lesson 2.

**Q6.** Five rails become progressively shorter although the first remains within limits. What is the best quality response?
A. Wait until a rail definitely fails
B. Investigate possible process drift now
C. Increase the tolerance
D. Stop measuring
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** A trend can reveal setup or process movement before rejects multiply.
**Traceability:** Lesson 2.

**Q7.** A steel rule has a damaged end and gives suspicious readings. The learner should:
A. compensate by guessing
B. continue because small tools cannot affect quality
C. stop relying on it and follow the verification/replacement process
D. enlarge every component
**Correct answer: C.**
**Explanation:** Inspection equipment must be suitable and serviceable.
**Traceability:** Lesson 2.

**Q8.** Which measurement approach best reduces accumulated marking error on repeated shelf positions?
A. Measure each new position from the previous mark only
B. Use a consistent baseline/datum where appropriate
C. Estimate equal spaces visually
D. Change rulers between every mark
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Referencing a common datum can prevent cumulative error.
**Traceability:** Lesson 2.

### Lesson 3 — Checking Square, Level, Plumb & Alignment

**Q9.** A cabinet carcass has equal controlled diagonals and square corners but rocks on an uneven floor. Which quality condition should be investigated during installation?
A. Level/support
B. Timber species name
C. Paint brand only
D. Joint vocabulary
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Square geometry does not guarantee a level, supported installation.
**Traceability:** Lesson 3.

**Q10.** A door gap widens from bottom to top. Before adjusting hinges, the carpenter should first:
A. diagnose carcass/frame geometry and installation condition
B. sand the door immediately
C. paint the gap
D. replace all handles
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Hinge adjustment should not conceal an underlying geometry problem.
**Traceability:** Lesson 3.

**Q11.** Which statement is correct?
A. Level and plumb mean exactly the same thing
B. Square and alignment are always interchangeable
C. Level concerns horizontal relation to gravity; plumb concerns vertical relation
D. A square cabinet must be level
**Correct answer: C.**
**Explanation:** These are distinct geometric conditions.
**Traceability:** Lesson 3.

**Q12.** An installation wall is out of plumb. The carpenter should:
A. weaken fixings until the cabinet looks straight
B. follow approved adjustment, support and fixing methods
C. ignore the condition
D. invent a site tolerance
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Site irregularity must be handled within safe specified installation methods.
**Traceability:** Lesson 3.

### Lesson 4 — Joint & Fixing Inspection

**Q13.** An open joint shoulder is found before gluing. What is the strongest response?
A. Fill it immediately
B. Diagnose fit, debris, machining and alignment before final assembly
C. Add more finish
D. Drive an unrelated nail through it
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** The underlying cause should be identified while correction is still controlled.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4.

**Q14.** Why does visible glue squeeze-out not by itself prove a sound bond?
A. Adhesive never works
B. It shows only an edge observation, not complete internal preparation, coverage and curing
C. Squeeze-out means the joint is dry
D. Bonding is unrelated to fit
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Bond quality depends on the full adhesive system and joint condition.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4.

**Q15.** Screws repeatedly split timber near the same edge. What should be investigated?
A. Pilot-hole requirements, fastener selection, edge distance and material condition
B. Only paint colour
C. Customer address
D. Packaging labels
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Repeated splitting suggests a controlled fixing/process issue.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4.

**Q16.** A hinge is firmly fixed but the door binds because the hinge is incorrectly positioned. The fixing is:
A. automatically acceptable because it is tight
B. non-conforming in position/function
C. acceptable if hidden
D. unrelated to quality
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Secure fastening does not compensate for incorrect location or function.
**Traceability:** Lesson 4.

### Lesson 5 — Surface & Finish Inspection

**Q17.** Cross-grain scratches become visible after clear coating. Which stage should be investigated first?
A. Surface preparation/sanding
B. Door hardware
C. Packaging quantity
D. Shelf spacing
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Finish often highlights underlying preparation defects.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5.

**Q18.** Why is raking light useful during surface inspection?
A. It cures every finish
B. It makes irregularities such as scratches and ripples easier to see
C. It measures moisture content
D. It replaces the specification
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Shallow-angle light exposes surface texture and defects.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5.

**Q19.** A coating is touch-dry. The learner may assume it is fully cured and ready for any service.
A. True
B. False
C. Only on MDF
D. Only if glossy
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Drying, recoating, handling and full cure times are product-specific.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5.

**Q20.** A finish repair should be selected primarily according to:
A. whichever coating is cheapest
B. the approved compatible finish system, defect and manufacturer/workplace procedure
C. a universal repair recipe
D. the darkest available stain
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Compatibility and controlled procedure prevent secondary failure.
**Traceability:** Lesson 5.

### Lesson 6 — Diagnosing Common Carpentry Defects

**Q21.** A drawer sticks. What should happen before material is removed?
A. Diagnose geometry, fit, hardware and likely cause
B. Plane every edge
C. Increase all clearances
D. Discard the drawing
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Removing material before diagnosis can hide or worsen the actual defect.
**Traceability:** Lesson 6.

**Q22.** Twenty panels have breakout at the same machining location. This pattern most strongly suggests:
A. a systematic material/tool/setup/process cause should be investigated
B. every panel failed independently by coincidence
C. inspection should stop
D. the tolerance should be enlarged
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Repeated patterns are evidence of a common cause.
**Traceability:** Lesson 6.

**Q23.** Why should the original defect be documented before corrective work where practicable?
A. To preserve evidence for diagnosis and traceability
B. To prevent any repair
C. To avoid measuring
D. To increase paperwork only
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Original condition helps establish cause and evaluate recurrence.
**Traceability:** Lesson 6.

**Q24.** After a suspected process cause is corrected, the best next step is to:
A. assume success
B. verify the process/item against requirements before normal continuation
C. erase the defect record
D. stop inspection permanently
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Corrective action requires verification.
**Traceability:** Lesson 6.

### Lesson 7 — Corrective Action, Rework & Waste Prevention

**Q25.** The immediate purpose of containing a non-conforming item is to:
A. make it look better
B. prevent unintended downstream use or delivery
C. increase production speed
D. avoid recording it
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Containment controls risk while disposition is decided.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7.

**Q26.** An undersize critical rail is discovered. The learner should:
A. assume sanding will restore size
B. follow authorised disposition rather than inventing a repair
C. stretch the timber
D. change the drawing
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Not every non-conformity can be reworked to original requirements.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7.

**Q27.** Why must reworked work be inspected again?
A. Rework can create secondary defects and conformity must be verified
B. It always changes timber species
C. Inspection is decorative
D. Only to count labour hours
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** The final condition must satisfy the applicable criteria.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7.

**Q28.** Which action best prevents waste from a moving machine stop?
A. Rework each defective part forever
B. Correct and verify the process cause, then inspect affected work
C. Stop recording measurements
D. Increase the allowed error
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Process corrective action prevents recurrence.
**Traceability:** Lesson 7.

### Lesson 8 — Final Inspection, Documentation & Handover

**Q29.** Which item belongs in a final cabinet inspection?
A. Door/drawer function
B. Only invoice colour
C. Employee lunch choice
D. Unrelated tool brands
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Functional performance is part of final conformity.
**Traceability:** Lesson 8.

**Q30.** A snag list should identify:
A. unresolved defects/incomplete items and their close-out status
B. only completed perfect work
C. only material suppliers
D. staff attendance
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Snagging controls outstanding close-out work.
**Traceability:** Lesson 8.

**Q31.** Why should final inspection use the latest approved job information?
A. Requirements may have approved revisions or changes
B. Old information is always better
C. It makes measuring unnecessary
D. It guarantees no defects without inspection
**Correct answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Handover must reflect the controlled final scope and requirements.
**Traceability:** Lesson 8.

**Q32.** Which statement best describes final inspection?
A. It replaces all earlier quality checks
B. It confirms the completed result but should be supported by in-process quality control
C. It is optional if the item looks clean
D. It is only a customer signature
**Correct answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Quality should be controlled throughout the work, with final inspection as the release check.
**Traceability:** Lesson 8.
