## Lesson 1 — Power Tool Safety Principles & Pre-use Checks

### Q1
A learner is given a portable power tool that is unfamiliar to them. What should happen first?
A. Start it briefly to learn how it behaves
B. Use it only after its correct use, controls, hazards and required supervision are understood
C. Ask another learner to demonstrate it while it is running
D. Use maximum speed so the task finishes quickly

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Competence and correct instruction come before operation. Familiarity must not be gained through uncontrolled trial and error.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — competence and safe selection.

### Q2
Which sequence best represents a professional pre-use check?
A. Switch on, inspect the result, then select PPE
B. Select the tool, inspect condition and guards, verify accessory/setup, prepare the work area and workholding, then operate
C. Fit any available accessory and test it on scrap
D. Check only the power lead because internal parts are more important

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** A pre-use check considers suitability, condition, guarding, accessory compatibility, workholding and the environment before operation.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — systematic pre-use checks.

### Q3
A guard is damaged but the machine still starts. What is the best action?
A. Continue if the operator wears gloves
B. Remove or control the equipment according to workplace procedure and report the defect
C. Hold the guard in position by hand
D. Work more slowly

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** A functioning motor does not make defective safety equipment acceptable. Defects affecting safe use require reporting and appropriate out-of-service control.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — guards and defect response.

### Q4
Why must an accessory be compatible with the specific power tool?
A. Compatibility only affects appearance
B. Incorrect accessories can create unsafe loading, poor control, damage or failure
C. Any accessory will work if the operator reduces speed
D. Compatibility matters only for cordless tools

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Accessory type, size, fixing method and operating limits must suit the tool and task.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — accessory compatibility.

### Q5
During operation a tool develops unusual vibration and a burning smell. What is the best response?
A. Finish the cut before stopping
B. Increase pressure so the tool cannot vibrate
C. Stop safely, isolate as appropriate and report/investigate the abnormal condition before reuse
D. Spray lubricant into the ventilation slots while it runs

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Abnormal vibration, smell, heat or sound can indicate a fault. Continuing can increase injury and equipment-damage risk.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — abnormal-condition response.

### Q6
Which statement about PPE is most accurate?
A. PPE makes guarding unnecessary
B. PPE is one part of risk control and does not justify bypassing safer controls
C. PPE is required only for cutting tools
D. PPE removes the need for training

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** PPE supplements, rather than replaces, suitable equipment, guarding, safe systems, workholding, extraction and competent operation.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1 — hierarchy of controls.

## Lesson 2 — Portable Circular Saws

### Q7
Before making a circular-saw cut, why should the operator identify the waste side of the line?
A. To know where the saw cord should lie
B. To account for the kerf and preserve the required finished dimension
C. To decide which hand holds the saw
D. To increase blade speed

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The blade removes material as kerf; planning which side is waste helps protect the required dimension.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — kerf and cut planning.

### Q8
A circular saw's lower guard sticks and does not return freely. What should the learner do?
A. Tie it open and continue
B. Hold it open manually throughout the cut
C. Remove the saw from use and report the defect according to procedure
D. Apply more cutting pressure

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** A defective guard is a safety-critical fault and must not be bypassed.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — guard inspection.

### Q9
Why is workpiece support important when using a portable circular saw?
A. It prevents the motor from starting too quickly
B. It helps prevent movement and reduces the chance of the kerf closing and trapping the blade
C. It makes PPE unnecessary
D. It guarantees a splinter-free cut

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Poor support can allow the workpiece or offcut to move, close the kerf and contribute to binding or kickback.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — work support and kickback prevention.

### Q10
The blade begins to bind halfway through a cut. What is the best immediate response?
A. Force the saw forward
B. Twist the saw sideways
C. Release the control, maintain control until the blade stops, then diagnose the cause safely
D. Pull the running saw backwards through the cut

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Forcing or twisting a binding saw can worsen loss of control. Stop first, then investigate support, alignment, blade condition and setup.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — binding response.

### Q11
Which factor should determine circular-saw blade selection?
A. The blade that is already fitted, regardless of material
B. Compatibility with the saw, material and intended cut, within manufacturer requirements
C. The largest blade available
D. Blade colour

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Blade suitability must be based on the tool, material, operation and manufacturer's limits.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — blade selection.

### Q12
Why should the operator trace the blade path below the workpiece before cutting?
A. To estimate battery life
B. To confirm the blade will not contact supports, clamps, cables or other hidden obstructions
C. To select hearing protection
D. To determine timber species

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The blade projects below the workpiece, so the complete cutting path must be clear.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — cut-path preparation.

### Q13
After completing a circular-saw cut, what should happen before the saw is set down?
A. The blade should be stopped and the guard allowed to return to its protective position
B. The blade should be slowed by pressing it against scrap
C. The guard should be held open to cool the blade
D. The saw should be unplugged while the blade is still cutting

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Maintain control through run-down and allow designed guarding to protect the blade before setting the tool down.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2 — completion and run-down.

## Lesson 3 — Jigsaws & Reciprocating Saws

### Q14
Which task is generally better suited to a jigsaw than a reciprocating saw?
A. Controlled curved cutting in a plywood panel
B. Rough removal of damaged framing where finish is unimportant
C. Rapid demolition cutting
D. Cutting inaccessible rough material with no finish requirement

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Jigsaws are suited to controlled curves, shapes and shorter precision-oriented cuts; reciprocating saws are generally rougher cutting tools.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — tool selection.

### Q15
Why should a jigsaw blade be selected for the material and thickness?
A. Blade selection has no effect if the speed is high
B. An unsuitable blade can cut poorly, deflect, bind or fail
C. Only the shoe controls the cut
D. Any blade can cut any material if forced slowly

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Blade design and compatibility influence cutting control, quality and safety.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — blade selection.

### Q16
A learner needs to make an internal opening in a board with a jigsaw. What is the safest general planning principle?
A. Plunge the moving blade into the surface regardless of tool design
B. Use an approved starting method such as a drilled starter hole unless the manufacturer specifically permits another method
C. Remove the jigsaw shoe
D. Start the saw with the blade pressed hard against the material

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Internal cuts require a safe blade-entry method. A starter hole is a controlled general approach where suitable; manufacturer instructions govern tool-specific methods.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — internal cuts.

### Q17
Why should a jigsaw not be forced sideways around a tight curve?
A. It makes the motor quieter
B. Side pressure can bend or deflect the blade, reduce accuracy and increase failure risk
C. It causes the cord to become longer
D. It changes timber moisture content

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The blade must be allowed to follow a radius it can cut without excessive lateral loading.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — controlled curved cutting.

### Q18
What is especially important before using a reciprocating saw in an existing wall or structure?
A. Confirm hidden services, fasteners and what lies beyond the cut
B. Remove the shoe from the saw
C. Use the longest blade regardless of the task
D. Hold the work with one hand

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Reciprocating blades travel through and beyond material; hidden electrical, plumbing or structural elements can create serious hazards.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — hidden-service awareness.

### Q19
A reciprocating-saw blade binds. What should the operator do?
A. Twist the running saw until it releases
B. Stop, allow movement to cease, isolate as needed and investigate support, blade and cut conditions
C. Increase force immediately
D. Grip the blade with gloves while it moves

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Binding is a stop-and-diagnose condition, not a reason to force or twist the running tool.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — fault response.

### Q20
Why should a recently used saw blade be treated cautiously after stopping?
A. It may remain hot enough to cause a burn
B. It becomes electrically charged permanently
C. It immediately becomes blunt
D. It changes size permanently

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Cutting friction can heat blades. Run-down and residual heat remain hazards after the trigger is released.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3 — post-use safety.

## Lesson 4 — Drills, Drivers & Boring Operations

### Q21
What is the main difference between drilling and driving in carpentry power-tool work?
A. Drilling creates or enlarges holes; driving installs or removes suitable fasteners
B. Driving creates holes and drilling sands surfaces
C. They are identical operations
D. Drilling is only for metal

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** The operations may use similar tool bodies but have different purposes, accessories and setup requirements.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — drilling versus driving.

### Q22
Why should a small workpiece normally be secured before drilling?
A. To make the drill heavier
B. To prevent the bit from rotating
C. To prevent the workpiece from spinning, shifting or being pulled by the bit
D. To eliminate the need for eye protection

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Workholding protects control, accuracy and the operator when the bit enters or exits the material.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — workholding.

### Q23
A keyed chuck has been tightened. What must happen before starting the drill?
A. Leave the chuck key in place for later adjustment
B. Remove the chuck key and verify the bit is secure
C. Hold the chuck key with the free hand
D. Start at maximum speed to eject the key

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** A chuck key left in the chuck can be thrown when the drill starts.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — chuck safety.

### Q24
What is a pilot hole commonly used for in screw fixing?
A. To guide the screw and reduce splitting or excessive driving resistance where appropriate
B. To increase the screw head diameter
C. To replace the screw
D. To make the timber wetter

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Correctly planned pilot holes can improve alignment and reduce splitting, especially near edges or in harder material.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — pilot preparation.

### Q25
Before drilling into an installed wall or fixed component, what additional check is essential?
A. Whether the drill matches the wall colour
B. Whether hidden electrical, plumbing or other services may be in the drilling path
C. Whether the operator can drill one-handed
D. Whether the bit is the longest available

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** The unseen path behind the surface must be considered before penetration.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — hidden services.

### Q26
A screw-driver bit repeatedly cams out of the screw head. What should the operator check first?
A. Increase force until the screw moves
B. Correct bit type/size, alignment, screw condition and driving setup
C. Lubricate the trigger
D. Hold the screw with fingers beside the rotating bit

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Cam-out often indicates mismatch, poor alignment or unsuitable settings and should be corrected rather than overcome with force.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — controlled driving.

### Q27
Why should a drill or driver be allowed to stop before being put down?
A. Rotating accessories can still catch material, clothing or surfaces
B. It preserves battery colour
C. It changes the bit diameter
D. It increases screw strength

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Residual rotation remains a mechanical hazard until the accessory has stopped.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4 — run-down control.

## Lesson 5 — Routers & Edge/Forming Operations

### Q28
Which operation is a router commonly suited to?
A. Forming an edge profile or controlled groove with the correct bit and guidance
B. Hammering nails
C. Measuring moisture content
D. Holding a workpiece during assembly

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Routers use rotating cutters for controlled shaping, trimming, grooving and related operations when correctly set up.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — router applications.

### Q29
Before changing a router bit, what is the correct general action?
A. Keep the router running at low speed
B. Disconnect/isolate the energy source according to the tool and procedure
C. Hold the bit with one hand and tap the switch
D. Remove the base while powered

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Bit changes and adjustments must not be made while the cutter can start or rotate unexpectedly.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — isolation before adjustment.

### Q30
Why must the router bit and collet be compatible and correctly secured?
A. To prevent unsafe movement or release of the rotating cutter and to maintain cutting accuracy
B. Only to improve appearance
C. To make the router lighter
D. Because collets determine timber species

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Secure, compatible cutter retention is safety-critical at router operating speeds.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — bit and collet setup.

### Q31
Why are several controlled passes often preferable to attempting an excessive cut in one pass?
A. They can improve control, reduce loading and support better finish quality
B. They remove the need for workholding
C. They allow damaged bits to be used
D. They eliminate dust

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Progressive material removal can reduce tool loading and improve control; actual limits must follow the tool, bit, material and manufacturer guidance.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — progressive depth control.

### Q32
What is the purpose of a fence, bearing, guide or template in routing?
A. To provide controlled reference for the cutter path when used as designed
B. To increase electrical voltage
C. To replace the router base
D. To stop the cutter instantly

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Guidance systems help control position and consistency of the cut.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — guidance methods.

### Q33
The router begins producing unusual vibration. What should the operator do?
A. Press harder against the workpiece
B. Stop safely and investigate bit security, damage, buildup, setup and tool condition before reuse
C. Remove the base while running
D. Ignore it if the cut still looks acceptable

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Abnormal vibration can indicate unsafe cutter or tool conditions and requires a stop-and-check response.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — fault diagnosis.

### Q34
Why should a workpiece be securely held during routing?
A. Routing forces can move the material and destroy control of the cutter path
B. It changes the router's rated speed
C. It makes the bit sharper
D. It eliminates noise

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Stable workholding is fundamental to control, safety and dimensional quality.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5 — workholding.

## Lesson 6 — Sanders, Abrasives & Dust Control

### Q35
What is the purpose of using an appropriate abrasive sequence rather than jumping immediately from very coarse to very fine abrasive?
A. To progressively remove previous scratches and refine the surface efficiently
B. To increase timber moisture
C. To avoid inspecting the surface
D. To make dust extraction unnecessary

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Abrasive progression is a controlled surface-preparation process; skipping unsuitable stages can leave defects visible.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — abrasive progression.

### Q36
Which sander is generally associated with rapid material removal on broad, suitable surfaces and therefore requires careful control to avoid gouging?
A. Belt sander
B. Screwdriver
C. Jigsaw
D. Drill press

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Belt sanders can remove material aggressively and must be selected and controlled for the task.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — sander selection.

### Q37
Why is wood dust control more than a housekeeping issue?
A. Fine airborne dust can create health exposure as well as visibility, cleanliness and other workplace hazards
B. Dust only changes surface colour
C. Dust cannot leave the sanding area
D. Dust is harmless when produced by power tools

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Sanding and other woodworking processes can generate fine dust. Suitable extraction, cleaning and respiratory controls may be required according to risk and workplace procedure.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — dust control.

### Q38
What should happen if a sander's extraction port is blocked and dust control is no longer effective?
A. Continue until the task is finished
B. Stop safely and correct/report the dust-control problem according to procedure before continuing
C. Blow the dust toward another worker
D. Remove all guarding

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** A failed control should be addressed rather than accepted as normal operating condition.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — extraction and fault response.

### Q39
A learner sees deep swirl marks after sanding. What is the best professional response?
A. Apply finish immediately to hide them
B. Identify likely causes such as abrasive condition, contamination, excessive pressure or poor technique and correct the surface systematically
C. Wet the timber heavily
D. Increase pressure without changing anything else

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Surface defects should be diagnosed and corrected before finishing, not concealed.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — sanding defect diagnosis.

### Q40
Why should a sanding workpiece be stable and adequately supported?
A. To maintain control and prevent movement that can cause injury or uneven surface preparation
B. To increase abrasive grit number
C. To remove the need for extraction
D. To change the machine's electrical rating

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Stable support improves both safety and surface quality.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6 — workholding and quality.

## Lesson 7 — Bench & Workshop Machinery Awareness

### Q41
A learner has watched another person operate a band saw but has not been trained or authorised. What should the learner do?
A. Operate it slowly without supervision
B. Use it only after required training, authorisation and machine-specific instruction
C. Remove the guard to see the blade better
D. Ask another learner to stand nearby

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Observation alone does not establish competence. Fixed woodworking machinery requires machine-specific safe operating knowledge and workplace authorisation.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — training and authorisation.

### Q42
Why are machine guards important on fixed woodworking machinery?
A. They reduce access to dangerous moving or cutting parts while allowing the machine to perform its intended function
B. They are mainly decorative
C. They make training unnecessary
D. They guarantee that no accident can occur

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Guarding is a primary engineering control but still works together with training, correct setup, workholding and safe procedures.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — guarding.

### Q43
A guard has been removed from a machine for maintenance and not refitted. What should an operator do?
A. Use the machine for small jobs only
B. Do not operate it; report the condition and ensure proper restoration by authorised personnel
C. Stand farther away
D. Wear an extra pair of gloves

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Required guarding must not be bypassed simply because the machine can still run.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — unsafe machine condition.

### Q44
Why should the area around fixed machinery be kept clear?
A. Operators need stable access, movement and escape space without avoidable trip or interference hazards
B. It improves timber colour
C. It changes cutting speed
D. It eliminates the need for guards

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Machine safety includes the operating zone, not only the machine itself.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — operating zones.

### Q45
What is the purpose of a correctly designed push device or jig where required by the machine/task?
A. To help control material while keeping hands farther from the danger zone
B. To increase blade speed
C. To replace all machine guards
D. To permit untrained operation

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Suitable push devices and jigs can improve workpiece control and hand separation, but they do not replace guarding or competence.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — workpiece control.

### Q46
A fixed machine produces a new knocking sound during operation. What is the best response?
A. Continue because the machine is bolted to the floor
B. Stop safely and have the abnormal condition assessed according to workplace procedure before reuse
C. Feed material faster
D. Remove the guard to inspect while running

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Unexpected noise can indicate a fault or loose/damaged component and requires controlled shutdown and assessment.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7 — abnormal-condition response.

## Lesson 8 — Maintenance, Accessories, Isolation & Fault Reporting

### Q47
Which task is most appropriate for an ordinary trained operator when workplace procedures allow it?
A. Routine cleaning and approved basic inspection within the operator's authorised scope
B. Rewiring a damaged motor without electrical competence
C. Modifying a guard to fit a different accessory
D. Bypassing an interlock to diagnose a fault

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Operators must remain within defined maintenance boundaries. Specialist repair and modification require appropriate competence and authorisation.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — maintenance boundaries.

### Q48
Why should a power source be isolated before changing an accessory or performing an adjustment that requires isolation?
A. To reduce the chance of unexpected startup or movement
B. To make the accessory sharper
C. To improve timber grain
D. To reset the learner's assessment score

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Isolation controls unintended energisation during tasks where hands or tools may enter danger areas.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — isolation.

### Q49
A learner discovers a cracked power-tool housing. What is the best response?
A. Cover it with tape and continue
B. Stop using the tool, control it according to the workplace out-of-service process and report the defect
C. Use it only for light work
D. Ask another learner to test it

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Structural or electrical defects should not be improvised around. Defective equipment must be controlled and assessed appropriately.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — defect reporting.

### Q50
What information makes a fault report most useful?
A. A factual description of the tool, observed condition, when/how it occurred and actions already taken
B. A guess about who caused the fault
C. Only the operator's name
D. A statement that the tool is 'bad'

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Clear factual information helps competent personnel assess the fault and prevents unsafe assumptions.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — fault reporting.

### Q51
Why should accessories be inspected and stored correctly after use?
A. Damage, contamination, corrosion or poor storage can affect future safe performance and cut quality
B. Storage changes manufacturer ratings
C. Accessories become safer when mixed together
D. Inspection is unnecessary if they worked once

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Care and inspection help identify deterioration before the next use and support reliable workmanship.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — accessory care.

### Q52
A machine has been isolated for repair by authorised maintenance personnel. A learner needs it for an urgent job. What should the learner do?
A. Remove the isolation because the job is urgent
B. Respect the isolation and use only an approved alternative after proper authorisation and planning
C. Restart the machine briefly to see whether it works
D. Ask another learner to remove the tag/control

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Isolation must not be defeated by unauthorised persons. Production pressure does not override safety controls.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8 — isolation integrity.
