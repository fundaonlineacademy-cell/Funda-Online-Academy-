## Lesson 1 — Power Tool Safety Principles & Pre-use Checks

### Q1
Before using a portable power tool, what should the operator establish first?

A. Whether the tool is the newest available model
B. Whether the tool and accessory are suitable for the intended task
C. Whether the tool can complete the task without workholding
D. Whether another worker has used the tool that day

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Safe operation begins with selecting a tool and accessory that are designed and rated for the intended task. A well-maintained tool can still be unsafe if it is unsuitable for the operation.

### Q2
During a pre-use check, a learner discovers that a safety guard does not move or return as designed. What is the best action?

A. Hold the guard away from the work by hand
B. Continue if the cut will take only a few seconds
C. Remove the tool from use and report the defect according to workplace procedure
D. Apply additional PPE and continue

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** A defective safety device is a reason to stop. PPE does not make bypassing or ignoring a defective guard acceptable.

### Q3
Which condition should normally trigger an immediate stop during power-tool operation?

A. The operator reaches the middle of the workpiece
B. The tool produces unexpected vibration, smell, sound or heat
C. Sawdust appears near the work area
D. The operator has already completed one similar task

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Unexpected vibration, smell, sound or heat can indicate a damaged accessory, loose component, electrical fault or other unsafe condition. The operator should stop safely and investigate only within their authorised competence.

### Q4
Why is secure workholding important when using many power tools?

A. It eliminates the need for pre-use checks
B. It prevents every possible tool defect
C. It helps prevent uncontrolled workpiece movement and improves accuracy
D. It allows guards to be removed when space is limited

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Stable workholding supports control, accuracy and safety. It does not replace guards, inspection or correct operating technique.

## Lesson 2 — Portable Circular Saws

### Q5
When planning a circular-saw cut, why should the operator consider the kerf and waste side of the line?

A. To decide where PPE should be stored
B. To preserve the required finished dimension
C. To increase the blade speed
D. To reduce the need for work support

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** The blade removes material as a kerf. Positioning the cut correctly relative to the marked line helps preserve the required finished dimension.

### Q6
What is the safest response if the lower guard of a portable circular saw sticks during its pre-use check?

A. Tie the guard open
B. Operate the saw slowly
C. Remove the saw from service and report the defect
D. Use the saw only on thin material

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** A circular saw must not be used with a defective guard. The defect requires proper reporting and authorised repair or maintenance.

### Q7
Which setup best reduces the likelihood of a circular-saw blade becoming pinched during a cut?

A. Supporting the work so the kerf can remain open and the workpiece is stable
B. Holding both sides of the workpiece loosely by hand
C. Removing the lower guard before starting
D. Increasing cutting speed whenever resistance is felt

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Correct support prevents the workpiece from closing onto the blade and reduces binding and loss of control.

### Q8
After completing a circular-saw cut, what should the operator do before setting the saw down?

A. Touch the blade to check its temperature
B. Allow the blade to stop and ensure the guard has returned correctly
C. Hold the guard open so dust can fall out
D. Immediately change the blade while it is slowing

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** The moving blade remains hazardous after the trigger is released. The operator should maintain control until the blade stops and the guard functions correctly.

## Lesson 3 — Jigsaws & Reciprocating Saws

### Q9
A learner needs to cut a controlled curved profile in a timber panel. Which tool is generally more appropriate?

A. Jigsaw with a suitable blade
B. Reciprocating saw with a demolition blade
C. Impact driver
D. Random-orbit sander

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** A jigsaw is commonly selected for controlled curved or shaped cuts when fitted with a blade suitable for the material and required cut.

### Q10
Why can excessive feed pressure cause poor jigsaw accuracy?

A. It makes the base plate larger
B. It can encourage blade deflection and wandering
C. It automatically changes the marked line
D. It prevents the motor from running

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Forcing a jigsaw can bend or deflect the blade, producing an angled or wandering cut and increasing the risk of blade damage.

### Q11
Before using a reciprocating saw in an existing wall or structure, what additional hazard requires particular attention?

A. The colour of the saw housing
B. Hidden electrical cables, pipes or other services
C. Whether the workpiece has been sanded
D. The brand of the extension lead

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** A long reciprocating blade can travel beyond the visible surface. Hidden electrical, plumbing or other services must be considered before cutting.

### Q12
What is the best response if a jigsaw blade repeatedly wanders away from the intended cut?

A. Apply much more sideways force
B. Continue and repair the work later
C. Stop and check blade suitability, condition, support and feed technique
D. Remove the base plate

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Repeated wandering indicates a setup or technique problem. The operator should diagnose the cause rather than force the tool.

## Lesson 4 — Drills, Drivers & Boring Operations

### Q13
What is the main purpose of a pilot hole when driving a screw into timber in an appropriate application?

A. To remove the need for a screwdriver bit
B. To guide the screw and help reduce splitting or excessive driving resistance
C. To make the screw longer
D. To replace the screw thread

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** A correctly selected pilot hole can guide the screw, reduce splitting risk and improve control. Exact pilot size depends on the fastener, material and manufacturer or project requirements.

### Q14
A drill bit has been fitted using a keyed chuck. What must be confirmed before the drill is started?

A. The chuck key has been removed
B. The chuck key is attached to the power cord
C. The drill is held by the chuck
D. The bit is touching the operator's hand

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** A chuck key left in the chuck can be thrown when the drill starts. It must be removed before energising the tool.

### Q15
Why should a small workpiece normally be clamped or otherwise securely held before drilling?

A. To make the drill battery last longer
B. To prevent the workpiece from spinning or moving unexpectedly
C. To eliminate the need to select the correct bit
D. To increase the drill's rated capacity

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** The drill bit can catch and rotate or move an unsecured workpiece. Secure workholding improves both safety and accuracy.

### Q16
A screw head is repeatedly being damaged while driving. Which response is most appropriate?

A. Increase force without checking anything
B. Check that the driver bit matches the screw head and is in good condition
C. Lubricate the trigger
D. Remove the workholding

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Poor bit-to-screw fit, worn bits, poor alignment or unsuitable torque can damage the screw head. Correct matching is a basic first check.

## Lesson 5 — Routers & Edge/Forming Operations

### Q17
Before changing a router bit, what should happen first?

A. The router should be disconnected or otherwise made safe against unintended start according to its design and workplace procedure
B. The bit should be held while the motor runs slowly
C. The depth setting should be increased to maximum
D. The workpiece should be removed from all support

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Accessory changes must not be made while the router can start unexpectedly. Isolation or disconnection must follow the tool design, manufacturer instructions and workplace procedure.

### Q18
Why are multiple controlled passes often preferable to trying to remove a large amount of material in one router pass?

A. They remove the need for a guide
B. They improve control and can reduce overload, tear-out and poor finish
C. They make an unsuitable router bit acceptable
D. They allow the operator to ignore grain direction

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Progressive material removal can improve control and finish while reducing excessive loading. The correct depth strategy must still follow the router, bit and material requirements.

### Q19
What is the primary purpose of a router guide, fence or suitable template?

A. To replace eye protection
B. To provide controlled guidance for the intended cut or profile
C. To increase the electrical supply voltage
D. To stop dust from being generated

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Guides, fences and templates help control the router's path and improve repeatability and accuracy.

### Q20
If a router produces severe unexpected vibration immediately after startup, what should the operator do?

A. Continue until the vibration disappears
B. Increase feed pressure
C. Stop safely and inspect/report the condition before further use
D. Remove the router base while it is running

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Severe unexpected vibration may indicate an incorrectly fitted, damaged or incompatible bit or another fault. Continuing can increase the hazard.

## Lesson 6 — Sanders, Abrasives & Dust Control

### Q21
What does a sensible abrasive sequence generally achieve?

A. It progressively removes earlier scratches and prepares the surface for the required finish
B. It makes dust extraction unnecessary
C. It guarantees that every timber species needs the same grit sequence
D. It replaces surface inspection

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Abrasives are normally selected progressively according to starting condition and required finish. The exact sequence depends on the material, defect and finishing specification.

### Q22
Why should a power sander not normally be pressed excessively hard against the work?

A. Excessive pressure can reduce control and contribute to uneven sanding, heat or premature abrasive wear
B. It always makes the abrasive finer
C. It changes the timber species
D. It improves dust extraction in every case

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** The machine and abrasive should do the cutting. Excessive pressure can reduce surface quality and place unnecessary load on the tool.

### Q23
Why is wood-dust control important during sanding?

A. Dust affects only appearance
B. Fine airborne dust can create health and workplace hazards and should be controlled at source where reasonably practicable
C. Dust is harmless if the timber is dry
D. PPE is the only possible dust control

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Sanding can generate fine airborne wood dust. Effective control may include suitable extraction and other workplace controls, with PPE selected as part of the overall risk-control system.

### Q24
After sanding, which inspection method gives the best basis for deciding whether the surface is ready for the next stage?

A. Looking only at the abrasive disc
B. Inspecting the work surface under suitable lighting and checking for scratches, swirls, unevenness or missed defects
C. Assuming it is ready because the sander ran for five minutes
D. Applying finish immediately without cleaning or inspection

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Surface readiness is judged from the workpiece, not simply sanding time. Good lighting and systematic inspection reveal defects that may become more visible after finishing.

## Lesson 7 — Bench & Workshop Machinery Awareness

### Q25
What is the most important rule for a learner who has not been trained or authorised to operate a particular workshop machine?

A. Operate it only at low speed
B. Ask another learner to switch it on
C. Do not operate it; obtain the required instruction, demonstration and authorisation
D. Remove the guard to study the cutting area

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Familiarity with other tools does not establish competence for a particular fixed machine. Machine-specific training and workplace authorisation must be respected.

### Q26
Why must machine guards be correctly positioned and functional before operation?

A. They are mainly decorative
B. They help prevent access or exposure to dangerous moving or cutting components as designed
C. They increase the length of the workpiece
D. They remove all need for training

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Guarding is a primary machinery control. It must not be bypassed merely for convenience or visibility.

### Q27
What is the purpose of a push stick, push block or other approved feeding aid on machinery where the procedure calls for one?

A. To keep hands farther from the danger zone while maintaining workpiece control
B. To test whether a guard can be removed
C. To increase the machine's rated speed
D. To replace all workpiece support

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Proper feeding aids can maintain control while increasing separation between the operator's hands and hazardous cutting areas.

### Q28
A fixed machine develops an unusual sound during operation. What should the operator do?

A. Ignore it if the workpiece is nearly finished
B. Stop the machine safely and follow fault-reporting and isolation procedures
C. Place a hand near the moving part to locate the sound
D. Increase feed speed

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** An unusual sound can indicate a fault or unsafe condition. The correct response is controlled shutdown and reporting, not investigation near moving machinery.

## Lesson 8 — Maintenance, Accessories, Isolation & Fault Reporting

### Q29
Which task is most clearly within an ordinary trained operator's routine maintenance role when permitted by the manufacturer and workplace procedure?

A. Rewiring a damaged motor
B. Bypassing an electrical interlock
C. Cleaning accessible surfaces and carrying out prescribed basic inspections with the tool made safe
D. Machining a replacement guard

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Routine operator care commonly includes authorised cleaning and basic inspection. Electrical repair, safety-device modification and specialist repair require appropriate competence and authority.

### Q30
Why must an accessory be compatible with the power tool?

A. Compatibility affects secure fitting, intended use and safe operation
B. Any accessory can be made safe by wearing gloves
C. Compatibility matters only for appearance
D. A smaller accessory always fits every larger tool

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Accessories must suit the tool, task and manufacturer's requirements. Improvised or incompatible accessories can fail, detach or operate outside their intended conditions.

### Q31
What is the purpose of an out-of-service tag or equivalent workplace control?

A. To show that a tool is available for any learner to test
B. To communicate that defective or unsafe equipment must not be used pending authorised action
C. To record the purchase price
D. To replace repair records

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** An out-of-service control helps prevent defective equipment from being returned to use accidentally. The exact tagging/isolation system must follow workplace procedure.

### Q32
Which fault report is most useful to a supervisor or maintenance person?

A. “Tool bad.”
B. “It doesn't feel right.”
C. “Portable sander: abnormal vibration began during operation; tool stopped, disconnected and removed from service; visible disc damage noted.”
D. “Someone should fix it sometime.”

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** A useful fault report is factual and specific: it identifies the equipment, observed condition, when it occurred and the immediate safety action taken without inventing a diagnosis.
