# Module 5 — Timber, Boards & Material Selection

## Module purpose
Develop the learner's ability to recognise, compare, select, handle and store timber and manufactured boards appropriately for carpentry work, while considering moisture, movement, defects, quality, waste and responsible sourcing.

## Teaching lessons
1. Wood Structure, Grain & Basic Properties
2. Softwoods, Hardwoods & Common Carpentry Uses
3. Moisture Content, Seasoning & Timber Movement
4. Timber Defects, Quality & Grading Awareness
5. Manufactured Boards: Plywood, MDF, Particleboard & OSB
6. Selecting Materials for Purpose, Performance & Environment
7. Timber Storage, Handling & Conditioning
8. Sustainable Sourcing, Material Planning & Waste Reduction
9. Formative Assessment
10. Summative Assessment

## Lesson production standard
Each teaching lesson must contain course/module/lesson identification, estimated study time, 3–5 measurable learning outcomes, Why This Lesson Matters, 5–8 meaningful bold key terms with definitions, substantive main lesson content (absolute minimum 500 words; normal target 700–1,000+), worked example/demonstration, practical activity, informal knowledge check, answer feedback/explanation, key takeaways, and a relevant educational visual with caption where it improves understanding. Written instruction must remain complete without optional video.

## Content controls
- Teach learners to identify material by observable properties rather than relying only on trade names.
- Explain grain direction and wood movement before learners are expected to make material-selection judgements.
- Do not imply that all hardwoods are physically hard or all softwoods are physically soft; botanical classification and practical properties must be distinguished.
- Explain moisture-related expansion, shrinkage, distortion and conditioning in practical terms without presenting a single moisture percentage as universally correct for every climate, use or workplace.
- Manufactured boards must be differentiated by construction, typical strengths, limitations, edge behaviour, moisture sensitivity, finishing considerations and suitable applications.
- Material selection must consider intended use, structural/performance needs, appearance, exposure conditions, machining/finishing requirements, cost, availability and workplace/project specification.
- Defects must be taught as a quality and suitability issue: knots, checks/splits, shakes, bow, cup, twist, wane and biological damage where relevant.
- Storage instruction must address support, stacking, protection from moisture/weather, ventilation/conditioning where appropriate, safe manual handling and prevention of damage.
- Sustainability content must focus on responsible sourcing, efficient cutting plans, reuse where appropriate, segregation of waste and avoiding unnecessary material loss; do not make unsupported environmental certification claims.

## Visual plan
Use educational visuals for: labelled grain direction/end grain/face grain; representative softwood and hardwood examples; moisture/movement concept; common timber defects; side-by-side plywood/MDF/particleboard/OSB identification; correct timber stacking/storage; and a simple cutting-plan/waste-reduction example. Captions must tell the learner what to observe.

## Assessment specification
### Formative Assessment
- Position: Entry 9
- Draw: 15 MCQs from a bank of at least 30
- Four options A–D
- Pass mark: 70%
- Maximum formal opportunities: 3
- Covers Lessons 1–8
- Include recognition, comparison, material-selection and basic application scenarios
- Provide answer rationale/feedback

### Summative Assessment
- Position: Entry 10
- Locked until Formative is passed
- Draw: 25 MCQs from a bank of at least 50
- Four options A–D
- Pass mark: 70%
- Maximum formal opportunities: 3
- Broader and more demanding than Formative
- Include scenario-based decisions involving material suitability, defects, moisture/movement, board selection, storage, quality and waste
- Questions must be traceable to taught content and must not assess untaught knowledge

## Module progression outcome
After successful completion, the learner should be able to identify major timber/material categories, recognise important defects and moisture/movement considerations, compare common manufactured boards, select materials for straightforward carpentry applications, and apply sound storage and waste-reduction practices.
