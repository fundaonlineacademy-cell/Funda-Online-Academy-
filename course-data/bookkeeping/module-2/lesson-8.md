# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 2: Source Documents & Transaction Analysis
### Lesson 8: Source-document Practical Workshop

**Estimated study time:** 60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Review a mixed batch of source documents systematically.
2. Identify missing, duplicate or inconsistent evidence.
3. Analyse routine transactions into appropriate accounts.
4. Separate VAT-inclusive and VAT-exclusive values in standard-rated examples.
5. Build a traceable transaction-processing checklist from receipt of evidence to filing.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Real bookkeeping rarely arrives as a perfectly organised set of documents. A bookkeeper may receive invoices by email, bank entries without references, handwritten petty-cash slips, credit notes, duplicated attachments and customer payments that do not identify the invoice being settled.

This practical lesson brings the Module 2 skills together. The objective is to process evidence carefully enough that another authorised person can understand why each accounting entry was made.

## Key Terms
- **Document batch** — Group of source documents processed together.
- **Exception** — Item that does not meet the normal processing rule and requires investigation or escalation.
- **Coding** — Selecting the appropriate ledger account and tax code for a transaction.
- **Matched transaction** — Transaction linked to sufficient relevant supporting evidence.
- **Unresolved item** — Transaction that cannot yet be completed accurately because required information is missing or inconsistent.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start with a control checklist
Before processing a batch, establish a repeatable review sequence:
1. identify the document type;
2. check date, party, number and amount;
3. look for duplicate risk;
4. confirm approval/receipt evidence where required;
5. determine the economic transaction;
6. select the account(s);
7. review VAT treatment where applicable;
8. capture or mark as exception;
9. link the document to the entry;
10. file in the authoritative location.

This prevents the bookkeeper from treating capture speed as the only goal.

### 2. Case document A: supplier invoice
You receive an invoice for R4,600 inclusive from a VAT vendor for office stationery. Assume the transaction is standard-rated at 15% and meets the normal requirements for the worked example.

VAT = R4,600 × 15/115 = R600.  
Exclusive value = R4,000.

If purchased on credit, the simplified accounting effect may be:
- Stationery Expense R4,000 debit;
- Input VAT/VAT control R600 debit, subject to the applicable rules;
- Creditors R4,600 credit.

Before capture, also confirm the supplier, invoice number, approval and duplicate status.

### 3. Case document B: duplicate-looking invoice
Two PDF files have the same supplier, date, invoice number and R4,600 amount. One is labelled `Invoice.pdf` and the other `Invoice-copy.pdf`.

Do not capture both. Confirm whether the second file is merely another copy. File the evidence appropriately without creating a second liability.

### 4. Case document C: customer receipt
The bank shows R8,050 received from a customer. The customer ledger shows an outstanding invoice of exactly R8,050.

Check the payer/reference and match the receipt to the invoice. If confirmed:
- Debit Bank R8,050;
- Credit Debtors R8,050.

Do not record Sales again if the original sale was already captured.

### 5. Case document D: company-card payment
A card statement shows R2,700 paid to an electronics store. The supporting invoice shows a monitor purchased for business use.

The account classification depends on the business's asset/capitalisation policy. Do not assume `Office Expense` merely because the item was paid by card. If required, obtain confirmation before coding.

### 6. Case document E: missing petty-cash receipt
A petty-cash voucher says R180 was paid for parking, but there is no receipt.

Do not invent a receipt. Apply the organisation's missing-evidence process. The amount may remain unresolved until authorised alternate evidence or approval is obtained.

### 7. Case document F: supplier credit note
The supplier issues a R575 credit note linked to an earlier invoice. If the original transaction was standard-rated and the credit properly adjusts it, the relevant purchase/expense and VAT components may also need adjustment according to the system and tax rules.

Match the credit to the original supplier transaction and ensure it reduces the creditor balance correctly.

### 8. Case document G: unexplained bank deposit
The bank shows R12,000 with reference `TRANSFER`. No customer matches immediately.

Do not code it as Sales to clear the bank reconciliation. Possible explanations include:
- customer payment;
- owner funding;
- loan receipt;
- transfer between business accounts;
- refund.

Investigate before classification.

### 9. Use an exception register
Unresolved items should remain visible. A useful exception register can contain:
- date;
- amount;
- source/reference;
- problem;
- person responsible for clarification;
- next action;
- due/follow-up date;
- status.

This prevents unclear transactions from disappearing between reporting periods.

### 10. Perform a batch quality review
After processing, check:
- all documents accounted for;
- no duplicate postings;
- debits/credits make sense;
- VAT codes reviewed;
- unresolved items listed;
- approvals attached where required;
- file references trace correctly.

## Worked Integrated Example
A document batch contains:
- Supplier invoice R2,300 VAT-inclusive for eligible standard-rated stationery.
- Customer receipt R5,000 against a previous invoice.
- Owner contribution R10,000.
- Supplier credit note R1,150 against a prior standard-rated purchase.

Using 15% for the VAT examples:
- stationery exclusive R2,000, VAT R300;
- customer receipt settles debtor, not new income;
- owner contribution increases bank and equity, not income;
- supplier credit exclusive R1,000, VAT R150, with the creditor and related purchase/VAT records adjusted according to the original transaction.

The bookkeeper should be able to explain each entry from its evidence.

## Practical Activity
Create a processing table with these columns:
`Document | Amount | Evidence Check | Account(s) | VAT Check | Debit | Credit | Exception? | Filing Reference`

Use it for:
1. R3,450 VAT-inclusive office-supply invoice.
2. R7,000 customer EFT against an existing debtor.
3. R25,000 loan received.
4. R650 petty-cash purchase with missing evidence.
5. Supplier credit note of R1,150.
6. Unexplained bank deposit of R4,000.

For VAT calculations, assume the stated purchase examples are standard-rated at 15% and otherwise eligible for the simplified exercise.

## Knowledge Check
1. What should happen before account coding?
2. Why should two identical invoice files not automatically both be captured?
3. What is the entry for a confirmed debtor receipt?
4. Why can a card purchase require classification clarification?
5. What should happen when a petty-cash receipt is missing?
6. Why should a supplier credit be matched to the original transaction?
7. Why is coding an unexplained deposit to Sales risky?
8. What is the purpose of an exception register?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Verify the document and understand the economic transaction.
2. They may be duplicate copies of the same liability.
3. Debit Bank and credit Debtors when settling an existing receivable.
4. The payment method does not decide whether the purchase is an expense or asset.
5. Follow the authorised missing-evidence process; do not fabricate evidence.
6. It ensures the correct creditor, expense/asset and VAT effect is reversed or reduced.
7. The receipt may represent funding, debt settlement, transfer or another non-income item.
8. It keeps unresolved transactions visible until correctly resolved.

## Key Takeaways
- A source-document batch should be processed through a consistent control checklist.
- Duplicate-looking documents require investigation before capture.
- Account classification follows economic substance, not payment method.
- VAT calculations must distinguish inclusive and exclusive amounts.
- Unresolved items should be tracked, not guessed away.
- Final quality review confirms completeness, coding and audit-trail integrity.
