# Module 2 Summative Assessment Bank
## Source Documents & Transaction Analysis

**Bank size:** 52 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 25 random questions per attempt  
**Pass standard:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Coverage:** All eight Module 2 lessons.

1. What is the primary bookkeeping role of a source document?  
A. Provide evidence of a transaction  
B. Replace all ledgers  
C. Guarantee payment approval  
D. Set accounting policy  
**Answer: A**

2. Which field is MOST useful for detecting a possible duplicate supplier invoice?  
A. File colour  
B. Supplier and invoice number  
C. Employee name only  
D. Folder size  
**Answer: B**

3. Why should invoice arithmetic be checked before capture?  
A. Software cannot calculate  
B. All invoices are wrong  
C. An incorrect document total can create an incorrect accounting entry  
D. Arithmetic is needed only for cash purchases  
**Answer: C**

4. What should happen if required evidence is missing?  
A. Invent a document  
B. Capture it twice  
C. Use a random account  
D. Apply the authorised exception process  
**Answer: D**

5. Why is a bank entry not always sufficient evidence for classification?  
A. It may show money movement without explaining purpose or approval  
B. Bank statements are optional  
C. Bank transactions do not enter the ledger  
D. Banks do not show amounts  
**Answer: A**

6. What is a strong audit trail?  
A. One spreadsheet with no references  
B. A traceable connection between source evidence, approvals and accounting entries  
C. Only a printed bank statement  
D. A monthly profit report  
**Answer: B**

7. A credit sale is recorded. Which balance normally increases?  
A. Creditors  
B. Loan payable  
C. Debtors  
D. Drawings  
**Answer: C**

8. What does a customer credit note normally do?  
A. Increase income  
B. Increase debtors  
C. Record a bank receipt  
D. Reduce an authorised amount previously invoiced  
**Answer: D**

9. A customer pays an existing invoice. Which entry is correct at a basic level?  
A. Debit Bank, credit Debtors  
B. Debit Debtors, credit Sales  
C. Debit Expense, credit Bank  
D. Debit Bank, credit Sales again  
**Answer: A**

10. Why should customer receipts be allocated?  
A. To create new invoice numbers  
B. To match money received to the correct outstanding transaction(s)  
C. To reduce sales income  
D. To increase creditors  
**Answer: B**

11. A customer pays more than the outstanding balance. What is the BEST response?  
A. Create a fake sale  
B. Delete the difference  
C. Investigate and process the overpayment/customer credit according to policy  
D. Post it to expenses  
**Answer: C**

12. What is a customer statement?  
A. A supplier payment list  
B. A bank reconciliation  
C. A tax return  
D. Summary of customer transactions and balance over a period  
**Answer: D**

13. A supplier invoice on credit normally increases:  
A. Creditors  
B. Debtors  
C. Capital  
D. Sales  
**Answer: A**

14. What does three-way matching compare?  
A. Invoice, bank statement and payroll  
B. Purchase order, receipt evidence and supplier invoice  
C. Three supplier quotes only  
D. Three accounting periods  
**Answer: B**

15. The PO approves 20 units but the supplier invoice bills 25 and only 20 were received. What should happen?  
A. Pay the invoice in full automatically  
B. Change the PO after payment  
C. Resolve the discrepancy before normal payment completion  
D. Record five units as Miscellaneous  
**Answer: C**

16. What is a supplier credit note?  
A. A new supplier invoice  
B. A customer receipt  
C. A bank loan  
D. A document reducing or reversing an amount previously billed by the supplier  
**Answer: D**

17. Why is a supplier statement useful?  
A. It helps reconcile the supplier's records with the business's creditor ledger  
B. It replaces every invoice  
C. It authorises all payments  
D. It sets VAT rates  
**Answer: A**

18. Which difference may appear in supplier reconciliation?  
A. Only exchange-rate differences  
B. Invoice on supplier statement not yet captured in the business ledger  
C. Only payroll errors  
D. Only bank fees  
**Answer: B**

19. What should happen when a duplicate-invoice warning appears?  
A. Always override it  
B. Rename the invoice  
C. Investigate the full transaction evidence  
D. Pay immediately  
**Answer: C**

20. Why should a supplier-bank change not be accepted solely from an unexpected email?  
A. Suppliers may never change banks  
B. Email cannot contain bank details  
C. Payments must always be cash  
D. The request may be mistaken or fraudulent and needs controlled verification  
**Answer: D**

21. Which evidence best explains what was bought with a company card?  
A. Itemised supplier receipt/invoice  
B. Card colour  
C. Employee signature only  
D. Bank balance  
**Answer: A**

22. What is the purpose of an EFT confirmation?  
A. Approve the expense automatically  
B. Evidence the electronic payment details  
C. Prove VAT deductibility  
D. Replace supplier documentation  
**Answer: B**

23. A petty-cash float should be R2,000. Cash plus valid vouchers totals R1,950. What is the BEST response?  
A. Add R50 to expenses with no evidence  
B. Ignore it  
C. Investigate the R50 difference  
D. Reduce the authorised float permanently  
**Answer: C**

24. What is an imprest petty-cash system?  
A. Unlimited cash for small purchases  
B. A cash sales register  
C. A bank overdraft  
D. A fixed approved float restored after supported expenditure  
**Answer: D**

25. Card sales are R20,000 and merchant fees are R500, so R19,500 settles to bank. What should bookkeeping preserve?  
A. Gross sales and the separate merchant-fee effect  
B. Only the net deposit as sales  
C. Only the merchant fee  
D. A debtor of R500  
**Answer: A**

26. Why should recurring debit orders be reviewed?  
A. Debit orders never change  
B. New, changed or unauthorised deductions may need investigation  
C. They are always expenses  
D. They do not appear on bank statements  
**Answer: B**

27. What should transaction analysis identify?  
A. Only whether cash moved  
B. Only the document type  
C. Economic event, affected accounts and their debit/credit effects  
D. Only VAT  
**Answer: C**

28. A previously captured supplier invoice is now paid. What should NOT happen?  
A. Bank decreases  
B. Creditors decreases  
C. Payment is matched to the supplier  
D. The original expense is recorded again  
**Answer: D**

29. A business receives R50,000 from a lender. Which classification is correct?  
A. Increase Bank and Loan Liability  
B. Increase Sales and Debtors  
C. Increase Expense and Bank  
D. Increase Drawings and Bank  
**Answer: A**

30. Why may a loan instalment require more than one accounting component?  
A. Bank loans are not recorded  
B. Principal repayment and interest can have different accounting effects  
C. Every loan instalment is sales income  
D. Principal is always an expense  
**Answer: B**

31. Which transaction shows why cash received is not always income?  
A. Cash sale  
B. Service fee earned  
C. Owner capital introduced  
D. Interest income received  
**Answer: C**

32. What should happen if the economic nature of a transaction cannot be determined from the available evidence?  
A. Use Miscellaneous automatically  
B. Pick last month's account  
C. Ignore the transaction permanently  
D. Clarify or escalate before final classification  
**Answer: D**

33. At 15%, what is VAT on R4,000 exclusive?  
A. R600  
B. R460  
C. R4,600  
D. R522  
**Answer: A**

34. At 15%, what is the VAT-inclusive value of R2,000 exclusive?  
A. R2,015  
B. R2,300  
C. R2,150  
D. R2,030  
**Answer: B**

35. At 15%, what is the VAT component of R1,150 inclusive?  
A. R172.50  
B. R115  
C. R150  
D. R1,000  
**Answer: C**

36. Which formula extracts VAT from a VAT-inclusive amount at 15%?  
A. Inclusive × 15%  
B. Inclusive ÷ 15  
C. Inclusive × 115/15  
D. Inclusive × 15/115  
**Answer: D**

37. What is output tax at a basic level?  
A. VAT charged by a vendor on taxable supplies made  
B. VAT on every bank deposit  
C. VAT paid only on salaries  
D. A type of income tax  
**Answer: A**

38. What is input tax at a basic level?  
A. VAT that is always refundable  
B. VAT incurred that may be deductible subject to applicable requirements  
C. VAT charged to customers  
D. VAT on exempt supplies only  
**Answer: B**

39. Which statement about zero-rated and exempt supplies is correct?  
A. They are always identical  
B. Both are taxed at 15%  
C. They are different VAT categories  
D. Neither can ever appear in a VAT system  
**Answer: C**

40. Why should VAT codes in accounting software be reviewed?  
A. Codes affect only screen colours  
B. Software codes are always correct  
C. Every transaction should use the standard code  
D. Wrong codes can distort VAT records even if the gross amount is right  
**Answer: D**

41. What is the main purpose of financial-document indexing?  
A. Make authorised evidence easier to retrieve and trace  
B. Increase the number of copies  
C. Remove confidentiality  
D. Avoid source references  
**Answer: A**

42. Why should payroll-support files have controlled access?  
A. Payroll data is public  
B. They can contain sensitive personal and financial information  
C. Only paper requires security  
D. Access controls slow bookkeeping  
**Answer: B**

43. What is an authoritative record location?  
A. Any employee's inbox  
B. A personal USB drive  
C. The recognised controlled repository for the official business record  
D. The oldest available copy  
**Answer: C**

44. Why can uncontrolled duplicate files create risk?  
A. Duplicates always change amounts  
B. They stop the ledger balancing  
C. They automatically breach POPIA  
D. Users may not know which copy is current or authoritative  
**Answer: D**

45. What should determine record retention?  
A. Applicable requirements and the organisation's authorised retention policy  
B. The bookkeeper's personal preference  
C. Available disk space only  
D. Whether the document is old  
**Answer: A**

46. What should secure disposal protect against?  
A. Proper audit trails  
B. Unauthorised disclosure after records are authorised for destruction  
C. Approved retention  
D. Reconciliation  
**Answer: B**

47. What should happen to an unresolved transaction during batch processing?  
A. Force a coding choice  
B. Delete it  
C. Put it on a visible exception register with next action  
D. Treat it as sales  
**Answer: C**

48. A company-card purchase could be either an asset or expense. What determines treatment?  
A. The card type  
B. The shop name  
C. The day of the week  
D. Economic nature and approved accounting policy  
**Answer: D**

49. Why should a supplier credit be linked to its original transaction?  
A. To ensure the correct creditor and related account/tax effects are adjusted  
B. To create another invoice  
C. To increase the supplier balance  
D. To avoid reconciliation  
**Answer: A**

50. What should a final batch review check?  
A. Only file names  
B. Completeness, duplicates, coding, VAT, exceptions, approvals and references  
C. Only the bank balance  
D. Only total expenses  
**Answer: B**

51. Which practice MOST improves transaction traceability?  
A. Using vague descriptions  
B. Storing evidence in personal inboxes  
C. Linking consistent source references to accounting entries  
D. Deleting processed source documents  
**Answer: C**

52. Which principle BEST summarises Module 2?  
A. Capture first and investigate later  
B. Bank descriptions are enough for coding  
C. Every receipt is income and every payment is expense  
D. Verify source evidence, understand the transaction, code it correctly, resolve exceptions and preserve the audit trail  
**Answer: D**
