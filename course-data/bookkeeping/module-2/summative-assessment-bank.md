# Module 2 Summative Assessment Bank
## Source Documents & Transaction Analysis

**Bank size:** 52 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 25 random questions per attempt  
**Pass standard:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Coverage:** All eight Module 2 lessons.

1. What is the primary bookkeeping role of a source document?  
A. Guarantee payment approval  
B. Set accounting policy  
C. Provide evidence of a transaction  
D. Replace all ledgers  
**Answer: C**

2. Which field is MOST useful for detecting a possible duplicate supplier invoice?  
A. File colour  
B. Folder size  
C. Employee name only  
D. Supplier and invoice number  
**Answer: D**

3. Why should invoice arithmetic be checked before capture?  
A. Software cannot calculate  
B. An incorrect document total can create an incorrect accounting entry  
C. All invoices are wrong  
D. Arithmetic is needed only for cash purchases  
**Answer: B**

4. What should happen if required evidence is missing?  
A. Apply the authorised exception process  
B. Use a random account  
C. Capture it twice  
D. Invent a document  
**Answer: A**

5. Why is a bank entry not always sufficient evidence for classification?  
A. It may show money movement without explaining purpose or approval  
B. Banks do not show amounts  
C. Bank statements are optional  
D. Bank transactions do not enter the ledger  
**Answer: A**

6. What is a strong audit trail?  
A. A monthly profit report  
B. A traceable connection between source evidence, approvals and accounting entries  
C. One spreadsheet with no references  
D. Only a printed bank statement  
**Answer: B**

7. A business issues a R12,000 sales invoice on credit. Which balance normally increases before payment is received?  
A. Creditors  
B. Debtors  
C. Loan payable  
D. Drawings  
**Answer: B**

8. What does a customer credit note normally do?  
A. Reduce an authorised amount previously invoiced  
B. Increase income  
C. Record a bank receipt  
D. Increase debtors  
**Answer: A**

9. A customer pays an existing invoice. Which entry is correct at a basic level?  
A. Debit Bank, credit Sales again  
B. Debit Bank, credit Debtors  
C. Debit Expense, credit Bank  
D. Debit Debtors, credit Sales  
**Answer: B**

10. Why should customer receipts be allocated?  
A. To reduce sales income  
B. To increase creditors  
C. To match money received to the correct outstanding transaction(s)  
D. To create new invoice numbers  
**Answer: C**

11. A customer pays more than the outstanding balance. What is the BEST response?  
A. Delete the difference  
B. Post it to expenses  
C. Create a fake sale  
D. Investigate and process the overpayment/customer credit according to policy  
**Answer: D**

12. What is a customer statement?  
A. Summary of customer transactions and balance over a period  
B. A bank reconciliation  
C. A supplier payment list  
D. A tax return  
**Answer: A**

13. A valid supplier invoice for office supplies is accepted on credit. Which balances normally increase at capture?  
A. Capital  
B. Creditors  
C. Sales  
D. Debtors  
**Answer: B**

14. What does three-way matching compare?  
A. Three supplier quotes only  
B. Invoice, bank statement and payroll  
C. Purchase order, receipt evidence and supplier invoice  
D. Three accounting periods  
**Answer: C**

15. The PO approves 20 units but the supplier invoice bills 25 and only 20 were received. What should happen?  
A. Pay the invoice in full automatically  
B. Record five units as Miscellaneous  
C. Change the PO after payment  
D. Resolve the discrepancy before normal payment completion  
**Answer: D**

16. What is a supplier credit note?  
A. A customer receipt  
B. A bank loan  
C. A document reducing or reversing an amount previously billed by the supplier  
D. A new supplier invoice  
**Answer: C**

17. Why is a supplier statement useful?  
A. It helps reconcile the supplier's records with the business's creditor ledger  
B. It authorises all payments  
C. It replaces every invoice  
D. It sets VAT rates  
**Answer: A**

18. Which difference may appear in supplier reconciliation?  
A. Invoice on supplier statement not yet captured in the business ledger  
B. Only payroll errors  
C. Only bank fees  
D. Only exchange-rate differences  
**Answer: A**

19. What should happen when a duplicate-invoice warning appears?  
A. Pay immediately  
B. Rename the invoice  
C. Always override it  
D. Investigate the full transaction evidence  
**Answer: D**

20. Why should a supplier-bank change not be accepted solely from an unexpected email?  
A. Suppliers may never change banks  
B. Payments must always be cash  
C. Email cannot contain bank details  
D. The request may be mistaken or fraudulent and needs controlled verification  
**Answer: D**

21. Which evidence best explains what was bought with a company card?  
A. Employee signature only  
B. Card colour  
C. Itemised supplier receipt/invoice  
D. Bank balance  
**Answer: C**

22. What is the purpose of an EFT confirmation?  
A. Approve the expense automatically  
B. Replace supplier documentation  
C. Prove VAT deductibility  
D. Evidence the electronic payment details  
**Answer: D**

23. A petty-cash float should be R2,000. Cash plus valid vouchers totals R1,950. What is the BEST response?  
A. Reduce the authorised float permanently  
B. Investigate the R50 difference  
C. Add R50 to expenses with no evidence  
D. Ignore it  
**Answer: B**

24. What is an imprest petty-cash system?  
A. Unlimited cash for small purchases  
B. A cash sales register  
C. A fixed approved float restored after supported expenditure  
D. A bank overdraft  
**Answer: C**

25. Card sales are R20,000 and merchant fees are R500, so R19,500 settles to bank. What should bookkeeping preserve?  
A. Only the merchant fee  
B. Gross sales and the separate merchant-fee effect  
C. A debtor of R500  
D. Only the net deposit as sales  
**Answer: B**

26. Why should recurring debit orders be reviewed?  
A. New, changed or unauthorised deductions may need investigation  
B. Debit orders never change  
C. They are always expenses  
D. They do not appear on bank statements  
**Answer: A**

27. What should transaction analysis identify?  
A. Only whether cash moved  
B. Only VAT  
C. Economic event, affected accounts and their debit/credit effects  
D. Only the document type  
**Answer: C**

28. A previously captured supplier invoice is now paid. What should NOT happen?  
A. Payment is matched to the supplier  
B. The original expense is recorded again  
C. Bank decreases  
D. Creditors decreases  
**Answer: B**

29. A business receives R50,000 from a lender. Which classification is correct?  
A. Increase Bank and Loan Liability  
B. Increase Sales and Debtors  
C. Increase Drawings and Bank  
D. Increase Expense and Bank  
**Answer: A**

30. Why may a loan instalment require more than one accounting component?  
A. Principal repayment and interest can have different accounting effects  
B. Bank loans are not recorded  
C. Every loan instalment is sales income  
D. Principal is always an expense  
**Answer: A**

31. Which transaction shows why cash received is not always income?  
A. Service fee earned  
B. Cash sale  
C. Owner capital introduced  
D. Interest income received  
**Answer: C**

32. What should happen if the economic nature of a transaction cannot be determined from the available evidence?  
A. Ignore the transaction permanently  
B. Use Miscellaneous automatically  
C. Pick last month's account  
D. Clarify or escalate before final classification  
**Answer: D**

33. At 15%, what is VAT on R4,000 exclusive?  
A. R4,600  
B. R600  
C. R522  
D. R460  
**Answer: B**

34. At 15%, what is the VAT-inclusive value of R2,000 exclusive?  
A. R2,150  
B. R2,015  
C. R2,300  
D. R2,030  
**Answer: C**

35. At 15%, what is the VAT component of R1,150 inclusive?  
A. R172.50  
B. R115  
C. R1,000  
D. R150  
**Answer: D**

36. Which formula extracts VAT from a VAT-inclusive amount at 15%?  
A. Inclusive × 115/15  
B. Inclusive × 15/115  
C. Inclusive × 15%  
D. Inclusive ÷ 15  
**Answer: B**

37. What is output tax at a basic level?  
A. VAT charged by a vendor on taxable supplies made  
B. VAT paid only on salaries  
C. A type of income tax  
D. VAT on every bank deposit  
**Answer: A**

38. What is input tax at a basic level?  
A. VAT charged to customers  
B. VAT on exempt supplies only  
C. VAT incurred that may be deductible subject to applicable requirements  
D. VAT that is always refundable  
**Answer: C**

39. Which statement about zero-rated and exempt supplies is correct?  
A. Neither can ever appear in a VAT system  
B. They are always identical  
C. Both are taxed at 15%  
D. They are different VAT categories  
**Answer: D**

40. Why should VAT codes in accounting software be reviewed?  
A. Codes affect only screen colours  
B. Software codes are always correct  
C. Every transaction should use the standard code  
D. Wrong codes can distort VAT records even if the gross amount is right  
**Answer: D**

41. What is the main purpose of financial-document indexing?  
A. Make authorised evidence easier to retrieve and trace  
B. Avoid source references  
C. Remove confidentiality  
D. Increase the number of copies  
**Answer: A**

42. Why should payroll-support files have controlled access?  
A. Only paper requires security  
B. Payroll data is public  
C. They can contain sensitive personal and financial information  
D. Access controls slow bookkeeping  
**Answer: C**

43. What is an authoritative record location?  
A. The oldest available copy  
B. Any employee's inbox  
C. A personal USB drive  
D. The recognised controlled repository for the official business record  
**Answer: D**

44. Why can uncontrolled duplicate files create risk?  
A. Users may not know which copy is current or authoritative  
B. They automatically breach POPIA  
C. Duplicates always change amounts  
D. They stop the ledger balancing  
**Answer: A**

45. What should determine record retention?  
A. The bookkeeper's personal preference  
B. Applicable requirements and the organisation's authorised retention policy  
C. Available disk space only  
D. Whether the document is old  
**Answer: B**

46. What should secure disposal protect against?  
A. Approved retention  
B. Proper audit trails  
C. Unauthorised disclosure after records are authorised for destruction  
D. Reconciliation  
**Answer: C**

47. What should happen to an unresolved transaction during batch processing?  
A. Delete it  
B. Put it on a visible exception register with next action  
C. Treat it as sales  
D. Force a coding choice  
**Answer: B**

48. A company-card purchase could be either an asset or expense. What determines treatment?  
A. The shop name  
B. The day of the week  
C. The card type  
D. Economic nature and approved accounting policy  
**Answer: D**

49. Why should a supplier credit be linked to its original transaction?  
A. To avoid reconciliation  
B. To increase the supplier balance  
C. To ensure the correct creditor and related account/tax effects are adjusted  
D. To create another invoice  
**Answer: C**

50. What should a final batch review check?  
A. Only file names  
B. Completeness, duplicates, coding, VAT, exceptions, approvals and references  
C. Only total expenses  
D. Only the bank balance  
**Answer: B**

51. Which practice MOST improves transaction traceability?  
A. Storing evidence in personal inboxes  
B. Deleting processed source documents  
C. Using vague descriptions  
D. Linking consistent source references to accounting entries  
**Answer: D**

52. Which principle BEST summarises Module 2?  
A. Verify source evidence, understand the transaction, code it correctly, resolve exceptions and preserve the audit trail  
B. Bank descriptions are enough for coding  
C. Every receipt is income and every payment is expense  
D. Capture first and investigate later  
**Answer: A**
