# Module 4 Summative Assessment Bank
## Cashbooks, Petty Cash & Bank Reconciliation

**Bank size:** 52 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 25 random questions per attempt  
**Pass standard:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Coverage:** All eight Module 4 lessons.

1. What is the main purpose of a cashbook?  
A. Record receipts and payments systematically  
B. Replace invoices  
C. Record payroll only  
D. Store customer master data  
**Answer: A**

2. A customer pays R5,000 toward an existing debtor balance. What is the normal entry?  
A. Debit Debtors; Credit Bank  
B. Debit Bank; Credit Debtors  
C. Debit Bank; Credit Sales  
D. Debit Sales; Credit Bank  
**Answer: B**

3. Why must the purpose of a bank deposit be identified before classification?  
A. Deposits are never income  
B. Only cash sales use bank  
C. The receipt may represent sales, debt collection, capital, loan or another item  
D. Bank statements cannot be trusted  
**Answer: C**

4. What should support a cashbook entry?  
A. Memory only  
B. An employee's guess  
C. No evidence if amount is small  
D. Appropriate transaction and banking evidence  
**Answer: D**

5. Which control BEST reduces unauthorised payments?  
A. Approved payment authority and restricted banking access  
B. Shared online-banking passwords  
C. No payment references  
D. One employee controlling all stages  
**Answer: A**

6. What does a debit order normally represent?  
A. A customer credit note  
B. An authorised bank-account collection  
C. A trial-balance adjustment  
D. A supplier statement  
**Answer: B**

7. Why may card-sale deposits be lower than gross card sales?  
A. VAT is removed by the bank  
B. Sales must be reduced  
C. Merchant fees may be deducted before settlement  
D. Debtors increase automatically  
**Answer: C**

8. A bank feed imports an EFT already captured manually. What should happen?  
A. Capture another payment  
B. Post it to an expense  
C. Delete the original transaction without checking  
D. Match the bank-feed item to the existing entry  
**Answer: D**

9. What is a reversal?  
A. A transaction undoing or returning an earlier banking movement  
B. A new supplier invoice  
C. A cash-count sheet  
D. A chart-of-accounts code  
**Answer: A**

10. Supplier banking details change unexpectedly. What should happen before payment?  
A. Use the emailed details immediately  
B. Follow the approved independent verification process  
C. Ignore all future supplier invoices  
D. Ask the supplier for the company's password  
**Answer: B**

11. What is petty cash intended for?  
A. Large asset purchases  
B. Payroll  
C. Small approved business expenses  
D. Personal staff loans  
**Answer: C**

12. What is the imprest principle?  
A. Increase the float every month  
B. Avoid vouchers  
C. Spend whatever remains  
D. Restore the fund to an approved fixed amount after valid expenditure is reconciled  
**Answer: D**

13. A R2,500 float has cash R900 and valid vouchers R1,600. What is the difference?  
A. No difference  
B. R100 shortage  
C. R100 overage  
D. R1,600 shortage  
**Answer: A**

14. A R2,000 float has cash R700 and vouchers R1,250. What should happen?  
A. Replenish immediately with R1,300  
B. Investigate the R50 shortage  
C. Ignore the difference  
D. Increase the float  
**Answer: B**

15. Why should petty-cash vouchers include purpose and approval?  
A. To make forms longer  
B. To replace receipts  
C. To show why the cash was used and that the use was authorised  
D. To avoid reconciliation  
**Answer: C**

16. Which practice weakens petty-cash control?  
A. Surprise counts  
B. Restricted custody  
C. Reconciliation before replenishment  
D. Personal borrowing from the fund  
**Answer: D**

17. What is a bank statement?  
A. The bank's record of account transactions and balance  
B. A supplier ageing report  
C. A petty-cash register  
D. A customer invoice  
**Answer: A**

18. Which item often needs to be added to the cashbook after statement review?  
A. Deposit in transit already captured  
B. Bank charge not yet captured  
C. Outstanding payment already captured  
D. Future supplier invoice  
**Answer: B**

19. Why should an identified customer direct deposit be allocated carefully?  
A. It always belongs to Sales  
B. It should always be Capital  
C. It may need to settle a specific debtor balance  
D. It should be ignored  
**Answer: C**

20. What should happen with a valid outstanding payment already in the cashbook?  
A. Capture it again  
B. Reverse it automatically  
C. Move it to Sales  
D. Leave it as a reconciling timing item until the bank processes it  
**Answer: D**

21. What is an adjusted cashbook balance?  
A. The book balance after valid statement-driven updates and corrections  
B. The bank statement balance  
C. Total deposits only  
D. The credit limit  
**Answer: A**

22. What is the purpose of bank reconciliation?  
A. Prepare VAT returns  
B. Explain and verify differences between bank and book balances  
C. Replace the cashbook  
D. Eliminate source documents  
**Answer: B**

23. What is a deposit in transit?  
A. An unknown deposit  
B. A bank error  
C. Receipt recorded by the business but not yet reflected by the bank  
D. An unrecorded bank charge  
**Answer: C**

24. What is an outstanding payment?  
A. A supplier invoice not yet approved  
B. A duplicate payment  
C. A payment not recorded by the business  
D. A payment recorded in the books but not yet processed by the bank  
**Answer: D**

25. Why should the cashbook be updated before final reconciliation?  
A. Known book-side omissions should be corrected first  
B. Timing differences should be deleted  
C. Bank statements should be changed  
D. Old balances should be ignored  
**Answer: A**

26. What should happen when the bank makes an error?  
A. Change the cashbook to copy it  
B. Report and resolve the bank error through the proper channel  
C. Ignore it forever  
D. Add a false journal  
**Answer: B**

27. Why do old outstanding payments need investigation?  
A. They always indicate theft  
B. They are automatically cancelled  
C. They may represent failed, cancelled or incorrectly recorded transactions  
D. They can remain forever  
**Answer: C**

28. What should a reconciliation reviewer examine?  
A. Only the final balance  
B. Only statement date  
C. Only the preparer's signature  
D. Cashbook updates, timing items, evidence and arithmetic  
**Answer: D**

29. What is an unmatched item?  
A. A transaction in one record without a confirmed match in the other  
B. A completed petty-cash voucher  
C. A supplier master record  
D. A trial-balance total  
**Answer: A**

30. Which situation suggests a duplicate transaction?  
A. One supplier payment with one bank reference  
B. Two identical supplier payments with matching invoice and amount details  
C. One bank charge  
D. One customer deposit  
**Answer: B**

31. How should an unknown deposit be handled?  
A. Post to Sales  
B. Refund immediately  
C. Investigate and use an approved unallocated-receipts process if needed  
D. Delete it  
**Answer: C**

32. What is exception ageing?  
A. Calculating asset depreciation  
B. Measuring employee service  
C. Tracking invoice due dates only  
D. Tracking how long unresolved reconciling items remain open  
**Answer: D**

33. Why should suspicious withdrawals be escalated quickly?  
A. They may involve fraud, security or unauthorised activity  
B. Every withdrawal is wrong  
C. Reconciliations are optional  
D. Bank statements are private  
**Answer: A**

34. What should happen when an incorrect cashbook amount is confirmed?  
A. Change the bank statement  
B. Correct the books using verified evidence and the authorised process  
C. Ignore it if small  
D. Delete the source document  
**Answer: B**

35. Why should resolved exceptions retain evidence?  
A. To increase filing volume  
B. To make reconciliation harder  
C. To show why and how the difference was cleared  
D. To prevent future transactions  
**Answer: C**

36. What does segregation of duties reduce?  
A. Bank balances  
B. Number of suppliers  
C. Customer receipts  
D. Risk from one person controlling incompatible stages  
**Answer: D**

37. Which is an example of segregation of duties?  
A. One employee prepares payments while another authorised person releases them  
B. One employee creates suppliers, pays them and reconciles bank alone  
C. Everyone shares one banking login  
D. No one reviews reconciliations  
**Answer: A**

38. What is a daily cash-position report?  
A. A customer statement  
B. A current view of bank/cash balances and material short-term commitments  
C. A VAT calculation  
D. A fixed-asset register  
**Answer: B**

39. Why should expected receipts be shown separately from confirmed cash?  
A. Expected receipts are never useful  
B. Bank balances exclude receipts  
C. Expected receipts may be delayed or not arrive  
D. Confirmed cash should be ignored  
**Answer: C**

40. What should NOT be included in a cash/bank handover?  
A. Reconciliation status  
B. Outstanding payments  
C. Unknown deposits  
D. Passwords and MFA codes  
**Answer: D**

41. Which item should be escalated in a daily cash review?  
A. Projected inability to meet a major due payment  
B. Correctly matched normal transaction  
C. Closed reconciliation  
D. Approved routine receipt  
**Answer: A**

42. A cashbook before update is R30,000 Dr. Fees R300 and insurance R1,200 are unrecorded, while interest R100 is unrecorded. What is adjusted balance?  
A. R31,600 Dr  
B. R28,600 Dr  
C. R28,400 Dr  
D. R30,800 Dr  
**Answer: B**

43. Statement balance is R20,900, deposit in transit R4,500 and outstanding payments R3,000. What reconciled balance results?  
A. R28,400  
B. R18,400  
C. R22,400  
D. R20,900  
**Answer: C**

44. If a reconciliation remains different after all known items are processed, what should happen?  
A. Post the difference to Miscellaneous Expense  
B. Adjust bank statement  
C. Ignore it if under R1,000  
D. Continue investigation and control it as an unresolved exception  
**Answer: D**

45. Why is forcing a balancing journal poor practice?  
A. It can conceal the real cause of the difference  
B. Journals are never allowed  
C. It always increases profit  
D. It changes the bank statement  
**Answer: A**

46. Which sequence BEST describes a sound month-end bank process?  
A. Reconcile → update cashbook → review statement  
B. Review statement → update cashbook → identify timing items → reconcile → investigate exceptions  
C. Delete old items → post balancing journal → reconcile  
D. Use statement balance as cashbook balance  
**Answer: B**

47. What should happen to a payment outstanding for 90 days?  
A. Leave it indefinitely  
B. Remove it silently  
C. Investigate whether it failed, was cancelled or requires correction  
D. Record it again  
**Answer: C**

48. Which factor is MOST important when classifying a bank entry?  
A. Description length  
B. Who captured it last month  
C. Whether it appears first or last  
D. Verified business purpose and supporting evidence  
**Answer: D**

49. Why are bank reconciliations important fraud-detection controls?  
A. They can reveal unusual, missing or duplicate movements requiring investigation  
B. They prove no fraud exists  
C. They replace approval controls  
D. They eliminate bank access risk  
**Answer: A**

50. What should happen when a customer receipt is reversed by the bank?  
A. Keep Debtors reduced permanently  
B. Restore the correct receivable or other account through the approved process  
C. Record another sale  
D. Delete the bank record  
**Answer: B**

51. Which statement about reconciliation is correct?  
A. Matching totals alone are sufficient  
B. Every difference should be posted to Suspense  
C. Each difference should be understood, supported and appropriately resolved or tracked  
D. Timing items are bookkeeping errors  
**Answer: C**

52. Which principle BEST summarises Module 4?  
A. The bank statement is always correct and complete  
B. Petty cash needs less control because amounts are small  
C. Electronic banking eliminates bookkeeping risk  
D. Reliable cash control combines accurate capture, evidence, reconciliation, exception follow-up and secure access
