# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 4: Cashbooks, Petty Cash & Bank Reconciliation
### Lesson 6: Investigating Reconciling Items and Errors

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Distinguish timing differences from bookkeeping and bank errors.
2. Investigate unmatched bank and cashbook items systematically.
3. Identify duplicate, omitted and incorrectly valued transactions.
4. Escalate suspicious or unresolved items appropriately.
5. Clear reconciling items without destroying the audit trail.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A reconciliation is valuable only if its unresolved items are investigated. Carrying the same unknown difference month after month can hide errors, failed payments or irregular activity. Bookkeepers need a disciplined method for explaining and clearing exceptions.

## Key Terms
- **Unmatched item** — Transaction appearing in one record but not yet matched in the other.
- **Timing difference** — Valid transaction recorded at different times by the business and bank.
- **Book error** — Error in the business accounting records.
- **Bank error** — Error originating in the bank's records.
- **Duplicate** — Same transaction recorded more than once.
- **Exception ageing** — Tracking how long an unresolved reconciling item remains open.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Categorise the difference
Start by asking whether the item is:
- a normal timing difference;
- a missing book entry;
- a duplicate;
- an incorrect amount;
- an unknown receipt/payment;
- a bank error;
- a possible fraud or security issue.

### 2. Trace references
Compare dates, amounts, beneficiary/payer details, invoice references and system transaction IDs. Small timing differences in dates do not necessarily mean different transactions.

### 3. Duplicate payments
If two identical supplier payments appear, compare payment approvals and bank references. Do not assume both are valid just because both cleared the bank.

### 4. Omitted entries
A bank fee or debit order may appear only on the statement. If valid, capture it in the books before finalising the reconciliation.

### 5. Incorrect values
If the cashbook records R7,450 but the authorised bank payment is R7,540, determine whether the capture or payment is wrong. Correct the business records only according to verified evidence.

### 6. Unknown deposits
Do not treat unidentified deposits as sales automatically. Investigate remittance information, customer references and bank details. Use the organisation's approved unallocated-receipts process if necessary.

### 7. Old items
Age reconciling items. A payment outstanding for one day may be normal; one outstanding for three months needs specific investigation.

### 8. Suspicious activity
Unexpected payments, changed beneficiary details or unexplained withdrawals may require immediate escalation to management, finance or bank security functions. Do not privately contact a suspicious beneficiary in a way that compromises an investigation.

### 9. Clearing items
When an item resolves, document the evidence and close it from the reconciliation. Do not simply delete it from the schedule without showing how it was resolved where controls require traceability.

## Worked Example
A R9,800 supplier EFT has appeared as outstanding for two months. Investigation shows the payment was rejected because the supplier account had been closed. The business cashbook still shows the payment as if successful.

The bookkeeper obtains bank evidence, follows the approved reversal process, restores the creditor balance where appropriate and records the failed-payment history. The supplier's new banking details must then be independently verified before any replacement payment.

## Practical Activity
For each item, classify it and state the next action:
1. Deposit recorded on 31 August, banked on 1 September.
2. Bank charge not in the cashbook.
3. Supplier EFT appears twice on the statement.
4. Customer deposit cannot be identified.
5. Payment outstanding for 90 days.
6. Statement shows an unfamiliar cash withdrawal.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. A transaction appearing in one record without a confirmed matching item in the other.
2. A timing difference is valid but appears at different times; a book error requires correction in accounting records.
3. Payment approvals, references, beneficiary, dates and supporting invoices.
4. No. Investigate before classification.
5. Long-outstanding items are higher-risk and need follow-up.
6. Escalate promptly through approved finance/security channels.
7. Verify source evidence, then use the authorised correction process.
8. To preserve the audit trail showing why the item was cleared.

## Key Takeaways
- Every unresolved bank difference should have a classification and owner.
- Old reconciling items deserve increasing attention.
- Unknown deposits and suspicious payments should not be guessed away.
- Corrections must follow evidence.
- Closure should preserve traceability.
