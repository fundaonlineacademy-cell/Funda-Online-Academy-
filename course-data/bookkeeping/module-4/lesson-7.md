# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 4: Cashbooks, Petty Cash & Bank Reconciliation
### Lesson 7: Cash Controls, Daily Cash Position and Handover

**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain key controls over business cash and banking.
2. Prepare a simple daily cash-position summary.
3. Distinguish available cash from committed or restricted cash.
4. Identify banking matters that require management attention.
5. Prepare a clear cash and bank handover.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Bookkeepers do more than record historical transactions. Managers may need to know whether the business has enough available cash for upcoming payments. Accurate daily cash information, combined with strong control and handover, helps prevent missed obligations and unauthorised banking activity.

## Key Terms
- **Cash position** — Current view of available cash and bank balances.
- **Available funds** — Funds currently available for authorised use, subject to restrictions and commitments.
- **Committed payment** — Payment already approved or expected to be made.
- **Segregation of duties** — Separation of incompatible responsibilities to reduce error or misuse.
- **Banking mandate** — Authorised rules defining who may access or approve bank activities.
- **Handover** — Structured transfer of outstanding responsibilities and information.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Cash controls
Strong controls may include:
- restricted online-banking access;
- dual authorisation for defined payments;
- independent bank reconciliation;
- locked petty-cash custody;
- payment-support documentation;
- supplier-bank-detail verification;
- daily review of unusual movements.

### 2. Segregation of duties
Where staffing allows, the person preparing a payment should not have sole authority to create the supplier, approve the payment, release the payment and reconcile the bank. Small businesses may need compensating manager review where full separation is not practical.

### 3. Daily cash position
A daily cash summary may show:
- opening/current bank balances;
- known receipts expected;
- approved payments due;
- available overdraft/credit only if relevant and authorised;
- restricted or ring-fenced funds;
- significant unresolved bank items.

### 4. Do not confuse balance with availability
A bank may show R100,000, but R70,000 may already be committed to payroll and suppliers. Management decisions should not treat the full balance as freely available.

### 5. Forecasting short-term needs
A bookkeeper can support a short-term cash view using approved payment schedules, debtor collections and recurring commitments. This is not the same as guaranteeing future cash receipts.

### 6. Red flags
Escalate issues such as:
- unexpected withdrawals;
- repeated failed payments;
- unusually large transfers;
- unexplained cash shortages;
- urgent requests to bypass payment controls;
- supplier bank-detail changes;
- projected inability to meet important due payments.

### 7. Handover
A strong handover identifies:
- latest reconciled date;
- current cashbook status;
- unreconciled items;
- payments awaiting approval/release;
- unknown deposits;
- bank-access or system issues;
- deadlines and responsible people.

### 8. Never hand over credentials
Passwords, MFA codes and personal authentication methods should not be transferred as part of a handover. Access should be granted through approved user-management processes.

## Worked Example
Bank balance at 09:00: R85,000.
Known incoming receipt today: R20,000.
Approved supplier payments due today: R35,000.
Payroll due tomorrow: R50,000.

Although today's bank balance appears healthy, the bookkeeper highlights that after expected receipt and commitments the available cushion is only R20,000. Management can then plan rather than assume R85,000 is free cash.

## Practical Activity
Prepare a one-page daily cash-position report using:
- Bank A: R60,000
- Bank B: R15,000
- Receipts expected today: R25,000
- Supplier payments due today: R30,000
- Payroll tomorrow: R55,000
- Unidentified bank deposit: R6,000

State which amounts are confirmed, expected or unresolved and identify any management concern.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Separating incompatible responsibilities among different people or controls.
2. Banking access can create or release real financial transactions.
3. A current summary of cash/bank balances and material short-term commitments.
4. Funds may already be committed, restricted or subject to pending payments.
5. Examples: unusual withdrawals, changed supplier details, failed payments, unexplained shortages or bypass requests.
6. Reconciliation status, unresolved items, payment status, deadlines and ownership.
7. No. Use approved access-management procedures.
8. Expected receipts may be delayed or fail to arrive.

## Key Takeaways
- Cash control combines accounting, access and approval controls.
- Available cash must be viewed alongside commitments.
- Daily cash reporting should separate confirmed facts from expectations.
- Red flags require prompt escalation.
- Good handover protects continuity without sharing credentials.
