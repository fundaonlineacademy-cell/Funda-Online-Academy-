# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 4: Cashbooks, Petty Cash & Bank Reconciliation
### Lesson 5: Preparing a Bank Reconciliation

**Estimated study time:** 55–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of a bank reconciliation.
2. Identify common timing differences between bank and book records.
3. Prepare a basic bank reconciliation from an adjusted cashbook balance.
4. Distinguish reconciling items from errors requiring correction.
5. Verify that the reconciliation explains the difference between the two records.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A bank reconciliation proves that the business's bank records and the bank's records can be explained even when their balances are temporarily different. It is one of the strongest routine bookkeeping controls because it can reveal missing entries, duplicate captures, timing differences and unusual transactions.

## Key Terms
- **Bank reconciliation** — Comparison explaining the difference between book and bank-statement balances at a date.
- **Outstanding payment** — Payment recorded by the business but not yet processed by the bank.
- **Deposit in transit** — Receipt recorded by the business but not yet reflected by the bank.
- **Reconciling item** — Valid item explaining a temporary difference between records.
- **Adjusted cashbook balance** — Book balance after known book-side updates and corrections.
- **Cut-off date** — Date at which the reconciliation is prepared.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start with an updated cashbook
Before reconciling, capture valid bank charges, interest, debit orders, reversals and other book-side items identified from the statement. Correct known cashbook errors.

### 2. Compare transactions
Match the cashbook and bank statement line by line or by reference. Mark items that appear in both records and investigate those appearing in only one.

### 3. Outstanding payments
A payment may be recorded in the cashbook on 29 August but only clear the bank on 1 September. At 31 August it is a valid timing difference.

### 4. Deposits in transit
A receipt recorded late on the final day of the month may only appear on the bank statement the next day. Again, this is a timing difference if supported by evidence.

### 5. Bank errors
Bank errors are uncommon but possible. Do not change the business books to imitate a bank error. Gather evidence and report the issue through the banking process.

### 6. Reconciliation format
One method begins with the bank-statement balance and adjusts for outstanding items until it agrees with the adjusted cashbook balance. Another format begins with the book balance. Use the organisation's approved format consistently.

### 7. Worked calculation
Adjusted cashbook balance: R22,400 Dr.
Bank-statement balance: R20,900 Dr.
Outstanding EFT payments: R3,000.
Deposit in transit: R4,500.

Starting with statement balance:
R20,900 + R4,500 deposit in transit - R3,000 outstanding payments = **R22,400**.

The reconciliation agrees to the adjusted cashbook.

### 8. Old reconciling items
An outstanding payment that remains unmatched for several months needs investigation. It may have been cancelled, rejected, duplicated or incorrectly recorded.

### 9. Review and sign-off
Where required, a second person should review the reconciliation, supporting statement, outstanding items and evidence. Reconciliation quality depends on genuine review, not a signature alone.

## Practical Activity
Prepare a reconciliation using:
- Adjusted cashbook balance: R35,800 Dr.
- Bank statement balance: R31,300 Dr.
- Deposit in transit: R7,000.
- Outstanding payments: R2,500.

Then explain what should happen if one outstanding payment of R900 has remained unreconciled for three months.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. To explain and verify differences between bank and book balances.
2. So remaining differences are genuine timing or unresolved items, not known missing book entries.
3. A payment recorded in the books but not yet processed by the bank.
4. A receipt recorded by the business but not yet reflected by the bank.
5. No. Report and resolve the bank error through the appropriate process.
6. Long-outstanding items may indicate errors, cancellation or failed transactions.
7. That all valid differences are explained and the adjusted balances agree.
8. It strengthens detection of mistakes or unsupported items.

## Key Takeaways
- Reconciliation begins after the cashbook is updated.
- Timing differences are not the same as bookkeeping errors.
- Every reconciling item needs evidence and a clear status.
- Old outstanding items require follow-up.
- A completed reconciliation should explain the entire difference.
