# Module 4 Formative Assessment Bank
## Cashbooks, Petty Cash & Bank Reconciliation

**Bank size:** 32 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 15 random questions per attempt  
**Pass standard:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Prerequisite:** Complete all eight Module 4 lessons.

## Lesson 1 — Cashbook Structure and Cash/Bank Transactions
1. What is the main purpose of a cashbook?  
A. Record receipts and payments  
B. Replace source documents  
C. Record only sales  
D. Prepare payroll  
**Answer: A**

2. A customer pays R4,000 toward an existing debtor balance. What is the normal entry?  
A. Debit Debtors; Credit Bank  
B. Debit Bank; Credit Debtors  
C. Debit Sales; Credit Bank  
D. Debit Bank; Credit Sales  
**Answer: B**

3. Why should an unexplained bank deposit not be posted to Sales immediately?  
A. Deposits are never income  
B. Sales cannot be received electronically  
C. The source and nature of the receipt must first be confirmed  
D. Bank statements are unreliable  
**Answer: C**

4. Which control MOST directly supports cashbook traceability?  
A. Random descriptions  
B. Personal notes  
C. No references  
D. Clear source-document and transaction references  
**Answer: D**

## Lesson 2 — EFTs, Debit Orders, Card Payments and Bank Entries
5. A supplier invoice was already captured and is now paid by EFT. What normally happens?  
A. The expense is recorded again  
B. Bank decreases and Creditors decreases  
C. Debtors increases  
D. Sales increases  
**Answer: B**

6. Why can a card settlement be lower than gross sales?  
A. Sales were necessarily overstated  
B. VAT disappears  
C. Merchant fees may be deducted before deposit  
D. Bank must be wrong  
**Answer: C**

7. What should happen when a bank-feed item duplicates a manually captured payment?  
A. Capture both  
B. Delete the manual record without review  
C. Post the imported item to Suspense  
D. Match the feed item to the existing transaction  
**Answer: D**

8. A supplier urgently emails new bank details. What is the BEST response?  
A. Use the approved independent verification process before payment  
B. Pay immediately  
C. Reply with online-banking credentials  
D. Ignore the supplier permanently  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 3 — Petty Cash and the Imprest System
9. What is the purpose of the imprest system?  
A. Allow unlimited petty cash  
B. Restore a controlled float after valid expenditure is reconciled  
C. Avoid receipts  
D. Replace normal purchasing  
**Answer: B**

10. A R2,000 float has R650 cash and R1,300 valid vouchers. What is the difference?  
A. No difference  
B. R100 overage  
C. R50 shortage  
D. R50 overage  
**Answer: C**

11. What should happen when a petty-cash receipt is missing?  
A. Create a replacement personally  
B. Ignore the transaction  
C. Increase the float  
D. Follow the authorised missing-evidence process  
**Answer: D**

12. Which is a strong petty-cash control?  
A. Restricted custody and periodic counts  
B. Shared unlocked cash box  
C. Personal borrowing  
D. Unsigned vouchers  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 4 — Bank Statements and Cashbook Updating
13. Which item normally requires a cashbook update when first discovered on the statement?  
A. Outstanding payment already recorded  
B. Bank charge not yet recorded  
C. Deposit in transit already recorded  
D. Future payment  
**Answer: B**

14. Why should a payment already recorded in the cashbook not be captured again merely because it appears on the bank statement?  
A. Bank payments are not bookkeeping transactions  
B. Bank statements cannot be used  
C. It would duplicate the transaction  
D. Cashbooks only contain receipts  
**Answer: C**

15. What should happen with an identified customer deposit not yet in the cashbook?  
A. Ignore it  
B. Post it to Capital  
C. Post it to Sales regardless of history  
D. Record it against the appropriate customer/receivable account  
**Answer: D**

16. What is an adjusted cashbook balance?  
A. Book balance after valid statement-driven updates and corrections  
B. Statement balance before review  
C. Total outstanding payments  
D. Bank limit  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 5 — Preparing a Bank Reconciliation
17. What is a bank reconciliation designed to explain?  
A. Payroll deductions  
B. Differences between bank and book balances  
C. Supplier ageing  
D. VAT output only  
**Answer: B**

18. What is a deposit in transit?  
A. Deposit not yet recorded by the business  
B. Bank fee  
C. Receipt recorded by the business but not yet reflected by the bank  
D. Unauthorised withdrawal  
**Answer: C**

19. What should happen to a bank error?  
A. Change the cashbook to imitate it  
B. Ignore it  
C. Post a false adjustment  
D. Report and resolve it through the appropriate bank process  
**Answer: D**

20. Why should old outstanding items be reviewed?  
A. They may indicate failed, cancelled or incorrectly recorded transactions  
B. They automatically clear after 90 days  
C. They are always fraud  
D. Reconciliations should keep them forever  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 6 — Investigating Reconciling Items and Errors
21. What is an unmatched item?  
A. A completed transaction  
B. A transaction appearing in one record without a confirmed match in the other  
C. A petty-cash voucher  
D. A trial-balance total  
**Answer: B**

22. Which is a likely cause of an unexplained difference?  
A. Correctly matched transactions  
B. Same opening balances  
C. Duplicate or omitted capture  
D. Properly cleared items  
**Answer: C**

23. Why should an unidentified receipt remain controlled as an exception?  
A. It is always sales  
B. It should be refunded immediately  
C. It can be allocated randomly  
D. Its source and correct account are not yet confirmed  
**Answer: D**

24. How should a resolved reconciling item be closed?  
A. With evidence showing how it was resolved  
B. By deleting history  
C. By removing all references  
D. By changing the statement  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 7 — Cash Controls, Daily Cash Position and Handover
25. What does segregation of duties mean?  
A. One person controls everything  
B. Incompatible responsibilities are separated where practical  
C. Every employee gets banking access  
D. Reconciliations are optional  
**Answer: B**

26. Why is bank balance not always equal to freely available cash?  
A. Bank balances are estimates  
B. Statements exclude cash  
C. Funds may already be committed or restricted  
D. Payments never reduce bank  
**Answer: C**

27. Which item is a banking red flag?  
A. Normal approved recurring payment  
B. Expected customer receipt  
C. Completed reconciliation  
D. Unexplained large withdrawal  
**Answer: D**

28. What should a handover include?  
A. Reconciliation status, unresolved items, payment deadlines and ownership  
B. Passwords and MFA codes  
C. Only completed work  
D. Personal opinions  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 8 — Bank-reconciliation Practical
29. Why should book-side items be updated before the final reconciliation?  
A. To make the statement balance larger  
B. So remaining differences are timing or unresolved exceptions rather than known missing entries  
C. To eliminate all outstanding payments  
D. To change bank records  
**Answer: B**

30. What should happen if a reconciliation still differs after known items are processed?  
A. Add a balancing journal  
B. Ignore it  
C. Continue investigating and control the difference as an exception  
D. Delete the statement  
**Answer: C**

31. What should a reviewer confirm before sign-off?  
A. Only the final total  
B. Only the bank name  
C. Only that a schedule exists  
D. Updates, reconciling items, arithmetic and supporting evidence  
**Answer: D**

32. What is the true objective of bank reconciliation?  
A. Prove and explain the relationship between business and bank records  
B. Force two numbers to agree  
C. Remove audit trails  
D. Replace the cashbook  
**Answer: A**
