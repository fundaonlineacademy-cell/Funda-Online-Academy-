# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 4: Cashbooks, Petty Cash & Bank Reconciliation
### Lesson 8: Bank-reconciliation Practical

**Estimated study time:** 60–70 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Update a cashbook from bank-statement information.
2. Identify and classify reconciliation differences.
3. Prepare a complete bank reconciliation.
4. Investigate unresolved items and document follow-up actions.
5. Apply control checks before sign-off.

## Why This Lesson Matters
This practical combines the full bank-control workflow. The aim is not only to make two balances agree, but to understand each difference, correct book-side errors, preserve timing items and show a clear audit trail.

## Practical Scenario
At 31 August, the cashbook of Khula Office Services shows a debit Bank balance of **R42,500** before final statement review.

The bank statement shows a closing balance of **R39,650**.

During review, the bookkeeper identifies:
1. Bank charges R250 not in the cashbook.
2. Interest received R100 not in the cashbook.
3. Insurance debit order R1,200 not in the cashbook.
4. Customer EFT R3,500 not yet captured in the cashbook and clearly identified against Debtors.
5. Deposit of R5,000 recorded in the cashbook on 31 August but only reflected by the bank on 1 September.
6. Supplier EFT R4,000 recorded in the cashbook on 31 August but only processed by the bank on 2 September.
7. A R700 payment in the cashbook was incorrectly entered as R770; source evidence and bank statement confirm R700.

## Step 1: Update the cashbook
Starting book balance: R42,500 Dr.

Adjust for statement items:
- Bank charges: -R250
- Interest received: +R100
- Insurance debit order: -R1,200
- Customer receipt: +R3,500
- Correct cashbook overstatement of payment by R70: +R70

Adjusted cashbook balance:
R42,500 - R250 + R100 - R1,200 + R3,500 + R70 = **R44,720 Dr**.

The deposit in transit and outstanding supplier EFT are not captured again because they already exist correctly in the cashbook.

## Step 2: Prepare the bank reconciliation
Statement balance: R39,650 Dr.
Add deposit in transit: +R5,000.
Subtract outstanding payment: -R4,000.

Reconciled statement balance = **R40,650**, which does not yet equal the adjusted cashbook balance of R44,720.

This is a deliberate warning: the reconciliation is not complete. The difference is **R4,070**.

A professional bookkeeper does not force the reconciliation. The next step is to continue tracing unmatched items until the full R4,070 is explained.

## Step 3: Investigate the unexplained difference
The investigation should include:
- recheck the arithmetic;
- compare every cashbook entry to the statement;
- check whether the R3,500 customer receipt is actually included in the bank-statement closing balance;
- verify cut-off dates;
- inspect duplicate or omitted entries;
- compare opening balances;
- review statement pages for completeness.

If the R3,500 customer receipt is already in the bank statement, then adding it to the cashbook is correct but it should also have been one of the previously unmatched statement items. If another R570 item remains unidentified, that too must be investigated.

The point of the exercise is that a reconciliation is not accepted merely because most items appear reasonable.

## Step 4: Control the exception
Until resolved, record the R4,070 difference in an exception schedule with:
- amount;
- date identified;
- investigation owner;
- evidence reviewed;
- next action;
- escalation deadline.

Do not post a false balancing journal simply to make the reconciliation equal.

## Step 5: Final sign-off checklist
Before sign-off confirm:
- correct bank account and period;
- cashbook fully updated;
- all book-side corrections supported;
- timing items supported by later bank evidence where available;
- unresolved differences separately controlled;
- preparer and reviewer evidence completed where required.

## Practical Activity
Using a fresh scenario, prepare a reconciliation:
- Cashbook before update: R28,400 Dr
- Statement balance: R25,050 Dr
- Bank charges not captured: R150
- Interest received not captured: R80
- Customer direct deposit not captured: R2,000
- Deposit in transit: R3,500
- Outstanding EFT: R2,220

First update the cashbook, then reconcile from the bank-statement balance and determine whether the records agree. If they do not, state the unexplained difference and investigation steps.

## Knowledge Check
1. Which items belong in the cashbook before reconciliation?
2. Which items remain only as timing differences?
3. Why must an unexplained reconciliation difference not be forced away?
4. What should happen when a cashbook payment amount is wrong?
5. Why should old outstanding payments be checked after the period?
6. What should an exception schedule contain?
7. What does a reviewer need to verify?
8. What is the real objective of bank reconciliation?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Valid unrecorded bank-originated items and verified book corrections.
2. Valid transactions already in the cashbook but not yet reflected by the bank, such as deposits in transit and outstanding payments.
3. It may conceal an error, omission or irregular transaction.
4. Correct the books to verified source evidence through the approved process.
5. To confirm they genuinely cleared or identify failed/cancelled transactions.
6. Amount, cause/status, owner, evidence, next action and timing.
7. That balances, updates, reconciling items and evidence genuinely support the reconciliation.
8. To explain and verify the relationship between business and bank records, not merely make totals look equal.

## Key Takeaways
- Update the cashbook before reconciling timing differences.
- Do not duplicate items already recorded correctly.
- Unexplained differences must remain visible and controlled.
- A reconciliation is complete only when every difference is understood.
- Review and evidence matter as much as arithmetic.
