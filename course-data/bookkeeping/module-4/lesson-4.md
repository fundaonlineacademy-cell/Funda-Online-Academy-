# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 4: Cashbooks, Petty Cash & Bank Reconciliation
### Lesson 4: Bank Statements and Cashbook Updating

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Read common information on a bank statement.
2. Identify bank-originated transactions not yet recorded in the cashbook.
3. Update the cashbook for valid charges, interest and direct transactions.
4. Distinguish cashbook corrections from reconciliation timing items.
5. Prepare the updated book balance for bank reconciliation.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A bank statement is an independent record maintained by the bank. The business cashbook may not yet contain every transaction appearing on the statement. Before preparing a bank reconciliation, the bookkeeper normally updates the cashbook for valid items that belong in the books.

## Key Terms
- **Bank statement** — Bank's record of transactions and balance for an account.
- **Book balance** — Balance according to the business cashbook or bank ledger.
- **Bank-originated item** — Transaction first identified from the bank statement, such as charges or interest.
- **Direct deposit** — Money deposited directly into the account, potentially before the business identifies the payer.
- **Returned item** — Payment or receipt reversed or dishonoured.
- **Cut-off** — Date separating one accounting or reconciliation period from another.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Why statement and cashbook differ
Differences can arise because:
- bank charges have not been captured;
- interest appears first on the statement;
- debit orders were processed automatically;
- customers deposited money directly;
- payments or deposits are still in transit;
- either the bank or business made an error.

### 2. Review the statement systematically
Check statement date range, opening balance, transaction dates, descriptions, references, amounts and closing balance. Confirm that you are working with the correct account and period.

### 3. Update valid book-side items
Examples:
- bank charges R220 → Debit Bank Charges Expense; Credit Bank.
- interest received R150 → Debit Bank; Credit Interest Income.
- debit order for insurance R1,800 → Debit Insurance/appropriate account; Credit Bank, subject to the underlying accounting setup.

### 4. Direct customer deposits
A deposit may need to reduce Debtors if it relates to an existing customer invoice. If the payer cannot be identified, do not assign it to a customer or sales account without evidence.

### 5. Reversals
If a customer payment previously recorded is reversed by the bank, the bookkeeper must restore the correct receivable or other account using the approved process.

### 6. Correcting cashbook errors
If the business recorded R2,450 but the actual authorised payment was R2,540, correct the cashbook to the source evidence. A cashbook error is not merely a reconciling timing difference.

### 7. Do not update for pure timing differences
A valid payment already recorded in the books but not yet processed by the bank generally remains a reconciliation item. Capturing it again would duplicate the transaction.

### 8. Prepare an adjusted book balance
After all valid book-side updates and corrections, recalculate the cashbook closing balance. This adjusted book balance becomes the starting point for comparing timing differences with the statement balance.

## Worked Example
Cashbook balance before update: R18,500 Dr.
Statement reveals:
- bank charge R200 not recorded;
- interest received R100 not recorded;
- debit order R1,400 not recorded.

Adjusted cashbook:
R18,500 - R200 + R100 - R1,400 = **R17,000 Dr**.

An outstanding EFT already in the cashbook is not captured again; it belongs on the reconciliation if the bank has not processed it by cut-off.

## Practical Activity
Starting cashbook balance: R30,000 Dr. Bank statement shows these unrecorded valid items:
- fees R320;
- interest R90;
- insurance debit order R2,200;
- identified customer deposit R4,500 against Debtors.

Calculate the updated cashbook balance and write the double-entry for each item.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Unrecorded transactions, timing differences and errors can exist between the two records.
2. Examples: fees, interest, debit orders and reversals.
3. Debit the relevant bank-charge expense and credit Bank.
4. Investigate before allocation.
5. A cashbook error needs correction in the books; a timing item may already be correctly recorded but not yet reflected by the bank.
6. It is already in the books; duplicating it would create a false second transaction.
7. The cashbook balance after valid statement-driven updates and corrections.
8. Correct bank account, statement period, opening/closing balances and completeness.

## Key Takeaways
- Update the books for valid statement items before reconciliation.
- Do not confuse unrecorded book items with timing differences.
- Unknown deposits require investigation.
- Reversals must restore the correct underlying account.
- The adjusted cashbook balance provides a cleaner basis for reconciliation.
