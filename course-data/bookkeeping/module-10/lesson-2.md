# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 10: Practical Bookkeeping Project
### Lesson 2: Build the Chart of Accounts and Opening Controls

**Estimated study time:** 50–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Build a simple chart of accounts for the simulated business.
2. Group accounts into assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses.
3. Enter and verify opening balances.
4. Design account-coding and naming controls.
5. Prevent duplicate or inappropriate ledger accounts.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A bookkeeping project becomes difficult to control when accounts are created without structure. A clear chart of accounts gives transactions consistent destinations and makes later reports understandable. Opening controls also ensure that the project starts from verified balances rather than from unexplained figures.

## Key Terms
- **Chart of accounts** — Structured list of ledger accounts used by the business.
- **Account code** — Identifier assigned to a ledger account.
- **Opening balance** — Balance brought forward into the current period.
- **Control account** — General-ledger account summarising related subsidiary records.
- **Duplicate account** — Two accounts representing the same underlying category without a valid reason.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start with the reporting needs
For Ubuntu Office Solutions, the chart should be detailed enough to support the project but not so detailed that every individual item becomes its own ledger account.

A practical structure may include:
- 1000–1999 Assets;
- 2000–2999 Liabilities;
- 3000–3999 Equity;
- 4000–4999 Income;
- 5000–5999 Expenses.

The exact numbering convention is an internal design choice. Consistency is the key.

### 2. Suggested project accounts
Assets:
- Bank;
- Petty Cash;
- Debtors Control;
- Equipment;
- Accumulated Depreciation where applicable.

Liabilities:
- Creditors Control;
- VAT Control where used in the simulation;
- Accrued Expenses where required.

Equity:
- Owner/Shareholder Equity;
- Drawings where relevant to the fictional structure.

Income:
- Service Revenue;
- Stationery Sales.

Expenses:
- Rent;
- Telephone/Data;
- Stationery/Consumables;
- Bank Charges;
- Cleaning;
- Depreciation;
- Bad Debts where used.

### 3. Avoid unnecessary accounts
Do not create a new ledger account every time a slightly different description appears on a source document. “Printer paper,” “pens” and “folders” may all belong to one stationery/consumables account depending on the organisation's reporting needs.

### 4. Check normal balance logic
Assets and expenses normally carry debit balances. Liabilities, equity and income normally carry credit balances. Exceptions can occur, but an unusual balance should prompt investigation.

### 5. Enter opening balances carefully
Use the approved opening schedule from Lesson 1. Enter each balance once. Then extract a trial balance or opening-balance check.

If opening debits and credits do not agree, do not proceed to current-period transactions until the difference is explained.

### 6. Control-account structure
If the project uses individual customer and supplier records, Debtors Control and Creditors Control in the general ledger should reconcile to the total subsidiary balances.

For example, if three customer balances are R2,000, R1,500 and R1,500, the Debtors Control account should equal R5,000.

### 7. Naming conventions
Use clear names such as `Telephone & Data Expense` rather than vague names such as `Other 1`. Avoid creating `Bank Charges`, `Bank Fees` and `Monthly Bank Cost` as separate accounts for the same category unless reporting requirements justify it.

### 8. Opening control checklist
Before processing transactions, confirm:
- chart contains all necessary categories;
- no obvious duplicates exist;
- opening balances agree to approved evidence;
- debit and credit totals agree;
- control accounts agree to subsidiary schedules;
- account codes are unique;
- account names are clear.

## Worked Example
Suppose the customer opening schedule shows:
- Customer A R2,500 Dr
- Customer B R1,750 Dr
- Customer C R750 Dr

Total subsidiary debtors = R5,000. The general-ledger Debtors Control opening balance should therefore be R5,000 Dr. If the control account contains R5,500, the R500 difference must be investigated before current transactions are processed.

## Practical Activity
Create a chart of accounts for the simulated business with at least 18 accounts. Assign codes and account classes. Then enter the approved opening balances and prepare an opening trial balance. Record any unusual or duplicate accounts you considered but rejected.

## Key Takeaways
- The chart of accounts is the structural foundation of the bookkeeping system.
- Account detail should match reporting needs, not every source description.
- Opening balances must be verified before live processing.
- Control accounts must reconcile to their subsidiary records.
- Clear account names and codes reduce classification errors.
