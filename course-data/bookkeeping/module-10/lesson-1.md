# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 10: Practical Bookkeeping Project
### Lesson 1: Capstone Brief and Simulated Business Setup

**Estimated study time:** 55–65 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Interpret a practical bookkeeping project brief.
2. Set up a fictional business profile and opening information.
3. Identify the source documents and records required for the project.
4. Define authority limits, control points and evidence requirements.
5. Plan a complete bookkeeping workflow from transaction capture to reporting.

## Why This Lesson Matters
The final module brings together the full course. Instead of treating bookkeeping as separate topics, the capstone asks you to operate a small simulated bookkeeping system from beginning to end. You will receive source documents, analyse transactions, maintain journals and ledgers, reconcile accounts, process adjustments and produce basic reports.

The project uses fictional information so that you can practise realistic bookkeeping without exposing real customer, employee or business data.

## Key Terms
- **Capstone** — Integrated final practical project demonstrating multiple course skills.
- **Opening balance** — Balance carried into the start of an accounting period.
- **Control point** — Required check or approval in a workflow.
- **Evidence index** — Register linking each project output to its supporting evidence.
- **Simulated business** — Fictional business created for safe training and assessment.
- **Authority limit** — Boundary defining which actions the learner may perform and which require referral or approval.

## Project Scenario
You are the junior bookkeeper for **Ubuntu Office Solutions (Pty) Ltd**, a fictional small South African office-services business. For this simulation, the business sells office-support services and selected stationery items.

The project period is one month. You will receive or create simulated evidence for:
- owner/shareholder funding;
- customer sales;
- customer receipts;
- supplier purchases;
- supplier payments;
- operating expenses;
- petty cash;
- bank charges;
- debtor and creditor balances;
- period-end adjustments.

Where VAT is used in the practical, use only the assumptions supplied in the activity. Do not generalise project assumptions into tax advice.

## Step 1: Create the business profile
Record:
- business name;
- simulated registration/reference details;
- financial period;
- bank account label;
- reporting currency: South African rand;
- bookkeeping basis used in the project;
- person responsible for bookkeeping;
- fictional manager/approver.

Do not use real personal identity numbers, bank accounts or customer information.

## Step 2: Establish opening balances
Your project may include opening balances such as:
- Bank;
- Debtors;
- Equipment;
- Creditors;
- Owner/Shareholder Equity.

Confirm that opening debit balances equal opening credit balances before processing current-period activity. If they do not agree, investigate before continuing.

## Step 3: Set authority limits
For this simulation, the learner may:
- analyse and capture supported transactions;
- prepare reconciliations;
- draft correction entries;
- prepare reports and schedules.

The fictional manager must approve:
- unusual manual journals;
- write-offs;
- changes to supplier banking details;
- significant corrections;
- final reporting pack.

This teaches an important principle: bookkeeping skill does not automatically create authority.

## Step 4: Create the project evidence index
Your evidence index should include columns for:
- evidence number;
- date;
- document type;
- transaction/reference;
- related lesson/task;
- file location;
- status;
- reviewer notes.

Example:
`E01 | 01 Sep | Opening balance schedule | OB-001 | Setup | /Opening/OB-001.pdf | Checked`

## Step 5: Build the workflow map
Use this project flow:
**Source Evidence → Transaction Analysis → Journal/Module Capture → Ledger → Reconciliation → Adjustment → Trial Balance → Reports → Review → Handover**

Every project output should link back to evidence.

## Step 6: Define quality controls
Your project controls should include:
- unique references;
- duplicate checks;
- arithmetic checks;
- approval checks;
- account-classification review;
- bank/debtor/creditor reconciliation;
- balanced trial balance;
- final cross-document consistency review.

## Worked Example
Opening balances are:
- Bank R30,000 Dr
- Equipment R20,000 Dr
- Debtors R5,000 Dr
- Creditors R10,000 Cr
- Equity R45,000 Cr

Total debits = R55,000 and credits = R55,000, so the opening position balances.

If Equity were entered as R44,000, the bookkeeper should not create a random R1,000 adjustment simply to force agreement. The opening schedule must be checked against approved evidence.

Create your capstone control pack containing:
1. business profile;

5. workflow map;
6. ten-point quality checklist.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why does the capstone use fictional data?
2. What is an opening balance?
3. Why must opening debits equal opening credits?
4. What is an authority limit?
5. What is the purpose of an evidence index?
6. Name four project control points.
7. Why should unusual journals require approval?
8. What is the full project workflow?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. To practise safely without exposing real sensitive information.
2. A balance brought forward into the start of the project period.
3. The accounting system must begin from a balanced position.
4. A boundary defining what a role may do or approve.
5. It makes project evidence traceable to transactions and outputs.
6. Examples include duplicate checks, approval checks, reconciliations and trial-balance review.
7. They can materially alter ledger balances outside routine transaction flows.
8. Evidence, analysis, capture, ledger, reconciliation, adjustment, trial balance, reporting, review and handover.

## Key Takeaways
- The capstone tests integrated bookkeeping, not isolated memory.
- Start from verified opening information.
- Every transaction should be traceable to evidence.
- Authority and bookkeeping responsibility are not the same thing.
- Controls should be designed before processing begins.
