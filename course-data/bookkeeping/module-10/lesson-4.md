# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 10: Practical Bookkeeping Project
### Lesson 4: Maintain Journals, Ledgers and Cashbooks

**Estimated study time:** 55–65 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Organise processed transactions into appropriate journals or transaction modules.
2. Post entries to general-ledger and subsidiary records.
3. Maintain cashbook records consistently.
4. Balance key ledger accounts.
5. Trace ledger balances back to transaction evidence.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Capturing individual transactions is only part of bookkeeping. Those transactions must accumulate into journals, ledgers and cash records that can later be reconciled and reported. This lesson tests whether your project records tell one consistent accounting story.

## Key Terms
- **Journal** — Chronological transaction record used before or during ledger posting.
- **General ledger** — Main set of accounts summarising the financial effects of transactions.
- **Subsidiary ledger** — Detailed customer, supplier or other supporting records linked to a control account.
- **Cashbook** — Record of bank and cash receipts/payments.
- **Posting reference** — Identifier linking journal and ledger records.
- **Balance** — Net debit or credit amount remaining in an account.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Group transactions logically
Use the project's approved journals or transaction modules. Routine credit sales, purchases, receipts and payments should flow through the appropriate structures, while authorised adjustments may use the general journal.

### 2. Preserve double-entry
Every transaction must maintain equal debit and credit effects. A batch can include multiple accounts, but total debits must equal total credits.

### 3. Post to the general ledger
Each journal entry should update the relevant ledger accounts. Review account balances after posting rather than waiting until the end of the project.

### 4. Maintain subsidiary records
Customer and supplier transactions should update individual accounts where the project requires them. Totals must reconcile to Debtors Control and Creditors Control.

### 5. Maintain the cashbook
Record bank receipts and payments, including items added from the bank statement such as charges or interest where applicable to the simulation.

The cashbook is the business's internal bank record; it should later reconcile to the external bank statement.

### 6. Use references
Use transaction numbers or references so a ledger entry can be traced to the journal and source evidence. Poor references make investigation unnecessarily difficult.

### 7. Balance accounts
At the end of the project period, calculate account balances. Assets and expenses normally close with debit balances; liabilities, equity and income normally close with credit balances, subject to the actual transactions.

Unusual balances should be reviewed.

### 8. Check control accounts
Compare:
- Debtors Control to total customer balances;
- Creditors Control to total supplier balances;
- Bank ledger/cashbook to the internal cashbook total before bank reconciliation;
- Petty Cash to the physical/simulated float and vouchers.

### 9. Investigate, do not force
If control totals disagree, trace transactions and references. Do not post unexplained journals merely to make totals agree.

## Worked Example
Customer accounts show:
- Customer A R2,900 Dr
- Customer B R1,600 Dr
- Customer C R500 Cr because of an overpayment

Net subsidiary debtors = R4,000 Dr. Debtors Control should agree to R4,000 Dr if all customer entries were posted correctly.

If Debtors Control is R4,500 Dr, investigate missing or duplicated postings before correcting anything.

## Practical Activity
Using your Lesson 3 batch:
1. prepare or update the relevant journals;
2. post all transactions to the general ledger;
3. update customer and supplier subsidiary records;
4. update the cashbook;
5. calculate closing balances;
6. reconcile Debtors and Creditors control accounts to their subsidiary totals.

Record any difference as an exception until explained.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. It accumulates the financial effects of transactions by account.
2. Detailed records supporting a control account, such as customer balances.
3. Double-entry bookkeeping requires equal debit and credit effects.
4. They allow transactions to be traced between evidence, journals and ledgers.
5. The net total of the customer subsidiary records.
6. The organisation's internal record of bank/cash movements.
7. They may reveal classification, posting or other errors.
8. Investigate source records and postings before making corrections.

## Key Takeaways
- Journals, ledgers and cashbooks must agree with one another.
- Subsidiary records support control accounts.
- Posting references are part of the audit trail.
- Account balancing should include reasonableness checks.
- Never create unexplained balancing entries.
