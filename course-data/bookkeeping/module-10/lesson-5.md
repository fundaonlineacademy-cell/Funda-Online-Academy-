# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 10: Practical Bookkeeping Project
### Lesson 5: Reconcile Bank, Debtors and Creditors

**Estimated study time:** 60–70 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Reconcile the bank account to the bank statement.
2. Reconcile Debtors Control to customer balances.
3. Reconcile Creditors Control to supplier balances.
4. Investigate reconciling items and unexplained differences.
5. Document reconciliation evidence and follow-up actions.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Reconciliation is one of the most important bookkeeping controls. It compares records that should describe the same financial position and explains any differences. A balanced ledger is not enough if the bank, customer or supplier detail tells a different story.

## Key Terms
- **Bank reconciliation** — Comparison of internal bank records to the external bank statement.
- **Reconciling item** — Timing or recording difference explaining why two related balances differ.
- **Outstanding deposit** — Receipt recorded internally but not yet reflected by the bank.
- **Unpresented payment** — Payment recorded internally but not yet reflected by the bank.
- **Debtors reconciliation** — Comparison of Debtors Control to individual customer balances.
- **Creditors reconciliation** — Comparison of Creditors Control to individual supplier balances.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Update the cashbook first
Before preparing the bank reconciliation, capture valid bank-statement items not yet in the cashbook, such as bank charges or authorised debit orders.

### 2. Compare transaction by transaction
Match deposits, EFTs, card settlements and charges between the cashbook and bank statement. Mark matched items and investigate unmatched entries.

### 3. Separate timing from errors
An outstanding deposit can be legitimate timing. A payment with the wrong amount may be an error. Do not treat all differences as timing items.

### 4. Reconcile the closing bank position
After updating the cashbook, prepare a reconciliation showing the adjusted internal balance and legitimate timing differences. The reconciled result should explain the external statement balance.

### 5. Debtors reconciliation
Add the closing balances of all customer accounts. Compare the net total to Debtors Control. Then investigate missing invoices, duplicated receipts, unallocated credits or posting differences.

### 6. Creditors reconciliation
Perform the same control for suppliers. In addition, compare significant supplier accounts to supplier statements where available.

### 7. Supplier statement differences
Common causes include:
- invoice not yet captured;
- credit note missing;
- payment not yet allocated;
- supplier error;
- timing difference;
- duplicate capture.

### 8. Record unresolved items
Every unresolved difference should have an owner, next action and target date. A reconciliation is incomplete if differences are merely listed and forgotten.

## Worked Example
The updated cashbook balance is R42,300 Dr. The bank statement shows R40,800 Dr. Investigation finds an outstanding deposit of R2,000 and an unpresented EFT of R500.

Bank-statement balance R40,800  
+ Outstanding deposit R2,000  
- Unpresented payment R500  
= R42,300

The difference is explained by timing items.

If the result had been R42,100, the remaining R200 difference would require further investigation.

## Practical Activity
Prepare three reconciliations for the capstone:
1. Bank reconciliation.
2. Debtors Control to customer schedule.
3. Creditors Control to supplier schedule.

For each, include:
- opening comparison;
- reconciling items;
- corrected entries where authorised;
- unresolved exceptions;
- final reconciled balance;
- evidence references.

## Knowledge Check

3. What is an outstanding deposit?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Internal records must first include valid known bank items.
2. A difference that explains why related records do not yet agree.
3. A receipt recorded internally but not yet shown by the bank.
4. Timing differences are valid transactions appearing at different times; errors require correction.
5. The net total of customer balances.
6. Missing invoices, credits, payments, timing, duplicate capture or supplier errors.
7. Record, assign and follow them up until resolved or appropriately escalated.
8. Reconciliation compares independent records and can reveal errors that still preserve debit-credit equality.

## Key Takeaways
- Update internal records before reconciling.
- Separate genuine timing differences from errors.
- Reconcile control accounts to subsidiary detail.
- Supplier statements provide external evidence for creditor review.
- Unresolved differences need ownership and follow-up.
