# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 10: Practical Bookkeeping Project
### Lesson 6: Process Period-end Adjustments

**Estimated study time:** 55–65 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Identify common period-end adjustments in the capstone.
2. Prepare supported adjustment journals.
3. Explain how accruals, prepayments, depreciation and bad-debt adjustments affect accounts.
4. Preserve approval and audit-trail requirements.
5. Prepare an adjusted trial balance after approved entries.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Before financial reports are prepared, some balances may need adjustment so that the period reflects the correct income, expenses, assets and liabilities. These entries should not be invented to improve results. They must be based on evidence, reasonable calculations and authorised accounting treatment.

## Key Terms
- **Period-end adjustment** — Entry made to update accounts before final reporting.
- **Accrual** — Expense or income recognised before cash settlement where applicable.
- **Prepayment** — Payment relating partly or wholly to a future period.
- **Depreciation** — Allocation of the depreciable amount of an asset over its useful life under the chosen accounting policy.
- **Bad debt** — Receivable written off when authorised and considered irrecoverable under policy.
- **Adjusted trial balance** — Trial balance after approved period-end adjustments are posted.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start from evidence
Adjustment entries should come from schedules, invoices, contracts, asset records, customer reviews or authorised accounting instructions.

### 2. Accrued expenses
If an expense relates to the current period but the invoice has not yet been recorded, an accrual may be required according to the accounting basis used.

Example: telephone service for the month is estimated from reliable evidence at R1,200 but the invoice will arrive next month. A supported accrual may debit Telephone Expense and credit Accrued Expenses.

### 3. Prepayments
If a payment covers future periods, part may need to remain as an asset rather than being fully expensed immediately.

Example: R12,000 insurance paid for 12 months with only three months relating to the current project period. Under the project's assumptions, the appropriate portion is expensed and the balance remains prepaid.

### 4. Depreciation
Use the asset register, approved method and project rate. Do not invent useful lives. Record the calculated depreciation according to the supplied policy.

### 5. Bad debts
A customer balance should not be written off merely because it is overdue. Follow the simulated authority and evidence requirements. Where an approved write-off is provided, record the appropriate bad-debt entry and update debtor records.

### 6. Corrections versus adjustments
A correction fixes an error; an adjustment updates the period for accounting purposes. Keep these concepts separate even if both use journal entries.

### 7. Journal support
Every adjustment should include:
- date;
- accounts affected;
- debit and credit amounts;
- calculation or schedule;
- narration;
- evidence reference;
- required approval.

### 8. Post and recheck
After posting adjustments, update ledgers and prepare an adjusted trial balance. Confirm total debits equal credits and review whether balances remain reasonable.

## Worked Example
The asset register shows equipment cost of R24,000. The project policy requires depreciation of R4,800 for the period under the supplied assumptions.

Adjustment:
- Dr Depreciation Expense R4,800
- Cr Accumulated Depreciation R4,800

The journal is supported by the asset schedule and approved calculation.

## Practical Activity
Prepare four supported adjustment journals for your simulated business:
1. one accrual;
2. one prepayment adjustment;
3. one depreciation entry;
4. one authorised debtor adjustment or correction.

Then prepare the adjusted trial balance and attach each journal's evidence reference.

4. A payment relating to future accounting periods.

## Key Takeaways
- Adjustments are controlled accounting entries, not tools for manipulating results.
- Evidence and approval are essential.
- Accruals, prepayments and depreciation affect period accuracy.
- Corrections and period-end adjustments are different concepts.
- Recheck the full trial balance after posting.
