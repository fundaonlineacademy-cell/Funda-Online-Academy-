# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 10: Practical Bookkeeping Project
### Lesson 7: Prepare Basic Financial Reports and Control Schedules

**Estimated study time:** 60–70 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Prepare basic financial reports from an adjusted trial balance.
2. Explain the relationship between profit or loss and financial position.
3. Prepare supporting control schedules.
4. Perform cross-document consistency checks.
5. Present basic bookkeeping results clearly to a manager.

## Why This Lesson Matters
The purpose of bookkeeping is not merely to collect transactions. Reliable records should produce information that managers and other authorised users can understand. This lesson converts the adjusted bookkeeping records into basic reports and schedules while checking that the figures remain consistent across the project.

## Key Terms
- **Statement of Profit or Loss** — Report summarising income and expenses for a period.
- **Statement of Financial Position** — Report showing assets, liabilities and equity at a point in time.
- **Control schedule** — Supporting schedule explaining a ledger balance.
- **Cross-check** — Comparison between related reports or records.
- **Material difference** — Difference significant enough to require investigation or explanation.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start with the adjusted trial balance
Use only the final reviewed trial balance after all approved adjustments. Do not mix figures from earlier drafts.

### 2. Prepare the profit-or-loss report
Group income and expenses logically.

For example:
Revenue R120,000  
Less Expenses R88,000  
Profit R32,000

The exact headings depend on the project chart of accounts.

### 3. Prepare the statement of financial position
Group:
- assets;
- liabilities;
- equity.

The accounting equation should remain consistent:
**Assets = Liabilities + Equity.**

Where the project requires closing profit to affect equity, ensure the reporting treatment is internally consistent.

### 4. Prepare control schedules
Useful schedules may include:
- bank reconciliation;
- debtors list and age analysis;
- creditors list;
- fixed-asset/depreciation schedule;
- accrual/prepayment schedule;
- VAT control schedule where used;
- payroll control schedule where applicable.

A report total should be traceable to the supporting schedule and ledger.

### 5. Cross-document consistency
Check that:
- Bank on the financial position agrees to the reconciled ledger balance;
- Debtors agrees to the debtor schedule/control account;
- Creditors agrees to the creditor schedule/control account;
- depreciation expense agrees to the asset schedule;
- reported income and expenses agree to the adjusted trial balance;
- report period and entity name are consistent.

### 6. Explain rather than decorate
A useful management note focuses on important results and exceptions. For example:
`Debtors increased by R18,000, mainly due to two large invoices issued in the final week. R6,500 is overdue and is being followed up.`

Avoid making unsupported predictions or hiding unfavourable information.

### 7. Review unusual balances
A negative cash balance, large suspense balance, unexpected credit debtor or sudden expense increase should be understood before final reporting.

### 8. Draft versus final
Mark draft reports clearly until review is complete. The final reporting pack should be approved or accepted through the simulated authority process.

## Worked Example
Adjusted trial-balance figures include:
- Service Revenue R95,000 Cr
- Stationery Sales R15,000 Cr
- Rent R24,000 Dr
- Wages R36,000 Dr
- Telephone/Data R6,000 Dr
- Consumables R7,000 Dr
- Bank Charges R1,000 Dr
- Depreciation R4,000 Dr

Total revenue = R110,000.  
Total listed expenses = R78,000.  
Profit = R32,000.

The learner should then verify that all figures agree to the adjusted trial balance and that the closing profit treatment is consistent with the equity section.

## Practical Activity
Prepare a final reporting pack containing:
1. adjusted trial balance;
2. statement of profit or loss;
3. statement of financial position;
4. bank reconciliation;
5. debtors schedule;
6. creditors schedule;
7. adjustment schedule;
8. one-page management summary explaining three significant results or exceptions.

## Knowledge Check
1. Which trial balance should be used for final reports?
2. What does the statement of profit or loss show?
3. What does the statement of financial position show?
4. What is a control schedule?
5. What should Bank on the report agree to?
6. Why are cross-document checks important?
7. Should unusual balances be ignored if the trial balance balances?
8. Why should draft reports be identified clearly?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. The final reviewed adjusted trial balance.
2. Income, expenses and resulting profit or loss for the period.
3. Assets, liabilities and equity at a reporting date.
4. Supporting detail explaining a ledger or report balance.
5. The reconciled bank ledger/cashbook balance.
6. They detect inconsistencies between reports, ledgers and schedules.
7. No. Balanced debits and credits do not prove classification or reasonableness.
8. To prevent unfinished information being mistaken for approved final reporting.

## Key Takeaways
- Financial reports should flow from the adjusted trial balance.
- Every major reported balance should be traceable to supporting records.
- Cross-document consistency is a core quality control.
- Explain significant results factually and clearly.
- Final reports should be reviewed before release.
