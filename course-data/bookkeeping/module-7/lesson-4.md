# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 7: Computerised Bookkeeping & QuickBooks Fundamentals
### Lesson 4: Capturing Sales, Purchases and Expenses

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Capture customer sales and supplier purchases through appropriate software workflows.
2. Distinguish invoice-based transactions from immediate cash/bank transactions.
3. Apply account, date, reference and tax-code checks.
4. Prevent duplicate capture and incorrect allocation.
5. Trace software transactions to underlying ledger effects.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Accounting software makes transaction capture fast, but the bookkeeper still chooses the customer, supplier, date, account and tax treatment. Good capture habits prevent errors from flowing automatically into reports.

## Key Terms
- **Sales invoice screen** — Software workflow recording a customer credit sale.
- **Supplier bill/purchase invoice** — Workflow recording a creditor obligation.
- **Expense transaction** — Direct purchase/payment recorded without a creditor invoice workflow where appropriate.
- **Tax code** — System setting controlling tax treatment of a line or transaction.
- **Reference** — Source-document identifier used for traceability.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Capture the transaction type correctly
A credit customer sale normally uses a sales invoice so Debtors and Sales update. A cash sale already paid may use a receipt/sales workflow appropriate to the software.

### 2. Supplier purchases
A supplier invoice on credit should be captured through the supplier/payables module where applicable. Paying it later should settle the payable, not record the expense again.

### 3. Direct expenses
A small expense paid immediately by card or bank may be recorded directly to the relevant expense account if that matches the organisation's workflow and supporting evidence.

### 4. Verify fields
Before saving, check:
- customer/supplier;
- date;
- invoice/reference number;
- description;
- account/product mapping;
- amount;
- tax code;
- payment terms;
- attachments/support.

### 5. Duplicate controls
Search invoice number and supplier/customer history where warnings appear. Do not alter a supplier invoice number simply to bypass duplicate detection.

### 6. Credits and corrections
Use the appropriate credit-note or correction workflow so customer/supplier balances remain linked to original transactions. Avoid deleting posted transactions merely to make reports cleaner where audit-trail controls require reversal/correction.

### 7. Underlying accounting
A customer credit invoice may create:
- Debit Debtors.
- Credit Sales and relevant tax control account if applicable.

A supplier invoice may create:
- Debit expense/asset and tax account if applicable.
- Credit Creditors.

### 8. Review reports
After batch capture, review transaction lists, sales/purchase reports and control balances. Software acceptance of a transaction is not proof that the accounting is correct.

## Worked Example
A bookkeeper captures a R6,000 supplier invoice for cleaning as a direct bank expense, even though it has not yet been paid. This wrongly reduces Bank immediately instead of creating Creditors. The source invoice and payment status reveal the mistake. The entry is corrected using the authorised software process and captured as a supplier bill.

## Practical Activity
For eight sample transactions, decide whether each belongs in customer invoice, supplier bill, direct expense, customer receipt, supplier payment or credit-note workflow. Write the expected debit and credit accounts for each.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. It creates and tracks the customer receivable correctly.
2. The original bill already recorded the expense/asset and payable.
3. Customer/supplier, date, reference, amount, account, tax code and support are examples.
4. Investigate before overriding.
5. They preserve correct customer/supplier balances and audit trail.
6. System setting defining transaction tax treatment.
7. To detect misclassification, duplicates or unusual entries.
8. Creditors / Accounts Payable.

## Key Takeaways
- Choose the software workflow that matches the real transaction.
- Verify source fields before saving.
- Do not bypass duplicate controls.
- Credits and corrections should remain traceable.
- Understand the double entry behind every software screen.
