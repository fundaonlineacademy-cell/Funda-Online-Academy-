# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 7: Computerised Bookkeeping & QuickBooks Fundamentals
### Lesson 7: Producing Reports and Exporting Evidence

**Estimated study time:** 45–50 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Produce common bookkeeping reports from accounting software.
2. Apply date, basis and filter checks before relying on reports.
3. Export reports and supporting evidence in controlled formats.
4. Distinguish live reports from static exported copies.
5. Protect confidential information during report distribution.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Computerised systems can produce reports instantly, but the wrong date range, account filter or accounting basis can make a report misleading. Bookkeepers need to verify report settings and control exported copies so users know what information they are looking at.

## Key Terms
- **Report parameter** — Setting such as date range, account, customer or basis controlling report output.
- **Trial balance report** — List of ledger balances at a selected date or period.
- **General ledger detail** — Transaction-level activity by account.
- **Age analysis** — Customer or supplier balances grouped by age.
- **Export** — Creating a file such as PDF or spreadsheet from system data.
- **Static copy** — Report snapshot that does not update automatically after export.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Common reports
Bookkeepers may use:
- trial balance;
- profit and loss;
- balance sheet / statement of financial position;
- general ledger detail;
- customer age analysis;
- supplier age analysis;
- bank reconciliation reports;
- transaction or audit-history reports.

### 2. Check the report date
A report at 31 August is different from one at 30 September. Confirm the requested period before interpreting results.

### 3. Check filters
A report can exclude accounts, customers or transactions if filters remain from prior work. Review filters before exporting or sending.

### 4. Compare to control schedules
A debtor age analysis should agree to the relevant debtor control balance after considering system design. Bank reports should agree to reconciliations.

### 5. Export formats
PDF is useful for a fixed reference copy; spreadsheets support further analysis but can be edited. Choose the format according to purpose and organisational controls.

### 6. Label exports clearly
Useful filenames can include report name, period and status, such as `Trial_Balance_2026-08-31_Final.pdf`. Avoid vague names such as `reportnew2.pdf`.

### 7. Live versus static reports
An exported report does not update when later transactions are posted. If books change after export, regenerate the report or clearly identify the version/status.

### 8. Confidentiality
Reports can contain salaries, customer balances, supplier information or bank details. Send only to authorised recipients and use approved storage/sharing methods.

### 9. Evidence packs
For review or audit support, create a controlled pack linking report totals to reconciliations and source schedules rather than sending random files with no index.

## Worked Example
A manager asks for the August debtor age analysis. The bookkeeper accidentally exports a year-to-date sales report with a customer filter from last week's task. Before sending, the bookkeeper checks the title, date and filters, runs the correct report, reconciles the total to Debtors Control and exports a clearly named PDF.

## Practical Activity
Prepare a report-distribution checklist covering requested report, date range, accounting basis, filters, reconciliation status, confidentiality, filename, recipient and version.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Trial balance, profit/loss, balance sheet, ledger, ageing and reconciliation reports are examples.
2. Different dates produce different balances and activity.
3. Old filters may unintentionally exclude information.
4. The relevant debtor-control records/schedules.
5. Live reports update with system data; static exports do not.
6. For controlled fixed reference output where editing is not required.
7. They can be changed independently of the accounting system.
8. To make evidence traceable and easy to review.

## Key Takeaways
- Instant reports still require parameter checks.
- Reconcile reports before relying on them.
- Exported reports are snapshots, not live data.
- Clear filenames and status labels prevent version confusion.
- Protect confidential financial reports during sharing.
