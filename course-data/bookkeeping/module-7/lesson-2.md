# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 7: Computerised Bookkeeping & QuickBooks Fundamentals
### Lesson 2: Setting Up a Company File and Chart of Accounts

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Identify core information required when creating an accounting company file.
2. Build or review a practical chart of accounts.
3. Distinguish account types and reporting impact.
4. Explain why opening dates, currencies and tax settings require care.
5. Apply setup controls before live transaction capture.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A computerised bookkeeping system is only as reliable as its setup. A poor chart of accounts, duplicate accounts or incorrect opening settings can distort reports across every later transaction.

## Key Terms
- **Company file** — Accounting-system environment containing the organisation's financial records and settings.
- **Chart of accounts** — Structured list of ledger accounts used for transaction classification.
- **Account type** — Classification such as asset, liability, equity, income or expense.
- **Opening date** — Date from which the system's starting balances and transaction history are established.
- **Opening balance** — Balance carried into the new system at implementation.
- **Mapping** — Linking transaction categories or integrations to ledger accounts.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Company identity
Setup normally includes business name, contact details, reporting year, currency and tax-related configuration appropriate to the organisation.

### 2. Chart of accounts design
The chart should be detailed enough for useful reporting but not so fragmented that similar transactions are spread across dozens of nearly identical accounts.

### 3. Account types matter
Choosing the wrong account type can affect where balances appear in reports. For example, a loan should not be created as Sales merely because money enters the bank.

### 4. Use meaningful names and codes
Names such as `Bank Charges`, `Office Rent` and `Trade Debtors` are more useful than vague accounts such as `Other 1`. Account codes can support logical grouping where the organisation uses them.

### 5. Avoid duplicates
Before adding a new account, search existing accounts. `Telephone`, `Phone Expense` and `Telecoms` may represent the same purpose and weaken reporting consistency.

### 6. Opening balances
When migrating from manual books or another system, opening balances should come from an approved reconciled trial balance and supporting schedules. Do not type approximate balances merely to start using the software.

### 7. Customer and supplier control accounts
Many systems manage Debtors and Creditors through built-in modules. Avoid creating manual duplicate control accounts unless the system design specifically requires them.

### 8. Tax configuration
Tax settings can affect every invoice and purchase. Use current authorised tax configuration and accountant/SARS guidance where applicable rather than guessing.

### 9. Setup review
Before live use, test sample sales, purchases, receipts and payments. Run the trial balance and reports to confirm accounts behave as expected.

## Worked Example
A new company file is created with `Loan Received` incorrectly set up as an income account. The first R100,000 loan deposit increases reported profit. A setup review identifies that the amount should be a liability. The account mapping is corrected before further transactions are processed.

## Practical Activity
Design a chart of accounts for a small South African office-services business with at least:
- 5 assets;
- 4 liabilities;
- 3 equity accounts/categories as appropriate;
- 5 income accounts;
- 12 expense accounts.

Explain why each account type was chosen and identify three accounts that should not be duplicated manually if the accounting software manages them automatically.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. The accounting-system environment containing company records/settings.
2. Account type affects transaction logic and report presentation.
3. Duplicates split similar activity and weaken reporting consistency.
4. An approved reconciled source such as the prior trial balance and schedules.
5. Linking transactions or categories to ledger accounts.
6. Incorrect settings can misstate many transactions.
7. To verify setup and report behaviour before real data volume increases.
8. Profit and income can be overstated while liabilities are understated.

## Key Takeaways
- Company-file setup creates the foundation for all later bookkeeping.
- The chart of accounts should be logical, stable and free of unnecessary duplicates.
- Opening balances must be reconciled and approved.
- Tax and integration mappings require careful review.
- Test before going live.
