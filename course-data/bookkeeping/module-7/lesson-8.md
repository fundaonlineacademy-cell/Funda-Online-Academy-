# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 7: Computerised Bookkeeping & QuickBooks Fundamentals
### Lesson 8: QuickBooks-style End-to-End Practice Workflow

**Estimated study time:** 60–70 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Process an end-to-end bookkeeping workflow in accounting software.
2. Move from master-data checks through transaction capture and banking.
3. Reconcile customer, supplier and bank records.
4. Correct a software error using a traceable method.
5. Produce controlled reports and evidence for review.

## Why This Lesson Matters
This practical combines the full computerised-bookkeeping cycle. The focus is not on memorising one software interface. It is on applying reliable bookkeeping logic through a QuickBooks-style accounting environment.

## Practical Scenario
A small office-services business begins the month with reconciled opening balances:
- Bank R35,000 Dr
- Debtors R12,000 Dr
- Equipment R20,000 Dr
- Creditors R9,000 Cr
- Loan R8,000 Cr
- Owner Equity balancing the opening trial balance.

During the month:
1. Customer A receives a credit invoice R8,000.
2. Customer A pays R10,000, partly settling the opening balance and new invoice.
3. Supplier X sends an invoice for cleaning materials R4,500.
4. Business pays Supplier X R6,000 against opening and new balances.
5. Office rent R5,000 is paid directly from Bank.
6. Bank feed imports a R180 bank charge.
7. Owner contributes R7,000.
8. The cleaning-material invoice was mistakenly posted to Equipment and must be corrected.

## Step 1: Check setup
Confirm customer and supplier masters, chart-of-accounts mapping, opening balances and reporting date.

## Step 2: Capture transactions
Use the appropriate customer, supplier and expense workflows rather than generic journals where normal modules are available.

Expected accounting effects include:
- Customer invoice: Dr Debtors / Cr Sales.
- Customer receipt: Dr Bank / Cr Debtors.
- Supplier bill: Dr Cleaning Materials / Cr Creditors.
- Supplier payment: Dr Creditors / Cr Bank.
- Rent: Dr Rent / Cr Bank.
- Bank charge: Dr Bank Charges / Cr Bank.
- Owner contribution: Dr Bank / Cr Equity/Capital.

## Step 3: Use the bank feed
Match the customer receipt, supplier payment, rent and owner contribution to existing transactions. Add the genuinely new R180 bank-charge item. Avoid duplicate capture.

## Step 4: Correct the misclassification
The supplier bill exists and is valid, but the expense/asset account is wrong. Follow the approved software correction method. The correction should move R4,500 from Equipment to Cleaning Materials without creating a second creditor liability.

## Step 5: Reconcile subledgers
Check Customer A's balance after the R10,000 receipt. Check Supplier X's balance after the invoice and payment. Confirm Debtors/Creditors control totals agree with subsidiary records.

## Step 6: Reconcile Bank
Use the statement ending balance and date, match cleared items and investigate any remaining difference. Do not force a reconciliation adjustment without evidence.

## Step 7: Produce reports
Generate:
- Trial Balance;
- Profit and Loss;
- Balance Sheet;
- Debtor Age Analysis;
- Creditor Age Analysis;
- Bank Reconciliation report;
- relevant audit/correction history.

## Step 8: Export evidence
Create clearly named PDF or spreadsheet evidence files according to purpose. Include report date, status and an index connecting source evidence to outputs.

## Practical Activity
Repeat the workflow using your own fictional company with at least 12 transactions, including one customer credit note, one supplier credit note, one bank-feed match, one bank-feed new item and one controlled correction.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should setup be checked before capture?
2. Which workflow should record a customer credit sale?
3. What should happen when a bank feed finds an existing supplier payment?
4. Why should the wrong account on a supplier bill be corrected without creating a second liability?
5. What must Debtors Control agree with?
6. Why should bank reconciliation not be forced?
7. Which reports should be produced at the end?
8. What should the evidence pack prove?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Setup errors affect every later transaction and report.
2. The customer sales-invoice workflow.
3. Match it to the existing payment.
4. The original bill already created the creditor obligation; only classification is wrong.
5. Total customer subsidiary balances.
6. Unexplained differences may reveal errors or missing transactions.
7. Trial balance, core financial reports, ageing, bank reconciliation and relevant audit history.
8. That transactions, corrections, reconciliations and reports are traceable and controlled.

## Key Takeaways
- Computerised bookkeeping should follow the same evidence and double-entry discipline as manual books.
- Use transaction modules appropriately.
- Match bank feeds to existing entries before adding new ones.
- Correct errors without duplicating transactions or losing audit history.
- Reconciliation and controlled reporting complete the workflow.
