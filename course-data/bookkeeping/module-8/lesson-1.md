# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 8: VAT, Tax Records & SARS Awareness
### Lesson 1: VAT Fundamentals for Bookkeepers

**Estimated study time:** 55–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain VAT at a practical bookkeeping level.
2. Distinguish output tax from potentially deductible input tax.
3. Identify standard-rated, zero-rated and exempt supplies at awareness level.
4. Recognise current South African VAT-registration thresholds.
5. Explain the bookkeeper's role boundaries in VAT work.

## Why This Lesson Matters
VAT affects invoices, purchases, reconciliations and tax returns for registered vendors. A bookkeeping error in tax coding can distort both accounting records and the VAT201 declaration. Bookkeepers therefore need strong operational awareness while recognising that complex VAT treatment may require an accountant, tax practitioner or SARS guidance.

## Current South African Regulatory Note
For this course build, SARS information was checked against current 2026 published guidance. The standard VAT rate is **15%**. From **1 April 2026**, compulsory VAT registration generally applies when taxable supplies exceed **R2.3 million per annum**, while voluntary registration can generally be considered where taxable supplies exceed **R120,000** under the applicable rules and exceptions. Rates and thresholds can change, so live work must always verify current SARS requirements.

## Key Terms
- **VAT** — Value-Added Tax charged on taxable supplies under South African VAT rules.
- **Vendor** — Person registered or required to register for VAT.
- **Output tax** — VAT charged on taxable supplies made by the vendor.
- **Input tax** — VAT incurred that may be deductible when the legal requirements are met.
- **Standard-rated supply** — Taxable supply subject to the standard VAT rate.
- **Zero-rated supply** — Taxable supply charged at 0% where the VAT Act permits.
- **Exempt supply** — Supply exempt from VAT; it is not the same as zero-rating.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. VAT is transaction-based
A VAT vendor charges output tax on taxable sales and may deduct qualifying input tax on business purchases. The difference can result in an amount payable to SARS or a refund, depending on the tax period.

### 2. Output tax
For a standard-rated sale of R10,000 excluding VAT at 15%:
- VAT = R1,500.
- Total invoice = R11,500.

The VAT component is not simply additional sales income; it is tracked through a VAT control account according to the accounting system.

### 3. Input tax
A VAT amount on a supplier document is not automatically deductible. The vendor must meet the applicable VAT requirements, including valid supporting documentation and the correct business/tax treatment.

### 4. Zero-rated is not exempt
Both may result in no VAT charged to the customer, but the legal consequences differ. Bookkeepers should use the correct tax code only when the treatment is confirmed.

### 5. Registration thresholds
Registration is based on taxable supplies and applicable rules, not simply bank deposits or profit. A business near a threshold should be referred to the authorised tax/compliance person for formal assessment.

### 6. VAT control accounts
Many systems maintain output VAT, input VAT and a VAT payable/receivable control. These balances should be reconciled to the VAT201 working papers before submission.

### 7. Role boundaries
A bookkeeper can capture authorised tax codes, maintain records, reconcile VAT accounts and prepare schedules. The bookkeeper should not invent VAT treatment for unusual transactions or provide personalised tax advice.

## Worked Example
A VAT-registered business makes standard-rated sales of R46,000 excluding VAT and has qualifying purchases of R23,000 excluding VAT, all assumed at 15% for this exercise.

Output VAT = R46,000 × 15% = R6,900.
Input VAT = R23,000 × 15% = R3,450.
Net before other adjustments = R3,450 payable.

This simplified calculation is only a learning illustration; actual VAT201 preparation depends on the full tax-period records and applicable rules.

## Key Takeaways
- VAT bookkeeping separates the business amount from the tax component.
- Output and deductible input VAT must be supported and reconciled.
- Standard-rated, zero-rated and exempt treatment are different.
- Current thresholds must be checked against SARS before live application.
- Complex VAT questions should be escalated rather than guessed.
