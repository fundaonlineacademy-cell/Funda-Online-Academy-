# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 8: VAT, Tax Records & SARS Awareness
### Lesson 4: VAT-inclusive and VAT-exclusive Calculations

**Estimated study time:** 55–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Calculate VAT on VAT-exclusive amounts at the current standard rate used in the course.
2. Extract VAT from VAT-inclusive amounts.
3. Convert accurately between exclusive, VAT and inclusive values.
4. Check invoice arithmetic and rounding.
5. Avoid applying the standard rate to transactions whose VAT treatment is not confirmed.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Bookkeepers repeatedly move between exclusive prices, VAT amounts and inclusive totals. A small formula error repeated across many transactions can materially distort VAT control accounts and reports.

## Current Rate Note
The standard South African VAT rate used in this course is **15%**, based on current SARS 2026 guidance. Always verify the current rate and transaction treatment before live application.

## Key Terms
- **VAT-exclusive amount** — Price before VAT is added.
- **VAT-inclusive amount** — Total price including VAT.
- **VAT fraction** — Fraction used to extract VAT from an inclusive amount.
- **Rounding** — Applying consistent rules to fractional cents.
- **Tax code** — System classification determining VAT treatment.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. From exclusive to VAT
For a standard-rated amount:

**VAT = Exclusive amount × 15%**

Example: R8,000 exclusive.
VAT = R8,000 × 15% = R1,200.
Inclusive total = R9,200.

### 2. From exclusive to inclusive
At 15%:

**Inclusive = Exclusive × 1.15**

R2,500 × 1.15 = R2,875.

### 3. Extracting VAT from inclusive amounts
Do not multiply an inclusive total by 15%. At a 15% rate, the VAT fraction is:

**15 / 115**

For R11,500 inclusive:
VAT = R11,500 × 15/115 = R1,500.
Exclusive = R10,000.

### 4. Why 15% of an inclusive amount is wrong
R11,500 × 15% = R1,725, which overstates the VAT. The inclusive total already contains the tax, so the VAT fraction must be used.

### 5. Rounding
Accounting systems may calculate VAT per line or on totals depending on configuration and applicable rules, which can produce small rounding differences. Follow the approved system and do not manually alter cents simply to force agreement without understanding the cause.

### 6. Discounts and VAT
Where a taxable selling price is reduced by an authorised discount, VAT generally follows the taxable consideration under the applicable rules. Use the software's approved discount/tax workflow.

### 7. Credit notes
A credit note reducing an original taxable sale should reduce the related sales and VAT amounts consistently.

### 8. Do not assume every transaction is standard-rated
A correct 15% calculation is still wrong if the transaction is zero-rated, exempt or outside the standard treatment. Confirm the tax category first.

## Worked Example
A supplier invoice total is R34,500 inclusive at the standard 15% rate.

VAT = R34,500 × 15/115 = R4,500.
Exclusive amount = R34,500 - R4,500 = **R30,000**.

Check: R30,000 × 15% = R4,500; total R34,500.

## Key Takeaways
- Exclusive × 15% gives VAT at the standard rate.
- Inclusive × 15/115 extracts VAT at a 15% rate.
- Always cross-check the three values.
- Rounding must be controlled consistently.
- Correct arithmetic cannot compensate for an incorrect tax category.
