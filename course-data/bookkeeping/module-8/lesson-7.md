# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 8: VAT, Tax Records & SARS Awareness
### Lesson 7: Income-tax and Provisional-tax Recordkeeping Awareness

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the bookkeeper's supporting role in income-tax records.
2. Describe provisional tax at an operational-awareness level.
3. Identify records used to support taxable-income estimates and tax submissions.
4. Distinguish bookkeeping records from final tax calculations and advice.
5. Escalate tax estimates and classifications requiring professional judgement.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Bookkeeping records are a major source of information used for income-tax and provisional-tax work. If income, expenses, assets and owner transactions are incomplete or incorrectly classified, tax calculations can also become unreliable. The bookkeeper's role is to maintain accurate records and schedules—not to invent tax treatment or promise a tax outcome.

## Current SARS Source Note
SARS describes provisional tax as a method of paying income tax in advance during the year rather than as a separate tax. Companies generally fall into the provisional-tax system, and IRP6 returns are submitted through eFiling according to the applicable rules. Current due dates, thresholds and exemptions must be checked against SARS for the taxpayer and tax year concerned.

## Key Terms
- **Income tax** — Tax determined under the applicable income-tax rules on taxable income.
- **Provisional tax** — Advance payment system for income tax based on estimated taxable income.
- **IRP6** — SARS provisional-tax return.
- **Taxable income** — Amount determined under tax law after applicable inclusions, deductions and adjustments.
- **Estimate** — Forward-looking amount used for provisional-tax purposes under the applicable rules.
- **Supporting schedule** — Bookkeeping record used to substantiate income, expenses or other tax-relevant information.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Book profit is not automatically taxable income
The accounting profit in the books is an important starting point, but tax law may treat some amounts differently. Final taxable-income calculations may require tax adjustments outside routine bookkeeping.

### 2. Bookkeeper support
Bookkeepers can provide:
- reconciled income and expense ledgers;
- fixed-asset schedules;
- debtor and creditor reports;
- bank reconciliations;
- owner/director loan records;
- VAT records;
- payroll summaries;
- prior-period comparisons.

### 3. Provisional tax
Provisional tax spreads expected income-tax payments across the year. It is based on an estimate under SARS rules rather than simply a fixed percentage of turnover or bank receipts.

### 4. Companies
Current SARS guidance states that companies automatically fall into the provisional-tax system. The tax practitioner, accountant or authorised taxpayer process should determine the actual estimate and submission obligations.

### 5. Estimate support
A reliable estimate may require current-year management accounts, year-to-date profit, forecasts and known tax adjustments. Bookkeepers should ensure source records are current and explain unusual movements.

### 6. Timing awareness
SARS generally provides for at least two provisional-tax payments during the year, with a possible third payment depending on circumstances. Because dates depend on the tax year and taxpayer, verify the live SARS calendar instead of memorising one universal date.

### 7. Tax records and audit trail
Keep the reports and schedules supplied for tax preparation identifiable by period and version. If books change after an estimate is prepared, record the updated report and communicate the change to the responsible tax person.

### 8. Role boundaries
Do not decide whether an expense is tax-deductible merely because it is correctly recorded as an accounting expense. Tax deductibility is a legal/tax question and may need specialist judgement.

## Worked Example
A company shows year-to-date accounting profit of R420,000. The bookkeeper notices that a R90,000 equipment purchase was incorrectly expensed rather than capitalised under the approved accounting policy. Before tax estimates are prepared, the accounting error is corrected and the revised management accounts are supplied. The final taxable-income adjustment remains the responsibility of the authorised tax process.

## Key Takeaways
- Accurate bookkeeping supports accurate tax work.
- Provisional tax is an advance-payment system for income tax, not a separate tax.
- Book profit and taxable income can differ.
- Bookkeepers prepare reliable evidence; authorised tax professionals make specialist tax decisions.
- Current SARS dates and rules should be verified for every live submission.
