# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 8: VAT, Tax Records & SARS Awareness
### Lesson 8: SARS eFiling, Compliance Records and Bookkeeper Role Boundaries

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain how eFiling fits into a controlled tax-compliance workflow.
2. Prepare bookkeeping records for authorised SARS submissions.
3. Maintain submission, payment and correspondence evidence.
4. Identify tax-compliance issues that require escalation.
5. Apply clear professional boundaries when supporting tax work.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Tax compliance is more than pressing Submit. Reliable submissions depend on accurate books, reconciled schedules, authorised review, proof of submission and follow-up on SARS correspondence. Bookkeepers may support these steps without acting outside their authority.

## Key Terms
- **SARS eFiling** — SARS electronic platform used for tax returns, declarations, payments and related services.
- **Submission evidence** — Proof that a return or declaration was successfully submitted.
- **Statement of account** — SARS record showing assessed/declared amounts, payments and balances for the relevant tax type.
- **Compliance correspondence** — SARS notice, request or communication requiring review/action.
- **Tax practitioner** — Person registered/authorised to provide tax services where applicable.
- **Role boundary** — Limit defining what the bookkeeper may prepare, approve or advise on.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Prepare before eFiling
A return should begin from completed bookkeeping controls:
- relevant period closed or controlled;
- bank and control accounts reconciled;
- tax schedules prepared;
- exceptions resolved or clearly escalated;
- authorised reviewer satisfied with the figures.

### 2. Use authorised access
The organisation should control who is permitted to submit returns, view tax information and make payments. Bookkeepers should use only authorised access arrangements and follow delegated roles.

### 3. Verify the tax period and return
Before submission, confirm taxpayer/entity, tax type, period and version of the working papers. Filing the right figures for the wrong period is still an error.

### 4. Keep submission evidence
After authorised submission, retain the return/declaration, confirmation/reference, supporting working papers and payment evidence where applicable.

### 5. Reconcile payments
A tax payment should be matched to the correct SARS tax type and period/reference. Payment from the bank does not by itself prove it was allocated correctly by SARS.

### 6. Monitor correspondence
SARS may issue notices, requests for information or statements showing balances. Route correspondence promptly to the responsible person and track deadlines.

### 7. Do not ignore discrepancies
If the accounting VAT payable differs from a SARS statement, investigate timing, payments, prior-period balances, assessments or other causes. Do not alter the books merely to make them match SARS without understanding the reason.

### 8. Bookkeeper boundaries
A bookkeeper can prepare evidence, reconcile records, capture authorised transactions and assist with submission administration. Complex interpretations, objections, voluntary disclosures, disputes and tax advice should be handled by appropriately authorised professionals.

### 9. Current information
Tax rates, thresholds, filing dates and SARS procedures can change. A professional workflow includes checking current SARS guidance instead of relying solely on old course notes.

## Worked Example
A VAT201 working paper shows R18,600 payable. The authorised return is submitted and the company pays R18,600. Later the SARS statement still shows an outstanding amount. The bookkeeper checks the payment reference, payment date and statement allocation, keeps the evidence and escalates the discrepancy rather than posting another tax expense into the books.

## Practical Activity
Design a tax-compliance handover checklist containing tax type, period, return status, amount, submission reference, payment status, SARS statement status, correspondence, unresolved exceptions, owner and deadline.

## Knowledge Check
1. What should happen before an authorised eFiling submission?
2. Why must tax type and period be checked?
3. What submission evidence should be retained?
4. Why should tax payments be reconciled to SARS records?
5. What should happen with SARS correspondence?
6. Should the books be changed automatically when a SARS balance differs?
7. Name two matters that should generally be escalated to an authorised tax specialist.
8. Why must current SARS guidance be checked regularly?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Complete and review the relevant bookkeeping reconciliations, schedules and exceptions.
2. A correct amount submitted to the wrong tax period/type creates a compliance problem.
3. Return/declaration, confirmation/reference, working papers and payment evidence where relevant.
4. Bank payment does not guarantee correct SARS allocation.
5. Route it promptly, record it and track any required response deadline.
6. No. Investigate the cause first.
7. Examples include complex tax interpretation, objections, disputes or voluntary-disclosure matters.
8. Rates, thresholds, procedures and deadlines can change.

## Key Takeaways
- eFiling is the final administrative step in a broader controlled tax workflow.
- Submission and payment evidence must be retained and reconciled.
- SARS correspondence requires ownership and follow-up.
- Bookkeepers should support compliance without exceeding professional authority.
- Current SARS guidance takes precedence over old memorised figures.
