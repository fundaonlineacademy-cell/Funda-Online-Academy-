# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 8: VAT, Tax Records & SARS Awareness
### Lesson 5: VAT Records, Reconciliations and Return Preparation Support

**Estimated study time:** 55–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the records needed to support VAT return preparation.
2. Reconcile VAT control accounts to transaction schedules.
3. Identify differences before a VAT201 is submitted.
4. Prepare a structured VAT working-paper pack for review.
5. Explain the bookkeeper's supporting role in VAT201 preparation.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A VAT201 should not be prepared by copying a single ledger balance without review. The tax-period return needs to be supported by sales, purchase, credit-note, adjustment and control-account information. Bookkeepers create value by assembling a complete, reconciled evidence pack before authorised submission.

## Current SARS Source Note
SARS states that a VAT vendor must submit a VAT201 for every tax period and retain relevant VAT records for **five years**. SARS also states that VAT due is based broadly on output tax less deductible input tax, subject to the VAT rules. Current SARS guidance should always be checked for due dates and any special circumstances.

## Key Terms
- **VAT201** — VAT declaration submitted for a tax period.
- **Tax period** — Reporting period assigned to the VAT vendor.
- **VAT reconciliation** — Comparison of VAT ledger/control balances to supporting transaction data.
- **Working paper** — Schedule showing calculations, sources, adjustments and review evidence.
- **Exception** — Item requiring correction, support or tax-treatment confirmation before completion.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Build the return from transaction evidence
A VAT working pack may include:
- sales summaries by tax code;
- purchase/input-tax schedules;
- credit and debit notes;
- VAT control-account detail;
- prior-period adjustments where applicable;
- documentary exceptions;
- reconciliation to the accounting system.

### 2. Reconcile output tax
Compare taxable sales reports to the output-VAT account. Investigate manual journals, unusual tax codes and differences between invoicing and ledger totals.

### 3. Reconcile input tax
Review purchase transactions and supporting tax invoices. Remove or separately control amounts whose deductibility or documentation has not been confirmed.

### 4. Credit notes and reversals
Ensure credits and reversals affect both the underlying transaction and VAT schedule consistently. A credit note missing from the VAT working paper can overstate output or input tax.

### 5. Control-account movement
Reconcile opening VAT balances, current-period postings, payments/refunds and closing balances. The return amount should fit the ledger story.

### 6. Exception register
Examples include:
- missing tax invoices;
- unknown VAT codes;
- duplicate invoices;
- manual VAT journals;
- large adjustments;
- unreconciled control balances;
- transactions near period cut-off.

### 7. Submission role boundaries
The bookkeeper may prepare the schedules, reconcile figures and provide evidence. Submission, tax treatment approval and declarations should follow the organisation's authorised process.

### 8. Record retention
Store returns, calculations, tax invoices and supporting records in the approved system for the required retention period. Do not create uncontrolled personal archives.

## Worked Example
Output VAT per ledger = R28,500. Sales-tax-code report = R27,900. Difference = R600.

Investigation identifies a manual journal that credited Output VAT R600 but has no approved support. The VAT201 working paper should not silently accept the ledger total. The R600 is recorded as an exception and referred for review before finalising the return.

## Practical Activity
Create a VAT201 preparation checklist containing at least 15 controls. Include sales, purchases, tax invoices, credit notes, VAT codes, manual journals, control-account reconciliation, exceptions, approval, submission evidence and retention.

3. Purchase records, valid supporting evidence and the applicable VAT rules.
4. They can change tax balances outside normal sales/purchase workflows.
5. Unsupported, unusual, duplicated or unreconciled VAT items.
6. The accounting records and return should form one traceable story.
7. Prepare/reconcile schedules and evidence within delegated authority.
8. Five years under the current SARS vendor-obligations guidance used for this lesson.

## Key Takeaways
- VAT return support begins with reconciled transaction records.
- Output and input VAT should be independently checked.
- Exceptions must remain visible until resolved.
- Submission should follow authorised review and declaration procedures.
- VAT records need controlled retention.
