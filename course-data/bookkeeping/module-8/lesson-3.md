# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 8: VAT, Tax Records & SARS Awareness
### Lesson 3: Input Tax, Output Tax and VAT Control Accounts

**Estimated study time:** 55–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Distinguish output tax from deductible input tax.
2. Explain the purpose of VAT control accounts.
3. Record simple VAT-inclusive sales and purchase transactions.
4. Reconcile VAT ledger balances to supporting schedules.
5. Identify transactions that require tax-treatment review before coding.

## Why This Lesson Matters
VAT amounts move through accounting records before they reach a VAT201 return. If VAT is posted to the wrong control account, omitted or claimed without sufficient support, the return can become unreliable even when the underlying purchase or sale amount is correct.

## Key Terms
- **Output VAT** — VAT arising on taxable supplies made by a vendor.
- **Input VAT** — VAT incurred that may be deductible if applicable requirements are met.
- **VAT control account** — Ledger account accumulating VAT amounts for reconciliation and return preparation.
- **VAT payable** — Net VAT liability after the applicable calculation for the tax period.
- **VAT refundable** — Net position where deductible input exceeds output tax, subject to SARS processing and applicable rules.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Sales transaction
A standard-rated sale of R11,500 including VAT at 15% can be separated into:
- Sales excluding VAT: R10,000.
- Output VAT: R1,500.
- Debtor/Bank total: R11,500.

A credit-sale entry may therefore debit Debtors R11,500 and credit Sales R10,000 plus Output VAT R1,500.

### 2. Purchase transaction
Assume an eligible business purchase is R5,750 including VAT at 15%, and valid documentation supports the treatment:
- Expense/asset excluding VAT: R5,000.
- Input VAT: R750.
- Creditor/Bank total: R5,750.

### 3. VAT is not automatically profit or expense
Output VAT collected is accounted for separately from sales revenue. Deductible input VAT is generally separated from the underlying expense or asset where the accounting system requires this.

### 4. Control-account design
Software may use separate Input VAT and Output VAT accounts or a combined VAT control structure. The bookkeeper must understand the organisation's chart of accounts and report logic before reconciling.

### 5. Net VAT concept
In a simplified example:
- Output VAT R18,000.
- Deductible Input VAT R11,500.
- Net VAT before other adjustments = R6,500 payable.

Actual VAT201 preparation may contain additional categories, adjustments and rules.

### 6. Reconciliation
A VAT reconciliation may compare:
- sales reports to output VAT;
- purchase reports to input VAT;
- credit/debit notes;
- VAT ledger/control balances;
- VAT201 working papers;
- prior-period carried items where applicable.

### 7. Incorrect tax codes
A purchase can be correctly posted to Office Expense but still have the wrong VAT code. Review exception reports and unusual tax-code combinations.

### 8. Unsupported input VAT
If documentation is defective or deductibility is uncertain, do not claim the input VAT merely because software suggests it. Record the exception and refer the treatment as required.

## Worked Example
Standard-rated sales excluding VAT total R80,000. Output VAT = R12,000. Qualifying purchases excluding VAT total R40,000. Input VAT = R6,000.

Simplified net VAT = R12,000 - R6,000 = **R6,000 payable** before other VAT201 adjustments.

If one R11,500 purchase lacks acceptable evidence, the R1,500 VAT component should not simply remain in the deductible-input schedule without review.

## Practical Activity
Prepare a VAT-control schedule for four sales and five purchases. Separate exclusive amount, VAT and inclusive total. Flag one purchase as documentary exception and explain how it should be controlled before return preparation.

## Key Takeaways
- VAT control accounts connect transaction capture to VAT201 preparation.
- Inclusive totals must be separated correctly.
- Tax coding requires both accounting and documentary checks.
- VAT balances should be reconciled before return preparation.
- Unsupported or unusual VAT items should remain visible as exceptions.
