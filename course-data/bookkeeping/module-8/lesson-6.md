# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 8: VAT, Tax Records & SARS Awareness
### Lesson 6: Common VAT Errors, Exceptions and Escalation

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Identify common VAT bookkeeping errors.
2. Distinguish capture errors from tax-treatment uncertainty.
3. Maintain a VAT exception register.
4. Correct supported bookkeeping errors without hiding the audit trail.
5. Escalate unusual VAT matters through the appropriate authorised channel.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Many VAT problems are not complex tax-law disputes. They begin with ordinary bookkeeping failures: a duplicate supplier invoice, wrong tax code, missing credit note or VAT claimed on poor evidence. A disciplined exception process prevents uncertainty from being buried inside a return.

## Key Terms
- **VAT exception** — Transaction or balance requiring clarification, correction or approval before final treatment.
- **Tax-code error** — Incorrect VAT category applied in the accounting system.
- **Duplicate claim** — VAT amount included more than once because a transaction was duplicated.
- **Cut-off error** — Transaction included in the wrong reporting period.
- **Escalation** — Referral to a person with appropriate tax/accounting authority.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Wrong tax code
A transaction may be correctly posted to an expense but incorrectly coded for VAT. Review tax-code reports and unusual combinations before return preparation.

### 2. Duplicate invoices
A duplicated supplier invoice can duplicate both the expense and input-VAT amount. Duplicate-number warnings and supplier reconciliations help detect this.

### 3. Missing credit notes
If a supplier or customer credit is omitted, VAT balances can remain overstated. Credits should be linked to the original transaction and reflected in the appropriate tax period under the authorised process.

### 4. Defective supporting evidence
Do not repair a third-party tax invoice yourself. Request corrected evidence or refer the item. Keep the VAT component separately controlled until treatment is resolved.

### 5. Private or mixed-purpose expenditure
A payment from the business bank account does not automatically create deductible input tax. When business/private use or deductibility is unclear, refer the transaction rather than selecting a convenient tax code.

### 6. Registration-status errors
VAT should not be charged merely because an invoice template contains a VAT field. Confirm vendor registration and authorised system configuration.

### 7. Manual VAT journals
Manual entries directly affecting VAT control accounts deserve additional review because they bypass normal invoice tax coding.

### 8. VAT exception register
A practical register can include transaction reference, date, supplier/customer, amount, VAT amount, issue, evidence missing, owner, deadline, status and final resolution.

### 9. Escalation triggers
Escalate where:
- legal treatment is uncertain;
- material amounts are involved;
- unusual zero-rated/exempt treatment appears;
- documentary evidence cannot be corrected;
- manual VAT adjustments lack support;
- a possible compliance breach is identified.

### 10. Correcting errors
Use the accounting system's authorised correction method. Preserve the original transaction and adjustment history where required. Never hide an error by changing unrelated balances.

## Worked Example
A supplier invoice for R23,000 inclusive was captured twice, each time with R3,000 input VAT. Supplier reconciliation identifies the duplicate before the VAT201 is submitted. The duplicate transaction is reversed or corrected through the approved workflow, removing the second expense, creditor and VAT amount while preserving traceability.

## Practical Activity
Create a VAT exception register for:
1. duplicate supplier invoice;
2. missing VAT registration number;
3. zero-rated code with no supporting reason;
4. private-looking purchase through company card;
5. manual VAT journal of R18,000;
6. customer credit note not yet captured.

Assign each a next action and escalation owner.

## Key Takeaways
- VAT errors often originate in routine capture and document control.
- Tax-treatment uncertainty should be escalated rather than guessed.
- VAT exceptions need owners and deadlines.
- Manual VAT entries require strong evidence and review.
- Corrections should improve accuracy without destroying history.
