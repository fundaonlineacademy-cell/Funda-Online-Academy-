# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 8: VAT, Tax Records & SARS Awareness
### Lesson 2: Tax Invoices and VAT Source-document Requirements

**Estimated study time:** 55–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain why valid tax invoices matter for VAT records.
2. Distinguish full and abridged tax-invoice requirements at a practical level.
3. Check key supplier and recipient details on VAT documents.
4. Identify common invoice defects requiring correction or escalation.
5. Maintain a traceable VAT source-document file.

## Why This Lesson Matters
VAT deductions and VAT201 preparation depend heavily on source documents. A supplier invoice showing a VAT amount is not automatically sufficient. Bookkeepers need to recognise the document requirements and refer defects before claiming or coding VAT incorrectly.

## Current SARS Source Note
The SARS VAT 404 Guide for Vendors states that a **full tax invoice** applies where consideration is **R5,000 or more**, while an **abridged tax invoice** may apply where consideration is **less than R5,000**, subject to the VAT rules and exceptions. Because SARS publications can be updated, live work must use the current guide and organisational tax procedure.

## Key Terms
- **Tax invoice** — Document meeting prescribed VAT requirements for a taxable supply.
- **Consideration** — Amount payable for the supply, including VAT where applicable.
- **Full tax invoice** — Tax invoice containing the fuller set of prescribed details.
- **Abridged tax invoice** — Simplified tax invoice permitted for lower-value supplies under the applicable rule.
- **VAT registration number** — Number identifying a VAT-registered vendor.
- **Debit/Credit note** — VAT document adjusting a prior taxable transaction.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Why document quality matters
The bookkeeping system may accept any PDF attachment, but VAT treatment depends on the underlying transaction and legal documentation. The bookkeeper should check documents before relying on them for input-tax support.

### 2. Full tax-invoice fields
At awareness level, a full tax invoice should include the required invoice wording, supplier name/address/VAT registration number, recipient information where prescribed, serial number and issue date, description and quantity/volume where applicable, and the price/VAT information in an approved form.

### 3. Abridged invoices
Abridged invoices have reduced information requirements compared with full invoices. Do not remove fields merely because an invoice is under the threshold if the organisation's system already captures them.

### 4. Supplier identity
Check that the VAT number and supplier details relate to the actual supplier. Do not alter a supplier invoice yourself to “correct” the vendor's VAT number.

### 5. Recipient details
Where the full-invoice requirements apply, recipient information can be important. If the document is materially defective, request a corrected invoice or refer it according to procedure.

### 6. Serial numbers and dates
Unique invoice references support duplicate detection and the audit trail. The issue date also supports period/cut-off review.

### 7. Credit and debit notes
An authorised credit note should link back to the original supply where required and adjust the accounting and VAT treatment consistently.

### 8. Electronic documents
Electronic invoices still need the required information. Store them in approved systems with access and retention controls.

### 9. Bookkeeper role boundary
The bookkeeper can run a tax-invoice checklist, maintain exception logs and request corrected documents. Complex questions about alternative documentary evidence or special VAT rules should be escalated.

## Worked Example
A supplier sends an invoice for R8,625 including VAT. The document shows the supplier name, invoice number and amount but no VAT registration number. The bookkeeper should not type a VAT number onto the PDF or assume the input tax is safe to claim. The invoice is placed on the VAT-document exception list and a corrected document is requested through the approved process.

## Practical Activity
Create a VAT source-document checklist with columns for supplier, VAT number, invoice number, date, recipient details, description, amount, VAT presentation, approval status and exception action. Review five fictional invoices and identify which should be referred.

## Key Takeaways
- VAT source documents require more than a visible VAT amount.
- Use a consistent invoice checklist.
- Full and abridged invoice requirements differ.
- Never manufacture or alter third-party tax evidence.
- Keep documentary exceptions visible until resolved.
