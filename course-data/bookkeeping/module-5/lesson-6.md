# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 5: Debtors, Creditors, Invoicing & Credit Control
### Lesson 6: Creditors Reconciliation and Payment Preparation

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Reconcile Creditors Control to supplier balances.
2. Reconcile individual supplier accounts to supplier statements.
3. Prepare a controlled supplier-payment schedule.
4. Identify credits, duplicates and disputed invoices before payment.
5. Apply approval and bank-detail verification controls.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Supplier payment preparation is a high-risk bookkeeping activity because errors turn into real cash movements. A strong process reconciles what is owed, checks supporting evidence, confirms credits and obtains the right approval before money leaves the bank.

## Key Terms
- **Creditors reconciliation** — Comparison of Creditors Control with individual supplier balances.
- **Payment run** — Group of supplier payments prepared for approval/release.
- **Due date** — Date payment is contractually or operationally expected.
- **Payment schedule** — List of proposed payments with amounts, references and approval information.
- **Supplier statement reconciliation** — Comparison of the business supplier ledger to the supplier's statement.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Control-account reconciliation
The total of individual creditor balances should agree with Creditors Control. Differences may come from manual journals, unposted batches, opening balances or integration problems.

### 2. Supplier-statement reconciliation
For each important supplier, compare invoices, credit notes and payments. Identify items appearing only on one side and investigate.

### 3. Missing invoices and credits
If a supplier statement includes an invoice not in the books, obtain and verify the underlying invoice before capture. If the supplier issued a credit note, ensure it is reflected before payment.

### 4. Payment preparation
A payment schedule can include supplier, invoice references, gross amount due, credits, amount proposed, due date, banking reference, approval status and exceptions.

### 5. Do not pay the statement total blindly
The statement may include disputed invoices, unrecorded credits, payments in transit or errors. Payment should be based on reconciled, approved obligations.

### 6. Bank-detail verification
Changed bank details are a fraud risk. Follow the organisation's independent verification process before updating supplier master data or releasing payment.

### 7. Segregation of duties
Where practical, supplier setup, invoice capture, payment preparation, payment approval/release and bank reconciliation should not all be controlled by one person.

### 8. Payment evidence
Retain approval, remittance and bank evidence according to the organisation's records process. This allows later tracing from invoice to payment.

## Worked Example
Supplier ledger balance: R24,000 Cr.
Supplier statement balance: R28,500 Cr.
Difference: R4,500.

Investigation finds:
- invoice R6,000 on statement, not yet in books;
- credit note R1,500 on statement, not yet in books.

Net difference = R4,500. After verifying both documents, the supplier ledger can be updated. Only then should the payment schedule be finalised.

## Practical Activity
Build a payment schedule for four suppliers. Include one disputed invoice, one supplier credit note and one changed-bank-detail request. Mark which payments can proceed and which require investigation or approval.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Creditors Control with total individual supplier balances.
2. To identify missing invoices, credits, payments or other differences.
3. Statements can contain disputed or unmatched items.
4. Supplier, invoices, amount, due date, credits, reference and approval status.
5. Verify and allocate it so the amount owed is reduced correctly.
6. Fraudsters can redirect valid payments to false accounts.
7. It reduces the risk from one person controlling incompatible stages.
8. Approval, payment/remittance and banking evidence according to procedure.

## Key Takeaways
- Supplier payments should start from reconciled records.
- Credits and disputes must be resolved before payment where appropriate.
- Bank-detail changes require independent verification.
- Payment schedules make review and approval easier.
- The audit trail should link supplier invoice to settlement.
