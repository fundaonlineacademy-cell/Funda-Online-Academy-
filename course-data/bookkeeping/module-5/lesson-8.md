# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 5: Debtors, Creditors, Invoicing & Credit Control
### Lesson 8: Debtors-and-Creditors Practical

**Estimated study time:** 60–70 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Process a small debtor and creditor transaction set.
2. Calculate individual and control-account balances.
3. Prepare a debtor age analysis and supplier payment schedule.
4. Identify allocation, statement and reconciliation exceptions.
5. Apply collection and payment controls to a complete workflow.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Debtors and creditors are linked to cash flow from opposite directions. This practical requires you to control both: collect what customers owe and pay valid supplier obligations without losing traceability or authority.

## Practical Scenario
Opening balances:
- Debtors Control R30,000 Dr
- Customer A R18,000 Dr
- Customer B R12,000 Dr
- Creditors Control R22,000 Cr
- Supplier X R14,000 Cr
- Supplier Y R8,000 Cr

Transactions:
1. Credit sale to Customer A R9,000.
2. Customer A pays R15,000.
3. Customer B receives an approved credit note R2,000.
4. Supplier X sends a new invoice R6,000.
5. Supplier Y issues a credit note R1,000.
6. Business pays Supplier X R10,000.

## Step 1: Update debtors
Customer A:
R18,000 + R9,000 - R15,000 = **R12,000 Dr**.

Customer B:
R12,000 - R2,000 = **R10,000 Dr**.

Debtors Control closing balance = R22,000 Dr.

## Step 2: Update creditors
Supplier X:
R14,000 + R6,000 - R10,000 = **R10,000 Cr**.

Supplier Y:
R8,000 - R1,000 = **R7,000 Cr**.

Creditors Control closing balance = R17,000 Cr.

## Step 3: Reconcile control accounts
Individual debtors total:
R12,000 + R10,000 = R22,000 — agrees to Debtors Control.

Individual creditors total:
R10,000 + R7,000 = R17,000 — agrees to Creditors Control.

## Step 4: Add an allocation error
Assume Customer A's R15,000 payment was accidentally posted to Customer B. Debtors Control would still reduce by R15,000, but individual balances would be wrong.

This demonstrates why control-account agreement alone is not enough. Customer allocations need independent review and statements.

## Step 5: Add a supplier-statement exception
Supplier X's statement shows R12,500 owing while the ledger shows R10,000. The difference is a R2,500 invoice not found in the books.

The bookkeeper should obtain and verify the invoice before changing the ledger or paying it.

## Step 6: Prepare collection and payment actions
Customer A R12,000 and Customer B R10,000 should be aged according to invoice/due-date data. Any overdue portion enters credit-control follow-up.

Supplier X and Supplier Y should be included in a payment schedule only for reconciled, approved amounts due, considering credits and disputes.

## Practical Activity
Create your own end-to-end schedule with:
- three customers;
- three suppliers;
- two credit sales;
- two customer receipts;
- one customer credit note;
- two supplier invoices;
- one supplier credit note;
- two supplier payments;
- one allocation error;
- one statement difference.

Prepare control balances, subsidiary balances, an age analysis, a supplier reconciliation and a payment/collection action list.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should subsidiary balances agree with control accounts?
2. Can control accounts agree while individual allocations are wrong?
3. What should happen when a supplier statement contains an invoice missing from the books?
4. Why should customer balances be aged?
5. Why should supplier credits be processed before payment?
6. What should happen with a disputed customer balance?
7. What controls should precede supplier payment?
8. What does a complete debtor-creditor workflow achieve?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. They provide the detail behind the general-ledger summary.
2. Yes. A receipt can be allocated to the wrong customer while the control total remains correct.
3. Obtain and verify the source document before capture or payment.
4. To identify overdue amounts and prioritise follow-up.
5. Credits reduce the valid amount owed.
6. Record, route and resolve it through the appropriate dispute process.
7. Reconciliation, evidence, approval, duplicate checks and bank-detail verification.
8. Accurate receivables/payables, controlled cash flow and traceable follow-through.

## Key Takeaways
- Control accounts and individual balances must both be accurate.
- Customer receipts need correct allocation.
- Supplier statements need evidence-based reconciliation.
- Collection and payment decisions depend on reliable records.
- A complete workflow links invoice, settlement, reconciliation and follow-up.
