# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 5: Debtors, Creditors, Invoicing & Credit Control
### Lesson 3: Customer Receipts, Allocations and Statements

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Record customer receipts against debtor balances.
2. Allocate receipts accurately to customer accounts and invoices.
3. Handle unidentified and partial payments appropriately.
4. Explain the purpose of customer statements.
5. Reconcile customer balances and resolve allocation differences.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Receiving money is not the end of debtor control. A payment must be matched to the correct customer and, where required, the correct invoice. Poor allocation can make a paid customer appear overdue while another account appears to have a credit balance.

## Key Terms
- **Receipt allocation** — Matching money received to the correct customer and invoice(s).
- **Remittance advice** — Information supplied by a customer explaining which invoices a payment covers.
- **Part-payment** — Payment covering only part of an outstanding amount.
- **Unallocated receipt** — Receipt whose customer or invoice has not yet been confirmed.
- **Customer statement** — Summary of transactions and balance on a customer's account.
- **Credit balance** — Balance indicating the customer may have overpaid or received credits exceeding charges.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Customer receipt entry
If Customer B pays R7,000 toward existing invoices:
- Debit Bank R7,000.
- Credit Debtors R7,000.

The subsidiary customer account should also be updated.

### 2. Use remittance information
A customer may pay several invoices in one EFT. Use invoice references, remittance advice and customer account details to allocate accurately.

### 3. Partial payments
If an invoice is R10,000 and the customer pays R6,000, the remaining R4,000 stays outstanding. Do not close the invoice unless the system and authorised process support a valid adjustment.

### 4. Unidentified deposits
An unexplained bank deposit should not be assigned to the largest debtor or treated as sales automatically. Investigate payer references and contact information, then place it in an approved unallocated process if necessary.

### 5. Overpayments
If a customer pays more than the balance due, investigate before refunding. The excess may relate to another invoice, advance payment or duplicate payment. Refunds should follow approval controls.

### 6. Customer statements
Statements show opening balance, invoices, receipts, credits and closing balance over a period. They support collection and reconciliation but do not replace original invoices or credit notes.

### 7. Allocation errors
If a R5,000 receipt belonging to Customer A is posted to Customer B, the Debtors Control total may remain correct while individual balances become wrong. Correct the subsidiary allocation through the authorised process.

### 8. Reconciliation
Compare customer-account balances to source documents, receipts and statements. Investigate disputed invoices, unapplied credits and missing receipts.

## Worked Example
Customer account opening balance: R15,000.
New invoice: R8,000.
Receipt: R10,000.
Credit note: R1,500.

Closing balance = R15,000 + R8,000 - R10,000 - R1,500 = **R11,500 Dr**.

## Practical Activity
Allocate a R12,000 customer EFT against three invoices: R5,000, R4,500 and R6,000, where remittance states the first two invoices are paid in full and the remainder should be applied to the third. Calculate the remaining invoice balance.

2. Customer information explaining how a payment should be applied.
3. The unpaid balance remains outstanding.
4. Investigate and use an approved unallocated-receipts process if necessary.
5. The excess may have another valid explanation and refunds require control.
6. Opening balance, invoices, receipts, credits and closing balance.

## Key Takeaways
- Customer receipts need accurate allocation, not just bank capture.
- Partial payments leave genuine outstanding balances.
- Unknown deposits must be investigated.
- Customer statements support control but do not replace source documents.
- Allocation errors can hide inside a correct control-account total.
