# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 5: Debtors, Creditors, Invoicing & Credit Control
### Lesson 5: Debtors Reconciliation and Age Analysis

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Reconcile Debtors Control to individual customer balances.
2. Explain the purpose of an age analysis.
3. Identify overdue and disputed customer balances.
4. Distinguish allocation errors from genuine unpaid debt.
5. Prepare a concise debtor-exception report.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A Debtors Control balance tells the business how much customers owe in total. An age analysis shows how long those balances have been outstanding. Together, these controls support collection, cash-flow planning and error detection.

## Key Terms
- **Debtors reconciliation** — Comparison of Debtors Control with individual customer balances and supporting records.
- **Age analysis** — Report grouping receivables according to how long they have been outstanding.
- **Current** — Amount not yet overdue according to agreed terms/report logic.
- **Overdue** — Amount beyond its payment due date.
- **Disputed balance** — Amount a customer challenges or queries.
- **Unallocated receipt** — Customer payment not yet matched correctly.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Reconcile the control account
Add all individual customer balances and compare the total to Debtors Control. Differences may arise from posting only to one side, incorrect opening balances, manual journals or system integration issues.

### 2. Reconcile individual accounts
Check invoices, receipts, credit notes and adjustments. A customer can appear overdue simply because a receipt was allocated to the wrong invoice or account.

### 3. Age analysis
A typical report may show current, 30-day, 60-day, 90-day and older balances. Exact ageing buckets depend on the system and business terms.

### 4. Age from the correct date
Ageing may be based on invoice date or due date depending on the system. Bookkeepers should understand the configured logic before interpreting reports.

### 5. Investigate old balances
An old balance can represent:
- unpaid debt;
- disputed invoice;
- missing credit note;
- unallocated receipt;
- duplicate invoice;
- historical data error.

### 6. Credit balances
A customer credit balance may arise from overpayment, advance payment, unallocated credit note or incorrect allocation. Investigate rather than automatically refund.

### 7. Exception reporting
Useful debtor exceptions include:
- balances beyond agreed terms;
- customers over credit limits;
- large disputed amounts;
- unallocated receipts;
- unexplained credit balances;
- accounts with no recent follow-up.

### 8. Reconciliation ownership
Bookkeepers maintain accurate records, but credit-control decisions such as extending limits, legal collection or writing off debt require the relevant authority.

## Worked Example
Debtors Control = R86,000.
Individual customer balances total R82,500.
Difference = R3,500.

Investigation identifies a R3,500 manual journal posted to Debtors Control but not allocated to a customer. The journal should be reviewed and corrected or allocated through the authorised process. The bookkeeper should not alter random customer balances to force agreement.

## Practical Activity
Prepare an age analysis for:
- Customer A: R8,000 current
- Customer B: R12,000 30 days
- Customer C: R6,500 60 days
- Customer D: R9,000 90+ days
- Customer E: R2,000 credit balance

Identify three accounts requiring investigation or follow-up and explain why.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Debtors Control with total individual customer balances and supporting records.
2. A report grouping outstanding receivables by age.
3. Invoice-date and due-date methods can produce different interpretations.
4. Examples: missing credits, wrong allocations, duplicate invoices or unallocated receipts.
5. Investigate its cause before refund or reallocation.
6. One-sided postings, manual journals, opening-balance errors or integration issues.
7. Material overdue, disputed, unallocated or unusual balances.
8. The authorised manager/credit-control or finance role.

## Key Takeaways
- Control totals and individual customer records must agree.
- Ageing supports collection but must be interpreted correctly.
- Old balances are not automatically bad debts.
- Credit balances and unallocated receipts need investigation.
- Exception reporting should direct attention to the highest-risk items.
