# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 5: Debtors, Creditors, Invoicing & Credit Control
### Lesson 4: Supplier Invoices, Payments and Supplier Statements

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Record supplier invoices and payments correctly.
2. Match supplier documents to the correct creditor account.
3. Explain the purpose of supplier statements.
4. Identify missing invoices, duplicate payments and supplier credits.
5. Maintain a clear supplier audit trail.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Creditor records affect cash flow, supplier relationships and expense accuracy. If invoices are duplicated, omitted or paid against the wrong supplier, the business can overpay or miss valid obligations.

## Key Terms
- **Supplier invoice** — Document requesting payment for goods or services supplied.
- **Creditor account** — Record of amounts owed to a supplier.
- **Supplier statement** — Supplier's summary of invoices, credits, payments and balance.
- **Remittance advice** — Information sent with payment explaining which items are being settled.
- **Supplier credit note** — Document reducing an amount previously billed by the supplier.
- **Duplicate payment** — Payment of the same obligation more than once.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Supplier invoice capture
Ignoring VAT for a simple example, a R9,000 supplier invoice for repairs creates:
- Debit Repairs Expense R9,000.
- Credit Creditors R9,000.

### 2. Supplier payment
When R6,000 is later paid:
- Debit Creditors R6,000.
- Credit Bank R6,000.

The expense is not recorded again.

### 3. Supplier account allocation
Invoices and payments must be posted to the correct supplier. Similar supplier names or duplicate master records can cause errors.

### 4. Supplier statements
A supplier statement helps identify invoices, credits or payments that one party has but the other does not. It is a reconciliation tool, not a replacement for original source documents.

### 5. Missing invoices
If the statement shows an invoice not in the accounting system, request and verify the source document before capture. Do not post from the statement alone if policy requires an invoice or equivalent evidence.

### 6. Supplier credits
Credit notes reduce amounts owed. They should be allocated to the correct original invoice or account and reflected before payment preparation.

### 7. Duplicate-payment prevention
Before payment, check invoice number, supplier, amount, payment status and prior bank references. System warnings should be investigated rather than overridden casually.

### 8. Payment remittance
After payment, remittance information helps the supplier allocate the payment correctly. Clear remittance reduces disputes and statement differences.

## Worked Example
Supplier opening balance: R12,000 Cr.
New invoice: R5,000 Cr.
Credit note: R1,200 Dr.
Payment: R8,000 Dr.

Closing supplier balance = R12,000 + R5,000 - R1,200 - R8,000 = **R7,800 Cr**.

## Practical Activity
Reconcile a supplier account where the supplier statement is R18,400 but the ledger shows R15,900. The statement includes an invoice R2,500 not found in the books. Explain the evidence you would obtain and the steps before changing the ledger.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Debit the relevant expense/asset; Credit Creditors.
2. Debit Creditors; Credit Bank.
3. To compare supplier records with the business creditor account.
4. Statements may show amounts without sufficient underlying transaction evidence.
5. It reduces the supplier obligation.
6. Invoice number, amount, supplier, prior payment status and bank reference.
7. Information explaining which invoices a payment settles.

## Key Takeaways
- Supplier invoices create liabilities; supplier payments reduce them.
- Statements support reconciliation but do not replace source evidence.
- Credits must be applied before payment decisions.
- Duplicate-payment prevention is a key creditor control.
- Clear supplier references and remittance strengthen the audit trail.
