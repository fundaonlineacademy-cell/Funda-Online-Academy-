# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 9: Payroll Administration & Payroll Records
### Lesson 3: Deductions, Net Pay and Payroll Control

**Estimated study time:** 55–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Distinguish statutory, authorised voluntary and other payroll deductions.
2. Calculate net pay from gross pay and approved deductions.
3. Explain why deduction codes and limits require accurate setup.
4. Reconcile payroll deductions to control accounts and payment schedules.
5. Identify deduction exceptions requiring escalation.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Employees experience payroll through the amount deposited into their bank accounts, but net pay is the result of multiple controlled calculations. Incorrect deductions can underpay employees, create employer liabilities or cause statutory reporting errors.

## Key Terms
- **Deduction** — Amount withheld from gross remuneration according to law, agreement or authorised payroll instruction.
- **Net pay** — Amount payable to the employee after applicable deductions.
- **Statutory deduction** — Deduction required under applicable law, such as Employees' Tax or employee UIF where applicable.
- **Voluntary deduction** — Authorised deduction such as certain benefits or contributions under workplace arrangements.
- **Payroll control account** — Ledger account accumulating payroll liabilities until settlement.
- **Garnishee/other legal deduction** — Deduction processed under valid legal or authorised instruction where applicable.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Net pay formula
At a basic level:

**Gross pay - employee deductions = Net pay**

Employer contributions may affect payroll expense/liabilities but are not simply deducted from the employee's net pay unless the applicable rules say so.

### 2. Deduction categories
Payroll can include Employees' Tax (PAYE), UIF employee contribution, retirement or medical-related deductions, union or benefit deductions, loan repayments and other authorised items. The exact tax treatment varies.

### 3. Use current configured rules
PAYE is not a flat percentage. Payroll systems use current SARS deduction tables/rules and employee information. Do not calculate PAYE by applying one percentage to everyone.

### 4. Deduction authority
Voluntary deductions should have appropriate employee or contractual authorisation. Legal deductions require valid instructions. Payroll should not add deductions because a colleague asks informally.

### 5. Net-pay calculation example
Gross pay: R20,000.
Assume configured payroll produces:
- PAYE R2,100
- UIF employee deduction R177.12
- authorised benefit deduction R500
Net pay = R20,000 - R2,100 - R177.12 - R500 = **R17,222.88**.

The PAYE amount above is illustrative only; live payroll must use current SARS tables/software.

### 6. Payroll control accounts
A payroll posting may create liabilities for PAYE, UIF, benefits and net salaries. These liabilities are cleared when payments are made to employees, SARS or other parties.

### 7. Exception checks
Review:
- negative net pay;
- unusually large deductions;
- duplicated deductions;
- employee deduction without authority;
- new deduction codes;
- statutory amounts inconsistent with configured rules.

### 8. Reconciliation
Total deductions per payroll register should agree to relevant payroll control accounts and payment declarations/schedules.

## Worked Example
Payroll register shows total PAYE R42,000 and employee UIF R6,500. The general ledger shows PAYE payable R42,000 but UIF payable R7,100. The R600 difference should be investigated before statutory payment; payroll should not simply adjust one total to match the other without evidence.

## Key Takeaways
- Net pay is produced from controlled gross earnings and deductions.
- Statutory calculations must use current payroll/SARS rules.
- Every deduction needs a valid basis.
- Payroll liabilities remain in control accounts until paid.
- Reconciliation catches errors before payments and declarations.
