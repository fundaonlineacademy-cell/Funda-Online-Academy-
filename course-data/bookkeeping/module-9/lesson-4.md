# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 9: Payroll Administration & Payroll Records
### Lesson 4: PAYE, UIF and Statutory-deduction Awareness

**Estimated study time:** 60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain PAYE, UIF and SDL at a practical payroll-support level.
2. Recognise current UIF contribution percentages and the current SARS-published contribution ceiling.
3. Explain the purpose of the EMP201 declaration.
4. Distinguish employee deductions from employer contributions.
5. Use current SARS guidance rather than outdated payroll rates.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Payroll contains statutory amounts that employers must calculate, declare and pay correctly. Bookkeepers need enough knowledge to reconcile payroll and spot obvious exceptions, while using current payroll software and SARS guidance for actual deductions.

## Current South African Regulatory Note
For the **2026/2027 tax year (1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027)**, SARS publishes the current Employees' Tax deduction tables. PAYE uses progressive tax rules and is **not** a flat percentage.

Current SARS guidance states that UIF generally requires a **1% employee contribution and 1% employer contribution**, subject to the UIF remuneration rules and contribution ceiling. The current SARS employer guide lists the UIF ceiling as **R17,712 per month (R212,544 annually)**, which means a maximum ordinary 1% employee contribution of R177.12 per month at that ceiling.

Current SARS Budget 2026/SDL guidance states that SDL is generally **1% of leviable remuneration** for liable employers, with the current general exemption where total annual remuneration subject to SDL does not exceed **R500,000**, subject to applicable rules and other exemptions.

Rates and thresholds can change. Live payroll must verify current SARS publications and approved payroll configuration.

## Key Terms
- **PAYE / Employees' Tax** — Income tax withheld by employers from remuneration under the applicable deduction rules.
- **UIF** — Unemployment Insurance Fund contribution system.
- **SDL** — Skills Development Levy paid by liable employers.
- **EMP201** — Monthly Employer Declaration used to declare applicable PAYE, SDL, UIF and related amounts.
- **Contribution ceiling** — Maximum remuneration amount on which UIF contributions are calculated under the current rule.
- **Employer contribution** — Statutory amount borne by the employer rather than withheld from employee net pay.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. PAYE
PAYE is calculated using current SARS deduction tables/rules and payroll information. Employee earnings, tax status and other authorised factors can affect the calculation. Do not use a flat rate shortcut.

### 2. UIF employee and employer portions
At awareness level, if UIF-liable remuneration is R10,000, the ordinary employee contribution at 1% is R100 and the employer contribution is also R100, subject to the applicable UIF rules.

### 3. UIF ceiling
For remuneration above the current ceiling, the ordinary contribution is capped by the applicable ceiling. Payroll software should be configured from current authorised rules.

### 4. SDL
SDL is generally an employer cost, not an employee deduction. Payroll should calculate it only where the employer is liable under the applicable rules.

### 5. EMP201
The employer declaration combines applicable PAYE, SDL and UIF amounts for the month. The payroll register and ledger control accounts should reconcile to the declaration before payment.

### 6. Due-date awareness
Current SARS guidance generally requires monthly employer amounts to be declared/paid by the prescribed monthly deadline. Because weekends, public holidays and SARS changes can affect the practical date, verify the current SARS calendar rather than relying on memory.

### 7. Reconciliation
Compare payroll totals to:
- PAYE control;
- UIF employee/employer amounts;
- SDL control where applicable;
- EMP201 working paper;
- bank payment evidence.

### 8. Role boundaries
A payroll administrator may run configured calculations and reconcile them. Complex tax-status questions, fringe benefits and unusual remuneration should be escalated to an authorised payroll/tax specialist.

## Worked Example
Employee UIF-liable remuneration is R20,000. Because this exceeds the current R17,712 monthly ceiling used in the source check, the basic 1% employee UIF calculation is capped at **R177.12**, and the employer portion is also **R177.12**, subject to applicable UIF rules.

This example does not calculate PAYE because live PAYE should use current SARS tables/payroll software rather than an invented flat rate.

## Key Takeaways
- PAYE uses current SARS deduction rules, not one flat rate.
- UIF has employee and employer components subject to current rules and ceiling.
- SDL is generally an employer levy for liable employers.
