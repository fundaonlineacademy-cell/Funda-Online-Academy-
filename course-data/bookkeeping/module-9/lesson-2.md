# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 9: Payroll Administration & Payroll Records
### Lesson 2: Gross Pay, Ordinary Hours, Overtime and Allowances

**Estimated study time:** 55–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the difference between basic pay and gross pay.
2. Calculate simple hourly and salary-based payroll amounts from approved inputs.
3. Record overtime and allowances according to authorised rules.
4. Identify payroll inputs requiring HR or management confirmation.
5. Perform basic gross-pay reasonableness checks.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Gross pay is the starting point for many payroll deductions and statutory calculations. The bookkeeper or payroll administrator must calculate from authorised rates, hours and allowances rather than assumptions. Employment-law rules and collective agreements can affect overtime and leave treatment, so this lesson focuses on controlled payroll administration rather than legal advice.

## Key Terms
- **Basic pay** — Employee's agreed ordinary salary or wage before additions and deductions.
- **Gross pay** — Total payroll earnings before deductions for the period.
- **Ordinary hours** — Approved normal working hours for payroll purposes.
- **Overtime** — Approved work beyond ordinary hours subject to applicable employment terms and law.
- **Allowance** — Approved payroll amount provided for a specified purpose or condition.
- **Timesheet** — Record of hours worked used as payroll evidence where applicable.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start with authorised pay information
Monthly salary, hourly rate, work schedule and allowance rules should come from approved employment/HR records. Payroll should not change a rate simply because a manager verbally requests it after cut-off without the required approval.

### 2. Basic hourly pay
A basic wage calculation may be:

**Approved hours × approved hourly rate**

Example: 160 ordinary hours × R45 = R7,200 basic pay.

### 3. Salary-based employees
A monthly salary is normally processed from the approved master amount, with any pro-rating or unpaid time handled under the organisation's payroll rules.

### 4. Overtime
Overtime rates depend on applicable employment law, contracts and workplace arrangements. Payroll should use the authorised rate/multiplier provided by the approved payroll rule rather than inventing one.

If an authorised overtime rate is R67.50 per hour and 6 hours are approved, overtime pay = R405.

### 5. Allowances
Examples may include travel, shift, cellphone or other approved allowances. Tax treatment can differ, so the payroll administrator should use the configured payroll code and current authorised tax guidance.

### 6. Gross pay
In a simplified case:
- Basic pay R7,200
- Overtime R405
- Allowance R500
Gross pay = **R8,105** before deductions.

### 7. Evidence controls
Payroll inputs can include approved timesheets, leave records, overtime approvals, commission schedules and HR change forms. Late or unsupported inputs should be recorded as exceptions rather than silently estimated.

### 8. Reasonableness checks
Compare current gross pay with prior periods. Investigate large unexpected movements, duplicate allowances, unusual hours or missing employees.

## Worked Example
Employee A has approved basic monthly pay of R18,000, approved overtime of R1,350 and a recurring authorised allowance of R800. Gross pay before deductions is R20,150. If the prior month was R18,800, the R1,350 increase should be traceable to the overtime evidence.

## Practical Activity
Calculate gross pay for three fictional employees using approved basic pay, overtime and allowances. Then create an exception list for one missing timesheet, one unapproved allowance and one unexpectedly doubled overtime amount.

2. What evidence should support hourly payroll?

4. How are allowances included in gross pay?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Total earnings before payroll deductions.
2. Approved hours/timesheets and pay rates.
3. The approved employment/payroll rules and authorised HR/management process.
4. Add approved allowance earnings according to payroll configuration.
5. Estimates can create incorrect salary and statutory results.
6. Review of whether payroll amounts make sense compared with evidence and expected patterns.
7. To detect duplicates, missing inputs or unusual changes.
8. When authority, employment terms or tax treatment is uncertain.

## Key Takeaways
- Gross pay comes from authorised earnings inputs.
- Overtime and allowances require approved rules and evidence.
- Payroll should not invent employment terms.
- Reasonableness checks detect unusual changes before payment.
- Complex employment/tax treatment should be escalated.
