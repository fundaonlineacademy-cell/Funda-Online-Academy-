# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 9: Payroll Administration & Payroll Records
### Lesson 7: Payroll Confidentiality, Corrections and Audit Trails

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Apply confidentiality controls to payroll information.
2. Correct payroll errors through controlled processes.
3. Preserve audit trails for payroll changes.
4. Distinguish current-period corrections from prior-period issues.
5. Escalate material payroll errors and possible information breaches appropriately.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Payroll contains some of the organisation's most sensitive records. At the same time, payroll corrections can affect employee pay, statutory declarations and prior-period reports. Professional payroll administration protects both the information and the history of changes.

## Key Terms
- **Payroll confidentiality** — Restricting payroll information to authorised people and legitimate purposes.
- **Payroll correction** — Authorised change fixing an incorrect payroll input or result.
- **Audit trail** — Traceable history of payroll transactions, changes, approvals and users.
- **Prior-period correction** — Correction affecting a payroll period already finalised or reported.
- **Data incident** — Loss, exposure or inappropriate access to sensitive payroll information.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Need-to-know access
Managers may need some payroll information, but not every manager needs complete payroll reports. Give access according to role and authorised purpose.

### 2. Secure distribution
Payslips and payroll schedules should use approved secure channels. Check recipients carefully before sending sensitive attachments or reports.

### 3. Current-period corrections
If an error is found before payroll is finalised, correct the source input through the authorised workflow and rerun/review the calculation.

### 4. After payment
If an employee has already been underpaid or overpaid, do not privately alter the next payroll without documented authority. Follow the payroll correction/recovery process and communicate through the appropriate HR/payroll channel.

### 5. Prior-period statutory effects
A correction can affect PAYE, UIF, employer declarations or employee tax certificates. Refer statutory correction requirements to the authorised payroll/tax process and keep the original/revised schedules clearly identified.

### 6. Audit history
Retain evidence of:
- original input;
- error identified;
- authorised correction;
- recalculation;
- employee communication where required;
- revised statutory/payment records where applicable.

### 7. Information incidents
If a payroll report is sent to the wrong recipient or sensitive employee information is exposed, report promptly through the organisation's privacy/security process. Do not attempt to hide the event.

### 8. Handover
A payroll handover should state period status, unresolved corrections, statutory submissions, payment status and deadlines without sharing authentication credentials.

## Worked Example
An employee was paid R1,200 too little because approved overtime was omitted. Payroll verifies the overtime approval, calculates the correction, obtains the required authorisation, processes payment according to the organisation's correction cycle and determines whether any statutory payroll figures require update. The original payroll is not silently rewritten without traceability.

## Practical Activity
Create a payroll correction log with fields for employee, period, original amount, correct amount, reason, source evidence, approval, payment action, statutory impact, communication and closure status.

## Key Takeaways
- Payroll information requires strong confidentiality.
- Correct the source of errors rather than hiding differences.
- Post-payment and prior-period corrections need stronger control.
- Payroll changes should remain traceable.
- Data incidents must be escalated promptly.
