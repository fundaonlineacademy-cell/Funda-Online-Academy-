# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 9: Payroll Administration & Payroll Records
### Lesson 6: Payroll Reconciliation and Payment Controls

**Estimated study time:** 55–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Reconcile payroll-register totals to general-ledger control accounts.
2. Prepare employee and statutory payment schedules.
3. Identify payroll differences before payment release.
4. Apply segregation, approval and change controls to salary payments.
5. Clear payroll liabilities after verified payment.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Payroll errors become costly when incorrect amounts are released to employees or statutory authorities. Reconciliation before payment gives payroll one final opportunity to find duplicate employees, changed bank details, wrong deductions or ledger differences.

## Key Terms
- **Payroll reconciliation** — Comparison of payroll calculations to supporting records, ledger accounts and payment schedules.
- **Net-pay schedule** — List of employee net amounts approved for salary payment.
- **Payroll liability** — Amount owed to employees, SARS or another payroll-related party.
- **Payment release** — Authorised final action causing salary or statutory payment to leave the bank.
- **Variance** — Difference between two records that should agree.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Reconcile gross payroll
Compare total gross earnings on the payroll register to the payroll expense posting and major earning categories. Investigate unexpected changes from the prior period.

### 2. Reconcile net pay
The total employee net-pay schedule should agree with net-pay amounts on the payroll register. The bank payment file or approved payment batch should match that schedule.

### 3. Reconcile deductions
PAYE, UIF employee contributions, authorised benefit deductions and other liabilities should agree between payroll and the general ledger.

### 4. Reconcile employer contributions
Employer UIF, SDL where applicable, and employer benefit contributions should be separately checked. Employer costs are not simply employee deductions.

### 5. Employee-count controls
Compare current paid employees with the authorised active employee list. Investigate employees who left, new employees missing approval, duplicate records or unusual zero-pay employees.

### 6. Bank-detail change review
Before payment, review recent changes to employee bank details under the organisation's approved process. A payroll master change should not flow to payment without required verification.

### 7. Payment approval and release
Where possible, the person preparing payroll should not alone approve and release the payment batch. Use the organisation's banking mandate and segregation controls.

### 8. Clearing liabilities
After payment, match the bank transactions to net salaries and statutory/third-party liabilities. Outstanding control balances should be explained rather than left indefinitely.

### 9. Exception handling
Do not alter a payroll total merely to make the bank schedule agree. Find the underlying employee, deduction, rounding or posting difference.

## Worked Example
Payroll register net-pay total = R284,500. Salary payment file = R289,500. The R5,000 difference is traced to an employee who was accidentally included twice in the payment file. The payment file is corrected before release, while the payroll register remains unchanged because it was already correct.

## Practical Activity
Design a payroll reconciliation worksheet with sections for employee count, gross pay, PAYE, employee UIF, employer UIF, SDL, other deductions, net pay, ledger posting, payment file and exceptions. Include reviewer sign-off fields.

3. What should statutory deductions reconcile to?
4. Why should employee bank-detail changes be reviewed before payment?
5. What is segregation of duties in payroll payment?
6. What happens to payroll control accounts after payment?
7. What should happen if the payment file differs from the payroll register?
8. Why should old payroll liabilities be investigated?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Net-pay amounts on the approved payroll register.
2. To detect missing, duplicate or terminated employees and other master-data errors.
3. Payroll register, ledger controls and statutory working schedules.
4. Incorrect or unauthorised changes can misdirect salary payments.
5. Preparation, approval and payment release are separated where practical.
6. They should be cleared by the corresponding verified payments.
7. Investigate the cause before release; do not force either total.
8. They may indicate unpaid obligations, misallocations or posting errors.

## Key Takeaways
- Reconcile payroll before money is released.
- Employee count and master changes are important payment controls.
- Payroll, ledger and payment schedules should agree.
- Separate preparation from final payment approval where practical.
- Outstanding payroll liabilities need follow-up and evidence.
