# Module 9 Summative Assessment Bank
## Payroll Administration & Payroll Records

**Bank size:** 52 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 25 random questions per attempt  
**Pass standard:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Coverage:** All eight Module 9 lessons.

1. What is the primary purpose of employee master data in payroll?  
A. Store verified information needed to calculate, pay and report payroll correctly  
B. Replace employment contracts  
C. Approve salary increases automatically  
D. Record only annual leave  
**Answer: A**

2. Which payroll field requires especially strong verification before first payment?  
A. Favourite colour  
B. Internal nickname  
C. Employee banking details  
D. Office seat number  
**Answer: C**

3. Why should payroll changes have an effective date?  
A. To show when the authorised change begins to affect payroll  
B. To make forms longer  
C. To remove approval requirements  
D. To prevent reconciliations  
**Answer: A**

4. An employee advises new bank details verbally. What is the BEST response?  
A. Pay cash instead  
B. Update immediately without evidence  
C. Follow the organisation's authorised change and verification process  
D. Ask another employee to confirm  
**Answer: C**

5. Which payroll amount represents total earnings before authorised deductions?  
A. Pay after all deductions  
B. Total earnings before deductions  
C. Employer bank balance  
D. Tax only  
**Answer: B**

6. If ordinary pay is R12,000 and an authorised allowance is R1,500, gross earnings before any other items are:  
A. R12,000  
B. R15,000  
C. R10,500  
D. R13,500  
**Answer: D**

7. Why must overtime calculations use authorised hours and rates?  
A. Overtime is always tax-free  
B. Every employee receives the same overtime  
C. Payroll may not invent time or pay rules  
D. Overtime never affects gross pay  
**Answer: C**

8. Which source is strongest for payroll hours?  
A. A colleague's estimate  
B. Approved time/attendance evidence under company procedure  
C. A social-media message  
D. An employee's memory after payday  
**Answer: B**

9. Net pay is generally:  
A. Gross earnings plus deductions  
B. Taxable income before payroll  
C. Employer contributions only  
D. Gross earnings minus authorised deductions  
**Answer: D**

10. What is a payroll deduction?  
A. Any amount a bookkeeper chooses  
B. A supplier credit note  
C. A sales discount  
D. An authorised amount withheld from employee earnings under applicable rules or agreement  
**Answer: D**

11. Why should voluntary deductions be supported by proper authorisation?  
A. To prove the employee or applicable process authorised the deduction  
B. To remove payroll controls  
C. To avoid payslips  
D. To increase gross pay  
**Answer: A**

12. A deduction appears twice for one employee. What should happen?  
A. Leave it until year-end  
B. Investigate and correct through the authorised payroll process  
C. Delete the employee  
D. Hide the payslip  
**Answer: B**

13. PAYE is best described as:  
A. A voluntary savings account  
B. A supplier-payment tax  
C. A company VAT return  
D. A payroll mechanism through which employers withhold employees' tax according to applicable rules  
**Answer: D**

14. UIF contributions in payroll should be handled using:  
A. Last year's spreadsheet regardless of changes  
B. Current authorised payroll rules and official guidance  
C. A guessed percentage  
D. Employee preference only  
**Answer: B**

15. Why should payroll staff verify current statutory tables and rules?  
A. Old tax tables are always valid  
B. Rates, thresholds or requirements may change  
C. Software removes all responsibility  
D. Payroll law never changes  
**Answer: B**

16. SDL is generally an employer-side payroll-related levy where applicable. What should the bookkeeper do if applicability is uncertain?  
A. Guess  
B. Ignore it permanently  
C. Deduct it from every employee automatically  
D. Use current official guidance or refer to the authorised payroll/tax specialist  
**Answer: D**

17. Which document commonly shows an employee the calculation of their pay?  
A. Payslip  
B. Purchase order  
C. Sales invoice  
D. Supplier statement  
**Answer: A**

18. Why should payroll input forms be retained according to approved rules?  
A. They eliminate confidentiality requirements  
B. They replace payroll software  
C. They support the audit trail for changes and calculations  
D. They should be kept forever regardless of policy  
**Answer: C**

19. What should a payroll change form normally identify?  
A. Only the final net pay  
B. Employee, change, effective date and authorisation  
C. Only the manager name  
D. Only the employee name  
**Answer: B**

20. Why are payroll records sensitive?  
A. They may contain identity, banking, earnings and tax information  
B. Only managers' records are confidential  
C. Payroll information is public  
D. Payslips contain no personal information  
**Answer: A**

21. What is a payroll reconciliation?  
A. Deleting old reports  
B. Comparing payroll totals and related records to identify unexplained differences  
C. Increasing employee pay  
D. Printing every payslip twice  
**Answer: B**

22. Payroll bank payment total is R187,500 but approved net-pay report is R184,500. What should happen first?  
A. Change the payroll report to match  
B. Stop and investigate the R3,000 difference before release  
C. Pay R187,500 and investigate later  
D. Split the difference  
**Answer: B**

23. Why should payroll control totals be compared period to period?  
A. Unexpected movements can reveal errors or unusual changes  
B. Payroll should never change  
C. It removes the need for approvals  
D. Reconciliation is only for banks  
**Answer: A**

24. Which is a useful payroll control total?  
A. Number of office chairs  
B. Supplier invoice total  
C. Total gross earnings, total deductions and total net pay  
D. Number of emails sent  
**Answer: C**

25. Why should one person not control every payroll stage where segregation is practical?  
A. It makes payroll less accurate  
B. Payroll has no fraud risk  
C. One user should always approve themselves  
D. Separation reduces error and fraud risk  
**Answer: D**

26. What does payroll approval confirm?  
A. Tax law will never change  
B. Every employee is happy with their salary  
C. The authorised reviewer has accepted the payroll for release according to process  
D. No future correction will ever be required  
**Answer: C**

27. A payroll file is emailed to the wrong distribution list. What is the BEST response?  
A. Delete your sent email only  
B. Post a correction publicly  
C. Ignore it if nobody complains  
D. Follow the organisation's privacy/security incident process promptly  
**Answer: D**

28. Why should payroll access follow least-necessary access?  
A. Access control slows payroll  
B. Users should see only what they need for legitimate work  
C. Everyone should have payroll access  
D. Payroll is not sensitive  
**Answer: B**

29. Which behaviour weakens payroll audit trails?  
A. Change reports  
B. Unique user accounts  
C. Shared user credentials  
D. Approval logs  
**Answer: C**

30. What is the safest way to correct a payroll error after processing?  
A. Delete all evidence  
B. Backdate without approval  
C. Edit the payslip image only  
D. Use the authorised correction/reversal process and preserve traceability  
**Answer: D**

31. Why must payroll corrections be communicated appropriately?  
A. Payroll errors never affect employees  
B. Communication changes tax automatically  
C. Affected employees and authorised functions may need accurate updated information  
D. Corrections should always be secret  
**Answer: C**

32. What is an audit trail in payroll?  
A. A tax table  
B. A list of employee birthdays  
C. Traceable evidence of changes, calculations, approvals and actions  
D. A salary survey  
**Answer: C**

33. A new employee is missing approved banking details two days before payroll cut-off. What is the BEST action?  
A. Use a colleague's bank account  
B. Exclude the employee without telling anyone  
C. Guess from an old record  
D. Escalate and follow the approved exception/verification process  
**Answer: D**

34. What should happen before payroll is finalised?  
A. Only payslips should be printed  
B. Payroll should be released automatically  
C. All records should be deleted  
D. Input, changes, calculations, exceptions and control totals should be reviewed  
**Answer: D**

35. Why is a payroll cut-off date useful?  
A. It defines when routine inputs should be submitted for the current cycle  
B. It replaces employment policies  
C. It sets tax rates  
D. It prevents all later corrections  
**Answer: A**

36. A manager submits a late salary change after cut-off. What should the payroll administrator do?  
A. Change another employee's pay instead  
B. Ignore all future instructions  
C. Follow the authorised late-change/exception process and effective-date rules  
D. Apply it secretly  
**Answer: C**

37. What is the purpose of a payroll checklist?  
A. Replace payroll expertise  
B. Guarantee that legislation never changes  
C. Standardise critical controls across each payroll cycle  
D. Eliminate approvals  
**Answer: C**

38. Why should payroll totals be reconciled before payment-file release?  
A. To delay salaries  
B. To remove payslips  
C. To increase bank charges  
D. To verify the payment file agrees with the approved payroll  
**Answer: D**

39. Which item should be included in a payroll handover?  
A. Passwords for shared access  
B. Outstanding exceptions, deadlines, approvals and unresolved employee issues  
C. Personal opinions about employees  
D. Irrelevant office gossip  
**Answer: B**

40. Why should employee master-data changes be reviewed separately from ordinary payroll inputs?  
A. They have no impact after one month  
B. They are always temporary  
C. They cannot cause fraud  
D. They can affect recurring future payments and sensitive identity/payment information  
**Answer: D**

41. What is the BEST response to an unexplained large increase in payroll expense?  
A. Investigate headcount, pay changes, overtime, allowances and corrections before accepting it  
B. Assume it is correct because software calculated it  
C. Delete prior-period reports  
D. Reduce all salaries proportionally  
**Answer: A**

42. Why should payroll software output not be accepted blindly?  
A. Incorrect inputs or configuration can still produce incorrect results  
B. Software never uses data  
C. Manual work is always more accurate  
D. Payroll systems cannot calculate totals  
**Answer: A**

43. What is a reasonable role boundary for a junior payroll/bookkeeping administrator?  
A. Approve own salary changes  
B. Give personalised tax advice to employees  
C. Invent statutory rates  
D. Process according to approved rules and escalate tax/legal uncertainty  
**Answer: D**

44. A payslip includes an unfamiliar deduction code. What should happen?  
A. Investigate the code, authority and calculation before finalising  
B. Rename it “miscellaneous”  
C. Leave it because the system created it  
D. Add the same deduction to everyone  
**Answer: A**

45. Why should payroll records use authorised storage?  
A. Personal devices are always safer  
B. To support confidentiality, continuity, access control and retention requirements  
C. Payroll records do not need backup  
D. Shared public folders are easier  
**Answer: B**

46. Which payroll control most directly protects against unauthorised bank-detail changes?  
A. Independent verification and authorised change approval  
B. Printing the payroll twice  
C. Bigger fonts on payslips  
D. Sorting employees alphabetically  
**Answer: A**

47. What should happen when a payroll exception remains unresolved at payment deadline?  
A. Escalate to the authorised decision-maker with facts and impact  
B. Hide the issue  
C. Invent a solution without authority  
D. Pay an estimated amount automatically  
**Answer: A**

48. What is the relationship between gross pay and net pay?  
A. Gross pay is after deductions  
B. Net pay results after authorised deductions from gross earnings  
C. They are always equal  
D. Net pay excludes all earnings  
**Answer: B**

49. Why is payroll confidentiality still important after payment is complete?  
A. Old records can be emailed anywhere  
B. Paid payroll becomes public  
C. Records remain sensitive throughout storage, reporting and retention  
D. Bank details stop being personal information  
**Answer: C**

50. What should happen at payroll-cycle close?  
A. Reconcile outputs, secure records, communicate authorised results and carry forward unresolved actions  
B. Delete all working evidence  
C. Stop retaining records immediately  
D. Remove audit logs  
**Answer: A**

51. Which scenario BEST demonstrates proper payroll control?  
A. One employee captures, approves and releases their own salary change  
B. Payroll is paid before checking totals  
C. Approved changes are captured, independently reviewed, reconciled and authorised before payment  
D. Bank details are accepted by text message only  
**Answer: C**

52. Which principle is MOST important across the full payroll cycle?  
A. Relying on memory  
B. Accuracy, authorisation, confidentiality and traceability  
C. Avoiding all documentation  
D. Speed regardless of controls  
**Answer: B**
