# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 9: Payroll Administration & Payroll Records
### Lesson 8: Payroll-cycle Practical

**Estimated study time:** 65–75 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Process a controlled payroll cycle from approved inputs to payment.
2. Calculate gross pay, deductions and net pay from supplied authorised figures.
3. Reconcile payroll totals and statutory controls.
4. Identify payroll exceptions before release.
5. Prepare a complete payroll evidence pack and handover.

## Why This Lesson Matters
This practical combines employee master data, earnings, deductions, statutory awareness, payment controls and audit trails. The goal is not merely to produce a net-pay number, but to demonstrate a payroll process another reviewer can verify.

## Practical Scenario
A fictional company has three monthly employees.

### Employee A
- Approved basic salary: R18,000
- Approved overtime: R1,350
- Other authorised allowance: R650
- PAYE produced by approved payroll system: R2,250
- UIF employee deduction according to current configured rules: R177.12
- Other authorised deduction: R400

Gross pay = R20,000.
Net pay = R20,000 - R2,250 - R177.12 - R400 = **R17,172.88**.

### Employee B
- Approved basic salary: R14,000
- Allowance: R500
- Payroll-system PAYE: R1,250
- UIF employee deduction: R145
- Other authorised deduction: R250

Gross pay = R14,500.
Net pay = **R12,855**.

### Employee C
- Approved basic salary: R10,000
- Approved overtime: R750
- Payroll-system PAYE: R500
- UIF employee deduction: R107.50
- No other deduction

Gross pay = R10,750.
Net pay = **R10,142.50**.

The PAYE values above are supplied fictional payroll outputs for the exercise. They are not tax-table examples for live use.

## Step 1: Master-data review
Confirm each employee is active, pay rate is approved, bank details passed the required change controls, and no duplicate employee record exists.

## Step 2: Input review
Trace overtime and allowances to authorised evidence. Record missing or late information on an exception list.

## Step 3: Payroll calculation
Run the approved payroll configuration and compare gross pay and deductions to source inputs and prior-period patterns.

## Step 4: Statutory controls
Prepare a schedule for PAYE, employee UIF, employer UIF and SDL where the fictional employer is liable. For live payroll, use current SARS rules and configured payroll calculations.

## Step 5: Net-pay reconciliation
Total net pay in this exercise:
R17,172.88 + R12,855 + R10,142.50 = **R40,170.38**.

The salary payment schedule must equal R40,170.38 before approval.

## Step 6: Exception test
Assume the bank payment file totals R45,170.38. The R5,000 difference must be investigated before release. A duplicate or unauthorised payment should not be allowed merely because payroll itself is correct.

## Step 7: Accounting entry
A payroll journal may debit salary/wage and employer-contribution expenses and credit liabilities for net pay, PAYE, UIF, SDL and other deductions according to the chart of accounts. The exact journal depends on the payroll configuration.

## Step 8: Payment and clearing
After approved salary and statutory payments, match bank transactions to payroll liabilities and confirm outstanding balances are explained.

## Step 9: Evidence pack
Include:
- employee/master-change checklist;
- approved payroll inputs;
- payroll register;
- exception log;
- net-pay schedule;
- statutory reconciliation;
- ledger posting;
- payment approval and bank evidence;
- correction log if required;
- period handover.

## Practical Activity
Create a four-employee fictional payroll containing one overtime payment, one allowance, one employee with changed bank details, one late input and one correction. Produce a complete evidence pack and reconciliation without using real personal information.

## Knowledge Check
1. What should happen before payroll calculation starts?
2. Why are supplied PAYE values in the practical not suitable as live tax examples?
3. What should the net-pay payment schedule equal?
4. What should happen when the payment file differs from payroll?
5. What should statutory amounts reconcile to?
6. What happens to payroll liabilities after payment?
7. What should the payroll evidence pack contain?
8. What makes a payroll cycle controlled rather than merely calculated?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Verify master data and approved period inputs.
2. Live PAYE depends on current SARS tables, employee circumstances and payroll configuration.
3. Approved net-pay total from the payroll register.
4. Investigate and correct before payment release.
5. Payroll register, ledger controls and declaration/payment schedules.
6. They are cleared against verified payments.
7. Source, calculation, reconciliation, approval, payment and exception evidence.
8. Accuracy, authority, reconciliation, payment control, confidentiality and audit trail all work together.

## Key Takeaways
- Payroll starts with authorised employee and earnings data.
- Live statutory calculations must use current rules.
- Reconcile payroll before bank release.
- Payment-file differences are stop-and-investigate exceptions.
- A complete payroll cycle leaves a clear evidence trail.
