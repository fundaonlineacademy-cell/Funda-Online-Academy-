# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 9: Payroll Administration & Payroll Records
### Lesson 1: Payroll Information and Employee Master Data

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of payroll master data.
2. Identify essential payroll information and supporting records.
3. Distinguish employee master changes from routine payroll transactions.
4. Apply confidentiality and access controls to payroll data.
5. Reconcile opening payroll information when implementing a system.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Payroll calculations depend on employee master data. An incorrect bank account, tax number, pay rate or start date can affect salary payments and statutory reporting. Because payroll records contain highly sensitive personal and financial information, accuracy and confidentiality are equally important.

## Key Terms
- **Employee master record** — Core payroll record containing approved employee details used in payroll processing.
- **Remuneration** — Amounts paid or payable to an employee, subject to definitions used for payroll and statutory purposes.
- **Payroll period** — Weekly, fortnightly, monthly or other approved pay cycle.
- **Pay rate** — Approved wage/salary basis used in calculations.
- **Master-data change** — Authorised update to core employee information.
- **Payroll register** — Report listing payroll calculations and totals for a period.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Core employee information
Typical payroll master information includes employee identity, employment number, tax details, contact information, bank-payment details, start date, pay frequency, approved salary/wage rate and applicable deduction/benefit settings.

### 2. Use authorised source documents
Do not change salary rates or bank details from informal messages alone. Use the organisation's approved HR/payroll change process and evidence.

### 3. Employee classification matters
Employment type and remuneration structure can affect payroll treatment. Bookkeepers should capture authorised classifications but refer legal or tax-status questions to HR, payroll specialists or the authorised tax process.

### 4. Bank-detail changes
Salary bank changes are high-risk. Follow verification and approval procedures before updating the master record.

### 5. Tax and statutory identifiers
Capture tax and statutory details accurately from approved records. Do not invent missing numbers to make payroll software accept an employee.

### 6. Confidentiality
Payroll data may include identity numbers, salaries, bank details and tax information. Access should be restricted to legitimate roles, and reports should not be stored in open shared folders.

### 7. New employees and terminations
Starting and leaving dates affect payroll periods, pro-rating and reporting. Ensure authorised HR information reaches payroll before cut-off.

### 8. Opening payroll data
When moving to a new payroll system, reconcile employee-to-date figures, deductions and statutory totals to approved prior payroll reports. Incorrect openings can distort annual certificates and reconciliations.

## Worked Example
An employee emails payroll asking that next month's salary be paid into a new bank account. The bookkeeper does not change the master immediately. The request is routed through the approved employee-bank-change verification process, the authorised change is documented, and the updated details are checked before payroll payment.

## Key Takeaways
- Payroll accuracy begins with clean employee master data.
- Master changes require evidence and authority.
- Payroll information is highly confidential.
- Opening balances and year-to-date figures need reconciliation.
- Bookkeepers should escalate legal/tax classification questions rather than guess.
