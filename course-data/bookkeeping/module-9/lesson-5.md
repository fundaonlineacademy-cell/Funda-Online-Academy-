# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 9: Payroll Administration & Payroll Records
### Lesson 5: Payroll Source Documents and Payslip Records

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Identify key documents supporting payroll calculations.
2. Explain the purpose of a payroll register and payslip.
3. Check payroll source documents for completeness and approval.
4. Maintain payroll records securely and consistently.
5. Link payroll evidence to period totals and employee records.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Payroll is only as reliable as the records supporting it. Timesheets, approved salary changes, leave records, commission schedules and deduction authorities provide evidence for the figures on payroll. A payslip is an output of payroll; it does not by itself prove every input was correct.

## Key Terms
- **Payroll source document** — Evidence supporting a payroll input or change.
- **Payslip** — Employee-facing record summarising earnings, deductions and net pay for a payroll period.
- **Payroll register** — Detailed payroll report listing calculations and totals for employees.
- **Change form** — Authorised document supporting a master-data or pay change.
- **Cut-off** — Deadline by which payroll inputs must be approved for a pay cycle.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Common payroll source records
These may include employment/HR instructions, salary-change approvals, timesheets, overtime approvals, leave records, commission schedules, benefit instructions, deduction authorities and termination information.

### 2. Check before capture
Verify employee identity, period, approved amount/hours, authority and supporting calculation. Do not process an unsigned or ambiguous change simply because payroll cut-off is near.

### 3. Payroll register
The payroll register provides the detailed calculation base for review. It should show earnings, deductions, employer contributions and net-pay totals according to the payroll system.

### 4. Payslip
A payslip communicates the employee's period earnings and deductions. If an employee queries a payslip, trace the amount back to payroll inputs and approved records rather than altering the payslip independently.

### 5. Payroll cut-off
Late inputs need a controlled exception process. Some may be processed in the next payroll or through an authorised correction run depending on policy.

### 6. Record confidentiality
Store payroll source records and payslips in restricted systems. Avoid sending group emails that expose one employee's pay to another employee.

### 7. Version and correction control
If a payslip is corrected, retain the approved payroll correction history. Do not create an unofficial spreadsheet payslip that disagrees with the payroll system.

### 8. Period evidence pack
A payroll evidence pack can include source-input checklist, payroll register, exception log, payslip generation record, payment schedule, statutory schedules and review approval.

## Worked Example
A commission schedule adds R3,500 to Employee A's pay. The schedule has the correct employee and month but lacks the required manager approval. Payroll records the item as an exception and obtains approval before processing rather than guessing that the amount is valid.

## Practical Activity
Create a payroll-source checklist with at least 12 document types/fields. Then design a payroll evidence-pack index showing how a reviewer can trace from an employee payslip back to approved input and from the payroll register to the general ledger.

## Key Takeaways
- Payroll figures need source evidence.
- Payslips are outputs, not substitutes for approved inputs.
- Late changes require controlled handling.
- Payroll evidence should be traceable by employee and period.
- Confidentiality is essential throughout payroll recordkeeping.
