# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 3: Journals, Ledgers & Trial Balance
### Lesson 5: Preparing a Trial Balance

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of a trial balance.
2. Transfer ledger closing balances into debit and credit columns.
3. Calculate and compare trial-balance totals.
4. Identify common reasons a trial balance may not balance.
5. Explain the limitations of a balanced trial balance.

## Why This Lesson Matters
The trial balance is an important checkpoint between routine ledger processing and financial reporting. It summarises the closing balances of general-ledger accounts and tests whether total debit balances equal total credit balances.

A trial balance that does not balance indicates that something in the double-entry records or extraction process needs investigation. However, a trial balance that does balance is not proof that every transaction is correctly classified or complete.

## Key Terms
- **Trial balance** — List of ledger balances split into debit and credit columns.
- **Debit column** — Column containing accounts with debit balances.
- **Credit column** — Column containing accounts with credit balances.
- **Extraction error** — Error when transferring balances from the ledger to the trial balance.
- **Suspense account** — Temporary account sometimes used under controlled procedures while an imbalance is investigated.
- **Difference** — Amount by which trial-balance debit and credit totals disagree.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Prepare the ledger first
A trial balance should be based on the ledger after the relevant transactions have been posted and accounts balanced for the period.

The normal process is:
1. complete posting;
2. calculate each ledger balance;
3. list each balance once;
4. place it in the correct debit or credit column;
5. total both columns.

### 2. Example trial balance
Suppose the ledger balances are:
- Bank R20,000 Dr
- Debtors R15,000 Dr
- Equipment R30,000 Dr
- Rent Expense R5,000 Dr
- Creditors R12,000 Cr
- Loan R18,000 Cr
- Owner Capital R25,000 Cr
- Sales R15,000 Cr

Debit total = R70,000.  
Credit total = R70,000.

The trial balance agrees.

### 3. If the trial balance does not balance
Do not insert an unexplained figure merely to force equality. First determine the difference.

Possible causes include:
- one side of an entry not posted;
- amount posted differently on debit and credit sides;
- balance placed in the wrong column;
- ledger balance omitted from the trial balance;
- ledger balance entered twice;
- arithmetic error when balancing an account;
- transposed digits during extraction.

### 4. Use the difference as a clue
Certain patterns can guide investigation.

If the difference equals a known transaction amount, one side may be missing. If the difference is divisible by 9, it can sometimes indicate a transposition error, such as R540 entered as R450. This is only a clue, not proof.

A structured search is better than random changes.

### 5. Check extraction before changing ledgers
Sometimes the ledger is correct and the error occurred when the trial balance was prepared.

Check:
- every ledger account included once;
- correct balance transferred;
- correct debit/credit column;
- additions correct.

Only change the ledger when evidence confirms the ledger entry itself is wrong.

### 6. A balanced trial balance can still contain errors
Examples that may not disturb equality:
- transaction completely omitted;
- correct debit and credit amount posted to wrong accounts;
- duplicate transaction posted on both sides;
- compensating errors that offset each other;
- transaction recorded with the wrong date but balanced entry.

Therefore, trial-balance agreement must be combined with reconciliations, source-document review and reasonableness checks.

### 7. Suspense accounts require control
In some accounting environments, a suspense account may temporarily hold an unexplained difference so processing can continue while investigation takes place.

A suspense account is not a place to hide unresolved errors. It should have clear support, ownership and follow-up until resolved.

### 8. Review account reasonableness
Once the trial balance balances, ask whether balances make sense:
- Does Bank agree broadly with reconciled cash records?
- Is Debtors unusually negative?
- Is a major expense zero unexpectedly?
- Is an old suspense balance still present?
- Are income balances consistent with operational activity?

This review can reveal balanced but incorrect postings.

## Worked Example
The debit total is R128,450 and the credit total is R129,250. Difference = R800.

The bookkeeper checks the ledger extraction and finds Equipment balance of R8,000 was entered as R8,800 in the debit column. Correcting the trial-balance extraction reduces the debit total by R800 and the columns then agree.

No ledger journal is needed because the underlying ledger was already correct.

1. A list of ledger balances split between debit and credit columns.
2. They should be equal.
3. Examples: omission of one posting, wrong amount, wrong column, omitted balance, duplicate extraction or arithmetic error.
4. The ledger may already be correct.
5. Yes.

## Key Takeaways
- The trial balance tests debit-credit equality of ledger balances.
- Differences should be investigated systematically, not forced away.
- Check extraction before changing accounting records.
- Balanced does not mean error-free.
- Reconciliations and reasonableness checks remain essential.
