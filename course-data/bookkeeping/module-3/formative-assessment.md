# Module 3 Formative Assessment Bank
## Journals, Ledgers & Trial Balance

**Bank size:** 32 MCQs  
**Delivery:** 15 random questions per attempt  
**Pass standard:** 70%  
**Maximum attempts:** 3  
**Prerequisite:** Complete all eight Module 3 lessons.

## Lesson 1 — Journals and the Flow of Accounting Information
1. What is the main purpose of a journal?  
A. Store customer addresses  
B. Prepare bank reconciliations only  
C. Replace source documents  
D. Record or group transactions before ledger posting  
**Answer: D**

2. Which transaction is MOST likely to use a general journal?  
A. Normal supplier payment  
B. Approved reclassification correction  
C. Routine cash sale  
D. Customer receipt  
**Answer: B**

3. What must always be true of a balanced journal entry?  
A. It has no narration  
B. It affects only two accounts  
C. It includes cash  
D. Total debits equal total credits  
**Answer: D**

4. Why should journal narrations be meaningful?  
A. They make entries longer  
B. They help another reviewer understand why the entry was made  
C. They remove the need for source documents  
D. They replace approval  
**Answer: B**

## Lesson 2 — General Ledger and T-Account Logic
5. What is the general ledger?  
A. A payroll register  
B. A list of customers only  
C. The main collection of accounts used to accumulate transaction effects  
D. A bank statement  
**Answer: C**

6. In a T-account, the left side represents:  
A. credit  
B. narration  
C. closing balance only  
D. debit  
**Answer: D**

7. Which account normally increases with a debit?  
A. Loan liability  
B. Owner capital  
C. Bank asset  
D. Sales income  
**Answer: C**

8. Why are ledger accounts useful?  
A. They show the accumulated effect and balance of transactions by account  
B. They replace every source document  
C. They make trial balances unnecessary  
D. They eliminate errors  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 3 — Posting from Journals to Ledger Accounts
9. What does posting mean?  
A. Printing a journal  
B. Transferring journal effects to relevant ledger accounts  
C. Reconciling the bank  
D. Filing source documents alphabetically  
**Answer: B**

10. Why should posting references be used?  
A. To trace entries between journal and ledger  
B. To decorate ledger pages  
C. To calculate VAT automatically  
D. To replace account names  
**Answer: A**

11. A journal debits Rent Expense and credits Bank. What should happen when posted?  
A. Only Bank is updated  
B. Rent receives a debit entry and Bank receives a credit entry  
C. Both accounts receive credits  
D. Both accounts receive debits  
**Answer: B**

12. What is the main risk of posting the correct amount to the wrong ledger account?  
A. The books may still balance but account classification will be wrong  
B. The trial balance will always be unequal  
C. Cash disappears  
D. The source document becomes invalid  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 4 — Debits, Credits and Balancing Accounts
13. Which rule is correct for assets?  
A. Increases are normally debits  
B. They never have balances  
C. Increases are normally credits  
D. They are always expenses  
**Answer: A**

14. Which rule is correct for income?  
A. Increases are normally debits  
B. Increases are normally credits  
C. Income never affects equity  
D. Income is always an asset  
**Answer: B**

15. What is a closing balance?  
A. The first transaction of the month  
B. A bank-statement fee  
C. The net amount remaining in an account after its debits and credits are compared  
D. A source document total  
**Answer: C**

16. If Bank has debit entries of R20,000 and credits of R7,500, its balance is:  
A. R7,500 debit  
B. R12,500 debit  
C. R20,000 debit  
D. R27,500 credit  
**Answer: B**

## Lesson 5 — Preparing a Trial Balance
17. What is the primary purpose of a trial balance?  
A. List ledger balances and check whether total debits equal total credits  
B. Calculate profit directly  
C. Reconcile every customer account  
D. Replace the general ledger  
**Answer: A**

18. If total trial-balance debits exceed credits by R900, what should happen?  
A. Delete one debit account  
B. Ignore the difference  
C. Add a random credit of R900  
D. Investigate the source of the imbalance  
**Answer: D**

19. Which balance normally appears on the credit side of a trial balance?  
A. Equipment  
B. Rent expense  
C. Bank overdraft never  
D. Sales  
**Answer: D**

20. Does a balanced trial balance prove that every account is correctly classified?  
A. No, some errors can still leave debits and credits equal  
B. Yes, if software produced it  
C. Yes, always  
D. Yes, if prepared monthly  
**Answer: A**

## Lesson 6 — Finding and Correcting Bookkeeping Errors
21. Which error may NOT cause the trial balance to disagree?  
A. Omitting one credit side only  
B. Adding a ledger column incorrectly  
C. Posting only one side of a journal  
D. Posting an expense to the wrong expense account with equal debit and credit  
**Answer: D**

22. Why should errors be corrected through an authorised process?  
A. To preserve accuracy and traceability  
B. To avoid source documents  
C. To hide the original error  
D. To reduce account balances  
**Answer: A**

23. What is a transposition error?  
A. Omitting narration  
B. Recording the same transaction twice  
C. Posting to the wrong period  
D. Reversing digits, such as recording 54 instead of 45  
**Answer: D**

24. A trial balance amount was copied as R5,400 instead of the correct ledger balance R4,500. What should be corrected?  
A. The source invoice  
B. The trial-balance extraction figure  
C. The original transaction  
D. The bank statement  
**Answer: B**

## Lesson 7 — Control Accounts and Subsidiary Records
25. What is a control account?  
A. A tax invoice  
B. A password list  
C. A summary ledger account linked to detailed subsidiary records  
D. A petty-cash voucher  
**Answer: C**

26. What should the Debtors Control balance broadly agree with?  
A. Sales total only  
B. Total customer subsidiary balances  
C. Total supplier balances  
D. Bank balance  
**Answer: B**

27. Why should differences between a control account and subsidiary ledger be investigated?  
A. Control accounts are optional  
B. They are always caused by fraud  
C. They may indicate posting, timing or recording errors  
D. They can be ignored if small  
**Answer: C**

28. Which account commonly has a subsidiary ledger?  
A. Capital  
B. Rent Expense  
C. Debtors / Accounts Receivable  
D. Telephone Expense  
**Answer: C**

## Lesson 8 — Manual Books-to-Trial-Balance Practical
29. A credit sale of R8,000 normally records:  
A. Debit Debtors; Credit Sales  
B. Debit Sales; Credit Debtors  
C. Debit Bank; Credit Sales  
D. Debit Expense; Credit Creditors  
**Answer: A**

30. A customer pays R5,000 toward an existing debtor balance. What is the normal entry?  
A. Debit Expense; Credit Sales  
B. Debit Debtors; Credit Bank  
C. Debit Sales; Credit Bank  
D. Debit Bank; Credit Debtors  
**Answer: D**

31. Why should a bank balance on the trial balance later be reconciled to the bank statement?  
A. Trial balance already proves bank accuracy  
B. Bank statements are optional  
C. Timing differences and errors may exist between accounting records and the bank  
D. Reconciliation changes every journal  
**Answer: C**

32. A R4,000 rent payment is mistakenly posted to Stationery Expense but Bank is credited correctly. What is true?  
A. The trial balance must fail  
B. Sales are understated  
C. The trial balance may still balance even though classification is wrong  
D. Bank becomes a liability  
**Answer: C**
