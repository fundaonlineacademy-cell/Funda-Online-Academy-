# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 3: Journals, Ledgers & Trial Balance
### Lesson 3: Posting from Journals to Ledger Accounts

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of posting.
2. Transfer simple journal entries into ledger accounts.
3. Use posting references to maintain an audit trail.
4. Distinguish individual postings from batch/control totals.
5. Check that journal-to-ledger posting is complete and balanced.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A journal entry records the debit and credit logic of a transaction. Posting places those amounts into the relevant ledger accounts so balances can be calculated. If a transaction is recorded in the journal but not posted correctly, the final ledger and reports will be incomplete or wrong.

In computerised systems posting may happen automatically when a transaction is saved. The underlying logic still matters because a bookkeeper must be able to diagnose errors, understand audit reports and confirm that the correct accounts received the entry.

## Key Terms
- **Posting** — Transferring journal information to ledger accounts.
- **Posting reference** — Cross-reference linking journal and ledger records.
- **Batch** — Group of transactions processed together.
- **Control total** — Independent total used to check completeness of processing.
- **Contra entry** — Corresponding entry on the opposite side of another account in a double-entry transaction.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. From one journal entry to two ledger effects
Suppose the cash-payments journal records Rent R4,000:
- Debit Rent Expense R4,000.
- Credit Bank R4,000.

Posting means entering R4,000 on the debit side of Rent Expense and R4,000 on the credit side of Bank.

The same journal reference should make the relationship traceable.

### 2. Use references consistently
A posting reference might be `CPJ-0826-014` or another format required by the organisation.

The ledger entry should show enough information to trace back to the journal or source evidence. In a digital accounting package, this may be provided by a transaction ID, document number and system audit log.

### 3. Posting a credit sale
Customer invoice INV-450 records R5,750 including the relevant transaction components. Ignoring VAT for this simplified posting example:
- Debit Debtors R5,750.
- Credit Sales R5,750.

The customer subledger may also record the invoice against the individual customer while the general ledger contains the debtor-control total.

### 4. Posting creditor transactions
A supplier invoice for R3,600 office cleaning on credit:
- Debit Cleaning Expense R3,600.
- Credit Creditors R3,600.

When payment is made later:
- Debit Creditors R3,600.
- Credit Bank R3,600.

These are separate entries. The payment settles the liability rather than recording the cleaning expense a second time.

### 5. Batch posting and control totals
Manual systems may post a journal total to a general-ledger control account while individual items update subsidiary accounts.

Example: five customer invoices total R42,000.
- Individual invoices update five customer accounts.
- The total R42,000 may update Debtors Control and Sales through the journal/posting process.

The sum of individual customer movements should agree with the control-account movement.

### 6. Completeness checks
After posting, check:
- every journal line has been posted;
- amounts agree with the journal;
- debit was not posted as credit;
- correct account was used;
- references are present;
- batch/control totals agree.

A single omitted posting can cause a subsidiary ledger or control account to differ.

### 7. Transposition errors
A common error is entering R5,460 as R5,640. This may be detected through comparison to source records or control totals.

If one side of a manual double entry is posted incorrectly while the other is correct, the trial balance may also fail by the difference.

### 8. Wrong-account errors
If both debit and credit values are correct but the debit goes to the wrong expense account, the trial balance may still balance.

Example:
- Correct: Debit Insurance R2,000 / Credit Bank R2,000.
- Wrong: Debit Stationery R2,000 / Credit Bank R2,000.

Total debits still equal credits, but expense classification is wrong. This is why trial-balance equality is only one control.

### 9. Automated posting
In software, an invoice screen can generate multiple underlying ledger entries automatically. The bookkeeper should understand:
- which account mapping the system uses;
- whether tax codes are correct;
- whether customer/supplier control accounts update;
- whether the posting date is correct.

Automation reduces repetitive work but can repeat a setup error across many transactions.

## Worked Example
Cash Receipts Journal contains:
- Customer A R4,000.
- Customer B R6,000.
Total R10,000.

Posting:
- Debit Bank R10,000.
- Credit Debtors Control R10,000.
- Credit Customer A R4,000 in the customer subledger.
- Credit Customer B R6,000 in the customer subledger.

The customer-detail total R10,000 should agree to the R10,000 reduction in Debtors Control.

## Practical Activity
Post these journal entries into simple T-accounts:
1. Debit Rent R3,500 / Credit Bank R3,500.
2. Debit Debtors R7,000 / Credit Sales R7,000.
3. Debit Bank R4,000 / Credit Debtors R4,000.
4. Debit Stationery R2,200 / Credit Creditors R2,200.
5. Debit Creditors R1,200 / Credit Bank R1,200.

Add a reference to each posting and calculate the account movements.

## Key Takeaways
- Posting moves journal information into account balances.
- References preserve traceability.
- Batch and control totals help test completeness.
- Wrong-account errors may not disturb trial-balance equality.
- Automated posting still depends on correct configuration and review.
