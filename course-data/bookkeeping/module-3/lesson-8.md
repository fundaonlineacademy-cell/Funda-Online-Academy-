# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 3: Journals, Ledgers & Trial Balance
### Lesson 8: Manual Books-to-Trial-Balance Practical

**Estimated study time:** 60–70 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Process a small set of transactions from source evidence into journals.
2. Post journal entries to ledger accounts.
3. Calculate closing ledger balances.
4. Prepare a simple trial balance.
5. Investigate basic posting and extraction differences.

## Why This Lesson Matters
This lesson combines the core manual bookkeeping skills from Module 3. The aim is to understand the accounting flow well enough that software later becomes a tool rather than a mystery.

A bookkeeper who can trace a transaction from source document to journal, ledger and trial balance is better able to diagnose errors in any accounting system.

## Practical Scenario
A small office-services business begins the month with:
- Bank R20,000 debit
- Equipment R15,000 debit
- Creditors R5,000 credit
- Owner Capital R30,000 credit

During the month, it completes these transactions:
1. Credit sale to Customer A: R8,000.
2. Customer A pays R5,000 by EFT.
3. Supplier invoice for stationery: R2,500 on credit.
4. Supplier paid R3,000 against an existing creditor balance.
5. Rent paid by EFT: R4,000.
6. Owner adds R6,000 capital.

For this practical exercise, ignore VAT so you can focus on the journal-ledger-trial-balance flow.

## Step 1: Analyse each transaction
### Transaction 1 — Credit sale R8,000
- Debit Debtors R8,000.
- Credit Sales R8,000.

### Transaction 2 — Customer receipt R5,000
- Debit Bank R5,000.
- Credit Debtors R5,000.

### Transaction 3 — Stationery invoice R2,500
- Debit Stationery Expense R2,500.
- Credit Creditors R2,500.

### Transaction 4 — Supplier payment R3,000
- Debit Creditors R3,000.
- Credit Bank R3,000.

### Transaction 5 — Rent payment R4,000
- Debit Rent Expense R4,000.
- Credit Bank R4,000.

### Transaction 6 — Owner capital R6,000
- Debit Bank R6,000.
- Credit Owner Capital R6,000.

## Step 2: Post to ledger balances
### Bank
Opening balance R20,000 Dr  
+ Customer receipt R5,000 Dr  
- Supplier payment R3,000 Cr  
- Rent payment R4,000 Cr  
+ Owner capital R6,000 Dr

Closing Bank = R24,000 Dr.

### Debtors
+ Credit sale R8,000 Dr  
- Customer receipt R5,000 Cr

Closing Debtors = R3,000 Dr.

### Creditors
Opening R5,000 Cr  
+ New supplier invoice R2,500 Cr  
- Payment R3,000 Dr

Closing Creditors = R4,500 Cr.

### Equipment
Opening R15,000 Dr; no movement.  
Closing Equipment = R15,000 Dr.

### Stationery Expense
R2,500 Dr.

### Rent Expense
R4,000 Dr.

### Sales
R8,000 Cr.

### Owner Capital
Opening R30,000 Cr + R6,000 Cr = R36,000 Cr.

## Step 3: Prepare the trial balance
Debit balances:
- Bank R24,000
- Debtors R3,000
- Equipment R15,000
- Stationery Expense R2,500
- Rent Expense R4,000

Total debits = R48,500.

Credit balances:
- Creditors R4,500
- Sales R8,000
- Owner Capital R36,000

Total credits = R48,500.

The trial balance agrees.

## Step 4: Perform reasonableness checks
Agreement does not end the review. Ask:
- Does the R3,000 debtor balance match Customer A's remaining amount?
- Does the R4,500 creditor balance reconcile to supplier detail?
- Does Bank later reconcile to the bank statement?
- Is every source document linked to the journal or transaction reference?

## Step 5: Investigate an error scenario
Assume the trial balance instead showed Bank R24,900 because the closing balance was copied incorrectly.

Debit total would exceed credit total by R900. Before changing any ledger entry, compare the trial balance to the ledger. If the ledger itself shows R24,000, the problem is an extraction error—not a transaction error.

Correct the trial-balance figure rather than posting a false R900 journal.

## Step 6: Investigate a balanced classification error
Assume the R4,000 rent payment was posted as Stationery Expense instead of Rent Expense:
- Debit Stationery R4,000.
- Credit Bank R4,000.

The trial balance would still balance. The error would be discovered through account review, budgeting, source-document checking or reasonableness analysis.

The correction would move R4,000 from Stationery to Rent using the approved correction process.

## Practical Activity
Repeat the full workflow using these transactions:
Opening balances:
- Bank R12,000 Dr
- Equipment R8,000 Dr
- Creditors R4,000 Cr
- Capital R16,000 Cr

Transactions:
1. Credit sale R6,000.
2. Customer pays R2,500.
3. Supplier invoice for cleaning R1,500.
4. Supplier payment R2,000.
5. Telephone expense paid R900.
6. Owner contributes R3,000.

Prepare:
- journal entries;
- ledger balances;
- trial balance;
- one control check for each major balance.

## Knowledge Check
1. What is the first step before journal entry preparation?
2. What happens to Debtors when a credit sale is recorded?
3. What happens to Debtors when the customer pays?
4. Why does paying a creditor not create the expense again?
5. What should total trial-balance debits equal?
6. If the trial balance differs by an amount caused by a copied balance, should a ledger journal be posted?
7. Can a wrong expense account still leave the trial balance balanced?
8. What checks should follow trial-balance preparation?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Analyse the transaction and source evidence.
2. Debtors increases with a debit.
3. Debtors decreases with a credit while Bank increases.
4. The original supplier invoice already created the expense/asset and liability.
5. Total trial-balance credits.
6. No. Correct the extraction if the ledger itself is right.
7. Yes.
8. Reconciliation, source-document tracing and reasonableness review.

## Key Takeaways
- Manual bookkeeping follows a clear flow from transaction analysis to trial balance.
- Posting must preserve equal debit and credit effects.
- Ledger balances should be independently checked.
- Trial-balance differences require investigation, not forced journals.
- A balanced trial balance can still contain classification errors.
