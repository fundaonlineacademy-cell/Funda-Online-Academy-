# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 6: Adjustments & Basic Financial Reporting
### Lesson 5: Adjusted Trial Balance and Closing Checks

**Estimated study time:** 50–55 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of an adjusted trial balance.
2. Post approved adjustments and recalculate ledger balances.
3. Perform closing checks before preparing reports.
4. Identify unresolved suspense or reconciliation items.
5. Distinguish arithmetic agreement from accounting accuracy.

## Why This Lesson Matters
The adjusted trial balance is the bridge between bookkeeping records and financial reports. It includes period-end adjustments such as accruals, prepayments and depreciation. Before reports are prepared, the bookkeeper should confirm that balances are supported, reconciled and ready for use.

## Key Terms
- **Adjusted trial balance** — Trial balance after approved period-end adjustments.
- **Closing check** — Review performed before reports are finalised.
- **Suspense account** — Temporary account used under controlled circumstances for unresolved differences.
- **Reconciliation status** — Evidence showing whether a balance agrees to supporting records.
- **Material exception** — Difference or issue significant enough to require attention before reporting.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start from the unadjusted trial balance
Confirm the initial trial balance is complete and agrees arithmetically.

### 2. Post approved adjustments
Examples:
- accruals;
- prepayments;
- depreciation;
- bad-debt adjustments;
- reclassifications;
- cut-off corrections.

Each needs a clear journal reference and support.

### 3. Recalculate balances
After posting, extract a fresh trial balance. Total debits should still equal total credits.

### 4. Reconcile major accounts
Typical checks include:
- Bank to bank reconciliation;
- Debtors Control to customer balances;
- Creditors Control to supplier balances;
- VAT/control accounts to supporting schedules where applicable;
- fixed assets to asset register;
- accruals and prepayments to schedules.

### 5. Review suspense
A suspense account should not become a permanent home for unexplained items. Investigate material balances and clear them through approved entries where evidence permits.

### 6. Reasonableness review
Compare balances with prior periods, budgets or expected activity. Unexpected movements do not automatically mean error, but they should be explainable.

### 7. Check account direction
A normal debit account showing an unusual credit balance may indicate overpayment, reversal or misclassification. Investigate rather than automatically changing it.

### 8. Confirm period cut-off
Review transactions around period end for invoices, receipts and payments recorded in the correct period.

### 9. Lock or close the period
Where software supports period locking, authorised closure can prevent accidental backdated changes after reports are finalised. Follow organisation policy.

## Worked Example
Unadjusted trial balance agrees at R450,000 each side. Adjustments include:
- Accrued electricity R2,000: Dr Expense / Cr Accrual.
- Prepaid insurance R6,000: Dr Prepayment / Cr Insurance Expense.
- Depreciation R8,000: Dr Depreciation / Cr Accumulated Depreciation.

Total debits and credits remain equal because each journal is balanced. The adjusted trial balance is then reviewed against schedules.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. The trial balance after approved period-end adjustments.
2. To confirm updated balances and continued debit-credit agreement.
3. Examples: Bank, Debtors, Creditors, VAT, fixed assets, accruals and prepayments.
4. They represent unresolved items that can distort reports.
5. No. Classification, omission and valuation errors can still exist.
6. Comparing balances to expected patterns, prior periods or operational information.
7. They may reveal misclassification, overpayment or reversal problems.
8. To prevent unauthorised or accidental changes to a closed reporting period.

## Key Takeaways
- The adjusted trial balance contains the final approved period-end entries.
- Reconciliations and support schedules are essential before reporting.
- Suspense and unusual balances need explanation.
- Arithmetic agreement is necessary but not sufficient.
- Closing controls protect the integrity of final reports.
