# Professional Certificate in Bookkeeping
## Module 6: Adjustments & Basic Financial Reporting
### Lesson 3: Depreciation and Asset-record Basics

**Estimated study time:** 55–60 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain why depreciation is recorded.
2. Distinguish asset cost, accumulated depreciation and carrying amount.
3. Calculate simple straight-line depreciation.
4. Record a basic depreciation journal.
5. Maintain essential fixed-asset register information.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Long-term assets such as computers, furniture and vehicles usually provide benefit over more than one period. Depreciation allocates the depreciable amount over the asset's useful life instead of treating the entire cost as one period's expense, subject to the organisation's accounting policy.

## Key Terms
- **Fixed asset / Property, plant and equipment** — Long-term tangible resource used in the business.
- **Depreciation** — Systematic allocation of a depreciable asset amount over its useful life.
- **Useful life** — Period over which the asset is expected to provide benefit for accounting purposes.
- **Residual value** — Estimated value remaining at the end of useful life, where applicable.
- **Accumulated depreciation** — Total depreciation recorded on an asset to date.
- **Carrying amount** — Asset cost less accumulated depreciation and other applicable adjustments.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Asset versus expense
A low-value item may be expensed immediately under policy, while a material item expected to provide future benefit may be capitalised. Bookkeepers should follow the approved capitalisation policy rather than invent thresholds.

### 2. Depreciation is not a cash payment
Depreciation is an accounting expense. The cash outflow usually occurred when the asset was bought. Depreciation allocates the asset's cost over periods of use.

### 3. Straight-line method
A simple straight-line calculation is:

**(Cost - Residual Value) ÷ Useful Life**

Example: Equipment costs R60,000, residual value R0, useful life 5 years.
Annual depreciation = R60,000 ÷ 5 = **R12,000**.

### 4. Journal entry
A common entry is:
- Debit Depreciation Expense R12,000.
- Credit Accumulated Depreciation R12,000.

The asset cost remains visible while accumulated depreciation grows separately.

### 5. Carrying amount
After two years at R12,000 per year:
- Cost R60,000
- Accumulated depreciation R24,000
- Carrying amount R36,000.

### 6. Partial periods
If an asset is acquired partway through a year, depreciation may be apportioned according to the organisation's accounting policy. Use the approved method consistently.

### 7. Fixed-asset register
A useful register can include:
- asset ID;
- description;
- purchase date;
- supplier/invoice;
- cost;
- location/custodian;
- useful life/rate/method;
- accumulated depreciation;
- carrying amount;
- disposal details.

### 8. Physical verification
Periodically compare the register to actual assets. Missing, damaged or disposed items require investigation and authorised accounting treatment.

### 9. Policy and judgement
Useful lives, residual values and capitalisation decisions can require accounting judgement. Bookkeepers should prepare data and calculations but follow the accountant or authorised policy.

## Worked Example
Computer equipment costs R36,000 with a three-year straight-line life and no residual value.
Annual depreciation = R12,000.
After 18 months, if policy apportions monthly:
R12,000 × 18/12 = R18,000 accumulated depreciation.
Carrying amount = R18,000.

## Key Takeaways
- Depreciation allocates asset cost over useful periods.
- Asset cost and accumulated depreciation are shown separately.
- Straight-line depreciation uses a consistent periodic charge.
- Asset registers support calculation, custody and audit trails.
- Accounting policies determine useful lives, methods and capitalisation rules.
