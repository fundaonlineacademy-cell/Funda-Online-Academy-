# Funda Online Academy — Premium Course Overview Framework

## Purpose

The public Course Overview page is both the Academy's sales page and the academic front door for a course. A prospective learner must be able to understand the programme before creating an account or paying, while protected lesson teaching, learner activities, assessment banks and restricted resources remain behind approved course access.

Retail Management & Team Leadership is the flagship reference implementation for this framework.

## Required public-page sequence

1. Premium Course Hero / Introduction
2. Course at a Glance
3. About This Short Course
4. Who This Course Is For
5. What You Will Learn
6. Course Curriculum
7. How You Will Learn
8. Assessment & Academic Requirements
9. Practical & Workplace Application
10. Learner Support
11. Certificate of Completion
12. Important Course Classification
13. Course Fee & Enrolment

## Non-negotiable content rules

- The visual structure may be reused across courses, but the academic and marketing copy must be written specifically for the subject.
- Do not reuse a generic introduction, outcomes, learner profile or workplace-value paragraph simply by changing the course name.
- Public curriculum information should show programme structure and meaningful module descriptions without exposing paid lesson teaching or assessment-bank content.
- Workplace application must be realistic and subject-specific. Do not promise employment, promotion, salary increases or appointment to a particular role.
- Where a programme is a non-qualification short course, say so clearly. Do not imply NQF registration, accreditation, occupational licensing or qualification status unless the Academy has verified authority to make that exact claim.
- Certificate language should use "Certificate of Completion" and tie eligibility to applicable learning and assessment requirements.
- Fees must be transparent before registration or payment.

## Academic structure conventions

The Academy's standard module pattern is:

- 8 substantive teaching lessons per module
- Formative Assessment after the teaching lessons
- Summative Assessment after the formative stage

Therefore a module normally contains 10 learner-facing units: 8 lessons + 2 assessment units. The number of modules may vary by course according to subject depth; module count is not required to be identical across all courses.

## Presentation standard

- Premium navy/gold visual foundation with restrained supporting accent colours.
- Strong information hierarchy and readable typography.
- Short scan-friendly summaries supported by deeper detail below.
- Cards, expandable curriculum sections and deliberate spacing instead of long uninterrupted text walls.
- Mobile-first refinement: avoid duplicate fee/CTA surfaces, preserve readable line lengths, and make primary actions full-width where appropriate.
- Maintain keyboard focus states and meaningful labels for interactive controls.

## Flagship reference — Retail Management & Team Leadership

Public title: **Retail Management & Team Leadership — Advanced Professional Short Course**

Current reference facts:

- 8 management modules
- 64 teaching lessons
- 8 lessons per module
- formative and summative assessment after each module
- R3,900 course fee
- 8-week planned duration
- non-qualification professional short course classification

The Retail implementation is the benchmark to inspect and approve before the framework is rolled out to the remaining catalogue.
