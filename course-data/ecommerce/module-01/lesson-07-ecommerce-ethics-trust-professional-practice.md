# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 1: E-commerce Foundations & Digital Business Models
### Lesson 7: E-commerce Ethics, Trust & Professional Practice

**Estimated study time:** 40–45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:
1. explain why ethical conduct is commercially important in e-commerce;
2. identify misleading or unfair online selling practices;
3. distinguish ethical principles from specific legal obligations;
4. apply professional standards to product information, marketing and customer communication; and
5. respond appropriately when the business makes an error.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Online commerce depends heavily on trust because customers often pay before receiving a product. A seller's descriptions, promises, prices, policies and communications therefore carry significant responsibility. Ethical practice protects customers, strengthens reputation and reduces avoidable disputes. It also prepares learners to understand the more detailed compliance requirements taught later in the course.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Business ethics** — principles used to judge responsible and fair business conduct.
- **Transparency** — providing material information clearly enough for people to make informed decisions.
- **Misrepresentation** — presenting false or misleading information that creates an inaccurate impression.
- **Disclosure** — making relevant information known to a customer before or during a transaction where appropriate.
- **Conflict of interest** — a situation in which personal or competing interests may improperly influence a business decision.
- **Dark pattern** — interface design that manipulates or misleads users into choices they may not otherwise make.
- **Accountability** — accepting responsibility for decisions, records, corrections and outcomes within one's role.

## Main Lesson Content
Ethics asks what a responsible business should do, while law establishes enforceable requirements. The two overlap but are not identical. A practice can damage trust even when a learner cannot immediately identify a specific legal provision. Professional e-commerce therefore requires both legal compliance and sound judgement.

Truthful product representation is fundamental. Titles, descriptions, photographs, specifications and availability information should create an accurate overall impression. A seller should not describe a used item as new, enlarge an image to imply a quantity that is not supplied, omit a material limitation that would influence a reasonable purchase decision, or claim performance that cannot be supported. Where images are illustrative, that should be clear when necessary to avoid misunderstanding.

Pricing communication must also be transparent. Customers should be able to understand what they will actually be expected to pay and what is included. Artificially inflating a reference price merely to advertise an impressive discount can mislead. So can hiding unavoidable charges until late in checkout. Promotional conditions, eligibility, expiry and stock limitations should be communicated accurately.

Scarcity and urgency require care. Genuine limited stock or a real promotion deadline can be useful information. False countdown timers, fabricated "only one left" messages, or claims that many people are viewing an item when the business has no basis for the statement manipulate decision-making. Short-term conversion achieved through deception can create complaints, refunds and reputational damage.

Customer reviews and social proof must be handled honestly. Businesses should not invent reviews, disguise paid endorsements as independent opinions, selectively alter the meaning of customer comments, or pressure staff to pose as ordinary buyers. Where an endorsement has a commercial relationship that should be disclosed under applicable rules or platform requirements, the business must follow those requirements.

Professional communication matters when mistakes happen. Examples include selling stock that is unavailable, sending the wrong item, publishing an incorrect specification or missing a promised delivery window. Concealing the problem usually increases risk. A professional response records what happened, informs the affected customer appropriately, offers lawful and reasonable remedies, corrects the underlying information and investigates how recurrence can be prevented.

Respect for personal information is another ethical obligation and also a legal compliance area. An online store may receive names, contact details, addresses, transaction information and support records. Staff should access information only for legitimate work purposes and should not casually share customer information. Detailed privacy and security obligations are addressed later in the course, including South African requirements where applicable.

The Consumer Protection Act provides a national framework intended to promote fair markets, improved consumer information and protection against unfair marketing practices. Electronic transactions are also governed by the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act, which contains specific consumer-protection provisions for online transactions. Learners should understand these as real operational responsibilities, not merely theory. Exact legal decisions should rely on current legislation, regulations and competent advice where required. 

Ethical design includes giving customers meaningful control. A store should not make a purchase easy while intentionally making cancellation, opting out or accessing important terms confusing. Consent should not be manufactured through deceptive interface choices. Forms should request information that has a legitimate purpose rather than collecting data simply because the technology allows it.

Professional practice also applies internally. Staff should follow documented procedures, maintain accurate records, escalate issues outside their authority, protect access credentials and disclose conflicts of interest. If a staff member is offered a personal benefit to favour a supplier, for example, the matter should be handled according to the business's conflict-of-interest rules rather than hidden.

Trust accumulates through consistency. A polished website cannot compensate indefinitely for inaccurate orders, poor communication or unfair treatment. Conversely, a smaller store can build strong trust by being clear about what it offers, keeping promises, correcting mistakes and treating customer information responsibly. Reputation is an operational outcome, not only a marketing asset.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
An online appliance seller accidentally publishes a product as including an accessory that is not actually supplied. After orders are placed, staff notice the error. An unethical response would silently edit the page and hope customers do not notice. A professional response is to preserve a record of the issue, correct the listing, identify affected orders, communicate transparently, apply the business's lawful remedy process and investigate why the incorrect specification passed quality control.

## Practical Activity
Audit a sample online-store page. Identify at least eight trust or ethics issues to check, covering product claims, pricing, urgency, reviews, customer data, policies, contact information and interface design. For each issue, write the potential customer harm and the corrective action.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why are ethics and legal compliance related but not identical?
2. Give two examples of misleading scarcity or urgency.
3. Why should a business keep a record when correcting a material listing error?
4. What makes a customer review practice unethical?
5. What is accountability in an e-commerce role?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Law sets enforceable duties while ethics also guides responsible judgement beyond a specific rule. 2. Examples include a fake countdown or unsupported "only one left" claim. 3. Records support accountability, customer remediation and process improvement. 4. Fabrication, deceptive editing or undisclosed manipulation undermines authenticity. 5. It means accepting responsibility for decisions, records, escalation and corrective action within one's role.

## Key Takeaways
- Accurate representation and transparent communication are foundations of online trust.
- Never manufacture urgency, reviews, discounts or claims.
- Treat customer information responsibly and only for legitimate purposes.
- Correct mistakes openly and investigate their cause.
- Professional e-commerce combines ethical judgement with current legal compliance.
