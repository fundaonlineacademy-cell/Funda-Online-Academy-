# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 5: Customer Service & Returns
### Lesson 4: Returns, Exchanges & Reverse Logistics

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the operational stages of a product return.
2. Distinguish between return, exchange and refund outcomes.
3. Identify why returned goods require condition checks.
4. Apply a controlled reverse-logistics workflow.
5. Record return reasons for operational improvement.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A return reverses part of the normal order journey. The product may travel from the customer back to the business, be inspected, restocked, repaired, replaced, quarantined or written off. Money may also need to be refunded. If these activities are not linked correctly, the store can refund the wrong amount, restore damaged goods to available stock or lose track of returned items.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Return** — Goods sent back by the customer under an applicable return process.
- **Exchange** — Replacement of one item with another approved item.
- **Reverse logistics** — Movement and processing of goods from the customer back to the business or another approved destination.
- **Return authorisation** — Approval or reference used to control a return where the business process requires it.
- **Condition check** — Inspection of returned goods to determine their state and next action.
- **Restock** — Return of suitable goods to available inventory.
- **Quarantine** — Separation of goods that should not be returned to saleable stock until reviewed.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start with the reason for return
Return reasons may include wrong item, incorrect size, damage, defect, changed mind, unsuitable product or delivery error. The reason matters because it can affect logistics, responsibility for return costs and the appropriate resolution.

### 2. Link the return to the original order
Staff should identify the order, item, SKU, quantity, payment and previous service history. A return should not be processed against a similar-looking product or the wrong customer transaction.

### 3. Control the reverse movement
Where goods must physically return, the business should explain the approved return method and required packaging or handover steps. Tracking or proof of handover may be useful for valuable items.

### 4. Inspect returned goods
Receiving a parcel does not automatically mean the product is saleable. Staff should verify the item, quantity, condition and accessories. Goods may be restocked, quarantined, repaired, returned to a supplier or written off depending on the reason and applicable rules.

### 5. Keep refund and inventory controls separate
A refund changes the financial record. A stock adjustment changes inventory. These actions may occur at different times and should not be confused. A refunded item should not automatically be added back to saleable stock before it is physically received and checked where a return is required.

### 6. Record return reasons consistently
Standard return-reason codes help identify patterns. A high rate of “wrong size” returns may indicate sizing information problems. Repeated “item arrived damaged” returns may indicate packaging or courier issues.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A customer returns a pair of shoes because the wrong size was shipped. Staff match the return to the original order and SKU, receive the parcel, confirm the shoes are unused and the packaging is intact, correct the inventory record and process the approved exchange. The return reason is recorded as a fulfilment error so the warehouse can investigate the picking process.

## Practical Activity
Design a return workflow from customer request to final closure. Include order verification, return method, receipt, condition check, inventory action, financial action and reason coding.

5. How can return reasons improve the business?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. It is the controlled movement and processing of goods back from the customer.
2. It ensures the correct item, payment and customer record are used.
3. Returned goods may not be suitable for resale.
4. Money and physical stock are separate records and may require different actions.
5. Patterns can reveal product, fulfilment, packaging or information problems.

## Key Takeaways
- Returns require both customer-service and operational control.
- Returned goods should be traced to the original transaction.
- Condition checks protect inventory accuracy and product quality.
- Refunds and stock adjustments should be controlled separately.
- Return data can reveal recurring business weaknesses.
