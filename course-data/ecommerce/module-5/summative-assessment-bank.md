## Questions 1–6 — Lesson 1: Customer Service Foundations in E-commerce

### Q1
What is the main purpose of customer service in e-commerce?
A. To support customers before, during and after transactions
B. To replace all website content
C. To set tax rates
D. To operate courier vehicles

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Customer service supports the full online shopping journey.

### Q2
A customer asks whether a charger fits a specific phone model. What should staff do?
A. Guess based on appearance
B. Verify compatibility using approved product information
C. Tell the customer to buy and test it
D. Ignore the question

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Pre-purchase support should be based on accurate product information.

### Q3
Why is a case record important when more than one staff member may handle the same customer?
A. It supports continuity and prevents unnecessary repetition
B. It changes the refund value
C. It replaces the order number
D. It increases product stock

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Case history helps staff understand prior actions and commitments.

### Q4
A customer says a parcel is late. Which response is strongest?
A. “The courier is useless.”
B. “I have checked your tracking and will investigate the stalled shipment. I will update you by tomorrow afternoon.”
C. “It will definitely arrive today.”
D. “Not our problem.”

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** It is factual, professional and gives a realistic next step.

### Q5
Which action best protects customer information?
A. Access only information needed for the support task through approved systems
B. Share order screenshots in a public group
C. Publish addresses in review responses
D. Copy customer data into personal notes

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Customer information should be accessed and shared only as necessary and authorised.

### Q6
When should staff escalate a case?
A. When the issue requires authority or expertise beyond their role
B. Every time a customer asks a question
C. Only after deleting the case record
D. Never

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Escalation is appropriate when risk, authority or expertise requires it.

## Questions 7–12 — Lesson 2: Communication Channels, Response Standards & Service Tone

### Q7
What is the main risk of offering too many service channels without enough staff?
A. Channels may become inconsistent or unattended
B. Product prices automatically increase
C. Payment gateways stop working
D. SKUs duplicate

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Every service channel requires monitoring and ownership.

### Q8
What should a meaningful first response do?
A. Acknowledge the issue and provide useful next steps where possible
B. Send only an automated emoji
C. Promise immediate resolution regardless of facts
D. Close the case

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** First responses should help the customer understand what happens next.

### Q9
Why should public social-media support avoid discussing payment or address details?
A. Those details are personal and should be handled privately
B. They affect product categories
C. They reduce website traffic
D. They change refund rules

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Public channels are inappropriate for sensitive order information.

### Q10
Which service tone is most appropriate?
A. Clear, respectful and factual
B. Defensive and sarcastic
C. Aggressive and vague
D. Highly technical regardless of customer understanding

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Professional service communication should reduce confusion and conflict.

### Q11
A template says “Your refund of R1,200 is complete,” but the actual case is R850 and still pending. What should staff do?
A. Send the template unchanged
B. Verify and adapt the message to the actual case
C. Increase the refund to R1,200
D. Delete the order

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Templates must never replace case verification.

### Q12
What supports continuity when a customer moves from chat to email?
A. Accurate case notes and order references
B. Asking the customer to restart every time
C. Deleting the chat history
D. Using a different customer name

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Shared records reduce repetition and preserve context.

## Questions 19–24 — Lesson 4: Returns, Exchanges & Reverse Logistics

### Q19
A return arrives at the warehouse. What should happen before the item is added to saleable stock?
A. Verify the item and inspect its condition
B. Restock automatically
C. Refund twice
D. Remove the SKU

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Returned goods require physical verification and condition control.

### Q20
What is an exchange?
A. Replacement of one item with another approved item
B. A payment failure
C. A product review
D. A delivery zone

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** An exchange replaces the returned item with another approved item.

### Q21
Why should a return reason be coded consistently?
A. To enable trend analysis
B. To make all customers receive the same outcome
C. To avoid inspection
D. To change product titles

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Consistent return reasons make recurring problems visible.

### Q22
A refunded item has not yet been physically received by the business. Should it automatically be restored to available stock?
A. Yes
B. No
C. Only if expensive
D. Only if the customer asks

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Refund and inventory status are separate controls.

### Q23
A product is returned damaged and missing accessories. What should staff do?
A. Follow the approved condition and inventory process
B. Mark it new
C. Ignore the missing parts
D. Sell it immediately without review

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** The item's condition determines the correct inventory action.

### Q24
A store receives many “wrong item” returns. What operational area should be investigated first?
A. Picking and packing accuracy
B. Website font size only
C. Social-media followers
D. Domain registration

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Wrong-item returns often indicate fulfilment-control problems.

## Questions 25–32 — Lesson 5: South African Consumer Rights, Cooling-off & Defective Goods

### Q25
Which statement is correct?
A. A store policy can remove all statutory consumer rights
B. Applicable consumer law may provide rights beyond store policy
C. Online retailers do not need return policies
D. Every return is governed only by courier rules

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Store policy must operate consistently with applicable law.

### Q26
For qualifying goods transactions under ECTA section 44, the general cooling-off period runs for:
A. 2 days after order placement
B. 5 business days after payment
C. 7 days after receipt of the goods
D. 30 days after dispatch

**Correct answer:** C
**Rationale:** ECTA provides seven days after receipt for qualifying goods transactions.

### Q27
For qualifying services under ECTA section 44, the general cooling-off period is measured from:
A. The date of conclusion of the agreement
B. The date of first complaint
C. The date of refund
D. The date of review publication

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** The section measures qualifying service cancellation from conclusion of the agreement.

### Q28
Which type of goods may fall within an ECTA cooling-off exclusion?
A. Clearly personalised goods
B. Every standard clothing item
C. Every electronic product
D. Every book

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** ECTA includes exclusions for certain personalised goods and other categories.

### Q29
Under the CPA implied warranty of quality, what general period is particularly important for qualifying defective goods?
A. Six months after delivery
B. Seven years
C. One day
D. Fourteen calendar days only

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** The CPA provides a six-month period linked to the implied warranty of quality.

### Q30
A customer reports a laptop defect two months after delivery. The store's voluntary change-of-mind policy is 14 days. What is the best approach?
A. Reject automatically because 14 days passed
B. Assess the issue under the applicable defective-goods process
C. Ignore the complaint
D. Treat it only as a delivery delay

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Defective-goods rights differ from voluntary change-of-mind returns.

### Q31
Why should staff identify whether goods are rapidly perishable?
A. It may affect whether certain cooling-off rights apply
B. It changes the order number
C. It determines website colour
D. It eliminates all consumer rights

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** ECTA contains exclusions for certain rapidly deteriorating goods.

### Q32
What is the safest action when a return has an unclear legal basis?
A. Guess
B. Escalate and verify the applicable rule
C. Reject every case
D. Approve every case

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Uncertain legal cases should be verified rather than improvised.

## Questions 45–50 — Lesson 8: Customer-service Metrics, Return Analysis & Continuous Improvement

### Q45
What does first-contact resolution measure?
A. Whether the issue is resolved during the first meaningful interaction without avoidable repeat contact
B. The first product sold
C. The fastest courier
D. The first refund of the day

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** It measures resolution efficiency, not merely response speed.

### Q46
A team replies to every email within two minutes but most customers must contact them three more times. What does this suggest?
A. Fast first response but weak resolution quality
B. Excellent service by definition
C. Perfect product quality
D. No backlog

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Speed alone does not prove the issue was resolved.

### Q47
A store has 1,000 orders and 80 returns under its chosen order-based measurement. What is the return rate?
A. 0.8%
B. 8%
C. 12.5%
D. 80%

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** 80 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 8%.

### Q48
Forty of 100 monthly returns are coded “wrong size.” What percentage of returns are wrong-size returns?
A. 4%
B. 25%
C. 40%
D. 60%

**Correct answer:** C
**Rationale:** 40 ÷ 100 × 100 = 40%.

### Q49
Customers frequently ask “Where is my order?” What is the best next step?
A. Investigate possible root causes such as dispatch delays, missing tracking or unclear delivery information
B. Assume customers are impatient
C. Remove customer service
D. Increase prices

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Repeated questions should trigger root-cause investigation.

### Q50
Which action best demonstrates evidence-based improvement?
A. Update size guidance after repeated wrong-size returns and then monitor whether the return pattern improves
B. Change the logo because returns increased
C. Delete return records
D. Stop measuring complaints

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Improvement should connect evidence, corrective action and re-measurement.

## Questions 51–52 — Integrated Whole-Module Scenarios

### Q51
A customer buys headphones online. Two months later one side stops working. The customer posts an angry public review and requests a refund. The store's voluntary change-of-mind policy is 14 days. What is the best response?
A. Reject immediately because 14 days passed
B. Protect personal details publicly, move the case to private support, verify the order and defect claim, assess it under the applicable defective-goods process, and provide the approved remedy or escalation
C. Argue publicly that the customer used the headphones incorrectly
D. Delete the review and case record

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** The response integrates privacy, service tone, evidence, statutory defective-goods awareness and controlled resolution.

### Q52
A store has rising returns. Data shows 35% are wrong size, 25% wrong item and 20% damaged in transit. Customers also complain that support replies quickly but gives inconsistent answers. What is the strongest improvement plan?
A. Focus only on response speed
B. Improve size information, investigate picking accuracy and packaging, standardise service guidance and case records, then monitor return and resolution metrics
C. Stop accepting returns
D. Increase all prices

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** The strongest response uses return analysis, root-cause thinking, operational correction and service-quality controls.
