# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 5: Customer Service & Returns
### Lesson 1: Customer Service Foundations in E-commerce

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the role of customer service across the e-commerce journey.
2. Identify common online customer-service needs.
3. Distinguish service quality from speed alone.
4. Apply a basic customer-service workflow.
5. Record customer interactions accurately and professionally.

## Why This Lesson Matters
In e-commerce, customers often cannot speak to a salesperson before buying and may never visit a physical store. When something goes wrong, customer service becomes one of the strongest signals of whether the business is trustworthy. A delayed parcel, incorrect product, failed refund or unanswered enquiry can quickly damage confidence even when the product itself is good.

Strong customer service is therefore not an after-sales extra. It supports customers before, during and after a transaction. It can reduce avoidable returns, improve repeat purchasing and help the business identify weaknesses in products, delivery or website information.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Customer service** — Assistance provided to customers before, during and after a transaction.
- **Service standard** — A defined expectation for how customer enquiries should be handled.
- **First response time** — The time between a customer enquiry and the first meaningful response.
- **Resolution** — The point at which a customer issue has been appropriately addressed.
- **Escalation** — Referring an issue to someone with greater authority or specialist knowledge.
- **Case record** — The documented history of a customer-service interaction.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Support the complete customer journey
Customer service can begin before purchase. Shoppers may ask about size, compatibility, stock, delivery or payment methods. After purchase, they may need tracking information, cancellation help, return instructions or support with a defective product.

Staff should therefore understand enough of the store's products, policies and operational systems to give accurate answers or know where to escalate the enquiry.

### 2. Balance speed with accuracy
Fast replies are valuable, but an incorrect answer can create a larger problem. Staff should use verified order, payment, stock and policy information rather than assumptions. If information is uncertain, it is better to investigate and provide a realistic update than to make a promise that may not be honoured.

### 3. Use a controlled service workflow
A practical customer-service process is:
1. Identify the customer and relevant order or enquiry.
2. Read the full message before responding.
3. Verify the facts in the approved systems.
4. Explain the next step clearly.
5. Escalate if authority or specialist support is required.
6. Record the interaction and outcome.
7. Follow up when the issue is not immediately resolved.

### 4. Communicate professionally
Good service communication is clear, respectful and specific. Avoid blaming the customer, courier or another department before the facts are known. Explain what has happened, what the business can do next and when the customer should expect an update.

### 5. Protect customer information
Customer-service records may contain names, contact details, addresses and order information. Staff should use approved channels and avoid exposing customer information to unauthorised people. Only information necessary for the support task should be accessed or shared.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A customer says, “My order never arrived.” Instead of replying that the courier lost it, the staff member checks the order number, verifies dispatch and tracking, confirms the latest courier scan and then explains the current position. If the parcel appears stalled beyond the expected delivery period, the issue is escalated for investigation and the customer is given a realistic update time.

## Practical Activity
Create a customer-service flow for three scenarios: a pre-purchase product question, a delayed delivery and a refund enquiry. For each, identify the information staff should verify, the response required and when escalation is necessary.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why is customer service important before purchase as well as after purchase?
2. Why can a fast but incorrect reply be harmful?
3. What should staff verify before answering an order-specific question?
4. What is escalation?
5. Why should customer interactions be recorded?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Customers may need information before deciding whether or what to buy.
2. Incorrect information can create false expectations and additional complaints.
3. Relevant order, payment, product, stock or delivery records should be checked.
4. Escalation means referring an issue to someone with appropriate authority or expertise.
5. Records support continuity, accountability and investigation.

## Key Takeaways
- Customer service supports the full e-commerce journey.
- Accuracy is as important as response speed.
- Staff should verify facts before making commitments.
- Clear escalation and case records improve consistency.
- Customer information should be handled carefully.
