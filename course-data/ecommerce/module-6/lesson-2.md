# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 6: Digital Marketing & Social Media
### Lesson 2: Brand Messaging, Content & Promotional Copy

**Estimated study time:** 30–35 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the role of consistent brand messaging.
2. Write customer-focused marketing content.
3. Distinguish useful promotional claims from misleading exaggeration.
4. Adapt content to different digital formats.
5. Apply a basic content-quality check before publication.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Marketing content shapes what customers expect from a business. Strong content helps the customer understand the offer, while weak or exaggerated content can create confusion and complaints. The same brand may communicate through product pages, email, short-form video, social posts and advertisements, but the core message should remain accurate and recognisable.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Brand message** — The consistent idea or promise a business communicates about its value.
- **Value proposition** — A clear explanation of why an offer is useful or different for the intended customer.
- **Call to action (CTA)** — A prompt asking the customer to take a next step.
- **Content pillar** — A recurring topic or theme used to organise marketing content.
- **Promotional claim** — A statement about the qualities, benefits, price or performance of an offer.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start with customer value
Good marketing copy answers: what is being offered, who it is for, what problem it solves and why the customer should care. A value proposition should be specific enough to be meaningful rather than relying only on words such as “amazing,” “best” or “premium.”

### 2. Keep claims supportable
Marketing should not promise facts that the business cannot verify. Claims about discounts, performance, quality, delivery or product features should be accurate. Promotional language can be persuasive without being deceptive.

### 3. Build useful content pillars
An e-commerce brand may use pillars such as product education, customer problems, demonstrations, behind-the-scenes content, seasonal ideas and customer stories. This creates variety without posting randomly.

### 4. Adapt to the format
A social caption may need a short hook and clear CTA. An email can explain more detail. A short video should make the key idea understandable quickly. The content should suit the channel without changing the underlying facts.

### 5. Check before publishing
Review spelling, links, prices, dates, product availability, claims and CTA destinations. Confirm that promotional content still matches the live store. An advert should not continue sending customers to an expired or unavailable offer.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
Weak copy: “Best headphones ever! 100% perfect sound!” Stronger copy: “Wireless over-ear headphones with active noise cancellation, up to the verified battery duration stated in the product specification, and a foldable design for travel.” The second version is more useful because it focuses on supportable features and customer value.

## Practical Activity
Create three pieces of content for one product: a social caption, a short email paragraph and a paid-ad headline. Keep the core value proposition consistent while adapting the format.

## Knowledge Check
1. What is a value proposition?
2. Why should promotional claims be supportable?
3. What is a content pillar?
4. Why should content be adapted to the channel?
5. Name three items to check before publication.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. A clear statement of customer value.
2. Unsupported claims can mislead customers and create trust or compliance problems.
3. A recurring topic used to organise content.
4. Different formats have different space, attention and interaction patterns.
5. Examples include price, links, dates, claims and product availability.

## Key Takeaways
- Marketing content should communicate clear customer value.
- Brand messages should remain consistent across channels.
- Claims must be accurate and supportable.
- Content pillars improve planning and variety.
- Every campaign asset should be checked against the live offer.
