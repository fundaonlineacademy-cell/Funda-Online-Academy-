# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 6: Digital Marketing & Social Media
### Lesson 5: Email, SMS & Direct Marketing in South Africa

**Estimated study time:** 40–45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the role of email and SMS in e-commerce marketing.
2. Distinguish transactional communication from direct marketing.
3. Identify important POPIA and CPA requirements relevant to electronic direct marketing.
4. Apply permission, identity and opt-out principles correctly.
5. Build a compliant basic email or SMS campaign workflow.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Email and SMS can be valuable for retention, product launches and promotions, but they involve personal information and direct communication. South African businesses must therefore consider privacy and consumer-protection rules rather than treating every customer contact detail as unrestricted marketing data.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Direct marketing** — Communication intended to promote or offer goods or services to a person.
- **Consent** — A voluntary, specific and informed indication of agreement where consent is the lawful basis required.
- **Existing customer** — A customer relationship that may support limited direct marketing under specific POPIA conditions.
- **Opt-out** — A mechanism allowing a person to object to or stop further marketing communications.
- **Transactional message** — A message primarily needed to operate or service an existing transaction, such as an order confirmation.
- **Marketing list** — A controlled set of contacts used for promotional communications.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Separate service messages from marketing
An order confirmation, delivery update or password-reset message serves an operational purpose. A message promoting a sale or product is direct marketing. The business should understand the purpose of each communication because marketing rules can differ from transactional communication.

### 2. Apply POPIA section 69 principles
POPIA regulates direct marketing by unsolicited electronic communications such as email and SMS. In broad terms, electronic direct marketing is prohibited unless the data subject has consented or the existing-customer conditions in section 69 are met. For existing customers, the contact details must have been obtained in the context of a sale, marketing must concern the business's own similar products or services, and the customer must have been given a reasonable free opportunity to object when the details were collected and in each marketing communication if they did not initially refuse.

A person whose consent is required may generally be approached only once to request that consent if they have not previously withheld it, using the prescribed process.

### 3. Identify the sender and provide a stop mechanism
Direct-marketing communications should identify the sender and provide contact details or another practical way for the recipient to request that the communications stop. Opt-out processes should not be hidden, unnecessarily difficult or charged to the customer.

### 4. Respect CPA direct-marketing rights
The CPA recognises consumers' rights to refuse or require discontinuation of direct marketing. Businesses should also consider restrictions on marketing contact times and any other applicable consumer-protection rules when designing campaigns.

### 5. Manage lists carefully
Marketing lists should record the source of the contact, relevant permission or customer relationship, opt-out status and campaign history where needed. Once a person has opted out, the suppression should be respected across connected marketing systems rather than re-importing the address later.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A customer buys running shoes and provides an email address during checkout. The store wants to promote similar sportswear later. Before using the address, the business checks whether the existing-customer conditions are satisfied and whether the customer received a clear opportunity to object. Each marketing email identifies the sender and provides a practical unsubscribe option.

## Practical Activity
Design a basic email-marketing compliance checklist covering contact source, purpose, consent or customer basis, similar products, sender identity, opt-out mechanism and suppression-list handling.

## Knowledge Check
1. What is the difference between a transactional message and direct marketing?
2. What two broad bases does POPIA section 69 recognise for electronic direct marketing?
3. What should each marketing message provide regarding opt-out?
4. Why should opt-out status be retained in a suppression system?
5. Why is buying an unknown contact list risky?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Transactional messages support an existing service; direct marketing promotes goods or services.
2. Consent, or qualifying existing-customer circumstances.
3. A practical, free method to request that marketing stop.
4. To prevent the person from being re-added accidentally.
5. The business may not have a lawful basis or reliable permission history for the contacts.

## Key Takeaways
- Email and SMS marketing involve privacy and consumer-protection responsibilities.
- Transactional and promotional messages should not be confused.
- POPIA section 69 places specific conditions on unsolicited electronic direct marketing.
- Opt-outs must be respected and operationally controlled.
- Marketing lists should have traceable, legitimate contact sources.
