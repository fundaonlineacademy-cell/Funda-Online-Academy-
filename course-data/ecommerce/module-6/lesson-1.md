# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 6: Digital Marketing & Social Media
### Lesson 1: Digital Marketing Strategy & the Customer Journey

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of digital marketing in e-commerce.
2. Map basic customer-journey stages from awareness to repeat purchase.
3. Match marketing objectives to appropriate tactics.
4. Distinguish traffic, engagement, conversion and retention goals.
5. Build a simple digital-marketing plan for an online store.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Digital marketing is not simply posting on social media. An online store needs a clear reason for each marketing activity. Some campaigns introduce the brand to new people, some help interested shoppers evaluate products, some encourage purchase and others bring previous customers back. Without clear objectives, a business may spend time and money without knowing whether the activity worked.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Digital marketing** — Marketing activity delivered through digital channels.
- **Customer journey** — The stages a customer moves through before, during and after purchase.
- **Awareness** — The stage where a potential customer first discovers the business or product.
- **Consideration** — The stage where a customer evaluates whether the offer suits their needs.
- **Conversion** — Completion of a desired action such as a purchase.
- **Retention** — Activity aimed at encouraging existing customers to return.
- **Campaign objective** — The specific result a marketing campaign is intended to achieve.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start with the business objective
A campaign should answer a business question. Is the store trying to reach new customers, sell a seasonal product, increase repeat purchases, clear selected stock or introduce a new category? The objective influences channel, message, budget and measurement.

### 2. Understand the customer journey
A basic journey can be represented as awareness, consideration, conversion and retention. A customer may first see a short video, later visit a product page, compare alternatives, purchase, receive follow-up communication and eventually buy again.

Customers do not always move through these stages in a straight line. They may leave and return several times or encounter the brand through multiple channels.

### 3. Match tactics to stages
Awareness tactics may include social content, search visibility and paid reach. Consideration can be supported through product education, reviews, comparison information and useful email content. Conversion tactics may include strong landing pages, relevant offers and low-friction checkout. Retention may involve post-purchase service, useful follow-up and permission-based marketing.

### 4. Define a target audience
Marketing should be based on a realistic customer need rather than vague assumptions. A useful audience profile can include the problem the customer is trying to solve, likely product interests, buying barriers and preferred channels. Avoid treating stereotypes as evidence.

### 5. Set measurable outcomes
A campaign objective should connect to a meaningful metric. Awareness may use reach or qualified traffic. Conversion may use completed purchases, conversion rate or revenue. Retention may use repeat-purchase measures. Metrics should fit the objective rather than being chosen only because they look impressive.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A South African online stationery store wants to increase back-to-school sales. The campaign objective is to drive qualified traffic to school-bundle pages and convert that traffic into orders. Social posts and paid ads create awareness, product bundles simplify consideration, and campaign landing pages support conversion. The team measures qualified visits, add-to-cart activity, orders and revenue.

## Practical Activity
Choose an imaginary online store and create a four-stage customer journey. For each stage, identify one customer need, one marketing tactic and one metric.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should a campaign start with an objective?
2. What is the difference between awareness and conversion?
3. Name two tactics that can support consideration.
4. Why are stereotypes a weak basis for audience planning?
5. What should determine the metric used for a campaign?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. The objective guides channel, message, budget and measurement.
2. Awareness introduces the business; conversion completes a desired action.
3. Examples include product education, reviews or comparison information.
4. They are assumptions rather than verified customer evidence.
5. The metric should reflect the campaign objective.

## Key Takeaways
- Digital marketing should support defined business objectives.
- Customers move through awareness, consideration, conversion and retention stages.
- Different journey stages require different tactics.
- Audience planning should be evidence-based.
- Metrics should match the objective being pursued.
