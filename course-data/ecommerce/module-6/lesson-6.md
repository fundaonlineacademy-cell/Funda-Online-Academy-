# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 6: Digital Marketing & Social Media
### Lesson 6: Paid Advertising, Budgets & Return on Ad Spend

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of paid digital advertising.
2. Identify common campaign objectives and audience approaches.
3. Calculate basic advertising metrics.
4. Distinguish revenue from profit when evaluating campaigns.
5. Apply basic budget and testing discipline.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Paid advertising can create traffic quickly, but spending money does not guarantee profitable sales. A campaign may produce many clicks while attracting the wrong audience, or generate revenue that looks impressive but is not commercially sustainable after product costs, delivery subsidies and advertising spend are considered.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Impression** — An instance in which an advertisement is displayed.
- **Click-through rate (CTR)** — Percentage of impressions that result in clicks.
- **Cost per click (CPC)** — Advertising spend divided by clicks.
- **Conversion rate** — Percentage of relevant visitors or clicks that complete a desired action.
- **Cost per acquisition (CPA)** — Advertising spend divided by acquired customers or conversions under the chosen definition.
- **Return on ad spend (ROAS)** — Revenue attributed to advertising divided by advertising spend.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Set the campaign objective first
Paid platforms may optimise for reach, traffic, leads, sales or other actions. The selected objective should match the business goal. Optimising for clicks when the real objective is profitable sales can lead to misleading performance conclusions.

### 2. Build relevant audiences
Audience selection can use location, interests, behaviour, customer lists where lawful, search intent or platform-defined signals. Narrow targeting is not automatically better; the audience should be large enough to learn while still relevant to the offer.

### 3. Understand basic calculations
If an advert receives 20,000 impressions and 400 clicks, CTR is 400 ÷ 20,000 × 100 = 2%. If spend is R2,000, CPC is R2,000 ÷ 400 = R5 per click.

If 20 purchases are attributed to the campaign, CPA is R2,000 ÷ 20 = R100 per purchase. If attributed revenue is R8,000, ROAS is R8,000 ÷ R2,000 = 4, often expressed as 4:1 or 400% depending on the reporting convention.

### 4. Do not confuse ROAS with profit
A 4:1 ROAS does not mean the campaign generated R6,000 profit. Product costs, payment fees, fulfilment, delivery, discounts, returns and other business expenses still matter.

### 5. Test systematically
Changing audience, creative, budget and landing page at the same time makes it difficult to learn what caused the result. Good testing changes one important variable or uses a controlled platform experiment where available.

### 6. Stop waste responsibly
Campaigns should have spending limits, review points and clear criteria for scaling, adjusting or stopping. Avoid chasing losses simply because money has already been spent.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A store spends R3,000 on ads and attributes R12,000 in revenue to the campaign. ROAS is 4.0. However, the products sold have low margins and the store subsidises delivery. The team therefore reviews contribution and profitability before increasing the budget.

## Practical Activity
Create a campaign with a R5,000 budget. Assume 50,000 impressions, 1,000 clicks, 40 purchases and R20,000 attributed revenue. Calculate CTR, CPC, CPA and ROAS, then explain what additional cost information is needed before deciding whether the campaign is profitable.

## Knowledge Check
1. What is CTR?
2. How is CPC calculated?
3. What does ROAS compare?
4. Why is ROAS not the same as profit?
5. Why should campaign testing be controlled?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. The percentage of impressions that generate clicks.
2. Advertising spend divided by clicks.
3. Attributed revenue with advertising spend.
4. Other product and operating costs still reduce profit.
5. Controlled testing helps identify which change affected performance.

## Key Takeaways
- Paid advertising should have a defined objective.
- Audience relevance and landing-page quality affect results.
- CTR, CPC, CPA and ROAS measure different parts of performance.
- Revenue performance should not be confused with profitability.
- Budgets should be tested and reviewed systematically.
