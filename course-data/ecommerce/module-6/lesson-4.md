# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 6: Digital Marketing & Social Media
### Lesson 4: Search Engine Optimisation for E-commerce

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of search engine optimisation (SEO).
2. Identify important on-page SEO elements for product and category pages.
3. Distinguish useful keyword research from keyword stuffing.
4. Recognise technical and content factors that affect search visibility.
5. Apply a basic e-commerce SEO checklist.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Search engines can help customers discover an online store while they are actively looking for products or information. SEO is the process of making pages easier for search engines and users to understand. It does not guarantee rankings, and it should not be treated as a trick. Good e-commerce SEO usually improves clarity, structure and usefulness for customers as well.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **SEO** — Search engine optimisation.
- **Keyword** — A word or phrase people may use when searching.
- **Search intent** — The purpose behind a search query.
- **Title tag** — A page title used by browsers and search systems.
- **Meta description** — A short page description that may appear in search results.
- **Internal link** — A link connecting one page of a site to another page on the same site.
- **Indexing** — The process by which search engines store and organise discovered pages.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Start with search intent
Keyword research should focus on what customers are actually trying to find. A broad query such as “shoes” may indicate exploration, while “men's black trail running shoes size 9” shows stronger product-specific intent.

### 2. Optimise product pages naturally
Product titles, headings, descriptions, image alternatives where appropriate and URLs should clearly describe the product. Do not repeat the same keyword unnaturally. Keyword stuffing makes content harder to read and is not a substitute for useful information.

### 3. Build useful category pages
Category names and introductory content can help search engines understand the product group. Categories should be based on customer language and supported by logical internal links.

### 4. Avoid duplicate and thin content
Copying identical supplier descriptions across hundreds of pages can make the store less useful. Where practical, product pages should contain accurate, distinctive information that helps customers understand the offer.

### 5. Support technical quality
Search visibility can also be affected by mobile usability, page speed, crawlability, broken links and site structure. SEO therefore depends on both content and technical quality.

### 6. Measure outcomes
SEO performance may be monitored through search impressions, clicks, organic traffic, landing-page behaviour and conversions. Ranking for a phrase has limited value if the traffic does not match the intended customer or product.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A store sells stainless-steel lunch boxes. Instead of naming a page “Product 103,” the business uses a clear product title, accurate description, relevant category, descriptive URL and internal links from the lunch-box category page. The page answers product-specific questions while remaining readable.
