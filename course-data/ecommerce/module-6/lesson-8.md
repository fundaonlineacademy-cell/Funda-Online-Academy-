# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 6: Digital Marketing & Social Media
### Lesson 8: Campaign Planning, Optimisation & Ethical Marketing

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Build a structured digital campaign plan.
2. Coordinate audience, message, channel, budget and measurement.
3. Identify weak campaign performance and form improvement hypotheses.
4. Apply basic optimisation discipline.
5. Recognise manipulative or misleading marketing practices that should be avoided.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A successful campaign is not one advertisement or one social post. It is a coordinated plan that connects a business objective to an audience, offer, creative message, destination page, budget and measurement process. Campaigns also need review after launch. Optimisation means improving performance based on evidence rather than constantly changing things without a clear reason.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Campaign plan** — A structured document defining objective, audience, offer, channels, budget and measurement.
- **Landing page** — The page a customer reaches after clicking a campaign link.
- **Creative** — The visual, video, copy or other advertising asset used in a campaign.
- **Optimisation** — Evidence-based adjustment intended to improve campaign results.
- **A/B test** — A controlled comparison between two versions of an important variable.
- **Dark pattern** — A manipulative interface or design technique intended to push users toward choices they may not otherwise make.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Build from objective to execution
A campaign plan should define the commercial objective, intended audience, product or offer, core message, channel mix, landing page, budget, campaign dates and KPIs. These elements should agree with one another.

### 2. Make the destination match the message
If an advert promotes “20% off selected backpacks,” the click should lead to the relevant products and the discount should be clear and active. Sending customers to an unrelated homepage creates friction and weakens trust.

### 3. Monitor performance in context
Campaigns should be reviewed against the original objective and expected commercial outcome. Early data can be noisy, so teams should avoid overreacting to very small samples while also avoiding uncontrolled spend on clearly broken campaigns.

### 4. Form a hypothesis before changing things
If clicks are strong but purchases are weak, possible causes include poor landing-page relevance, unexpected delivery costs, weak product information or checkout friction. The team should investigate before assuming the advertisement itself is the only problem.

### 5. Optimise one important variable at a time
Where practical, test creative, audience, offer or landing-page elements separately. Controlled learning is more valuable than changing everything simultaneously and losing the ability to identify what worked.

### 6. Market ethically
Avoid false scarcity, fake countdown timers, hidden costs, fabricated reviews, misleading price comparisons or interfaces designed to make refusal unnecessarily difficult. Sustainable marketing should help customers understand the offer rather than trick them into a transaction.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A store advertises a weekend shoe promotion. Click-through rate is strong, but conversion is poor. The team discovers that the landing page still displays the normal price until the customer reaches checkout. Instead of increasing ad spend, the store corrects the landing-page presentation and retests conversion.

## Practical Activity
Create a one-page campaign plan for a three-week product launch. Include objective, audience, message, channels, budget, landing page, three KPIs, one risk and one optimisation test.

## Knowledge Check

2. Why should a landing page match the advert?
3. What is an A/B test?
4. Why should optimisation begin with a hypothesis?
5. Give two examples of manipulative marketing practices.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Objective, audience, offer, channels, budget, destination and measurement.
2. Message mismatch creates confusion and weakens conversion.
3. A controlled comparison between two versions.
4. It helps the team test a specific possible cause rather than changing randomly.
5. Examples include fake scarcity, fabricated reviews, hidden costs and misleading price comparisons.

## Key Takeaways
- Campaigns should be planned as connected systems.
- Ads and landing pages must tell the same truthful story.
- Optimisation should be based on evidence and testable hypotheses.
- Controlled testing improves learning.
- Ethical marketing supports long-term customer trust.
