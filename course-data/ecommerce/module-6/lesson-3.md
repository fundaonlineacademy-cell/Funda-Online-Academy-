# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 6: Digital Marketing & Social Media
### Lesson 3: Social Media Channels, Content Planning & Community Engagement

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Compare the roles of common social-media channels.
2. Plan a basic social content calendar.
3. Match content formats to campaign objectives.
4. Apply professional community-engagement practices.
5. Review social content using business-relevant metrics.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Social media can help an online store reach customers, demonstrate products and maintain relationships, but posting without a plan often produces inconsistent results. Each platform has different audience behaviour, content formats and strengths. The goal is not to be everywhere. The goal is to use the channels the business can manage well and connect activity to a clear objective.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Organic social media** — Unpaid content published through a brand's social account.
- **Content calendar** — A schedule of planned posts, themes and campaign dates.
- **Engagement** — Interactions such as comments, replies, shares, saves or clicks.
- **Reach** — Number of people exposed to content.
- **Community management** — Ongoing interaction with people engaging with the brand.
- **Social proof** — Evidence from genuine customer behaviour or feedback that may influence others.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Select channels intentionally
A fashion store may benefit from visual short-form platforms, while a specialised B2B seller may gain more value from professional networks or search-led content. Channel choice should reflect the audience, product and available resources rather than trends alone.

### 2. Plan a balanced content mix
Useful social content may include demonstrations, product education, customer questions, behind-the-scenes material, promotional offers and service information. A content calendar can coordinate launches, seasons, campaigns and operational deadlines.

### 3. Match format to objective
Short video may work well for demonstrations. Carousel-style content may explain several features. Stories or temporary formats may support time-sensitive updates. The format should serve the message, not replace it.

### 4. Engage with the community professionally
Comments and direct messages are part of customer experience. Staff should respond respectfully, avoid exposing private order information and move account-specific issues to appropriate private support channels where necessary.

### 5. Measure meaningful results
Likes alone may not indicate commercial value. Depending on the objective, the business may review reach, engagement quality, clicks, product-page visits, leads, orders or revenue influenced by social activity.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A beauty retailer launches a new skincare set. It uses a short demonstration video for awareness, a carousel explaining product use for consideration, customer FAQs in stories and a link to the product page for conversion. The team measures reach, link clicks, product-page visits and resulting orders rather than likes alone.

## Practical Activity
Create a seven-day social content calendar for an imaginary online store. Include the content objective, format, topic, CTA and metric for each post.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Each channel requires resources and should match the audience and objective.
2. A schedule of planned content and campaign activity.
3. Likes may not lead to traffic, enquiries or sales.
4. When order-specific or personal information is involved.
5. Examples include short video, carousel posts, stories or static images.

## Key Takeaways
- Social channels should be selected strategically.
- A content calendar improves consistency and coordination.
- Content format should match the marketing goal.
- Community management is part of customer experience.
- Metrics should connect social activity to meaningful outcomes.
