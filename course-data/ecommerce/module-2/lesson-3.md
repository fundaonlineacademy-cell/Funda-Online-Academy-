# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 2: Online Store Planning, Platforms & Setup
### Lesson 3: Domain Names, Hosting & Store Identity

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the roles of domain names, hosting and DNS at a practical level.
2. Select a domain using clarity, relevance and legitimate brand identity.
3. Identify ownership, renewal and access-control risks.
4. Explain why consistent store identity supports trust.
5. Create a basic digital-asset ownership checklist.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A domain name is part of the store's identity and a gateway customers use to reach it. Losing control of a domain, allowing it to expire or linking it to the wrong service can interrupt sales even when the store platform itself is working. Store identity should therefore be treated as an operational asset, not merely a branding detail.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Domain name** — A human-readable internet address associated with a website or online service.
- **Registrar** — A service through which a domain is registered and managed.
- **DNS** — The system that connects domain names with internet services and technical destinations.
- **Hosting** — Infrastructure or a service that makes website resources available online.
- **SSL/TLS** — Technology used to protect data transmitted between systems, commonly reflected by HTTPS on websites.
- **Renewal** — Extending registration or subscription before expiry.
- **Store identity** — The consistent business name, visual identity and customer-facing information associated with the store.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. The role of a domain
A domain helps customers reach and recognise a store. It should normally be concise, memorable, easy to spell and reasonably connected to the legitimate business identity.

A domain does not by itself create a website. It must point through DNS to the relevant hosting or platform service.

### 2. Domain-selection principles
A useful domain avoids unnecessary complexity. Consider:
- relevance to the business;
- spelling and pronunciation;
- risk of customer confusion;
- legitimate rights and brand considerations;
- long-term suitability if the business grows.

Deliberately imitating an unrelated established brand can create customer confusion and legal risk.

### 3. Understand hosting
Hosted commerce platforms usually bundle hosting as part of the service. Self-hosted solutions require the merchant or technical provider to arrange infrastructure. Hosting decisions affect performance, reliability, security and maintenance responsibility.

### 4. Protect registrar and DNS access
A person with control of the registrar or DNS may be able to redirect the domain, interrupt service or change email routing. Administrative access should therefore use strong credentials, MFA where supported and controlled ownership.

### 5. Manage renewals
Domain expiry can make the store unavailable and may put the domain at risk. Businesses should record renewal dates, payment methods and responsible owners. Automatic renewal can help, but it should not replace monitoring because payment cards and contact details can expire.

### 6. Keep business ownership clear
Critical digital assets should not depend entirely on one employee's personal email or private account. The business should know who owns the domain registration, platform subscription and primary administrator accounts, and how access can be recovered.

### 7. Maintain consistent store identity
The business name, logo, domain, support details, checkout information and policies should not contradict one another. Inconsistent identities can make customers question whether they are dealing with the same legitimate business.

### 8. HTTPS and customer confidence
Modern stores should use secure transport provided through appropriate HTTPS/TLS configuration. HTTPS protects data in transit but does not prove that every business practice or website claim is trustworthy. It is one control, not a complete trust guarantee.

### 9. Plan for change
Businesses may rebrand, change platforms or add services. Keep records of DNS configuration and ownership so changes can be controlled rather than improvised. Migration should consider email, redirects, search visibility and customer communication where relevant.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
An owner registers the store domain using a staff member's personal email. Two years later the employee has left and the domain renewal fails. The better design is controlled business ownership with current recovery details, MFA and documented renewal responsibility.

## Practical Activity
Create a Digital Asset Register for a fictional store containing domain, registrar, renewal date, platform/hosting, primary administrative owner, recovery contact, MFA status and backup administrator. Do not record real passwords in the exercise.

## Key Takeaways
- Domains are operational business assets as well as branding tools.
- DNS connects the domain to online services.
- Registrar and recovery access need strong control.
- Renewals must be monitored.
- Store identity should remain consistent across customer touchpoints.
- HTTPS is important but is not a guarantee of overall business trustworthiness.
