## Lesson 1 — Defining Store Requirements & Technical Needs

### Q1
A business plans to sell 40 physical products online. Before choosing a platform, what should it do first?
A. Buy the most expensive subscription available
B. Define the store's business, customer, operational and technical requirements
C. Copy the technology used by its largest competitor
D. Register as many domain names as possible

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Platform selection should follow a clear requirements analysis so that the technology supports the actual business and customer needs.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q2
Which requirement is primarily a functional requirement?
A. The store must allow customers to add products to a cart and complete checkout
B. The brand should feel trustworthy
C. The business hopes to grow in future
D. The owner prefers modern-looking websites

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Functional requirements describe what the system must actually do, such as search, cart, checkout, payment or order-management functions.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q3
Why should expected order volume be considered during store planning?
A. It determines the colour of the logo
B. It helps determine whether systems and operational processes can handle expected demand
C. It removes the need for inventory records
D. It guarantees customer loyalty

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Expected traffic and transaction volumes affect platform capacity, integrations, fulfilment processes and scalability requirements.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q4
A small retailer requires card payments, courier integration, inventory tracking and mobile-friendly pages. These should be documented as:
A. Random preferences after launch
B. Store requirements before platform selection
C. Marketing slogans
D. Competitor weaknesses only

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Important capabilities should be identified before selecting or configuring the store platform.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

## Lesson 2 — Hosted Platforms, Marketplaces & Self-hosted Stores

### Q5
What is a major characteristic of a hosted e-commerce platform?
A. The merchant must normally build every server component from scratch
B. The platform provider generally manages much of the underlying hosted service
C. Products cannot be sold online
D. Customers must install the store on their devices

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Hosted platforms generally provide the infrastructure and software service, reducing some technical administration for merchants.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q6
What is one potential advantage of selling through an established marketplace?
A. Guaranteed profit
B. Immediate ownership of the marketplace
C. Access to an existing audience of marketplace shoppers
D. Complete control over all marketplace rules

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Marketplaces may provide access to an existing customer audience, although merchants usually accept platform rules, fees and limitations.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q7
Which situation may favour a self-hosted solution?
A. The business needs greater technical control and has resources to manage that responsibility
B. The owner refuses to maintain any technology
C. The business has no products or services
D. The store does not need internet access

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Self-hosted solutions can provide greater control but normally place more responsibility for hosting, maintenance and security on the business or its technical provider.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q8
When comparing a marketplace with an independent online store, which is the best approach?
A. Compare only the monthly price
B. Consider audience access, control, fees, branding, data, operations and business goals
C. Always choose the marketplace
D. Always choose the independent store

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** No model is automatically best. A sound decision considers the full commercial and operational trade-offs.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

## Lesson 3 — Domain Names, Hosting & Store Identity

### Q9
What is the main purpose of a domain name in an online store?
A. It provides a human-readable web address associated with the store
B. It replaces product descriptions
C. It guarantees search-engine ranking
D. It automatically fulfils orders

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** A domain gives customers a recognisable address through which they can reach the online store.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q10
Which domain is generally easier for customers to remember and communicate?
A. A concise, relevant and clearly spelled domain
B. A long sequence of unrelated characters
C. A domain deliberately similar to an unrelated brand
D. A domain that changes every week

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Clear, memorable and relevant domains reduce customer confusion and support consistent store identity.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q11
Why should a business protect access to its domain registrar account?
A. Domain control is part of the business's digital identity and unauthorised changes can disrupt the store
B. Registrars write product descriptions automatically
C. Domains contain physical stock
D. Registrar security affects only printed advertising

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Losing control of domain settings can redirect or disable a store, making account security an important operational responsibility.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q12
What should be consistent across a professional online store identity?
A. Business name, domain, visual identity and customer-facing information
B. Random business names on each page
C. Different contact details on every page
D. Unrelated logos at checkout

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Consistency helps customers recognise the business and reduces uncertainty about who operates the store.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

## Lesson 4 — Store Structure, Navigation & User Experience

### Q13
What is the primary purpose of good store navigation?
A. To make customers explore as many irrelevant pages as possible
B. To help customers understand where they are and reach relevant products or information efficiently
C. To hide important policies
D. To increase the number of menu items without limit

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Navigation should reduce friction and help customers find products, categories and essential information efficiently.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4

### Q14
A store has a menu containing 45 unrelated links with no grouping. What is the likely problem?
A. Reduced cognitive load
B. Improved information architecture
C. Customer confusion and difficult navigation
D. Faster fulfilment

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Poorly organised menus increase cognitive load and make products harder to find.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4

### Q15
Which structure best supports a store selling men's, women's and children's clothing?
A. One unlabelled page containing every product
B. Logical top-level categories with meaningful subcategories
C. Products arranged only by date added
D. A menu containing internal product IDs only

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Logical categories reflect how customers understand and browse the catalogue.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4

### Q16
What does user experience refer to in an e-commerce context?
A. Only the colour of the website
B. The overall experience of finding information, interacting with the store and completing tasks
C. Only the merchant's experience
D. The courier's vehicle type

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** User experience covers the customer's overall interaction with the store, including usability, clarity, confidence and task completion.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 4

A. The internal preferences of one employee only
B. Logical customer-oriented groupings that make products easier to discover
C. Random numbers

**Traceability:** Lesson 5

### Q20

## Lesson 6 — Mobile-first Design & Accessibility Basics

Why is mobile-first thinking important for an online store?
A. Customers may shop using smaller screens and touch interfaces, so essential tasks must remain usable
B. Desktop customers are illegal
C. Mobile design eliminates security responsibilities
D. It guarantees higher profit

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Mobile-first design prioritises essential content and interactions for constrained screens while supporting a broader range of devices.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q22

A. Tiny buttons placed very close together
B. Clear readable text and controls that are easy to tap
C. Important actions that require hovering a mouse
D. Horizontal scrolling for every page

**Explanation:** Readable content and usable touch targets make key tasks easier on mobile devices.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q23

B. It increases the physical weight of the product
C. It replaces pricing

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Appropriate text alternatives support accessibility when visual information needs a non-visual equivalent.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q24
Which checkout practice supports accessibility and usability?
A. Forms with clear labels and understandable error messages
B. Unlabelled fields
C. Error messages communicated only by colour

## Lesson 7 — Store Policies, Contact Information & Trust Signals

### Q25
Why should return and refund information be easy to locate?
A. Customers need to understand important transaction conditions before buying and when resolving problems
B. Policies should be hidden until after payment
C. Policies are useful only to couriers
D. Clear policies prevent every return

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Accessible policies improve informed decision-making, trust and post-purchase service. South African stores must also consider applicable consumer-protection requirements.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q26
Which is the strongest trust signal?
A. Verifiable business and contact information that is consistent across the store
B. A fake accreditation badge
C. A countdown timer that restarts continuously
D. Anonymous ownership with no contact route

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Genuine, verifiable information is more trustworthy than fabricated or manipulative visual signals.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q27
A South African online supplier wants to hide the total delivery cost until after the customer has paid. Why is this poor practice?
A. Customers should be able to understand material pricing and transaction information before finalising an order
B. Delivery charges never affect buying decisions
C. Hidden costs improve informed consent
D. All online prices are optional

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Transparent pricing supports informed decisions, and applicable South African electronic-transaction rules require important supplier and transaction information to be available to consumers.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q28
Which policy-writing approach is best?
A. Copy another store's policy without checking it
B. Write clear policies that accurately reflect the business's actual processes and applicable requirements
C. Promise unlimited returns regardless of whether the business can honour them
D. Avoid all policy information

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Policies should be understandable, accurate, operationally realistic and reviewed against applicable legal requirements.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

## Lesson 8 — Testing & Launch-readiness of an Online Store

### Q29
What is the best way to test checkout before launch?
A. Assume it works because the page loads
B. Run realistic end-to-end test transactions through relevant devices and scenarios
C. Wait for the first paying customer to test it
D. Test only the homepage

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** End-to-end testing can reveal problems in cart, checkout, payment, confirmation and operational hand-offs before customers encounter them.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q30
A store looks correct on a laptop but its checkout button is partly off-screen on a phone. What does this show?
A. Cross-device testing is unnecessary
B. Mobile and responsive testing identified a launch defect
C. The courier is responsible
D. The product price is too low

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Stores should be tested across representative screen sizes and devices because layouts and controls may behave differently.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q31
Which issue should normally be treated as launch-critical?
A. A broken payment flow preventing customers from completing orders
B. A future blog topic has not been chosen
C. An optional decorative image could be replaced later
D. A social-media post has not been scheduled

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Defects that prevent purchasing, create serious security/privacy risk or cause materially incorrect transactions should be resolved before launch.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q32
What is the purpose of a launch-readiness checklist?
A. To provide structured evidence that important store functions, content and operational processes have been reviewed before launch
B. To guarantee the business will never encounter a problem
C. To replace all testing
D. To remove staff accountability

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** A checklist supports disciplined review and makes important launch requirements less likely to be overlooked.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8
