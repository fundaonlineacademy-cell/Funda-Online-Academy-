# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 2: Online Store Planning, Platforms & Setup
### Lesson 2: Hosted Platforms, Marketplaces & Self-hosted Stores

**Estimated study time:** 40–45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Distinguish hosted e-commerce platforms, marketplaces and self-hosted stores.
2. Compare control, cost, technical responsibility and audience access across models.
3. Explain platform dependency and total-cost-of-ownership considerations.
4. Match a platform model to validated business requirements.
5. Identify situations where a multi-channel approach may be appropriate.

## Why This Lesson Matters
There is no single best e-commerce platform model for every business. A hosted service can reduce infrastructure administration, a marketplace can provide access to an existing audience, and a self-hosted solution can offer greater technical control. Each choice creates different responsibilities, costs and dependencies. Learners should compare models against requirements rather than treating one option as automatically superior.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Hosted platform** — An e-commerce service where the provider manages much of the underlying hosted software and infrastructure.
- **Marketplace** — A third-party platform where multiple sellers reach customers under marketplace rules.
- **Self-hosted store** — A commerce application deployed and maintained on infrastructure controlled directly or through the merchant's technical provider.
- **Total cost of ownership (TCO)** — The broader cost of operating a solution, not only the advertised subscription price.
- **Platform dependency** — Reliance on a provider's technology, rules, fees or availability.
- **Multi-channel** — Selling through more than one customer-facing sales channel.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Hosted e-commerce platforms
Hosted platforms generally provide store-building tools, hosting, updates and a managed service environment. Merchants still configure products, payments, users, apps and policies, but they do not normally build the underlying platform infrastructure from scratch.

Advantages can include faster setup, integrated features, support and reduced server administration. Limitations may include subscription costs, platform rules, extension dependence and constraints on custom functionality.

### 2. Marketplaces
A marketplace may provide existing customer traffic, standardised checkout and a trusted platform brand. This can reduce the difficulty of finding the first audience.

However, sellers usually accept marketplace fees, listing rules, performance standards, competition beside their own products and reduced control over the full customer relationship. Marketplace policy or algorithm changes can affect a seller significantly.

### 3. Self-hosted solutions
A self-hosted store can give a business greater control over code, hosting choices and custom integrations. That flexibility also creates responsibility for hosting, security, backups, updates, performance and technical support.

Self-hosting is not automatically cheaper. Development and maintenance can exceed the price of a managed subscription if the business requires specialist technical work.

### 4. Compare total cost, not only monthly price
Useful cost categories include:
- subscription or hosting;
- transaction fees;
- payment fees;
- premium themes or extensions;
- custom development;
- maintenance;
- support;
- integrations;
- staff administration;
- migration costs if the business later changes platform.

A low advertised monthly price may still produce a high total cost when essential extensions are added.

### 5. Compare control and responsibility
Greater control usually brings greater responsibility. A self-hosted platform can provide deep customisation but requires stronger technical ownership. A marketplace may handle much of the customer-facing infrastructure but gives the seller less control over rules and branding.

### 6. Consider data and customer relationships
Businesses should understand what customer and transaction information each model makes available and under what conditions. The ability to export products, orders or customer records can matter if the business changes systems later.

### 7. Consider audience access
A standalone store must normally generate its own traffic through marketing, search, repeat customers or referrals. A marketplace can provide discoverability, but the seller competes within the marketplace environment.

### 8. Multi-channel strategies
Some businesses use both an independent store and marketplaces. This can diversify demand but increases catalogue, stock and order-management complexity. Prices, inventory and product information may need to remain consistent across channels.

### 9. Match the model to requirements
A microbusiness with limited technical staff may prefer a suitable hosted service. A specialist business with a technical team and unusual integration needs may justify greater self-hosted control. A new seller testing demand may use a marketplace first. The correct answer depends on validated needs and resources.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A small skincare brand wants to launch quickly, has no developer and needs local payment and courier integrations. A hosted platform that supports those requirements may be a better initial fit than building a custom store. The brand may also list selected products on a marketplace for additional reach while keeping its own store as the primary branded channel.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Some underlying hosting and platform infrastructure administration.
2. Advantage: existing audience; risk: dependency on third-party rules or fees.
3. Development, security and maintenance require resources.
4. The broader cost of setup, operation, integrations and maintenance.
5. Catalogue, stock and orders must be coordinated across channels.

## Key Takeaways
- Hosted, marketplace and self-hosted models create different trade-offs.
- Greater technical control normally creates greater technical responsibility.
- Marketplace reach comes with platform dependency.
- Total cost matters more than one advertised fee.
- Platform choice should follow business requirements and resources.
