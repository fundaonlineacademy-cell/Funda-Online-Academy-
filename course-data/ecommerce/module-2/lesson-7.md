# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 2: Online Store Planning, Platforms & Setup
### Lesson 7: Store Policies, Contact Information & Trust Signals

**Estimated study time:** 40–45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain how transparent store information supports customer trust.
2. Identify essential customer-facing contact, delivery, returns and privacy information.
3. Distinguish genuine trust evidence from misleading trust signals.
4. Review a store for contradictory or missing transaction information.
5. Apply a practical pre-launch trust audit.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Customers cannot normally inspect an online business physically before paying. They judge credibility from business identity, product information, contact routes, policies, pricing, checkout and communication. A professional store reduces uncertainty by making important information accurate, consistent and reasonably easy to find.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Store policy** — A documented rule or procedure explaining how the business handles matters such as delivery, returns, refunds or privacy.
- **Trust signal** — Genuine evidence or information that supports confidence in the store.
- **Disclosure** — Information made available so a customer can make an informed decision.
- **Privacy notice** — Information explaining relevant personal-information practices.
- **Transparency** — Presenting material business and transaction information clearly rather than hiding or misrepresenting it.
- **Terms and conditions** — Rules governing website use or transactions, subject to applicable law.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Trust requires useful information
A polished logo is not enough. Customers may ask: Who operates this store? How do I contact them? What is the full price? When will delivery happen? What if the product is wrong or defective? How is my information handled?

When answers are missing or contradictory, perceived risk increases.

### 2. Contact information should be real and monitored
A store may provide email, telephone, messaging, contact forms or relevant address information. The business should publish channels it actually monitors. Contact details should be consistent across the footer, contact page, checkout and order communications.

### 3. Policies should reflect operations
Delivery information should match actual geographic coverage, charges and likely timeframes. Returns and refund information should describe the business process without pretending to remove statutory rights. Privacy information should reflect the data the store actually collects and uses.

Copying another business's policy is risky because products, operations and legal circumstances differ.

### 4. Price transparency matters
Customers should understand material charges at the appropriate stage of the journey. Avoid presenting a low apparent price and revealing unavoidable charges only after substantial commitment. Promotions should also state important conditions and not create a false impression of savings.

### 5. Genuine trust signals
Useful trust signals can include:
- consistent business identity;
- accurate product information;
- monitored support channels;
- clear delivery and return processes;
- legitimate payment methods;
- authentic customer reviews;
- secure transaction technology;
- understandable policies.

A fabricated review, fake accreditation badge or unsupported “100% secure” claim is not a genuine trust signal.

### 6. Placement matters
Important information should appear where customers need it. Full policies may live on dedicated pages, but product or checkout pages can provide concise summaries and links. Mobile customers should not lose access to these details.

### 7. Trust must match behaviour
A policy promise is only valuable when operations support it. If a store promises one-day replies but routinely ignores support for a week, the promise damages credibility. Customer-facing information should be updated when processes change.

### 8. South African context
Online stores should consider applicable ECTA, CPA and POPIA requirements where relevant. Store policy should not be treated as more powerful than legislation. Exact legal questions should be verified using current authoritative sources or qualified advice.

### 9. Conduct a trust audit
Before launch, check:
- business identity;
- contact channels;
- product accuracy;
- price and delivery charges;
- delivery time information;
- returns/refunds information;
- privacy information;
- terms;
- checkout messages;
- confirmation communications;
- mobile visibility;
- broken or contradictory links.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A desk-accessories store has attractive product pages but no support email, incomplete returns information and delivery charges shown only at the final checkout step. The owner adds monitored contact details, clear delivery information, a completed returns process, privacy information and consistent policy links. The improvement reduces transaction uncertainty rather than merely changing the visual design.

## Practical Activity
Create a Trust and Information Checklist for a fictional store under Business Identity, Contact, Product Information, Pricing, Delivery, Returns, Privacy, Terms, Reviews and Mobile Visibility. Record where each item appears and who is responsible for keeping it accurate.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why can a professional-looking website still have weak trust?
2. What makes contact information useful?
3. Why is copied policy wording risky?
4. Give two examples of misleading trust signals.
5. Why should policies be reviewed when operations change?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Appearance alone does not answer important transaction and support questions.
2. It should be genuine, monitored, consistent and easy to find.
3. Another business may have different processes and obligations.
4. Examples include fabricated reviews and fake awards or security claims.
5. Policies should describe current operational reality.

## Key Takeaways
- Trust depends on transparency and reliable operations.
- Customers should understand who they are dealing with and the material transaction conditions.
- Policies should reflect the actual business and applicable requirements.
- Trust signals must be truthful.
- Important information must remain usable on mobile.
- A structured trust audit belongs in launch preparation.
