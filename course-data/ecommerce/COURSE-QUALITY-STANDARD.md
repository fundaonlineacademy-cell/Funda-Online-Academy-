# E-commerce & Online Store — Course Quality Standard

## Purpose
This file records the quality rules to be used when the complete E-commerce & Online Store course is reviewed, maintained or deployed into the academy learning system.

## Course Structure
- 8 modules.
- Target structure: 8 teaching lessons per module.
- Each lesson must have a clear title and a logical place in the course progression.
- Lessons should avoid unnecessary duplication across modules.
- Later modules should apply knowledge established earlier in the course.

## Teaching Lesson Standard
A complete lesson should normally contain:
1. Estimated study time.
2. Learning outcomes.
3. Why the lesson matters.
4. Key terms / vocabulary.
5. Structured main lesson content.
6. A worked example, demonstration or scenario where appropriate.
7. A practical learner activity.
8. A knowledge check.
9. Feedback / explanations.
10. Key takeaways.
11. Visual learning guidance where it adds value.

Lesson length is based on instructional depth rather than a rigid number. Narrow topics may be approximately 500–700 words. Deeper lessons involving calculations, legal/compliance awareness, operational workflows, security, analysis or integrated application may be approximately 700–1,000 words or longer where genuinely required. Content must not be padded merely to reach a word target.

## Accuracy Standard
- Calculations must be worked and checked.
- Product, pricing, payment, marketing, security and operational terminology must be used consistently.
- South African legal and compliance content must distinguish between legislation and voluntary store policy.
- Legal content is educational and operational awareness, not individual legal advice.
- Time-sensitive statutory claims should be verified before major course revisions or deployment.
- Do not hard-code a tax rate where the teaching objective can be achieved by teaching the applicable principle, unless the current rate has been intentionally verified and maintenance responsibility is clear.

## Formative Assessment Standard
Each module formative assessment should use:
- 32 MCQs in the bank.
- Exactly 4 questions traceable to each of the 8 lessons.
- 15 questions randomly selected per attempt.
- 70% pass requirement.
- Maximum 3 attempts.
- Passing the formative assessment unlocks that module's summative assessment.
- Questions must assess material actually taught in the corresponding lesson.
- One clearly best answer per MCQ.
- Explanations should reinforce learning rather than only state the letter of the answer.

## Summative Assessment Standard
Each module summative bank should contain:
- 52 MCQs.
- Coverage of all 8 lessons.
- A mix of knowledge, application, judgement, scenario and calculation questions where appropriate.
- One clearly best answer per question.
- Correct answer and rationale for each question.
- Integrated questions may combine concepts from multiple lessons after lesson-level coverage has been established.

The learner-facing delivery rules for summative assessments should be configured consistently in the LMS when the final assessment policy is implemented. The question-bank file itself must not silently invent progression, attempt or certification rules that conflict with academy policy.

## Content Boundaries by Module
To reduce duplication:
- Product/catalogue content belongs primarily in Module 3.
- Order, payment, fulfilment and delivery operations belong primarily in Module 4.
- Customer service, returns and consumer-remedy handling belong primarily in Module 5.
- Marketing and social-media acquisition/retention belong primarily in Module 6.
- Security and fraud controls belong primarily in Module 7.
- Module 8 should integrate prior learning through a practical online-store project rather than reteach entire earlier modules.

## Practical Application Standard
Activities should produce something observable where practical: a workflow, catalogue record, pricing calculation, campaign brief, returns decision, access matrix, test result, project artefact or improvement plan. Activities should not rely only on learner opinion when a demonstrable skill can be assessed.

## South African Context
Examples should be understandable in a South African e-commerce environment. Currency examples may use rand (R). Relevant legislation may include ECTA, the CPA and POPIA where genuinely applicable. Legal references should be scoped carefully and uncertainty should be escalated rather than converted into overconfident rules.

## Final Course Audit Checklist
Before the course is treated as deployment-ready, verify:
- all 8 modules are present in the authoritative course source;
- each module has the intended 8 lessons;
- lesson numbering and titles are consistent;
- formative banks contain exactly 32 questions and four per lesson;
- summative banks contain exactly 52 questions and cover all lessons;
- calculations and answer keys are correct;
- no assessment tests material that is absent from the lessons;
- duplicate content has been reduced or deliberately cross-referenced;
- South African legal statements have been reviewed for accuracy;
- examples, terminology and currency are consistent;
- spelling, grammar and formatting are clean;
- learner progression rules match academy policy;
- the deployed database/LMS version matches the approved repository version.

## Source-of-Truth Note
The repository course files and the production LMS/database must be reconciled before launch. Legacy course records should not be assumed to represent the approved rebuilt curriculum merely because they share the same course or module names.
