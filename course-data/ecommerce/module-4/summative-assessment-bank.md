## Questions 1–6 — Lesson 1: Order Lifecycle & Order Status Management

### Q1
Which sequence best represents a normal physical-order workflow?
A. Dispatch → payment → order creation → picking
B. Order creation → payment verification → stock allocation → picking → packing → dispatch
C. Refund → product listing → delivery → payment
D. Picking → marketing → payment → category creation

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** The workflow follows the order from creation through verified payment and fulfilment.

### Q2
Why should payment status and fulfilment status be treated separately?
A. They describe different parts of the transaction
B. They must always have the same label
C. They are both product attributes
D. They apply only to refunds

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Payment describes money collection while fulfilment describes operational preparation and dispatch.

### Q3
An order is marked paid but no stock is allocated. What should happen?
A. Mark it delivered
B. Investigate and allocate stock before fulfilment proceeds
C. Refund automatically
D. Delete the order record

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Paid status does not replace inventory and fulfilment controls.

### Q4
Which status change is most risky if done without evidence?
A. Draft to active product
B. Pending payment to paid
C. Category A to Category B
D. New image to old image

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Incorrectly treating a payment as successful can lead to financial loss.

### Q5
Why is a unique order number important?
A. It supports consistent traceability across systems and teams
B. It determines product colour
C. It calculates VAT
D. It replaces the customer name

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** The order number provides a common reference for payment, fulfilment and customer service.

### Q6
A customer asks to cancel an order already dispatched. What is the best first step?
A. Assume cancellation is complete
B. Check the order stage and applicable return/cancellation process
C. Delete courier tracking
D. Mark the order unpaid

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** The operational stage determines which process is appropriate.

## Questions 7–12 — Lesson 2: Payment Methods, Gateways & Payment Processing

### Q7
What is the safest evidence that an online payment succeeded?
A. A customer screenshot only
B. Confirmation in the approved payment/order system
C. A social-media message
D. The customer saying “I paid”

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Approved system records should be authoritative.

### Q8
Which payment state should normally prevent fulfilment until resolved?
A. Paid
B. Pending
C. Refunded after completed return
D. Settled

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Pending means successful collection is not yet confirmed.

### Q9
What does reconciliation between payments and orders help identify?
A. Unmatched, duplicate or missing transactions
B. Image-quality problems
C. Product-description grammar
D. Social-media reach

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Reconciliation detects differences between transaction and order records.

### Q10
Why should full card details not be requested through ordinary chat?
A. It exposes sensitive payment information unnecessarily
B. It reduces product margin
C. It slows warehouse picking
D. It changes delivery zones

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Sensitive payment data should be handled through approved secure processes.

### Q11
A payment was approved but the customer is charged twice. What should the business investigate?
A. Duplicate transaction records and order/payment references
B. Product image resolution
C. Category tags
D. Shipping packaging only

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Duplicate charges require transaction reconciliation.

### Q12
What is a refund?
A. Returning money after payment according to the approved process
B. Increasing order value
C. Creating a tracking number
D. Allocating stock

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** A refund returns all or part of a collected payment.

## Questions 13–18 — Lesson 3: Picking, Packing & Fulfilment Accuracy

### Q13
A customer orders SKU SHOE-BLK-08 but the picker selects SHOE-BLK-09. What type of error is this?
A. Payment error
B. Variant fulfilment error
C. Delivery-zone error
D. Marketing error

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** The wrong size/variant has been selected.

### Q14
What is the best control before sealing a parcel?
A. Final pack check against the order
B. Change the product title
C. Create a coupon
D. Mark the order refunded

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** A pack check catches incorrect items or quantities before dispatch.

### Q15
Why can excessive packaging be undesirable?
A. It can increase cost and waste unnecessarily
B. It makes payment fail
C. It changes the SKU
D. It removes tracking

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Packaging should protect products without avoidable cost and waste.

### Q16
A fragile item has no protective cushioning available. What should staff do?
A. Dispatch anyway
B. Stop and resolve the packaging risk before dispatch
C. Hide the item under clothing
D. Remove the order number

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Packaging must be suitable for the product risk.

### Q17
Why should similar-looking products be checked using SKU or barcode?
A. Appearance alone may not distinguish variants accurately
B. Barcodes set the selling price
C. SKUs replace courier tracking
D. It guarantees no returns

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Structured identifiers reduce wrong-item errors.

### Q18
If one of three ordered items is unavailable, what is the best response?
A. Substitute any similar product without authority
B. Record the exception and follow the approved resolution process
C. Mark all items delivered
D. Delete the customer's order

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Stock exceptions require documented resolution.

## Questions 19–24 — Lesson 4: Shipping Methods, Delivery Zones & Delivery Costs

### Q19
Which factor can legitimately affect a delivery charge?
A. Delivery location or parcel weight
B. Customer's favourite colour
C. Product review score
D. Number of website pages

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Shipping cost commonly depends on location, weight, size or order value.

### Q20
A store offers free delivery over R1,000. A basket totals R960. What happens under that rule?
A. Free delivery applies automatically
B. The order has not reached the threshold
C. The threshold becomes R960
D. The order must be cancelled

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** The basket is below the stated threshold.

### Q21
Why should stores test remote-area addresses?
A. To confirm delivery zones and rates work as intended
B. To change product images
C. To verify customer passwords
D. To calculate product margin only

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Remote locations may have different rates or service availability.

### Q22
What is a flat shipping rate?
A. One standard delivery charge under defined conditions
B. A changing price every minute
C. A payment refund
D. A product discount

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Flat rate shipping applies a standard charge under a rule.

### Q23
Why should delivery time estimates be realistic?
A. Unrealistic promises can cause complaints and loss of trust
B. They determine product colour
C. They replace tracking numbers
D. They guarantee sales

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Delivery expectations should match operational capability.

### Q24
Which store rule is most problematic?
A. Clearly stated collection option
B. Accepting orders from areas the business cannot actually serve
C. Tested delivery zones
D. Transparent delivery charges

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** A store should not accept orders it cannot fulfil operationally.

## Questions 25–30 — Lesson 5: Tracking, Delivery Exceptions & Proof of Delivery

### Q25

A. Correct it against the courier record and order
B. Leave it because tracking is optional
C. Mark the order refunded
D. Change the product SKU

### Q26
Which is a delivery exception?
A. Parcel delivered successfully
B. Parcel held because the address is incomplete
C. Correct courier scan
D. Normal collection confirmation

**Rationale:** An incomplete address prevents normal delivery.

### Q27
A customer disputes delivery but the courier shows a delivery scan. What is the best response?
A. Reject the complaint immediately
B. Review proof of delivery and available evidence before deciding
C. Delete the tracking data
D. Charge the customer again

**Rationale:** POD should support investigation rather than replace it.

### Q28
Why is customer communication important during delivery delays?
A. It helps manage expectations using verified information
B. It increases product stock
C. It changes payment status
D. It removes courier responsibility

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Customers need accurate updates when delivery does not proceed normally.

### Q29
A parcel has had no tracking movement for several days beyond the normal service period. What should staff do?
A. Ignore it
B. Escalate the shipment for investigation
C. Mark it delivered
D. Cancel the customer's account

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Stalled shipments require investigation.

### Q30
Why must tracking data be linked to the right order?
A. To preserve traceability and prevent disclosure of another customer's shipment information
B. To calculate markup
C. To create categories
D. To set product specifications

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Tracking is customer- and order-specific operational data.

## Questions 31–36 — Lesson 6: Cancellations, Refunds & Payment Reversals

### Q31
A customer paid R900 and receives an approved R250 partial refund. How much of the original payment remains after the refund?
A. R250
B. R550
C. R650
D. R1,150

**Correct answer:** C
**Rationale:** R900 − R250 = R650.

### Q32
Why should a refund amount be calculated from the order record?
A. To avoid guessing and financial error
B. To increase courier speed
C. To change product attributes
D. To create tracking data

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Refunds must correspond to the actual transaction.

### Q33
What is the greatest risk of processing the same approved refund twice?
A. Duplicate financial loss
B. Better customer experience
C. Faster fulfilment
D. Higher inventory accuracy

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Duplicate refunds return more money than authorised.

### Q34
A refund has been submitted to the payment provider but is not yet visible in the customer's bank account. What should staff say?
A. It is definitely completed in the bank
B. Explain that it has been submitted and provide the supported processing expectation
C. Promise it will arrive within five minutes
D. Submit another refund immediately

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Communication should match the actual refund stage.

### Q35
A returned product is damaged and not saleable. What should happen to inventory?
A. Restore it to available stock automatically
B. Follow the condition and inventory adjustment process
C. Ignore the damage
D. Increase its quantity twice

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Refunded items must not automatically be treated as saleable stock.

### Q36
Which order may require cancellation but no refund?
A. An unpaid order
B. A fully paid delivered order
C. A paid order already refunded
D. A returned paid order

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** No money needs to be returned when payment was never collected.

## Questions 37–42 — Lesson 7: Fraud Indicators, Suspicious Orders & Order Review

### Q37
Which statement about fraud indicators is correct?
A. One indicator always proves fraud
B. Indicators justify review but require context
C. Indicators should be ignored
D. Only payment amount matters

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Signals require evidence-based assessment.

### Q38
Which combination most reasonably justifies additional review?
A. New account, repeated failed payments, very high-value order and redirect request
B. One normal order with verified payment
C. Customer uses a common email domain
D. Customer buys one low-value item

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Multiple unusual signals can increase risk.

### Q39
What is a false positive in fraud screening?
A. A legitimate order incorrectly flagged as suspicious
B. A confirmed stolen payment
C. A duplicate tracking number
D. A refunded order

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Fraud controls can flag genuine transactions.

### Q40
Why should staff document fraud-review decisions?
A. To support consistency, investigation and future rule improvement
B. To change delivery rates
C. To calculate product margin
D. To replace customer records

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Recorded decisions create an audit trail.

### Q41
Which verification request is least appropriate?
A. Asking for a password
B. Reviewing approved provider risk signals
C. Confirming order details through the authorised process
D. Escalating a high-risk transaction

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Passwords should not be requested.

### Q42
Why is it dangerous to assume all unusual orders are fraudulent?
A. Legitimate customers may be unfairly rejected
B. It improves conversion
C. It increases delivery speed
D. It creates more stock

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Risk controls should avoid unfair, unsupported conclusions.

## Questions 51–52 — Integrated Whole-Module Scenarios

### Q51
A customer places a high-value order. Payment initially fails twice, then succeeds. The order is marked paid, but the selected variant is out of stock. The customer asks staff to send a different model to a new address. What is the best response?
A. Send any available model immediately
B. Hold the order, verify the payment and risk signals, record the stock exception, and resolve any substitution/address change through approved processes before fulfilment
C. Mark the order delivered
D. Refund twice and close the order

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** The scenario requires coordinated payment, fraud, stock and fulfilment controls.

### Q52
At daily close, a store finds one paid order without fulfilment, one dispatched order without tracking, one refund not reflected in the order system and one failed delivery marked complete. What should the team do?
A. Leave all statuses unchanged
B. Record the four exceptions, investigate each against the relevant payment, fulfilment and delivery evidence, correct the records and address any process weakness
C. Delete all four orders
D. Mark all four delivered

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Reconciliation requires evidence-based resolution of every mismatch.
