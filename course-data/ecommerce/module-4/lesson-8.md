# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 4: Orders, Payments, Fulfilment & Delivery
### Lesson 8: Reconciliation, Operational Controls & Daily Order Review

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain why e-commerce operations require reconciliation.
2. Match orders, payments, fulfilment and delivery records.
3. Identify discrepancies requiring investigation.
4. Apply a practical daily order-control routine.
5. Use operational records to reduce repeat errors.

## Why This Lesson Matters
E-commerce systems create many connected records: customer orders, payment transactions, stock movements, dispatch records, courier tracking and refunds. If these records do not agree, the business can lose money or provide incorrect customer service. Reconciliation helps confirm that the digital and operational records tell the same story.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Reconciliation** — Comparing related records to confirm that they agree.
- **Discrepancy** — A difference between records that should match.
- **Exception report** — A list of transactions requiring investigation.
- **Audit trail** — A history showing important actions and changes.
- **Control total** — A total used to compare records across systems.
- **Daily close** — A routine review performed at the end of an operational period.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Reconcile orders and payments
Every paid order should have a corresponding verified payment record. Unpaid or failed transactions should not appear as completed sales. Duplicate payments, unmatched references and partial payments should be investigated.

### 2. Reconcile orders and fulfilment
The items picked and dispatched should match the order record. If an order contains two units but only one is dispatched, the discrepancy should remain visible until resolved.

### 3. Reconcile delivery and completion
Orders marked delivered should have appropriate delivery evidence where the process supports it. Orders showing no courier movement or failed delivery should not be closed simply to reduce the open-order count.

### 4. Include refunds and cancellations
A refund changes the financial position of an order and may also affect stock. Daily review should identify completed refunds, pending refunds and cancellations that still require operational action.

### 5. Use exception reports
Instead of manually reading every normal order, teams can focus on exceptions such as paid-but-unfulfilled orders, fulfilled-but-unpaid orders, duplicate payments, orders without tracking, overdue shipments and refunds without matching records.

### 6. Learn from repeat discrepancies
Repeated errors often indicate a weak process. If the same SKU is frequently mis-picked, the problem may be warehouse labelling. If many payments are unmatched, the payment-reference process may need improvement.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
At daily close, a store has 45 orders. Forty-two reconcile correctly. One is paid but not allocated to stock, one is dispatched without a tracking number and one has a refund recorded in the payment system but not in the order system. These three become exceptions for investigation rather than being hidden by changing statuses.

## Practical Activity
Design a daily e-commerce reconciliation checklist covering orders, payment status, stock allocation, fulfilment, tracking, refunds and unresolved exceptions. Add an owner and resolution deadline for every discrepancy.

## Knowledge Check
1. What does reconciliation mean?
2. Give two examples of order/payment discrepancies.
3. Why are exception reports useful?
4. Why should failed deliveries remain visible?
5. What can repeated discrepancies reveal?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Comparing related records to make sure they agree.
2. Examples include paid orders without payment records or duplicate payments.
3. They direct attention to transactions requiring investigation.
4. The operational problem is not yet resolved.
5. They may reveal a weak workflow, system configuration or training problem.

## Key Takeaways
- Orders, payments, stock, fulfilment and delivery records should agree.
- Reconciliation identifies missing, duplicated or inconsistent transactions.
- Exception reports focus staff attention on unresolved risk.
- Refunds and cancellations must be included in operational control.
- Repeated discrepancies should lead to process improvement.
