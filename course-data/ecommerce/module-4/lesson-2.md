# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 4: Orders, Payments, Fulfilment & Delivery
### Lesson 2: Payment Methods, Gateways & Payment Processing

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the role of payment gateways and payment processors.
2. Distinguish between common online payment methods.
3. Identify important payment-status concepts.
4. Recognise payment-processing risks and controls.
5. Apply a basic payment-verification workflow.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Payment is one of the most sensitive stages in e-commerce. A customer may complete checkout successfully while a payment is still pending, declined or under review. Staff must understand how payment systems communicate status and must not rely on screenshots, customer statements or page appearance alone.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Payment gateway** — A service that securely transmits payment information and transaction requests.
- **Payment processor** — A service involved in processing payment instructions between relevant parties.
- **Authorisation** — Approval indicating that a payment request may proceed.
- **Capture** — The process of completing or collecting an authorised payment where the payment model uses separate stages.
- **Settlement** — Movement of funds through the payment system after processing.
- **Declined payment** — A payment request that was not approved.
- **Refund** — Money returned to a customer after a payment has been completed.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Understand the payment flow
A customer selects a payment method, submits payment details through the approved interface and receives a result from the payment service. The store then records the relevant payment status against the order.

Different payment methods may include cards, instant electronic payment options, bank transfer, digital wallets, buy-now-pay-later services or other approved methods supported by the merchant.

### 2. Trust system evidence
Staff should confirm payment through the store's approved payment or order-management system. A screenshot sent by a customer is not the same as verified payment confirmation. The business should define which system is authoritative.

### 3. Understand status differences
Payment states can include pending, authorised, paid, failed, cancelled, refunded or partially refunded. The labels depend on the provider, but staff must understand what each one permits operationally.

A pending payment should not automatically trigger dispatch. Likewise, a refund request does not mean money has already been returned until the refund has been processed and recorded.

### 4. Protect payment information
Businesses should avoid storing sensitive payment details unnecessarily and should use approved payment interfaces and provider processes. Staff should never ask customers to send full card details through ordinary email or chat.

### 5. Reconcile payments and orders
Payment reconciliation means matching payment records to the correct orders. This helps detect missing payments, duplicates, partial payments or mismatched references.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A customer says they paid for order #2041 and sends a bank screenshot. The order dashboard still shows payment pending. Staff do not release the parcel. They check the approved payment records, verify the correct reference and only continue fulfilment after the payment state is confirmed.

## Practical Activity
Create a payment-status table with the statuses pending, paid, failed, refunded and partially refunded. For each status, write what staff may or may not do with the order.

## Knowledge Check
1. What is the role of a payment gateway?
2. Why is a screenshot not sufficient payment evidence?
3. What is the difference between paid and refunded?
4. Why is reconciliation important?
5. What should staff avoid asking customers to send through ordinary chat?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. It securely handles payment requests between the store and payment service.
2. It may be incomplete, altered or unrelated to the actual order record.
3. Paid means money was successfully collected; refunded means money was subsequently returned.
4. Reconciliation helps match transactions to orders and detect errors.
5. Sensitive payment credentials such as full card details should not be requested through ordinary chat.

## Key Takeaways
- Payment status must be verified through approved systems.
- Different payment methods can follow different processing flows.
- Pending, paid, failed and refunded are operationally different states.
- Sensitive payment information must be handled carefully.
- Payment reconciliation supports financial and order accuracy.
