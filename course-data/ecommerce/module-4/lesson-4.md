# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 4: Orders, Payments, Fulfilment & Delivery
### Lesson 4: Shipping Methods, Delivery Zones & Delivery Costs

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain common e-commerce delivery methods.
2. Define delivery zones and shipping rules.
3. Calculate and review basic delivery charges.
4. Identify risks caused by unclear delivery information.
5. Match delivery options to customer and business needs.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Delivery is part of the customer's purchase decision. A store may offer good products at competitive prices but still lose sales if delivery charges are unclear, service areas are wrong or promised delivery times are unrealistic. Delivery rules must therefore be commercially practical and clearly communicated.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Delivery zone** — A geographic area served under a defined delivery rule.
- **Shipping rate** — The amount charged for delivery.
- **Flat rate** — One standard delivery charge under defined conditions.
- **Free-shipping threshold** — An order value above which delivery is not separately charged under the promotion or rule.
- **Collection** — A fulfilment option where the customer collects the order from an approved location.
- **Estimated delivery time** — The expected period between dispatch or order processing and delivery.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Choose delivery methods intentionally
Common methods include courier delivery, local same-day delivery, pickup-point delivery and customer collection. A business may offer more than one method, but every option must be operationally supported.

### 2. Build delivery zones
Delivery zones can be based on provinces, cities, postal codes or service regions. The business should understand which locations each courier or delivery partner can serve and what those services cost.

Incorrect zone settings can cause the store to accept an order for an area the business cannot serve or charge the customer an incorrect amount.

### 3. Set clear shipping rates
A rate may be flat, weight-based, value-based, location-based or calculated by an integrated service. The business must understand the commercial effect of the rule. A “free delivery” offer still has a cost that the business must absorb or recover through its pricing model.

### 4. Communicate delivery expectations
Customers should be able to understand material delivery charges and important conditions before finalising the transaction. Estimated timeframes should be realistic and should distinguish processing time from courier transit time where necessary.

### 5. Test delivery rules
Before launch or after changing rates, test several addresses and order values. Check normal delivery, remote-area cases, collection, free-delivery thresholds and unavailable regions.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A store charges R80 delivery for orders below R800 and offers free delivery from R800. A customer's basket is R750. Adding a R60 accessory increases the order to R810, so the shipping rule removes the separate R80 delivery charge. The business must still ensure that the offer remains commercially sustainable.

## Practical Activity
Design delivery rules for a fictional store serving Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal and the Western Cape. Include a normal rate, a free-shipping threshold, an unavailable region rule and a collection option.

3. What should happen when delivery rates are changed?
4. Why should processing time and transit time sometimes be separated?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. A geographic area governed by defined delivery rules.
2. The courier or fulfilment cost still exists even when the customer is not charged separately.
3. The rules should be tested using realistic addresses and order scenarios.
4. They are different stages and may affect the total expected delivery period.
5. Examples include courier delivery, pickup point or collection.

## Key Takeaways
- Delivery design affects conversion, cost and customer trust.
- Zones and rates must match real operational capabilities.
- Free delivery should be commercially understood.
- Delivery information should be clear before checkout completion.
- Shipping rules should be tested after configuration changes.
