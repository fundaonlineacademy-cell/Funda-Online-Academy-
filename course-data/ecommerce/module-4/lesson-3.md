# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 4: Orders, Payments, Fulfilment & Delivery
### Lesson 3: Picking, Packing & Fulfilment Accuracy

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of picking and packing controls.
2. Match products and variants accurately to customer orders.
3. Select appropriate packaging for common e-commerce orders.
4. Identify fulfilment errors before dispatch.
5. Apply a practical pack-check process.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A customer may have a perfect shopping and payment experience but still lose trust if the wrong item arrives. Fulfilment converts the digital order into a physical parcel. Accuracy, product protection and clear records therefore matter just as much as speed.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Picking** — Selecting the exact ordered items from stock.
- **Packing** — Preparing and protecting items for dispatch.
- **Pick list** — A record showing the items and quantities that must be selected.
- **Pack check** — A final verification that the parcel matches the order.
- **Fulfilment error** — A mistake such as wrong product, variant, quantity or missing item.
- **Packaging material** — Boxes, mailers, cushioning, tape and other materials used to protect an order.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Pick from the order, not memory
Staff should use the approved order or pick list and verify SKU, product name, variant and quantity. Similar products should never be distinguished by appearance alone when SKU or barcode information is available.

### 2. Prevent variant mistakes
Colour, size, storage capacity and model differences often look similar in a warehouse. The correct variant should be checked against the order before packing. One wrong size can create a return even when the product family is correct.

### 3. Choose suitable packaging
Packaging should protect the product while remaining practical. Fragile goods may need cushioning, liquids may require leak controls and clothing may require moisture protection. Excessive packaging can increase cost and waste, while insufficient packaging increases damage risk.

### 4. Perform a final pack check
Before sealing the parcel, confirm:
- order number;
- recipient details where operationally appropriate;
- correct products and variants;
- correct quantities;
- required accessories or inserts;
- product condition; and
- suitable packaging.

The pack check should happen before the parcel enters the dispatch process.

### 5. Record discrepancies
If an item is missing, damaged or unavailable, staff should stop and record the issue rather than substitute another product without authority. Customer communication may be required before the order proceeds.

## Practical Activity
Create a 10-point fulfilment checklist for a three-item order containing one fragile product, one clothing item and one accessory. Include product verification, packaging and final quality control.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should staff pick from an approved order record?
2. What information helps distinguish similar variants?
3. Why can too little packaging be costly?
4. When should a pack check occur?
5. What should staff do if the ordered item is unavailable?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. It reduces reliance on memory and supports traceability.
2. SKU, barcode, model, size and colour information can distinguish variants.
3. Insufficient packaging can cause damage, returns and customer complaints.
4. Before the parcel is sealed and dispatched.
5. Record and escalate the exception rather than substituting without authority.

## Key Takeaways
- Fulfilment accuracy is as important as fulfilment speed.
- Products and variants must be matched to the order record.
- Packaging should suit the product and delivery risk.
- Final pack checks catch errors before dispatch.
- Exceptions must be documented and resolved properly.
