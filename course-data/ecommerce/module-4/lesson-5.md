# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 4: Orders, Payments, Fulfilment & Delivery
### Lesson 5: Tracking, Delivery Exceptions & Proof of Delivery

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of shipment tracking.
2. Identify common delivery exceptions.
3. Use tracking information to support customer communication.
4. Explain the role of proof of delivery.
5. Escalate delivery problems using evidence.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Once a parcel leaves the warehouse, the customer still expects the store to remain responsible for communication. Tracking information, delivery records and exception handling help the business understand where the parcel is and what action is required when delivery does not proceed normally.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Tracking number** — A reference used to follow a shipment through a delivery network.
- **Delivery exception** — An event that interrupts normal delivery progress.
- **Failed delivery** — An unsuccessful delivery attempt.
- **Proof of delivery (POD)** — Evidence that a shipment was delivered or handed over.
- **Recipient** — The person or authorised party receiving the parcel.
- **Escalation** — Passing an unresolved issue to the person or provider with authority to act.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Record tracking information accurately
When a parcel is dispatched, the tracking number should be linked to the correct order. A wrong tracking number can cause confusion and expose another customer's delivery information.

### 2. Understand common exceptions
Exceptions may include invalid address information, recipient unavailable, weather disruption, damaged parcel, incorrect routing, delivery refusal or a parcel held at a depot. The correct response depends on the cause.

### 3. Communicate from evidence
Customer service should use current courier or delivery records rather than assumptions. If tracking shows a delay, staff should explain what is known, what action has been taken and when the next update can reasonably be expected.

### 4. Use proof of delivery carefully
Proof of delivery can include a delivery scan, signature, collection confirmation or other evidence supported by the delivery process. POD is useful when investigating claims that a parcel was not received, but staff should review the evidence rather than treating every delivery scan as automatically conclusive.

### 5. Escalate unresolved cases
A parcel that stops moving, is damaged, is delivered to the wrong place or remains unresolved after repeated attempts should be escalated according to the business's courier and customer-service procedure.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
Tracking for order #610 shows “delivery attempted — recipient unavailable.” The customer says no one called. Staff record the complaint, confirm the delivery details, contact the courier where required and arrange the next appropriate step instead of simply marking the order complete.

## Practical Activity
Create an exception-handling table for five situations: wrong address, failed delivery, damaged parcel, missing tracking updates and disputed delivery. For each, record evidence to check, action to take and who may need to be contacted.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why must a tracking number be linked to the correct order?
2. Give three examples of delivery exceptions.
3. What is proof of delivery?
4. Why should staff communicate from evidence rather than assumptions?
5. When should a delivery problem be escalated?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. It protects traceability and prevents customers from receiving incorrect shipment information.
2. Examples include invalid address, failed delivery and damaged parcel.
3. Evidence that the parcel was delivered or handed over.
4. Evidence supports accurate communication and fair investigation.
5. When normal resolution fails or the issue creates significant customer or operational risk.

## Key Takeaways
- Tracking links delivery events to the order record.
- Delivery exceptions require investigation and documented action.
- Proof of delivery supports dispute investigation.
- Customer communication should reflect verified information.
- Unresolved delivery problems should be escalated systematically.
