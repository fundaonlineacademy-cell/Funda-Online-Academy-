# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 3: Products, Listings, Pricing & Merchandising
### Lesson 7: Online Merchandising, Cross-selling & Product Discovery

**Estimated study time:** 30–35 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of online merchandising.
2. Distinguish between cross-selling and upselling.
3. Use product placement and collections to support discovery.
4. Identify merchandising practices that create poor customer experiences.
5. Recommend simple merchandising improvements for an online store.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Merchandising determines how products are presented across the store, not just how each product page looks. Good merchandising helps customers discover relevant products, compare options and understand relationships between items. Poor merchandising can bury strong products, promote irrelevant items or make the store feel disorganised.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Merchandising** — The planned presentation and arrangement of products to support customer needs and commercial goals.
- **Cross-sell** — Recommending a complementary product.
- **Upsell** — Recommending a higher-value or more capable alternative.
- **Featured product** — A product given prominent placement.
- **Related products** — Products connected by use, category or customer relevance.
- **Product discovery** — The process through which customers find relevant products.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Merchandise with a purpose
Products should not be featured randomly. A store may highlight best sellers, new arrivals, seasonal items, high-stock products or strategic ranges, but the reason should be clear.

A useful merchandising decision supports both customer relevance and business goals. For example, placing winter jackets prominently during cold weather is more useful than continuing to feature summer stock simply because it was previously selected.

### 2. Use cross-selling appropriately
Cross-selling recommends complementary products. A customer buying a laptop may also need a protective sleeve or mouse. A customer buying a drill may need suitable drill bits.

The recommendation should be genuinely relevant. Unrelated add-ons can feel intrusive and reduce trust.

### 3. Use upselling carefully
Upselling encourages the customer to consider a higher-value alternative, such as a larger storage capacity or a model with additional features.

The higher-priced option should offer a clear benefit. Simply pushing the most expensive product without explaining its value is poor merchandising.

### 4. Create useful product groups
Collections such as Best Sellers, New Arrivals, Staff Picks or Back to School can make browsing easier. These groups should be reviewed regularly so they remain accurate.

A “New Arrivals” collection containing products added two years ago weakens credibility. Automated collection rules can help, but they still require quality checks.

### 5. Consider placement and sequence
The order in which products appear affects what customers notice first. Sorting may use relevance, popularity, newest items, price or manual priority.

The appropriate method depends on the page. Search results should prioritise relevance, while a campaign landing page may use deliberate manual placement.

### 6. Avoid manipulative merchandising
Merchandising should help customers decide, not pressure them unfairly. False scarcity, fake popularity messages or misleading “only one left” notices can damage trust and may create compliance concerns.

Urgency indicators should be based on genuine information where they are used.

### 7. Review performance
Merchandising should be tested and reviewed. Useful indicators include clicks, product-page visits, conversion, average order value and attachment rate for recommended items.

A recommendation that receives many clicks but rarely converts may not be as useful as it appears.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
**Scenario:** An online beauty store sells shampoo, conditioner and hair treatments. Customers often buy shampoo alone.

**Improvement:** The store adds a “Complete the Routine” section recommending the matching conditioner and treatment. It also creates a seasonal collection for dry-hair products.

**Result:** Customers receive relevant guidance while the store creates a legitimate opportunity to increase basket value.

## Practical Activity
Choose five imaginary products and create a merchandising plan. Identify one featured placement, two cross-sell relationships, one upsell relationship and one themed collection. Explain the customer benefit of each choice.

## Knowledge Check
1. What is the difference between cross-selling and upselling?
2. Why should featured products be reviewed regularly?
3. Give one example of a useful cross-sell.
4. Why can false scarcity damage a store?
5. Name one measure that can help evaluate merchandising performance.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Cross-selling recommends complementary items; upselling suggests a higher-value alternative.
2. Customer needs, seasonality, stock and business priorities change over time.
3. For example, a phone case with a phone.
4. It can mislead customers and reduce trust.
5. Examples include conversion rate, clicks, average order value or attachment rate.

## Key Takeaways
- Merchandising connects product presentation with customer discovery.
- Cross-sells should be complementary and relevant.
- Upsells should offer clear additional value.
- Product collections and placement require regular review.
- Good merchandising supports decisions without misleading customers.
