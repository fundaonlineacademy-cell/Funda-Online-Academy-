# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 3: Products, Listings, Pricing & Merchandising
### Lesson 3: Product Images, Media & Digital Presentation

**Estimated study time:** 30–35 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain how visual presentation influences customer understanding and confidence.
2. Select appropriate product images and media for different product types.
3. Apply basic consistency, accessibility and quality standards to product media.
4. Identify misleading, incomplete or low-quality visual presentation.
5. Perform a simple product-media quality check before publication.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Online customers often depend heavily on images because they cannot physically handle the product before buying. Visual content therefore needs to do more than make a page attractive. It should help customers understand appearance, scale, colour, features, condition and important differences between variants.

Poor images can create uncertainty and returns. A customer may receive an item that looks different from the listing, choose the wrong colour because variant images are unclear, or misunderstand size because there is no useful scale reference. Accurate visual presentation is therefore part of both conversion and responsible selling.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Hero image** — The main product image shown first on a listing.
- **Gallery** — A collection of images or media showing different product views.
- **Variant image** — An image linked to a particular colour, style or configuration.
- **Alt text** — Text describing the purpose or important information of an image for users who may not see it.
- **Aspect ratio** — The proportional relationship between an image's width and height.
- **Resolution** — The amount of image detail available for display.
- **Lifestyle image** — An image showing a product in a realistic use context.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Show the real product clearly
The main image should make the product easy to recognise. It should be well lit, sharp enough for the intended display and free from unnecessary distractions. Where possible, the first image should show the complete product rather than an extreme close-up.

The visual must correspond to what is actually being sold. If accessories shown in an image are not included, the listing should make that clear. Do not use an image of a premium model when selling a lower specification simply because the products look similar.

### 2. Use multiple useful views
One image is often insufficient. Customers may need front, back, side, close-up and in-use views. Clothing may require fabric detail and fit context. Furniture may need dimensions or room-scale context. Electronics may require ports, controls and included accessories.

Every additional image should answer a useful customer question. Repeating nearly identical views adds clutter without adding information.

### 3. Match images to variants
When a product is sold in several colours or styles, images should clearly correspond to the selected variant. If a customer chooses black, the gallery should not continue showing only the white version without explanation.

Variant-image errors are especially risky because they can lead directly to wrong purchases and returns. The catalogue team should test that every selectable option links to the correct visual content.

### 4. Maintain visual consistency
A store looks more professional when product imagery follows common standards. Useful controls include consistent image proportions, background approach, framing, naming conventions and minimum resolution.

Consistency also improves browsing. When thumbnail sizes and crop styles change dramatically from product to product, customers may struggle to compare items quickly.

### 5. Optimise without destroying quality
Large images can slow page loading, especially for customers using mobile data. Images should therefore be compressed appropriately and served at sensible dimensions. The goal is to reduce file size without making the product blurry or difficult to inspect.

Video and 360-degree media can be useful for complex products, but they should add genuine information. Media that delays the page or distracts from important purchasing information can reduce usability.

### 6. Support accessibility
Meaningful product images should have appropriate alternative text where required by the platform. Alt text should communicate useful information rather than repeat file names such as `IMG0045.jpg`.

For example, “Black leather ankle boot with side zip and low heel” is more useful than “boot image”. Decorative graphics may not need descriptive alt text, depending on implementation.

### 7. Avoid misleading presentation
Editing should improve clarity, not change the apparent product. Excessive colour changes, unrealistic proportions, removed defects on second-hand goods or digitally added features can mislead customers.

Where a visual is a mock-up, illustration or representative image, the context should be clear. Accurate presentation protects customer trust and reduces disputes.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
**Scenario:** A homeware store sells a 1.2-metre wooden bench in three finishes. The current listing shows only one tightly cropped image.

**Improvement:** The store adds a full-product hero image, side view, close-up of the wood grain, an in-room lifestyle image and a dimensions graphic. Each finish has its own correctly linked variant image.

**Result:** Customers can better judge appearance, scale and finish before buying.

## Practical Activity
Choose one imaginary product and design a six-item media plan. Include the hero image, additional views, one detail image, one contextual image and any useful video or graphic. For each item, state what customer question it should answer.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should product images match the exact item or variant being sold?
2. What is the purpose of a hero image?
3. Give two reasons why multiple product views may be necessary.
4. Why should images be optimised for web use?
5. What makes alt text useful?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Incorrect images can mislead customers and cause wrong purchases, returns and complaints.
2. It provides the primary visual identification of the product.
3. They can show different angles, details, scale, features or included components.
4. Optimisation improves loading performance while retaining enough detail for customers.
5. It communicates meaningful visual information clearly for users who cannot access the image visually.

## Key Takeaways
- Product media should inform customers, not merely decorate the listing.
- Images must accurately represent the item and selected variant.
- Consistency improves professionalism and comparison.
- Useful visual context can reduce uncertainty and returns.
- Accessibility and page performance are part of good product presentation.
