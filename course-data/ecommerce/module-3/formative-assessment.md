## Lesson 1 — Product Information, SKUs, Variants & Catalogue Data

### Q1
What is the main purpose of a SKU in an e-commerce operation?
A. To replace the product title shown to customers
B. To uniquely identify a sellable item or variant for internal management
C. To guarantee that a product will sell
D. To calculate delivery distance

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** A SKU is an internal identifier used to distinguish and manage a specific sellable item or variant across stock, orders and fulfilment records.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q2
A T-shirt is sold in black and white, each in sizes Small, Medium and Large. If each combination is stocked separately, what is the best catalogue structure?
A. One product with six variants linked to separate stock records
B. Six unrelated products with no connection
C. One product with one shared stock quantity for all options
D. One product with no variant information

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Each colour-size combination is a distinct sellable variant and should be linked to the correct stock record.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q3
Which of the following is best classified as a product specification?
A. Brand
B. Colour
C. Weight: 1.8 kg
D. Category

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Specifications are precise technical or measurable facts such as dimensions, weight, capacity or power rating.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

### Q4
A supplier spreadsheet says a storage container holds 20 litres, while the manufacturer's current specification says 18 litres. What should the catalogue operator do?
A. Use 20 litres because it sounds more attractive
B. Use whichever value is easiest to type
C. Resolve the discrepancy against an approved source of truth before publication
D. Publish both values without explanation

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Conflicting product information should be verified before publication. Guessing can create misleading listings and customer complaints.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 1

## Lesson 2 — Writing Effective Product Titles & Descriptions

### Q5
Which product title is generally the clearest for an online listing?
A. Amazing Deal!!!
B. Shirt Product 004
C. Men's Cotton Oxford Shirt — Navy, Long Sleeve
D. Best Product Ever Buy Now

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** A strong title identifies the product clearly using useful distinguishing information without unnecessary hype.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q6
What is the main difference between a feature and a benefit in product copy?
A. A feature describes a product characteristic; a benefit explains why that characteristic matters to the customer
B. A feature is always emotional; a benefit is always technical
C. A feature is a price; a benefit is a SKU
D. There is no difference

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Features describe what the product has or does, while benefits connect those features to customer value or use.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q7
Which statement is the best practice when writing a product description?
A. Copy claims from competitors without checking them
B. Use clear, verifiable information and avoid unsupported promises
C. Hide important limitations in very small text
D. Repeat keywords unnaturally in every sentence

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Product descriptions should help customers make informed decisions using accurate, understandable and supportable information.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

### Q8
A seller describes an ordinary water bottle as “guaranteed to cure dehydration-related illness.” What is the main problem with this wording?
A. It is too short
B. It may be an unsupported or misleading claim
C. It does not include a SKU
D. It uses a benefit instead of a feature

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Product copy should not make claims that cannot be verified or that may mislead customers about the product's capabilities.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 2

## Lesson 3 — Product Images, Media & Digital Presentation

### Q9
What is the primary purpose of product images in an online store?
A. To decorate pages only
B. To help customers evaluate the product before purchase
C. To replace all written information
D. To hide product defects

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Product images help customers understand appearance, scale, features and relevant details they cannot inspect physically.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q10
Which image set is most useful for a physical product?
A. One blurry image taken from a distance
B. Several clear views showing the product and relevant details
C. Only the company logo
D. A stock image of a different model

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Multiple clear views reduce uncertainty and help customers evaluate important details.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q11
Why should product images accurately represent the item being sold?
A. Because inaccurate images can create unrealistic expectations and disputes
B. Because all online stores must use identical photography
C. Because images determine the SKU automatically
D. Because accurate images remove the need for product descriptions

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Images form part of the product representation and should not materially mislead customers about what they will receive.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

### Q12
What is the best use of alternative text for an informative product image?
A. Fill it with unrelated keywords
B. Describe the useful information or purpose of the image concisely
C. Leave every image blank regardless of purpose
D. Repeat the full product description word for word

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Meaningful alternative text can support customers who use assistive technology by communicating relevant visual information.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 3

## Lesson 5 — Pricing, Mark-up, Margin & VAT

### Q17
A product costs R400 and is sold for R500. What is the gross profit before other expenses?
A. R50
B. R100
C. R400
D. R900

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Gross profit on the item is selling price minus cost price: R500 − R400 = R100.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

### Q18
Which statement correctly distinguishes mark-up from margin?
A. Mark-up is based on cost, while margin is based on selling price
B. Mark-up and margin are always identical percentages
C. Margin is based on cost, while mark-up is based on tax
D. Mark-up is a stock quantity and margin is a delivery fee

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Mark-up expresses profit relative to cost, while gross margin expresses profit relative to selling price.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

### Q19
A product costs R200 and the business applies a 25% mark-up on cost. What is the selling price before any other adjustments?
A. R225
B. R240
C. R250
D. R275

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** A 25% mark-up on R200 is R50, giving a selling price of R250.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

### Q20
Why should a South African retailer be careful about whether displayed prices include VAT where applicable?
A. Customers need clear and accurate price information and the business must apply the correct tax treatment
B. VAT affects only product colours
C. VAT is optional for every seller
D. VAT replaces delivery charges

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Pricing should be transparent and consistent with the seller's tax obligations and customer-facing price presentation.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 5

## Lesson 6 — Discounts, Promotions & Promotional Pricing

### Q21
A product normally sells for R800 and is discounted by 20%. What is the promotional price?
A. R160
B. R620
C. R640
D. R780

**Correct answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Twenty percent of R800 is R160, so the discounted price is R800 − R160 = R640.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q22
Which promotional practice is the most responsible?
A. Claim a fake “was” price to make the discount look larger
B. Clearly state the offer, qualifying conditions and genuine promotional price
C. Hide exclusions until after checkout
D. Use a countdown timer that restarts indefinitely while claiming the sale is ending

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Promotions should be truthful, understandable and transparent about material conditions.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q23
What is a product bundle?
A. A group of products sold together as one offer
B. A duplicate SKU
C. A cancelled order
D. A courier invoice

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Bundles combine products into a single promotional or merchandising offer.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

### Q24
A store offers “10% off orders over R1,000.” A customer's cart is R950 before delivery. What should happen if the promotion rules are based on merchandise subtotal?
A. The discount should apply automatically
B. The customer does not yet meet the R1,000 threshold
C. The store should change the product prices secretly
D. The discount should be doubled

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** If the qualifying threshold is based on merchandise subtotal, R950 does not meet the R1,000 requirement.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 6

## Lesson 7 — Online Merchandising, Cross-selling & Upselling

### Q25
What is cross-selling?
A. Recommending a complementary product related to the customer's current choice
B. Increasing the price after checkout
C. Removing all related products
D. Changing a SKU after purchase

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Cross-selling suggests complementary items, such as a laptop sleeve with a laptop.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q26
What is upselling?
A. Encouraging the customer to consider a higher-value or upgraded version of the product
B. Refunding an order
C. Adding unrelated products to the cart without permission
D. Hiding cheaper alternatives

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Upselling presents a higher-specification, larger or more premium alternative where it may better meet the customer's needs.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q27
Which merchandising choice is most customer-focused?
A. Feature products based on relevance, season, availability and customer need
B. Always feature products with no stock
C. Recommend unrelated items purely because they are expensive
D. Hide important product information to force clicks

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Effective merchandising balances business goals with relevance and usefulness to the customer.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

### Q28
A customer is viewing a camera. Which recommendation is the strongest example of a relevant cross-sell?
A. A compatible memory card
B. A washing machine
C. A garden hose
D. An unrelated office chair

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** A compatible memory card complements the camera and is directly relevant to the purchase.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 7

## Lesson 8 — Catalogue Quality, Listing Maintenance & Product Performance

### Q29
Why should product listings be reviewed after they are published?
A. Product, price, stock and supplier information can change over time
B. Published listings can never become inaccurate
C. Reviews are needed only for website colours
D. Maintenance automatically reduces inventory

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Product records need ongoing maintenance because source information, availability and business conditions change.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q30
Which issue should normally receive the highest correction priority?
A. Slightly inconsistent thumbnail spacing
B. An incorrect selling price on a popular product
C. A minor punctuation difference in an internal note
D. A category image that could be sharper

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** An incorrect selling price can directly affect transactions, customer expectations and financial outcomes.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q31
What is the purpose of a catalogue change log?
A. To record important updates such as what changed, why, when and by whom
B. To replace product descriptions
C. To store customer card details
D. To calculate courier routes

**Correct answer:** A  
**Explanation:** Change logs improve traceability and help teams investigate when and why important catalogue information was modified.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8

### Q32
A product receives many page views but very few purchases. What is the best response?
A. Assume the product is defective immediately
B. Investigate possible causes such as price, images, description, availability and customer fit
C. Delete all performance data
D. Increase the price without analysis

**Correct answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Performance indicators identify where investigation is needed but do not prove the cause by themselves.  
**Traceability:** Lesson 8
