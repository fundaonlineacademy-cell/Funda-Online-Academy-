# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 8: Practical Online Store Project
### Lesson 8: Final Project Presentation, Performance Plan & Continuous Improvement

**Estimated study time:** 40–45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Present the capstone store as an integrated e-commerce project.
2. Explain key commercial, operational and compliance decisions.
3. Summarise test evidence and unresolved low-risk issues.
4. Define launch and post-launch performance measures.
5. Build a practical 30-day improvement plan.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A professional project is more than a collection of pages. The learner should be able to explain why the store was designed in a particular way, how the numbers work, how orders will be fulfilled, what customer protections are in place and how performance will be monitored. The final presentation demonstrates both practical execution and decision-making.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Project evidence** — Records showing the work completed and decisions made.
- **Executive summary** — A concise overview of the project and its key outcomes.
- **KPI baseline** — The starting value against which future performance is compared.
- **Improvement backlog** — Prioritised list of future changes or enhancements.
- **Post-launch review** — Structured review of performance and issues after launch.
- **Lessons learned** — Insights gained from building and testing the project.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Tell the project story
The presentation should explain the business concept, customer need, store requirements, catalogue, pricing, operations, marketing and launch-readiness outcome. The learner should show how these areas connect rather than presenting them as unrelated tasks.

### 2. Show evidence
Useful evidence includes product-data sheets, pricing calculations, policy drafts, test cases, campaign plans, screenshots and reconciliation records. Evidence should support the learner's claims about what works.

### 3. Explain key decisions
The learner should be able to justify category structure, delivery rates, promotion rules, security controls and marketing choices. A decision is stronger when it connects to customer needs, cost, risk or verified requirements.

### 4. Define launch KPIs
Choose a small set of meaningful measures. These may include conversion rate, average order value, order accuracy, delivery exceptions, return rate, first response time, customer complaints, paid-media CPA or repeat-purchase indicators.

### 5. Build a 30-day review plan
The first month should include regular checks of orders, payments, customer support, returns, marketing performance and technical issues. The goal is to identify problems early rather than waiting until the end of the month.

### 6. Prioritise improvements
Not every improvement deserves equal priority. Security, payment, pricing, customer harm and fulfilment risks should normally be addressed before cosmetic enhancements. The backlog should record priority, owner and expected outcome.

### 7. Reflect on lessons learned
Document what worked well, what was difficult and what would be done differently in a future store build. Reflection helps turn the capstone into transferable business skill.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
StudyBox SA presents its 12-product catalogue, pricing sheet, tested checkout and launch campaign. The learner explains that the main launch risks were delivery-zone configuration and mobile checkout usability. Both were corrected and retested. The 30-day plan monitors conversion, delivery exceptions, returns, support response time and campaign CPA.

## Practical Activity
Prepare a final project pack containing: executive summary, store map, product catalogue evidence, pricing sheet, policy checklist, order workflow, marketing plan, launch-test report, KPI dashboard design and 30-day improvement backlog.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should the final presentation include evidence?
2. What is a KPI baseline?
3. Why should the project explain decisions rather than only show pages?
4. Which types of improvements should normally receive highest priority?
5. What is the purpose of lessons learned?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Evidence demonstrates that the project was actually built and tested.
2. A starting performance value used for future comparison.
3. E-commerce work requires reasoning about customer, commercial and operational trade-offs.
4. Security, transaction, customer-harm and serious operational risks.
5. To capture insights that improve future projects.

## Key Takeaways
- The capstone should be presented as one connected e-commerce system.
- Evidence supports the credibility of project decisions.
- KPIs create a plan for post-launch measurement.
- Improvement work should be prioritised by risk and business impact.
- Reflection turns the project into reusable professional experience.
