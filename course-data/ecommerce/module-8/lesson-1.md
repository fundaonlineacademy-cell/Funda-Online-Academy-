# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 8: Practical Online Store Project
### Lesson 1: Capstone Brief, Business Concept & Store Requirements

**Estimated study time:** 40–45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Define a realistic online-store concept for the capstone project.
2. Identify target customers and the problem the store will solve.
3. Document functional, operational and commercial requirements.
4. Set project scope and success criteria.
5. Create a practical project brief before building.

## Why This Lesson Matters
The final project brings together the full course. A strong online store should not begin with random design choices. It begins with a clear business concept, customer need and set of requirements. The project brief becomes the reference point for later decisions about products, pricing, delivery, customer service, marketing and security.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Capstone project** — A final practical project combining knowledge and skills from the course.
- **Business concept** — The core idea describing what the store sells and who it serves.
- **Requirement** — A capability, condition or outcome the store must satisfy.
- **Scope** — The boundaries of what the project will and will not include.
- **Success criterion** — A measurable condition used to judge whether the project has achieved its goal.
- **Constraint** — A limit such as budget, time, stock or technical capability.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Choose a realistic concept
Select a store idea that is specific enough to build properly. Examples might include locally produced homeware, fitness accessories, stationery bundles or specialist clothing. The project should have a manageable catalogue rather than hundreds of imaginary products.

### 2. Define the target customer
Describe the customer problem, need or buying situation. Avoid vague statements such as “everyone is my customer.” A clearer target may be “South African university students looking for affordable study and stationery bundles delivered nationally.”

### 3. Document requirements
Functional requirements may include search, cart, checkout, payment, order confirmation and tracking. Operational requirements may include stock control, picking, packing, returns and support. Commercial requirements include pricing, margin targets, delivery rules and marketing objectives.

### 4. Set scope
The capstone should demonstrate quality, not unnecessary size. Define the number of products, categories, payment methods, delivery options and campaign assets you will build. Exclude features that cannot be completed or tested properly.

### 5. Define success criteria
Success criteria might include a complete customer journey, accurate product data, transparent pricing, tested checkout, documented policies, working delivery rules and a basic launch campaign.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A learner chooses a fictional store called StudyBox SA selling curated stationery bundles. The project scope includes 12 products, three categories, card/electronic payment through an approved test flow, courier delivery, a returns policy and a two-week launch campaign. The success criteria require a customer to find a bundle, understand the price, place a test order and receive accurate confirmation.

## Practical Activity
Write a one-page **Online Store Project Brief** containing store name, business concept, target customer, customer problem, product range, five functional requirements, five operational requirements, three commercial requirements, constraints and eight success criteria.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should the project start with requirements?
2. What is project scope?
3. Why should the target customer be specific?
4. Give two operational requirements.
5. What is a success criterion?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Requirements guide later platform, content and operational decisions.
2. The boundaries of what the project includes.
3. Specific customers make product, message and service decisions clearer.
4. Examples include stock control, picking, packing, returns or support.
5. A measurable condition used to judge project success.

## Key Takeaways
- The capstone should begin with a clear business concept and customer need.
- Requirements should cover technology, operations and commercial goals.
- Scope keeps the project achievable and testable.
- Success criteria make final evaluation objective.
- Every later project decision should connect back to the brief.
