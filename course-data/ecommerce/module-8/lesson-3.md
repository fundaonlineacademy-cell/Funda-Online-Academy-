# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 8: Practical Online Store Project
### Lesson 3: Set Pricing, Promotions & Merchandising

**Estimated study time:** 40–45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Calculate sustainable selling prices for project products.
2. Distinguish markup, gross profit and margin.
3. Design a transparent promotional offer.
4. Apply cross-selling, upselling and collection merchandising.
5. Review pricing and merchandising for customer and commercial fit.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A practical store needs prices that make commercial sense. Random pricing weakens the project because it disconnects the catalogue from costs, delivery, promotions and profitability. Merchandising should then help customers discover relevant products without misleading them or pushing unrelated items.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Cost price** — Amount paid to obtain or produce the item.
- **Markup** — Amount added to cost.
- **Gross profit** — Selling price less product cost.
- **Gross margin** — Gross profit expressed as a percentage of selling price.
- **Promotional price** — Temporary or conditional selling price used in a campaign.
- **Cross-sell** — A relevant complementary product recommendation.
- **Upsell** — A relevant higher-value or more capable alternative.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Establish cost assumptions
For every project product, document a realistic cost. Add other relevant assumptions such as packaging, payment fees, delivery subsidy or marketplace charges where applicable. The project should show that selling price was chosen deliberately.

### 2. Calculate price and margin
If an item costs R200 and sells for R320, gross profit is R120. Markup on cost is 60%, while gross margin is 37.5% because R120 ÷ R320 × 100 = 37.5%. These percentages are different and should not be confused.

### 3. Consider customer-facing price presentation
Prices should be clear and consistent. Where VAT treatment applies to the project, it should be configured and presented appropriately. Delivery costs and material transaction charges should not appear as unpleasant surprises late in checkout.

### 4. Create one genuine promotion
Build a promotion such as 10% off a selected collection, a real bundle saving or free delivery above a threshold. Record the normal price, promotional rule, campaign period and commercial impact. Do not manufacture a false “was” price.

### 5. Merchandise intentionally
Create featured products, best-seller or new-arrival labels only where the project has a defensible rule. Build related-product recommendations that make sense. A notebook may be cross-sold with pens; a larger stationery bundle may be an upsell from a starter bundle.

### 6. Review the customer journey
Check whether pricing, collections and recommendations help the shopper understand choices or create clutter. Merchandising should reduce decision friction rather than simply placing more products everywhere.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A StudyBox SA bundle costs R280 and sells for R420. Gross profit is R140 and gross margin is 33.3%. The learner creates a launch promotion at R399 for one week, calculates the reduced gross profit, and verifies that the promotion remains commercially acceptable before publishing it.

## Practical Activity
Create a pricing sheet for all project products showing cost, selling price, gross profit and gross margin. Add one promotional campaign and three merchandising placements. Explain why each placement is relevant to the customer.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should project prices be linked to cost assumptions?
2. What is the difference between markup and margin?
3. Why is a false reference price problematic?
4. Give one cross-sell example.
5. What should merchandising help the customer do?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. It demonstrates commercial reasoning.
2. Markup is measured against cost; margin is measured against selling price.
3. It can misrepresent the saving.
4. Example: pens recommended with a notebook.
5. Discover and compare relevant products more easily.

## Key Takeaways
- Practical pricing should be calculated rather than guessed.
- Markup and margin measure different relationships.
- Promotions must be transparent and commercially understood.
- Merchandising should be relevant to customer needs.
- Pricing and recommendations should support a clear customer journey.
