# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 2: Online Store Planning, Platforms & Setup
### Lesson 2: Hosted Platforms, Marketplaces & Self-hosted Stores

**Estimated study time:** 35–45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:
- distinguish between hosted e-commerce platforms, online marketplaces and self-hosted stores;
- compare the strengths, limitations, costs and responsibilities of each model;
- evaluate platform options against business requirements rather than popularity;
- recognise ownership, control and dependency risks; and
- recommend an appropriate store model for a basic business scenario.

## Why This Lesson Matters
The technology model used to sell online affects cost, control, maintenance, customer ownership, marketing freedom and long-term flexibility. A marketplace can provide fast access to buyers but may limit branding and customer relationships. A hosted store can reduce technical maintenance but may create platform dependency. A self-hosted store can offer more control but requires more technical responsibility. Understanding these trade-offs helps entrepreneurs and employees avoid choosing a sales channel that does not fit the business.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
**Hosted platform** — An e-commerce service in which the provider supplies and maintains the main technical infrastructure for the store.

**Marketplace** — A multi-seller online platform where different merchants list products or services for customers using a shared marketplace environment.

**Self-hosted store** — An online store where the business is responsible for arranging hosting, software setup, maintenance and a greater portion of technical administration.

**Platform dependency** — Reliance on a provider's pricing, rules, infrastructure or features to continue operating the store.

**Transaction fee** — A charge linked to a sale or payment, which may be imposed by a marketplace, platform, payment service or other provider.

**Customisation** — The ability to change the appearance, features, workflows or code of a store.

**Data portability** — The ability to export and move business data, such as products, orders or customer information, from one system to another where permitted.

## Main Lesson Content
There is no single best technology model for every e-commerce business. The correct choice depends on the business's products, customers, operational needs, skills, budget and growth plans. Three common models are hosted platforms, marketplaces and self-hosted stores.

A **hosted e-commerce platform** provides the main technical environment as a managed service. The merchant typically creates an account, selects a plan, configures a theme, adds products and connects payments and delivery services. The provider generally manages the core hosting infrastructure, software updates and much of the platform-level security. This makes hosted platforms attractive to businesses without in-house technical teams.

The main advantage of hosted platforms is convenience. Store setup can be faster, the administrative interface is usually designed for non-developers, and support documentation is often available. However, convenience does not mean that the business has no responsibilities. The merchant still needs secure staff passwords, accurate product information, lawful policies, correct pricing, safe account management and appropriate access controls. The provider protects the platform, while the business remains responsible for how it uses the platform.

Hosted platforms can also create dependency. The business may be affected by subscription increases, feature restrictions, changes to application support or transaction fees. Certain custom functions may require paid applications or developers. Therefore, the monthly subscription price should never be the only cost considered. A proper comparison should include payment charges, application costs, theme costs, domain fees, integration fees and any additional services needed for operations.

A **marketplace** is different because the merchant sells inside a shared environment where many sellers compete for buyers. Marketplaces can reduce the challenge of attracting initial traffic because consumers may already visit the platform. They can also provide standardised checkout, payment and sometimes fulfilment services.

The trade-off is reduced independence. Marketplace rules determine what can be sold, how products are listed, what fees apply and what behaviour is allowed. Branding opportunities may be limited, and customer information may be controlled by the marketplace. A business can therefore make sales without building the same level of direct customer relationship that it might build through its own store. Marketplace account suspension or policy changes can also affect revenue quickly.

A **self-hosted store** provides a different balance. The business arranges hosting and runs e-commerce software that it controls more directly. This can provide greater flexibility over design, functionality, integrations and technical architecture. It may also allow more control over how the store evolves over time.

However, greater control usually means greater responsibility. The business may need to manage software updates, backups, hosting quality, performance, security configurations, extensions and technical troubleshooting. A poorly maintained self-hosted store can become unstable or vulnerable. Businesses choosing this model need realistic access to technical knowledge or reliable service providers.

The most useful way to compare these models is to ask practical questions. How quickly must the store launch? Does the business have technical staff? How important is design flexibility? Does the business need unusual integrations? Is an existing marketplace already popular with the target market? How much control does the business require over customer experience and data? What are the total ongoing costs rather than only the advertised entry price?

Businesses can also use more than one channel. A company may operate its own hosted store while listing selected products on a marketplace. This is sometimes called a multi-channel approach. The benefit is wider reach, but the operational challenge is keeping stock, pricing and order processing accurate across channels. Multiple channels should be added because they serve a clear business purpose, not simply because they are available.

The final platform decision should return to the requirements identified in Lesson 1. Technology should serve the business model. A simple, manageable system that staff can use consistently is often more valuable than a powerful system that is difficult to maintain.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
Business A sells standard household products and wants to launch quickly with limited technical skills. A hosted platform may fit because the owner can manage products and orders without maintaining server software.

Business B makes custom industrial components and requires an unusual quotation workflow integrated with internal systems. A self-hosted or customisable solution may be more suitable if the business has technical support.

Business C has a small range of popular consumer products and wants immediate exposure to an existing audience. A marketplace may be useful as one sales channel, but the business should understand fees and the reduced control over customer relationships.

## Practical Activity
Create a three-column comparison titled Hosted Platform, Marketplace and Self-hosted Store. Compare each model on setup speed, technical responsibility, branding control, customer relationship, cost predictability, customisation and dependency. Then choose one model for a hypothetical business and explain your choice in five sentences.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why is a hosted platform usually easier for a non-technical owner to maintain?
2. What is one major disadvantage of relying only on a marketplace?
3. Why can a self-hosted store require more technical support?
4. What does platform dependency mean?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. The provider manages much of the core infrastructure and platform maintenance, reducing the technical work required from the merchant.
2. The business may have limited control over branding, customer relationships, fees and marketplace rules.
3. The business is responsible for more of the hosting, updates, security, backups and technical configuration.
4. It means the business depends on the provider's infrastructure, rules, pricing and continued support to operate.

## Key Takeaways
- Hosted platforms prioritise convenience and managed infrastructure.
- Marketplaces provide access to shared customer traffic but reduce independence.
- Self-hosted stores offer more control but also more technical responsibility.
- Compare total costs, not only advertised subscription prices.
- Select technology by matching it to business requirements and operating capability.
