# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 2: Online Store Planning, Platforms & Setup
### Lesson 3: Domain Names, Hosting & Store Identity

**Estimated study time:** 35–45 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:
- explain the purpose of a domain name and web hosting;
- choose a practical domain name for an online store;
- recognise the difference between a domain registrar, hosting provider and e-commerce platform;
- identify basic store-identity elements that improve customer trust and recognition; and
- apply simple controls for domain ownership, renewal and access security.

## Why This Lesson Matters
A store's web address and online identity affect credibility, discoverability and operational control. Poor domain decisions can confuse customers, weaken branding or even cause the business to lose access to its website if renewal and ownership are not managed properly. Understanding domains, hosting and identity also helps learners communicate effectively with developers, platform providers and service companies.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
**Domain name** — The human-readable internet address used to reach a website, such as example.co.za or example.com.

**Registrar** — A service provider authorised to register and manage domain names on behalf of customers.

**Web hosting** — The computing service that stores and serves website files or applications so that users can access them online.

**DNS** — Domain Name System; the system that directs a domain name to the correct internet service or server.

**SSL/TLS certificate** — A digital certificate used to support encrypted communication between a user's browser and a website, commonly shown through HTTPS.

**Brand identity** — The visual and verbal elements used to help customers recognise a business, including name, logo, typography, imagery and tone.

**Renewal** — The process of extending the registration period of a domain or service before it expires.

## Main Lesson Content
A domain name is one of the most visible pieces of an online store's identity. Customers may type it directly, see it in advertisements, receive it in emails or encounter it in search results. A good domain should therefore be clear, memorable and closely connected to the business.

Choosing a domain begins with the business name and target market. Shorter names are usually easier to remember and type, but clarity is more important than extreme brevity. Complicated spelling, unnecessary numbers, repeated hyphens or words that can easily be misunderstood may create mistakes. The business should also consider whether the name could be confused with another established brand. Before investing heavily in a domain or store identity, the business should conduct reasonable checks for name conflicts and, where necessary, obtain appropriate professional advice about trade marks or legal rights.

The ending of the domain, such as `.co.za` or `.com`, is sometimes called a top-level domain or domain extension. A South African business serving mainly local customers may find a `.co.za` address appropriate because it signals local relevance. A `.com` address may be suitable for a broader or international identity. The extension should support the brand and audience rather than being chosen only because it is fashionable.

A **domain registrar** manages the registration of the domain name. This is different from **hosting**. Hosting provides the infrastructure that makes a website or application available online. In some hosted e-commerce platforms, the platform supplies the hosting as part of the subscription. In a self-hosted arrangement, the business normally chooses a separate hosting provider. A business can also register a domain with one company and point it to a store hosted somewhere else.

The connection between the domain and the store is managed through **DNS records**. DNS acts like an internet directory. Instead of customers remembering a numerical server address, they use the domain name. Correct DNS configuration sends them to the right service. Businesses do not need to become network engineers to run an online store, but owners should understand that changing DNS incorrectly can make a website or business email unavailable.

Security and ownership are critical. The domain should be registered in a way that gives the legitimate business clear control over it. A common risk occurs when an employee, freelancer or developer registers the domain using only their personal account or personal email. If that relationship ends, the business may struggle to recover control. The organisation should keep ownership records, administrator access and recovery information under appropriate business control.

Domain accounts should use strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication where available. Access should be limited to trusted people who need it. Renewal dates should also be monitored carefully. If a domain expires, the website and email may stop working, and in some circumstances the name could eventually become available to someone else. Auto-renewal can help, but the business should still maintain valid payment details and calendar reminders rather than relying on automation without oversight.

Customers also judge a store through its **identity**. A consistent name, logo, colour system, writing style and contact information make the business easier to recognise. Store identity should be used consistently across the website, invoices, social channels, email messages and packaging where appropriate. Consistency does not require expensive design. It requires clear, repeated choices.

Trust also depends on professionalism beyond visual appearance. Customers should be able to identify who they are dealing with, understand how to contact the supplier, find relevant policies and navigate the store without confusion. South African electronic-commerce requirements can impose specific supplier-information and transaction-disclosure duties in applicable situations, so store-identity planning should not be treated as decoration alone. The identity and information architecture should help customers understand the business, products, prices and transaction process.

The store should use HTTPS so that information transmitted between the browser and site is encrypted in transit. Many modern hosted platforms manage certificates automatically, while self-hosted stores may require additional configuration. HTTPS is essential, but learners should understand that it does not prove that a merchant is trustworthy and does not protect against every form of fraud. It is one layer of a broader security approach.

Finally, domain and identity planning should consider future growth. A business may add email accounts, subdomains, new staff or additional storefronts. Keeping account ownership, brand files, renewal information and DNS documentation organised will make future changes safer and easier.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A business called Ubuntu Homeware is considering `ubuntu-homeware-store-2026.co.za`, `ubuntuhomeware.co.za` and a completely unrelated short domain. The second option is easier to remember and aligns with the business name. The owner registers it using a company-controlled email address, enables multi-factor authentication, records the renewal date and points the DNS to a hosted e-commerce platform. The store then uses the same business name, logo and contact information across its website, order emails and social accounts.

Create a domain and identity plan for a hypothetical online store. Include three possible domain names, your preferred option with a reason, the intended domain extension, who should control the registrar account, renewal controls, and five identity elements that should remain consistent across the customer's experience.

## Feedback / Explanation
1. A registrar manages domain registration; a hosting provider supplies infrastructure that serves the website or application.
2. The business could lose or struggle to recover control if the relationship ends or the individual becomes unavailable.
3. DNS directs a domain name to the correct internet service or server.
4. HTTPS encrypts data in transit between browser and site, but it does not by itself prove that the merchant is legitimate or eliminate all security risks.

## Key Takeaways
- Choose a domain that is clear, memorable and aligned with the business.
- Understand the separate roles of registrar, DNS, hosting and platform provider.
- Keep domain ownership, recovery and renewal under appropriate business control.
- Use strong account security and HTTPS.
- Treat store identity as part of customer trust and operational professionalism.
