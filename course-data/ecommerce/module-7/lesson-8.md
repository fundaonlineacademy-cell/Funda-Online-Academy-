# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 7: Online Security & Fraud Awareness
### Lesson 8: Incident Response, Reporting & Business Continuity

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Recognise when a security event should be treated as an incident.
2. Apply a basic incident-response sequence.
3. Preserve useful information during an investigation.
4. Explain why communication and escalation matter during incidents.
5. Distinguish incident response from business continuity and recovery.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Security controls reduce risk but cannot guarantee that incidents will never occur. A compromised account, exposed customer file, suspicious payment change or unavailable store may still happen. The quality of the response can significantly affect the final impact. Staff should know how to report incidents quickly and avoid actions that destroy evidence or worsen the problem.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Security event** — An observed occurrence that may or may not represent a security problem.
- **Security incident** — An event that threatens confidentiality, integrity, availability or business operations.
- **Containment** — Action taken to limit further harm.
- **Eradication** — Removal of the cause of an incident where appropriate.
- **Recovery** — Restoring normal trusted operation.
- **Business continuity** — Maintaining essential business functions during disruption.
- **Post-incident review** — Structured analysis of what happened and what should improve.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Report quickly
Staff should know where to report suspicious activity. A lost device, unexpected administrator login, accidental customer-data disclosure or unauthorised payment change should not be hidden because the staff member fears blame.

### 2. Verify and contain
Authorised responders should confirm what is happening and take proportionate containment steps. Depending on the incident, this may include disabling a compromised account, pausing a risky integration or isolating an affected device. Staff should avoid improvised technical actions beyond their authority.

### 3. Preserve evidence
Useful evidence can include timestamps, screenshots, system logs, order references, messages and affected accounts. Records should be preserved accurately rather than edited to make the event look better.

### 4. Communicate through approved channels
Incidents may require internal leadership, technical providers, payment partners, legal/compliance support or customer communication. Messages should be accurate and controlled. Speculation can create confusion.

### 5. Recover safely
Restoring service should include checking that the cause has been addressed. Simply turning a compromised account back on without changing credentials or reviewing access may reintroduce the problem.

### 6. Maintain business continuity
The business should identify critical functions such as receiving orders, accessing customer support, processing refunds and communicating with customers. Continuity plans define how essential work can continue or be paused safely during disruption.

### 7. Learn afterwards
A post-incident review should identify root cause, impact, response quality and corrective actions. The aim is improvement, not blame alone.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A staff member notices an administrator login from an unfamiliar location followed by a change to payout settings. The account is escalated immediately. Authorised staff secure access, verify payment settings, preserve relevant logs and review whether transactions were affected before normal administration resumes.

## Practical Activity
Create a one-page incident-response card with reporting contacts, five examples of incidents, first actions, evidence to preserve and escalation points.

## Knowledge Check
1. What is containment?
2. Why should staff preserve evidence?
3. Why can speculation be harmful during an incident?
4. What is business continuity?
5. What is the purpose of a post-incident review?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Limiting further harm while the incident is investigated.
2. Evidence supports accurate investigation and decisions.
3. Unverified claims can create confusion and poor decisions.
4. Maintaining essential functions during disruption.
5. To understand what happened and improve controls and response.

## Key Takeaways
- Security incidents should be reported promptly.
- Containment, evidence preservation and controlled communication matter.
- Recovery should restore trusted operation, not just availability.
- Business continuity plans support essential work during disruption.
- Incidents should lead to measurable improvement.
