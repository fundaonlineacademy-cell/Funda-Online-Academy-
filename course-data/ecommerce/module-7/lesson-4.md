# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 7: Online Security & Fraud Awareness
### Lesson 4: Payment Security Awareness & Safe Transaction Handling

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Explain why payment information requires strong protection.
2. Distinguish approved payment systems from unsafe workarounds.
3. Recognise sensitive payment information staff should not collect unnecessarily.
4. Apply safe payment-verification practices.
5. Escalate suspected payment-security incidents.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Payment systems are attractive targets because they connect directly to money and customer trust. Most online stores should rely on reputable payment providers and approved checkout flows rather than manually handling sensitive card information. Staff who understand the limits of their role are less likely to create avoidable payment risk.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Payment provider** — A service that supports or processes electronic payments.
- **Sensitive payment data** — Information such as full card credentials that requires strong protection.
- **Tokenisation** — Replacing sensitive payment data with a non-sensitive reference used by systems.
- **Payment verification** — Confirming transaction status through an approved source.
- **Chargeback** — A payment dispute process initiated through the card/payment ecosystem.
- **Payment incident** — A suspected security or integrity problem involving payment systems or information.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Use approved payment flows
Customers should enter sensitive payment information into the approved payment interface. Staff should not ask customers to send full card numbers, security codes or passwords through ordinary email, chat or social media.

### 2. Verify status from authoritative systems
A screenshot or customer statement is not the same as a verified payment record. Staff should confirm paid, pending, failed or refunded status through the store or payment provider's authorised systems.

### 3. Minimise sensitive-data handling
The less sensitive payment data the business directly handles, the smaller the exposure created by staff mistakes or insecure storage. Where the payment provider tokenises or hosts payment details, staff should use those supported features rather than creating manual copies.

### 4. Recognise suspicious payment situations
Warning signs can include repeated failed attempts, unusual requests to redirect refunds, pressure to process payment outside approved systems or unexpected changes to payment integration settings. These signs require review, not automatic accusations.

### 5. Escalate security concerns
If staff suspect that payment credentials, administrator access or provider settings have been compromised, normal transaction processing may need to pause while authorised people investigate. Staff should preserve relevant records and follow the incident process.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A customer says their card was charged but the store shows no paid order. Staff do not ask the customer to send full card details. Instead, they collect the minimum transaction references needed and check the authorised payment dashboard or escalate to the payment provider through the approved support route.

## Practical Activity
Create a payment-security do/don't table covering customer communication, screenshots, card data, refunds, provider dashboards and escalation.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why should full card details not be requested through ordinary chat?
2. What is the strongest source for payment status?
3. What does tokenisation do conceptually?
4. Why is data minimisation useful?
5. What should happen if payment integration settings appear compromised?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Ordinary channels are not appropriate for sensitive payment credentials.
2. The approved store or payment-provider system.
3. It replaces sensitive data with a reference used by systems.
4. Handling less sensitive data reduces exposure.
5. Escalate and follow the incident process rather than continuing blindly.

## Key Takeaways
- Sensitive payment information should remain inside approved payment systems.
- Staff should verify transactions through authoritative records.
- Data minimisation reduces payment-security exposure.
- Unusual payment activity should be reviewed carefully.
- Suspected compromise requires prompt escalation.
