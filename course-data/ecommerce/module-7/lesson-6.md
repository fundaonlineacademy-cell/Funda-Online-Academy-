# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 7: Online Security & Fraud Awareness
### Lesson 6: Fraud Indicators, Suspicious Orders & Chargeback Awareness

**Estimated study time:** 35–40 minutes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
1. Identify common fraud indicators in e-commerce orders.
2. Explain why indicators are not proof of fraud by themselves.
3. Apply a structured order-review process.
4. Explain the basic purpose of chargebacks.
5. Balance fraud controls with fair treatment of legitimate customers.

## Why This Lesson Matters
Fraud can cause lost goods, payment reversals and operational costs. At the same time, overly aggressive fraud controls can reject genuine customers. Good fraud awareness therefore focuses on patterns, evidence and escalation rather than assumptions about a customer's identity, location or behaviour.

## Key Terms / Vocabulary
- **Fraud indicator** — A signal that may justify additional review.
- **False positive** — A legitimate transaction incorrectly flagged as suspicious.
- **Chargeback** — A payment dispute process that may reverse a card transaction under applicable payment rules.
- **Order review** — A structured assessment of available transaction and risk information.
- **Velocity** — Unusually high frequency of transactions or attempts within a period.
- **Evidence package** — Relevant records assembled to respond to a dispute or investigation.

## Main Lesson Content
### 1. Look for combinations of signals
Possible indicators include repeated failed payment attempts, unusually high order values, sudden address changes, inconsistent customer information, unusual transaction velocity or requests to redirect delivery after purchase. One signal alone may have an innocent explanation.

### 2. Use approved risk tools
Payment providers and e-commerce platforms may provide fraud-risk signals. Staff should use these systems consistently and understand what the risk labels mean rather than inventing their own unsupported rules.

### 3. Review without stereotyping
Fraud controls should focus on transaction evidence. A customer's surname, nationality, language or neighbourhood should not be treated as proof of fraud. Evidence-based review reduces unfair decisions and false positives.

### 4. Understand chargebacks
A chargeback can occur when a cardholder disputes a transaction through the payment system. The merchant may need to provide evidence such as the order record, payment information available through approved systems, proof of fulfilment and customer communication. The exact process depends on the payment provider and card scheme.

### 5. Preserve useful records
Accurate order, delivery and communication records can support investigation. Staff should not fabricate evidence, alter records after the fact or submit unrelated personal information.

### 6. Escalate uncertain high-risk cases
High-value or unusual orders may require authorised review before dispatch. The goal is to reduce risk without automatically rejecting every unusual transaction.

## Worked Example / Demonstration
A new customer places a high-value order after several failed payment attempts and then requests a different delivery address immediately after approval. These signals justify additional review. Staff verify the payment and order information through approved systems and escalate according to the fraud-review procedure rather than accusing the customer.

## Practical Activity
Create a fraud-review checklist with five indicators, three verification steps and two escalation triggers. Include one example of a false positive.

## Knowledge Check
1. Why is one fraud indicator not proof by itself?
2. What is a false positive?
3. What is a chargeback?
4. Why should fraud controls avoid stereotypes?
5. What records may help in a payment dispute?

## Feedback / Explanation
1. Individual signals can have legitimate explanations.
2. A genuine transaction incorrectly flagged as suspicious.
3. A payment dispute process that may reverse a transaction.
4. Decisions should be based on transaction evidence and risk, not protected characteristics or assumptions.
5. Order, fulfilment, delivery and customer-communication records may be relevant.

## Key Takeaways
- Fraud indicators require context and evidence.
- False positives can harm legitimate customers.
- Chargebacks require accurate, preserved transaction records.
- Fraud review should be consistent and evidence-based.
- High-risk cases may require escalation before fulfilment.
