# E-commerce & Online Store
## Module 7: Online Security & Fraud Awareness
### Summative Assessment Bank — 52 Questions

## Assessment Rules
- **Question bank:** 52 multiple-choice questions.
- **Coverage:** All 8 Module 7 lessons.
- **Assessment focus:** Security fundamentals, access control, phishing, payment safety, privacy, fraud awareness, technical controls and incident response.
- **Question design:** One best answer per question.
- **Purpose:** Confirm that the learner can recognise and reduce common e-commerce security risks safely and responsibly.

## Questions 1–6 — Lesson 1: Security Fundamentals

### Q1
Which security objective is affected if customer data is viewed by an unauthorised person?
A. Confidentiality
B. Availability
C. Packaging
D. Conversion

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Confidentiality protects information from unauthorised access.

### Q2
Which objective is affected if product prices are changed without authority?
A. Integrity
B. Availability only
C. SEO
D. Fulfilment

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Integrity protects information from unauthorised change.

### Q3
Which objective is affected if the store becomes unavailable during a major sales period?
A. Availability
B. Confidentiality only
C. Product quality
D. Margin

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Availability means legitimate users can access systems when required.

### Q4
What is risk?
A. The possibility and impact of harm occurring
B. A guaranteed incident
C. A product discount
D. A customer return

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Risk considers both likelihood and impact.

### Q5
Why is security a shared responsibility?
A. Staff behaviour and business processes can create or reduce risk
B. Only developers can affect security
C. Customers control admin accounts
D. Security applies only to checkout

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Many e-commerce roles handle systems and information.

### Q6
Which is the strongest security approach?
A. Use several complementary controls
B. Depend on one password only
C. Ignore updates
D. Give everyone administrator access

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Layered controls reduce dependence on one defence.

## Questions 7–12 — Lesson 2: Passwords, MFA & Access Control

### Q7
What is the strongest password practice?
A. Reuse one strong password everywhere
B. Use unique passwords for different services
C. Share the owner password with staff
D. Write passwords publicly

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Unique passwords reduce credential-reuse risk.

### Q8
What does MFA help protect against?
A. A stolen password being sufficient by itself
B. Product returns
C. Courier delays
D. SEO problems

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** MFA requires an additional verification factor.

### Q9
A warehouse picker needs only fulfilment access. What principle should guide permissions?
A. Least privilege
B. Full administrator rights
C. Shared account access
D. No authentication

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Access should match the user's legitimate role.

### Q10
Why should former employees lose access promptly?
A. Their business need for access has ended
B. It changes stock
C. It improves social engagement
D. It increases margin

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Unnecessary active accounts create avoidable risk.

### Q11
What is a major weakness of shared administrator accounts?
A. Poor accountability
B. Better audit trails
C. Stronger MFA
D. Lower risk

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Shared accounts make actions difficult to attribute.

### Q12
What should an access review ask?
A. Whether each user's permissions remain necessary
B. Whether all users can be admins
C. Whether passwords can be removed
D. Whether customers can access the dashboard

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Reviews identify stale or excessive access.

## Questions 13–18 — Lesson 3: Phishing & Social Engineering

### Q13
Which message is most suspicious?
A. An unexpected urgent request to log in through an unfamiliar link
B. A normal internal message through the approved system
C. A customer order confirmation
D. A planned staff notice

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Unexpected urgency and unfamiliar login links are common phishing signals.

### Q14
What is the safest way to verify a suspicious supplier request?
A. Use an independently known contact route
B. Reply only to the suspicious email
C. Follow its link
D. Pay first, verify later

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Independent verification avoids relying on the potentially compromised channel.

### Q15
What is social engineering?
A. Manipulating people into bypassing controls
B. Building social-media campaigns
C. Writing product descriptions
D. Customer retention

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Social engineering targets human trust and behaviour.

### Q16
A message says “your account closes in 10 minutes unless you enter your password here.” What is the best response?
A. Verify through the known service independently
B. Enter the password immediately
C. Forward the password to support
D. Disable MFA

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Urgent login requests should be independently verified.

### Q17
Why should payment-detail changes receive strong scrutiny?
A. Fraudulent changes can redirect funds
B. They affect only product photos
C. They are always legitimate
D. They change customer reviews

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Payment redirection can create direct financial loss.

### Q18
What should happen after staff receive a convincing phishing message?
A. Report it through the security process
B. Delete it silently only
C. Share it widely without warning
D. Reply with credentials

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Reporting helps protect other users and investigate exposure.

## Questions 19–24 — Lesson 4: Payment Security Awareness

### Q19
Which information should customer-service staff avoid collecting through ordinary chat?
A. Full card credentials
B. Order number
C. Product SKU
D. Tracking number

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Sensitive payment credentials should stay within approved payment systems.

### Q20
Why is a customer screenshot weak payment evidence?
A. It is not the authoritative transaction record
B. Screenshots are always fraudulent
C. Screenshots change stock
D. Screenshots increase VAT

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Approved payment systems should confirm transaction state.

### Q21
What is tokenisation conceptually?
A. Replacing sensitive payment data with a reference
B. Increasing product price
C. Changing a customer's address
D. Creating a marketing coupon

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Tokenisation reduces direct use of raw sensitive data.

### Q22
Which is a suspicious payment request?
A. “Refund me to a completely different account outside your normal process.”
B. “Please confirm whether my order is paid.”
C. “What payment methods do you accept?”
D. “Can I have a receipt?”

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Requests to bypass normal refund processes require review.

### Q23
Why should staff use approved payment provider dashboards?
A. They provide authoritative and controlled transaction information
B. They eliminate every fraud risk
C. They replace all customer service
D. They allow passwords to be shared

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Approved systems support secure verification.

### Q24
Suspected payment compromise should trigger:
A. Incident escalation and investigation
B. More customer data collection
C. Ignoring the issue
D. Automatic dispatch of all orders

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Payment incidents require prompt controlled response.

## Questions 25–32 — Lesson 5: Customer Data, Privacy & POPIA Awareness

### Q25
Which is personal information in a normal e-commerce context?
A. Customer delivery address
B. Generic public product category
C. Warehouse shelf label
D. Store opening hours

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** A delivery address relates to an identifiable customer.

### Q26
What does data minimisation require?
A. Collect and use only information relevant to the purpose
B. Collect everything available
C. Publish customer data
D. Keep every record forever

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Unnecessary data increases privacy and security risk.

### Q27
Why should customer-service staff use approved systems?
A. They support controlled access and record handling
B. Personal spreadsheets are always safer
C. Approval removes every risk
D. It changes product margins

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Managed systems support organisational controls.

### Q28
A warehouse employee can view all marketing profiles and refund history although they need only shipping information. What principle is weak?
A. Least privilege
B. Availability
C. SEO
D. Merchandising

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Access exceeds the person's job need.

### Q29
What should happen after an invoice is accidentally sent to the wrong customer?
A. Report and assess the incident
B. Hide it
C. Delete all records
D. Send more invoices

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Possible privacy compromises require prompt assessment.

### Q30
Why can keeping unnecessary personal data be risky?
A. It increases exposure without adding legitimate value
B. It always increases revenue
C. It improves fulfilment automatically
D. It removes privacy obligations

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Data without a purpose creates avoidable risk.

### Q31
Who should handle formal privacy-rights requests?
A. The business's approved privacy process or authorised role
B. Any staff member improvising
C. A courier
D. A customer reviewer

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Privacy requests should be handled consistently and lawfully.

### Q32
Which is strongest privacy practice?
A. Purpose-based collection, limited access and prompt incident reporting
B. Open access for all staff
C. Public customer spreadsheets
D. Permanent unmanaged copies

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** These controls reduce privacy risk across the data lifecycle.

## Questions 33–38 — Lesson 6: Fraud Indicators & Chargebacks

### Q33
Which combination most reasonably justifies extra review?
A. Repeated failed payments, high-value order and immediate address-change request
B. One normal low-value order
C. A customer using a common email provider
D. A customer asking for tracking

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Multiple unusual transaction signals increase risk.

### Q34
Why should an unusual delivery location not automatically prove fraud?
A. Legitimate customers can have unusual circumstances
B. All locations have equal risk always
C. Location is irrelevant to delivery
D. Fraud never occurs

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Risk decisions require context and evidence.

### Q35
What is a false positive?
A. A legitimate order incorrectly blocked or flagged
B. A confirmed fraudulent order
C. A correct refund
D. A successful delivery

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Fraud controls can incorrectly flag genuine customers.

### Q36
What can help respond to a chargeback?
A. Accurate order, fulfilment and delivery evidence
B. Fabricated screenshots
C. Edited records after the dispute
D. Unrelated personal information

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Genuine transaction evidence supports dispute handling.

### Q37
Why should fraud outcomes be recorded?
A. To improve consistency and review rules over time
B. To identify customer race
C. To increase product weight
D. To change SEO

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Recorded outcomes support learning and auditability.

### Q38
Which is the strongest fraud principle?
A. Review transaction evidence without stereotyping
B. Reject all new customers
C. Trust every unusual order automatically
D. Approve every high-value order without checks

**Correct answer:** A
**Rationale:** Evidence-based review balances security and fairness.

## Questions 51–52 — Integrated Scenarios

### Q51
A staff member clicks a suspicious login link and enters the store administrator password. Soon afterwards, payout settings change. What is the strongest response?
A. Ignore it because MFA may help
B. Report immediately, secure the affected account through approved methods, review payout settings and logs, preserve evidence and follow the incident-response process
C. Delete the suspicious email and continue normally
D. Ask customers for card details

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** The scenario requires account security, payment protection, evidence preservation and incident response.

### Q52
A high-value order is flagged because of several failed payment attempts. The customer then asks for delivery to a different address. What is the best response?
A. Reject automatically because the customer is suspicious
B. Use approved risk information, verify transaction details, escalate if required and document the decision without stereotyping
C. Dispatch immediately without review
D. Request the customer's password

**Correct answer:** B
**Rationale:** Fraud controls should be evidence-based, proportionate and documented.
