# Carpentry — Module 4: Measurement, Marking & Technical Accuracy

## Module purpose
Develop reliable measurement, setting-out and marking skills so learners can translate job requirements into accurate timber components while controlling cumulative error, waste and rework.

## Standard applied to every teaching lesson
Each lesson will contain: lesson identification; 30–45 minute study estimate; 3–5 measurable learning outcomes; Why This Lesson Matters; 5–8 bold key terms with clear definitions; minimum 500 words of substantive Main Lesson Content with 700–1,000 words normally targeted; worked example/demonstration; practical activity; unique knowledge check with explanatory feedback; key takeaways; learner notes/progression; and a relevant instructional visual with caption/alt text where it improves understanding. Assessment questions must be traceable to taught content.

## 4.1 Measurement Principles, Units & Accuracy
Teach SI units used in carpentry, millimetres and metres, reading dimensions, accuracy versus precision, tolerances, estimating versus measuring, and avoiding unit-conversion errors.
Key terminology: Measurement; Dimension; Millimetre; Metre; Accuracy; Precision; Tolerance; Reference point.
Visual: tape/rule showing millimetre divisions and a correctly recorded component dimension.

## 4.2 Reading & Using Tape Measures, Rules and Measuring Tools
Teach safe and correct use of tape measures, steel rules and suitable measuring devices; reading scales; hook condition; straight measurement; internal/external dimensions; tool selection; inspection and care.
Key terminology: Tape measure; Steel rule; Scale; Graduation; Hook; Internal measurement; External measurement; Calibration/check.
Visual: labelled tape measure/rule with scale divisions and measurement examples.

## 4.3 Marking Tools, Pencil Lines, Knives & Gauges
Teach selection and use of pencils, marking knives, marking/mortise gauges and related tools; line thickness; datum faces; grain considerations; clear identification of waste side; preserving reference marks.
Key terminology: Marking knife; Marking gauge; Mortise gauge; Datum; Face side; Face edge; Waste side; Gauge line.
Visual: timber showing face-side/face-edge marks and gauge lines.

## 4.4 Squares, Straightness, Level, Plumb & Angles
Teach try/combination squares, straightedges, spirit levels and appropriate angle tools; checking square, straight, level and plumb; recognising tool error; using a reliable reference surface.
Key terminology: Square; Straightedge; Level; Plumb; Try square; Combination square; Angle; Reference surface.
Visual: square against timber plus level/plumb orientation diagram.

## 4.5 Setting Out from a Datum & Transferring Measurements
Teach establishing a datum, measuring consistently from a reference, transferring dimensions directly where appropriate, using story rods/templates where useful, and avoiding accumulated error.
Key terminology: Setting out; Datum line; Baseline; Transfer; Story rod; Template; Offset; Cumulative error.
Visual: component marked from one datum compared with chained measurements.

## 4.6 Reading Simple Drawings, Sketches & Cutting Information
Teach extracting dimensions and notes from simple carpentry sketches/drawings, recognising views and dimensions, checking missing/ambiguous information, creating a simple cutting list, and never scaling a drawing unless explicitly intended.
Key terminology: Drawing; Sketch; Plan; Elevation; Dimension line; Cutting list; Scale; Specification.
Visual: simple dimensioned wooden-frame drawing linked to a cutting list.

## 4.7 Marking Multiple Components & Controlling Cumulative Error
Teach batch marking, pairing/matching components, using stops/templates, checking first-off components, measuring from a common datum, and how small repeated errors can distort assemblies.
Key terminology: Batch; First-off; Repeatability; Stop; Pairing; Cumulative error; Verification; Consistency.
Visual: several rails/stiles marked together with matched component identifiers.

## 4.8 Final Measurement Checks, Tolerances & Quality Control
Teach pre-cut verification, measure-mark-check discipline, checking diagonals for square where applicable, comparing work with specifications/tolerances, recording discrepancies, and deciding when correction is required before proceeding.
Key terminology: Verification; Diagonal check; Tolerance; Deviation; Conformance; Inspection; Rework; Quality control.
Visual: rectangular frame with diagonal measurements illustrating a squareness check.

## 4.9 Formative Assessment
15 MCQs randomly selected from a bank of at least 30 unique questions. Coverage balanced across Lessons 4.1–4.8. Pass threshold: 70%. Maximum formal attempts: 3 under the Academy rule. Questions test reading measurements, tool choice, datum use, marking practice, drawing interpretation, cumulative error and verification. Feedback supports remediation and identifies lesson areas to revisit.

## 4.10 Summative Assessment
25 MCQs randomly selected from a bank of at least 50 unique questions. Covers the full module at a stronger application/judgement level. Pass threshold: 70%. Maximum formal attempts: 3 under the Academy rule. Scenarios require learners to select appropriate measurement/marking methods, interpret dimensions, diagnose accuracy problems and choose quality-control actions. No question may depend on untaught material.

## Module quality-control gates
- Eight teaching lessons are distinct and progress from fundamental measurement to applied verification.
- No lesson is a duplicate of another module.
- Terminology is lesson-specific and displayed in bold in the learner interface.
- Main content must meet the Academy depth standard before publication.
- Knowledge checks are unique to each lesson.
- Visuals must teach or clarify a concept rather than serve as decoration.
- Technical and safety statements must be accurate and should defer to manufacturer instructions/workplace procedures where tool-specific requirements vary.
- Assessment bank mapping must identify the source lesson and learning outcome for every formal question.
