# Carpentry Module 3 — Power Tools & Safe Operating Practice

## Premium curriculum build standard

Module purpose: develop safe, accurate and professional decision-making around portable and workshop power tools used in carpentry. Content must be taught with manufacturer instructions, workplace procedures and South African occupational safety requirements in mind. Learners must never be encouraged to bypass guards or safety devices.

### 3.1 Power Tools: Purpose, Selection & Limitations
Learning outcomes: distinguish common carpentry power tools; select tools according to operation/material; recognise tool limitations; explain why manufacturer ratings and instructions matter.
Key terminology: **Power tool**, **Rated capacity**, **No-load speed**, **Accessory**, **Duty cycle**, **Compatibility**, **Manufacturer instructions**.
Main-content scope: power-tool categories; matching tool to task; capacity and material considerations; electrical/battery/pneumatic differences; accessory compatibility; environmental limitations; professional decision-making.
Worked example: selecting between a circular saw, jigsaw and mitre saw for different cutting requirements.
Practical activity: build a tool-selection matrix for five carpentry operations.
Knowledge check focus: choosing a tool because it is appropriate rather than merely available.
Visual: labelled group of common carpentry power tools.

### 3.2 Electrical, Battery & Cord Safety
Learning outcomes: identify electrical and battery hazards; perform visual pre-use checks; manage cords and batteries safely; respond appropriately to defects.
Terminology: **Insulation**, **Power lead**, **Plug**, **Battery pack**, **Charger**, **Residual-current protection**, **Defect**.
Content scope: visual inspections; damaged leads/plugs; moisture; cable routing; battery damage/heat; correct chargers; removing defective equipment from service; workplace reporting.
Example: damaged cable discovered before a cutting operation.
Activity: create a pre-use electrical/battery inspection checklist.
Knowledge check: correct response to a damaged lead or abnormal battery condition.
Visual: safe versus damaged power-tool lead/battery examples.

### 3.3 Circular Saws & Straight Cutting
Learning outcomes: identify circular-saw components; explain guarding and blade-selection principles; plan straight cuts; recognise kickback and other hazards.
Terminology: **Circular saw**, **Blade guard**, **Riving knife**, **Kerf**, **Rip cut**, **Crosscut**, **Kickback**, **Shoe/base plate**.
Content scope: saw anatomy; blade condition/rating; material support; cutting line and setup; guard function; portable versus bench-saw principles; push sticks and riving knives where applicable; post-cut checks.
South African regulatory anchor: Driven Machinery Regulations 2015, regulation 4, including circular-saw guarding and bench-saw provisions.
Example: planning a straight rip cut while supporting the work and offcut correctly.
Activity: identify safety-critical components from a labelled saw diagram before any practical use.
Knowledge check: why a guard must never be defeated or tied open.
Visual: labelled circular saw and guarded bench saw.

### 3.4 Jigsaws, Reciprocating Saws & Curved Cutting
Learning outcomes: compare reciprocating cutting tools; choose blades; secure work; plan curved/internal cuts safely.
Terminology: **Jigsaw**, **Reciprocating saw**, **Blade stroke**, **Orbital action**, **Radius**, **Workpiece support**, **Splintering**.
Content scope: applications and limitations; blade selection; workpiece stability; line visibility; curve radius; starting/finishing cuts; avoiding forced feed.
Example: selecting a jigsaw blade and approach for a curved plywood component.
Activity: plan a curved cut on a paper/template exercise before tool operation.
Knowledge check: why forcing a blade through a tight radius is poor practice.
Visual: jigsaw blade types and curved-cut setup.

### 3.5 Drills, Drivers & Boring Operations
Learning outcomes: distinguish drilling/driving; select bits; control depth/alignment; recognise binding and workpiece hazards.
Terminology: **Drill**, **Driver**, **Chuck**, **Drill bit**, **Pilot hole**, **Countersink**, **Torque**, **Boring**.
Content scope: bit/material matching; pilot holes; countersinking; depth control; torque; clamping; breakthrough; battery/electrical safety; avoiding damaged accessories.
Example: pilot drilling and countersinking before installing a screw in timber.
Activity: match common bits to appropriate carpentry operations.
Knowledge check: purpose of a pilot hole in a splitting-prone timber component.
Visual: common drill/driver bits with labels.

### 3.6 Routers & Edge/Forming Operations
Learning outcomes: explain router applications; identify cutters and guards; plan feed/control; recognise high-speed cutter hazards.
Terminology: **Router**, **Router bit**, **Collet**, **Profile**, **Template**, **Fence**, **Feed direction**, **Plunge**.
Content scope: edge profiles, grooves and recesses; cutter compatibility/condition; secure installation; depth setting; workpiece restraint; fences/templates; controlled feed; multiple-pass planning; isolation before adjustment.
Example: planning a shallow edge profile using staged passes rather than one excessive cut.
Activity: design a router-operation planning card without operating machinery.
Knowledge check: why adjustments/accessory changes require appropriate isolation.
Visual: labelled router components and common profile examples.

### 3.7 Sanders & Powered Surface Preparation
Learning outcomes: compare sanding machines; select abrasive sequence; explain dust/guarding concerns; prevent surface damage.
Terminology: **Orbital sander**, **Belt sander**, **Disc sander**, **Abrasive grit**, **Scratch pattern**, **Dust extraction**, **Surface preparation**.
Content scope: machine types; grit progression; grain direction; pressure and dwell; extraction; abrasive condition; guarding of fixed sanding machinery; edge rounding and over-sanding risks.
South African regulatory anchor: Driven Machinery Regulations 2015 regulation 8 requires specified guarding arrangements for sanding machinery.
Example: choosing a sanding sequence for a prepared timber panel.
Activity: inspect sample surfaces/images and diagnose likely sanding errors.
Knowledge check: why excessive pressure is not a substitute for correct abrasive selection.
Visual: grit progression and common sanding defects.

### 3.8 Power-Tool Setup, Pre-use Inspection & Shutdown
Learning outcomes: perform systematic pre-use planning; recognise unsafe conditions; explain safe shutdown/storage; integrate safety with quality.
Terminology: **Pre-use inspection**, **Isolation**, **Guarding**, **Workholding**, **Serviceable**, **Shutdown**, **Lockout**, **Housekeeping**.
Content scope: tool/accessory inspection; guards; switches; power source; workholding; work area; PPE determined by risk; trial/setup considerations; abnormal vibration/noise; stopping and isolating; cleaning/storage; defect reporting.
Example: complete go/no-go decision after finding a missing/damaged safety component.
Activity: use a structured inspection sheet on a tool only when safe and authorised; otherwise complete from supplied images.
Knowledge check: what to do when a safety-critical defect is identified.
Visual: pre-use inspection sequence infographic.

### 3.9 Formative Assessment
15 randomly selected MCQs from a bank of at least 30. Pass mark 70%. Covers lessons 3.1–3.8. Questions must be traceable to taught content and include selection, hazard recognition, components, inspection and application scenarios.

### 3.10 Summative Assessment
25 randomly selected MCQs from a bank of at least 50. Pass mark 70%. Covers the complete module at stronger application/judgement level. Must not simply duplicate formative questions.

## Quality controls
- Each teaching lesson will contain 5–8 meaningful bold-defined terms.
- Main lesson content minimum 500 words; normal target 700–1,000 substantive words excluding outcomes, vocabulary, activities and checks.
- Every lesson receives a unique worked example, practical activity, knowledge check with explanatory feedback, takeaways and relevant educational visual/caption.
- Technical instructions must remain consistent with manufacturer guidance and applicable workplace rules.
- Safety-critical claims are checked against authoritative sources before publication.
